# Korea Jeonse Safety Calculator

Korea's **jeonse** (전세) system is unique: instead of monthly rent, tenants pay a large lump-sum deposit — typically 50–80% of the property's market value — and live rent-free. At the end of the contract, the full deposit is returned.

The critical question before signing is: **can you actually get your deposit back at the end?** The answer depends not on the landlord's character, but on the property's actual value versus total secured claims against it.

## How Safety Is Calculated

**Safety formula**: (Jeonse deposit + senior-priority mortgage) ÷ Market value × 100

| Ratio | Assessment | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ≤70% | ✅ Safe | Deposit recoverable even after auction |
| 70–80% | ⚠️ Caution | Risk of shortfall if auction price falls |
| 80–90% | 🔶 Risky | High probability of jeonse fraud scenario |
| >90% | 🚨 High risk | Strongly reconsider signing |

<Callout type="warning">
**80% threshold**: KB Kookmin Bank and the Korea Real Estate Board's "Safe Jeonse" apps treat 80% as the safety cutoff. Above 80%, always consider purchasing a Jeonse deposit return guarantee (HUG or SGI).
</Callout>

## Fraud Prevention Checklist

| Item | How to Verify |
|---|---|
| Property registry (등기부등본) | Pull on the day of signing AND on the day of final payment |
| Senior-priority mortgages | "Eul-gu" (乙區) section of the registry |
| Building permit records | Check for unauthorized construction |
| Landlord ID verification | Match ID to registry owner |
| Residency registration & notarization | Do both on the same day as final payment |
| Jeonse guarantee insurance | Apply for deposit protection |
