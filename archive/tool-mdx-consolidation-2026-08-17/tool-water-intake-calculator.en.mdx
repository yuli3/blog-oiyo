# Daily Water Intake Calculator

"Drink 2 liters of water a day" is a guideline most people have heard. But that number changes depending on **your weight, activity level, and the temperature outside**. A 65 kg desk worker and an 80 kg athlete have very different hydration needs.

## Calculation Formula

**Base amount** = Body weight (kg) × 30 ml  
**Activity bonus**: Light exercise +350 ml / Heavy exercise or physical labor +700 ml  
**Temperature bonus**: Warm (20–30°C) +150 ml / Hot (30°C+) +350 ml  
**Diuretic offset**: 1 coffee +150 ml / 1 alcoholic drink +250 ml

<Callout type="tip">
**Does coffee count against hydration?** Caffeine has a mild diuretic effect. An Americano-sized cup of coffee prompts roughly 150 ml of additional fluid loss — so add that back into your target.
</Callout>

## Signs of Dehydration

| Sign | Implication |
|---|---|
| Dark yellow urine | Early sign of insufficient fluids |
| Dry mouth and headache | Mild dehydration (−1–2% body water) |
| Dizziness and trouble concentrating | Moderate dehydration (−3–5%) |
| Severe fatigue and muscle cramps | Severe dehydration (rehydrate immediately) |

## Optimal Drinking Schedule

| Time | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Immediately after waking | 200 ml — replenish overnight fluid loss |
| 30 min before meals | 200 ml — promotes satiety, aids digestion |
| Mid-morning and mid-afternoon | 200 ml × 2–3 times |
| Before, during, and after exercise | 150–300 ml each |
| 1 hour before bed | ≤150 ml — avoid disrupting sleep with nocturia |

<Callout type="note">
Drinking 200 ml at a time throughout the day absorbs better than downing 500 ml at once. Setting a reminder every 2 hours for a glass of water is the most effective habit-building approach.
</Callout>
