# Chapter 2. Scope and Schedule: The Architecture of Time

A project without a clear scope is like a ship without a destination. <mark><strong>Scope Management</strong></mark> ensures that the project includes all the work required, and _only_ the work required, to complete the project successfully. Once the scope is defined, we translate it into a **Schedule.**

---

## 1. WBS: Breaking it Down

The **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)** is a hierarchical decomposition of the total scope of work. It turns a massive mountain of a project into a organized list of tasks.

## 2. The Critical Path Method (CPM)

In any project, some tasks can be done in parallel, while others _must_ follow a specific sequence. The <mark><strong>Critical Path</strong></mark> is the longest sequence of tasks that must be completed on time for the project to finish on its due date.

## 3. Fighting Scope Creep

**Scope Creep** is the uncontrolled growth of a project's scope without adjustments to time, cost, and resources. It is the number one project killer.

- **The Baseline**: Always establish a "Scope Baseline" (the approved plan).
- **Change Control**: Any request for "just one more feature" must go through a formal approval process.
- **The Power of 'No'**: A great project manager knows that saying no to out-of-scope requests is essential to protecting the team's focus.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The Critical Path Warning**: If any task on the Critical Path is delayed by even one day, the
  entire project's completion date will shift unless you can "crash" (add resources) or "fast-track"
  (do tasks in parallel) later stages.
</Callout>
