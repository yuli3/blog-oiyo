## Why Do We Have Time Zones?

Before the 19th century, every town kept its own local time based on the sun's position (Solar Time). When the sun was highest in the sky, it was noon. This worked fine until railroads connected distant cities. A train measuring time by Boston's noon would arrive in New York confused, because New York's noon happens a few minutes later.

To standardize schedules, Sir Sandford Fleming proposed **Standard Time Zones** in 1879. The world was divided into 24 zones, each roughly 15 degrees of longitude apart, representing one hour of the Earth's rotation.

## What is UTC?

**UTC (Coordinated Universal Time)** is the primary time standard by which the world regulates clocks and time. It is effectively the successor to GMT (Greenwich Mean Time).
*   It is not a time zone itself, but a standard.
*   Time zones are expressed as offsets from UTC (e.g., New York is UTC-5, Seoul is UTC+9).

## Daylight Saving Time (DST)

Many countries adjust their clocks forward by one hour in the summer to extend daylight in the evenings. This is called **Daylight Saving Time**.
*   **"Spring Forward"**: Clocks move ahead 1 hour (lose sleep).
*   **"Fall Back"**: Clocks move back 1 hour (gain sleep).

Not all countries observe DST. Most of Asia (including Korea, Japan, China) and Africa do not change their clocks, keeping a consistent UTC offset year-round.
