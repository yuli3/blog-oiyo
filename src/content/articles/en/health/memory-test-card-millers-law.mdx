# The Magical Number Seven

In 1956, cognitive psychologist George A. Miller published one of the most cited papers in psychology: *"The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two."*

## The Bottleneck

Miller discovered that the human "Working Memory" (our brain's RAM) has a hard limit. We can only hold roughly **7 items** in our head at once.
- Try to memorize this: `X F B D J K P L M` (9 letters). Hard.
- Now this: `IBM CIA FBI USA` (12 letters). Easy.

## Chunking: The Hack

Why was the second list easier? Because of **Chunking**.
Your brain recognized `IBM` not as three separate letters, but as *one concept*.
- You didn't memorize 12 letters.
- You memorized 4 chunks.

## Implications

1.  **Learning**: Don't try to memorize isolated facts. Connect them into existing models (chunks).
2.  **UI Design**: Never present more than 5-7 options in a navigation menu.
3.  **Communication**: Speak in "Rule of Three". Three points are easy to hold. Ten points are noise.

We are not computers. We are pattern-matching machines with very limited RAM. Respect the limit.
