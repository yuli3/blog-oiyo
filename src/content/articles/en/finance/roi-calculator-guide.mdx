## Return on Investment (ROI)

ROI is a financial metric used to calculate the probability of gaining a return from an investment. It measures the amount of return on an investment relative to the investment's cost.

### How to Calculate ROI
The simplest formula for ROI is:

**ROI = (Net Profit / Cost of Investment) × 100**

Where:
- **Net Profit** = Current Value of Investment - Cost of Investment

### Example
If you buy $1,000 worth of stocks and sell them a year later for $1,200:
1. Net Profit = $1,200 - $1,000 = $200
2. ROI = ($200 / $1,000) × 100 = **20%**

## Why ROI Metrics Matter
- **Comparison:** It allows you to compare the efficiency of different investments.
- **Simplicity:** It's easy to calculate and understand.
- **Versatility:** Can be applied to stocks, real estate, business projects, and marketing campaigns.

## Limitations
- **Time Factor:** Basic ROI doesn't account for the holding period (e.g., a 20% return over 1 year is great; over 10 years, it's poor). For that, you might need *Annualized ROI*.
- **Risk:** It doesn't factor in the risk associated with the investment.
