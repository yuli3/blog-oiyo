### Introduction: Searching for the Lost Year

Anyone who has lived in Korea has experienced the mental gymnastics involved in answering "So how old are you?" Three separate calculations would run through your head: the traditional **counting age** (born equals one year old), the **year age** (current year minus birth year), and the **international age** (one year added only after your birthday). This uniquely complex system — the only country in the world to use all three simultaneously — had long caused administrative confusion and unnecessary social friction.

In 2023, Korea finally unified its legal and social age-counting method to the international standard: the **international (Korean: "man") age**. In a sense, we all got the gift of becoming officially younger. Today, alongside our age calculator, we will find our precise legal position on the timeline and use the zodiac and life milestones to locate our personal coordinates in the great river of time.

<h2>2. The History of Korean Age and Understanding the Standard</h2>

<h3>① The Philosophy of Life Respect Embedded in the Counting Age</h3>
The traditional Korean method of counting a newborn as one year old carries deep philosophical meaning: it recognizes and respects the approximately ten months the fetus spent in the womb as a living being. Although this method has been retired in law, it remains a cultural legacy that reveals our ancestors' warm regard for life.

<h3>② The Unified International Age and Administrative Convenience</h3>
Hospital visits, government documents, voting rights, and national pension eligibility now all operate on the international age standard. There are occasionally awkward situations where friends born in the same year differ in age by one year depending on their birthday, but aligning with the global standard has been a meaningful step toward reducing unnecessary administrative errors and matching international norms.

<h3>③ The 12 Earthly Branches and Zodiac Signs — East Asia's Taxonomy of Time</h3>
The animal sign determined by your birth year is a unique social code used in East Asian cultures to estimate someone's generation or test compatibility. Rat, Ox, Tiger... this classification system, passed down for thousands of years, reminds us that we are beings situated within the cycles of the cosmos and nature.
