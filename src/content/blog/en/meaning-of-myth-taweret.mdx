Taweret is a goddess in Egyptian mythology and everyday religious practice who protects pregnancy, childbirth, and young children. She appears with a form that combines powerful animal traits: the body of a hippopotamus, the limbs of a lion, and the tail of a crocodile. Her intimidating appearance is not meant simply to frighten people. It is an image of protection, made to drive away the dangers that threaten mothers and children.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Taweret is revealed more strongly through everyday protective rites than through a grand mythic narrative. In ancient Egypt, childbirth was a dangerous event, and mothers and children were understood as beings exposed to unseen threats. Amulets and images of Taweret were used to guard this vulnerable time.

Her form combines the strength of several dangerous animals. The hippopotamus was especially known for the aggression of females protecting their young, while the lion and crocodile elements add defensive force. Taweret symbolizes not gentle motherhood alone, but "protection that does not retreat," showing that even a small life inside the home deserves cosmic protection.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Taweret is an archetype of the **protective mother** and the **guardian of the threshold**. Childbirth is the threshold through which a life enters the world, while also being a moment close to the danger of death. In modern terms, she resembles the delivery room, newborn care, family protection during crisis, and guardians who stand firm for vulnerable people.

The key to reading Taweret is that care does not always appear beautiful and gentle. Some care must be strong enough to look frightening and rough. For that reason, the Taweret archetype symbolizes an ethic of protection that raises the greatest strength in order to defend the smallest life.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Why does Taweret look frightening?**

A. Her appearance is a protective image meant to repel dangers that harm mothers and children. The threatening form frightens and drives away evil.

**Q. Was Taweret worshiped in large temples?**

A. She was especially important in household amulets, everyday rites, and childbirth protection contexts rather than large state temples.
