## Overview of Numerical Reasoning

**Numerical Reasoning** is one of the core competencies measured by WorkKeys and similar US workforce readiness assessments (O*NET, ACT WorkKeys, SHRM competency frameworks). It measures the ability to think mathematically and calculate accurately in a workplace context.

On these assessments, numerical reasoning is not a test for math prodigies. **Basic calculation and data interpretation are the heart of the section.**

**4 Sub-Competencies**:

| Sub-Competency | Description |
|---------------|-------------|
| **Basic Arithmetic** | Four operations, ratios, unit conversion |
| **Basic Statistics** | Mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation |
| **Data Interpretation** | Reading and analyzing numbers in tables and graphs |
| **Chart Construction** | Converting raw data into charts |

---

## Basic Arithmetic: High-Frequency Patterns

### Percentages and Ratios

**Ratio = Part / Whole × 100**

**Percent Change = (Comparison Period − Base Period) / Base Period × 100**

Example: Revenue $1,000,000 in 2023 → $1,250,000 in 2024
Percent change = (1,250,000 − 1,000,000) / 1,000,000 × 100 = **25%**

**Absolute change vs. percent change — a classic trap**:
- Absolute change: $250,000 (difference in dollar amount)
- Percent change: 25% (relative shift)

### Rate, Distance, and Time

**Distance = Rate × Time**

**Time = Distance / Rate**

Workplace application: Output = Processing Rate × Time

### Concentration Calculations (Weighted Mix)

**Concentration (%) = Solute / Solution × 100**

Mixing problems: use weighted average

Example: 100 g of 20% saltwater + 200 g of 10% saltwater — what is the combined concentration?
→ (20×100 + 10×200) / (100+200) = (2,000+2,000)/300 = **13.3%**

## Basic Statistics

### Measures of Central Tendency

| Concept | Calculation | Key Trait |
|---------|------------|-----------|
| **Mean** | Sum / Count | Sensitive to outliers |
| **Median** | Middle value when sorted | Robust to outliers |
| **Mode** | Most frequent value | Useful for categorical data |

**Why mean alone is not enough**:

Example: [1, 2, 3, 4, 100] → Mean = 22, Median = 3

A mean of 22 is far from most values. When extreme values exist (like income distribution), the median gives a more realistic picture.

### Measures of Dispersion

**Variance**: The average squared distance of each value from the mean.

**Standard Deviation** = √Variance

A larger standard deviation means values are more spread out around the mean.

Workplace applications: quality control, performance distribution, risk assessment

## Data Analysis Strategy

### Common Chart Types

**Bar chart**: Compare magnitudes across categories

**Line graph**: Trend over time (when does it rise or fall?)

**Pie chart**: Composition ratios

**Table**: Multi-dimensional data (year × category)

### 5-Step Data Analysis Strategy

**Step 1. Read the title**: What does this chart show? (Subject, time frame, units)

**Step 2. Check the axes**: What do the horizontal and vertical axes mean, and what are the units? (thousands, millions, etc.)

**Step 3. Identify the overall trend**: Is the general direction up, down, or mixed?

**Step 4. Spot anomalies**: Sudden spikes or drops, reversals, outliers

**Step 5. Compare against answer choices**: Does the claim in each answer choice actually hold up in the chart?

---

## Common Chart Traps

### Trap 1: Absolute Value vs. Ratio Confusion

The chart shows absolute values, but an answer choice claims "has the highest ratio."

Example: A may have a larger absolute number than B, but B's share of the total could still be higher.

### Trap 2: Absolute Change vs. Percent Change

Year-over-year dollar differences (absolute change) vs. year-over-year growth rate (percent change) are not the same.

### Trap 3: Base Year Confusion

"Compared to 2020" and "compared to the prior year" are different. Always confirm which year serves as the baseline.

### Trap 4: Unit Confusion

$10,000 units vs. $1,000,000 units — always verify units before calculating!

## Habits That Reduce Calculation Errors

**1. Estimate first**: Get a rough answer before calculating precisely. If the answer choices are far apart, a quick estimate can eliminate options.

Example: 1,234 × 198 ≈ 1,200 × 200 = 240,000 (exact: 244,332)

**2. Unify units**: Keep units consistent throughout and verify the final unit.

**3. Verify with back-calculation**: Confirm using an inverse operation or a different method.

**4. Process of elimination**: Remove obviously impossible answer choices first.

**5. Question extreme answers**: If your answer seems unreasonably large or small, recalculate.

## High-Frequency Calculation Types

### Work Rate Problems

A alone takes x days; B alone takes y days. How long together?

**Daily work rate**: A = 1/x, B = 1/y, Together = 1/x + 1/y

→ Days to completion = 1 / (1/x + 1/y) = xy / (x+y)

### Average Speed

A trip from A→B at 60 mph, and B→A at 40 mph. What is the average speed for the round trip?

**Average speed = 2 / (1/60 + 1/40) = 2 / (5/120) = 48 mph**

(Not the arithmetic average of 50 mph — this is a time-weighted harmonic mean.)

### Proportional Division

Divide 120 in the ratio A:B = 3:5:
- A = 120 × 3/(3+5) = 120 × 3/8 = **45**
- B = 120 × 5/8 = **75**
