# Burnout Self-Check

"I'm still tired after resting." "I don't feel anything about my work anymore." These may sound like ordinary fatigue, but they can also be signs of burnout. The World Health Organization describes burnout as the result of chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. Burnout is commonly understood through three dimensions in Maslach's model.

## 1. Emotional Exhaustion — Running Out of Energy

This is often the most noticeable signal. You feel depleted from the morning, as if your energy is already gone before the workday even begins. If a weekend or a short break no longer helps you recover, the issue may be more than simple tiredness.

## 2. Cynicism · Depersonalization — Emotional Distance

You may become numb, detached, or cynical toward work and people. Thoughts like "Nothing will change anyway" or "I don't care what anyone says" can start to feel familiar. This is not laziness. It can be a **self-protective response** from a mind that is trying not to be hurt or overwhelmed again.

## 3. Reduced Personal Accomplishment — Feeling Ineffective

Work you used to handle confidently may start to feel difficult. Even when you achieve something, it may feel unimportant or undeserved. As your sense of competence drops, burnout can become a cycle: lower confidence makes work feel heavier, and heavier work deepens the exhaustion.

## Burnout vs Ordinary Fatigue

- **Fatigue** usually improves with rest. **Burnout** often does not recover well with rest alone.
- Fatigue is closer to a body-energy problem. Burnout is also about **meaning, control, and reward**. If you keep thinking, "I don't know why I'm doing this anymore," that is an important burnout signal.

## Practical Ways to Recover

1. **Protect the smallest unit of recovery** — A perfect vacation is not always available. A short daily recovery routine, such as a walk, a real end to the workday, or protected sleep, is often more useful.
2. **Rebuild a sense of control** — Pick even one thing on your task list that you can decide for yourself. Helplessness feeds burnout.
3. **Set boundaries** — Turn off work notifications when possible. Say "I can't take that on" when you need to. Boundaries are not selfish; they are part of sustainability.
4. **Reconnect with meaning** — Redefine what the work means to you, or reduce work that has become purely meaningless where you realistically can.
5. **Stay connected** — Isolation makes burnout worse. Telling one trusted person what is going on can be the beginning of recovery.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is burnout the same as depression?**
No. Burnout is usually tied to work or role-related exhaustion, while depression is a clinical condition that can affect life more broadly. However, they can overlap. If daily functioning becomes difficult, or if low energy, loss of motivation, sleep changes, or appetite changes continue for more than two weeks, it is a good idea to seek help from a mental health professional or medical provider.

**Q. How long does burnout last?**
It depends on the person and the situation. If the causes remain unchanged, such as overload, lack of control, or an imbalance between effort and reward, burnout can last longer. Recovery becomes more realistic when the conditions around the burnout begin to change. The key is not simply to endure more, but to change what keeps draining you.

**Q. Can this test diagnose burnout?**
No. This test is a self-awareness tool, not a medical diagnosis. It can help you reflect on your current state, but it does not replace evaluation by a qualified professional. If your distress continues or interferes with everyday life, consider speaking with a mental health professional or healthcare provider.
