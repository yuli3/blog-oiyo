### Introduction: From Theory to Action—Redesigning Your Brain Energy

So far, we have learned through Dr. Christopher Palmer’s ([Christopher M. Palmer](https://www.brainenergy.com/)) 'Brain Energy' theory that the root of mental illness lies in the functional failure of mitochondria—the energy factories within our cells. However, the question remains: "Where exactly do I start?"

No matter how sophisticated a theory is, it remains dead knowledge if it cannot change a life. Chapter 20 of _Brain Energy_, 'Metabolic Treatment Plan Approach,' starts precisely at this point. It's not just clichéd advice like "eat well and exercise," but a **Personalized Treatment Roadmap** based on biological mechanisms.

Today, we will detail the core steps of a metabolic treatment plan to fundamentally restore your mental health.

### 1. Professional Assistance: Why Clinical Guidance is Essential

A metabolic treatment plan is not just about going on a diet; it's about changing your body's 'chemical environment.' Therefore, professional guidance is absolutely necessary, especially for patients currently taking medication.

- **Interaction with Medication:** Dietary strategies (especially the ketogenic diet) or high-intensity exercise lower insulin levels and change the sensitivity of neurotransmitters. This can affect the metabolism of existing psychiatric drugs, and side effects can occur if appropriate dosage adjustments are not made.
- **Managing Objective Indicators:** You should regularly monitor blood sugar, ketone levels, inflammatory markers (CRP), and cholesterol levels to adjust the direction of treatment.
- **Psychological Safety Net:** During the process of metabolic recovery, temporary mood swings or physical discomfort (such as the keto flu) may occur. At this time, the clinician acts as a reliable guide to support the patient so they do not give up halfway.

---

### 2. Starting the Treatment Plan: The Five Pillars of Metabolic Protocol

The treatment plan proposed by Dr. Palmer is divided into five main areas. Each area is organically connected to maximize mitochondrial function.

#### ① Dietary Strategy

The most powerful tool is the **Ketogenic Diet.** By limiting carbohydrates and increasing fat intake, the brain uses 'ketone bodies' as its primary fuel instead of glucose. Ketones are much cleaner and more efficient fuel than glucose and promote mitochondrial biogenesis.

> [!TIP]
> If a strict keto diet is difficult at first, it's good to start with a 'clean diet' that cuts out processed foods and sugar, and gradually reduce carbohydrates.

#### ② Sleep and Circadian Rhythm

[Sleep deprivation](file:///Users/seuncho/coding/blog-oiyo/src/content/blog/en/sleep-circadian-rhythm-energy.mdx) puts direct oxidative stress on mitochondria. Getting plenty of sunlight during the day and sleeping in complete darkness at night—observing 'light hygiene'—is the foundation of metabolic treatment.

#### ③ Exercise Optimization

Mindlessly running a lot isn't the answer. Like [testosterone-strengthening exercises](file:///Users/seuncho/coding/blog-oiyo/src/content/blog/en/how-to-increase-testosterone.mdx) that increase hormone levels, you should combine strength training and aerobic exercise appropriate for your fitness level. Exercise improves insulin resistance and increases brain blood flow, improving energy efficiency.

#### ④ Stress Management and Psychological Stability

Chronic stress raises cortisol levels, which in turn raises blood sugar and paralyzes metabolic functions. You must secure your own stress relief outlet, such as meditation, conversation through Havruta, or solitude.

#### ⑤ Environmental Detox

External factors that attack mitochondria, such as alcohol, smoking, and additives in processed foods, must be blocked. Filling up comes after emptying out.

### 3. Utilization of Inpatient Treatment and Rehabilitation Facilities

If symptoms are so severe that it is difficult to control daily life on your own, it may be a wise choice to seek help from a professional inpatient program or rehabilitation facility.

'Metabolic psychiatry-based rehabilitation centers' are increasing, centered in the US and Europe. Here, dietary management, exercise protocols, and group counseling are provided as a package so that patients can fundamentally acquire metabolic habits. A change in perspective is needed to view inpatient facilities not as "merely isolated spaces" but as "spaces for metabolic rebirth."
