# D-Day Calculator

"D-Day" originated as military terminology. The most famous D-Day — the Normandy landings of June 6, 1944 — gave the term its iconic status. "D" simply stands for "Day," with D−1 meaning the day before and D+1 the day after.

Today, D-Day is used for personal countdowns: university entrance exams, job interviews, weddings, trips, and fitness goals. The habit of consciously tracking days to a target is especially ingrained in Korea's exam culture.

**Features of this D-Day calculator:**
- Save up to 20 D-Days simultaneously (stored in browser localStorage)
- Quick-select national holidays
- Categorize with icons (heart, star, gift, study, travel, etc.)
- Automatically calculate elapsed days (D+N)

## The Psychology of D-Day

### The Deadline Effect

Psychological research consistently shows that the clearer a deadline, the sharper focus and execution become. **Parkinson's Law** states that "work expands to fill the time available." Setting a D-Day forces backward planning from the deadline, dramatically improving efficiency.

### The Motivational Power of a Countdown

Studies show that concretely visualizing a date boosts goal attainment rates. Simply checking "D−47" each morning activates the brain's goal-pursuit circuits.

<Callout type="tip">
**Effective D-Day strategy**: Beyond the final target date (e.g., exam day), set intermediate milestone D-Days. A "100 days to exam" marker, followed by "50 days," creates checkpoints that make progress feel tangible.
</Callout>
