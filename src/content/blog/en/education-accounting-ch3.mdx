The unadjusted trial balance of [chapter 2](/en/academy-accounting-principles-ch2/) contains only the transactions exchanged during the period. At year-end, however, revenue and expenses belonging to this period remain even where no money has changed hands. The procedure that reflects them is **adjustment**. This chapter defines what the accrual basis requires and calculates the six typical adjustments with one company's numbers.

## 1. Accrual basis and cash basis

The **cash basis** records revenue when cash comes in and expenses when it goes out. The **accrual basis**, regardless of cash flows, records revenue in the period it is earned and expenses in the period the resources used to earn it are consumed. Apart from the cash flow statement, K-IFRS financial statements are all prepared on the accrual basis (K-IFRS 1001, corresponding to IAS 1).

### A. Why the accrual basis

Suppose salaries for work done in December are paid in January. Under the cash basis, December's expense moves to January, inflating December profit and depressing January's. The accrual basis records salaries as an expense of the month the work was done, so that performance can be compared period by period. The price is the effort of estimating and recording, at every year-end, "the share where cash has not yet moved".

## 2. Calculating six adjustments for one company

Suppose a December year-end company has unadjusted profit of ₩10 million. At year-end it confirms the following.

  title="Adjusting entries at 31 December (unit: ₩10,000)"
  headers={["Item", "Debit", "Credit", "Effect on profit"]}
  rows={[
    ["① 1 Oct: paid one year's insurance 120 (recorded as asset)", "Insurance expense 30", "Prepaid insurance 30", "−30"],
    ["② 1 Nov: received six months' rent 60 (recorded as liability)", "Unearned rent 20", "Rent revenue 20", "+20"],
    ["③ December salaries 150 unpaid", "Salaries expense 150", "Salaries payable 150", "−150"],
    ["④ 1 Oct: time deposit 1,000 (6% a year, paid at maturity)", "Interest receivable 15", "Interest revenue 15", "+15"],
    ["⑤ 1 Jul: equipment bought 1,200 (straight-line, 5 years, no residual value)", "Depreciation expense 120", "Accumulated depreciation 120", "−120"],
    ["⑥ Supplies bought 50 (recorded as asset), 20 left at year-end", "Supplies expense 30", "Supplies 30", "−30"],
  ]}
/>

The workings are:

- ① Three months, October to December, have passed, so $120 \times 3/12 = 30$ is expense. The remaining 90 stays as prepaid insurance until September next year.
- ② Two months, November and December, $60 \times 2/6 = 20$, move to revenue. Unearned rent of 40 is an obligation for services not yet provided.
- ④ Interest of $1{,}000 \times 6\% \times 3/12 = 15$ has already accrued. It is received at maturity, but the revenue belongs to the period elapsed.
- ⑤ Annual depreciation is $1{,}200 / 5 = 240$; for six months it is 120.
- ⑥ Supplies used are $50 - 20 = 30$.

## 3. Same transaction, different initial recording

For prepaid expenses and unearned revenue, the adjusting entry depends on **whether the cash was initially recorded as an asset (liability) or as an expense (revenue)**. The end result must be the same.

### A. When the insurance was expensed on payment

If on 1 October the entry was Dr insurance expense 120 / Cr cash 120, at year-end the nine months not yet elapsed, 90, are reversed. The adjusting entry is Dr prepaid insurance 90 / Cr insurance expense 90. The final balances are insurance expense 30 and prepaid insurance 90, the same as in ①.

### B. The test is the closing balance

Whichever method was used, the year-end result must be **elapsed portion as expense, unexpired portion as asset**. Rather than memorizing adjusting entries, it is less error-prone to decide first "what must remain at year-end" and then adjust for the difference.

## 4. Adjusted trial balance and closing

After the adjusting entries are posted to the ledger, gathering the balances again gives the **adjusted trial balance**. The statement of financial position and the income statement are prepared directly from it.

Next, **closing entries** shut the revenue and expense accounts. Revenue accounts are debited and expense accounts credited to bring their balances to zero, and the difference, profit of ₩7.05 million, is credited to retained earnings. In practice an "income summary" account is sometimes used in between. After closing, only asset, liability and equity balances remain, becoming next year's opening balances.

## 5. The limits of adjustments that involve estimates

For ①–④, a contract and dates almost fix the amount. Depreciation in ⑤ and the loss allowance are different. The figures of a 5-year useful life and zero residual value are management estimates. With a 4-year life, six months' depreciation becomes ₩1.5 million and profit falls by another ₩300,000.

<Callout type="warning">
Adjustments involving estimates can become a channel for earnings management. Lengthening useful lives or reducing bad-debt estimates boosts current profit. That is why the standards require the basis of estimates to be disclosed in the notes and changes in estimates to be reflected only in later periods, without restating the past (K-IFRS 1008, corresponding to IAS 8; see [Intermediate Accounting chapter 16](/en/academy-intermediate-accounting-ch16/)). Check not only whether the adjusted amount is right, but whether the basis of the estimate is reasonable.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

On 1 March, a year's office rent of ₩2.4 million was paid in cash and recorded entirely as rent expense. What is the adjusting entry at 31 December, and what are rent expense in this year's income statement and prepaid rent in the closing statement of financial position?

Ten months, March to December, have elapsed, so this year's expense is $240 \times 10/12 = 200$ (₩10,000), i.e. ₩2 million. Since ₩2.4 million was already expensed, the unexpired ₩400,000 is reversed: Dr prepaid rent ₩400,000 / Cr rent expense ₩400,000. Rent expense in the income statement is ₩2 million and prepaid rent in the statement of financial position is ₩400,000. Had the payment first been recorded as an asset, the adjusting entry would be Dr rent expense 200 / Cr prepaid rent 200, with the same year-end result.
