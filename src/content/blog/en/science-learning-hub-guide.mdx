# Science Isn't a Subject to Memorize — It's a Map to Connect

As science content grows, what matters isn't the volume of individual articles but the **order you read them in**. The solar system, stars, black holes, neuroscience, sleep, and habits may look unrelated, but they all lead back to the same underlying question.

> What rules govern how the world works, and how am I living within those rules?

This hub is a starting point for anyone reading OIYO's science content for the first time. Instead of jumping straight into difficult formulas, it helps you grasp the big picture first and then move into the details.

## Connecting to Everyday Experiments

- [Sleep Optimization Science Guide](/en/sleep-science-optimization-guide)

To make sure you don't just read and move on, turn what you learn into a small experiment.

| Question | Experiment |
|---|---|
| Want to sleep better? | Fix your wake-up time first, for 7 days |
| Focus keeps breaking? | Turn off notifications and track 25-minute blocks |
| Feeling anxious? | Name the emotion and try 3 minutes of breathing |
| Habits won't stick? | Shrink the action down to 2 minutes |

## Science Content Worth Adding Next

1. An intro to biology that visualizes cells, DNA, and gene expression
2. An article that explains the brain's reward circuit and dopamine without hype
3. A lecture that explains weather and air pressure through everyday observation
4. A lecture that explains electricity and circuits through the analogy of flowing water
5. A lecture that explains heat and temperature through the analogy of molecular motion

Good science writing isn't about making you memorize more correct answers — it's about helping you ask better questions.
