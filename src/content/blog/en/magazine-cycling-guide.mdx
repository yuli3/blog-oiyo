## Why Cycling Is Worth It

**Cardiovascular fitness**: Sustained aerobic exercise → stronger heart, increased lung capacity.

**Lower body strength**: Quadriceps, glutes, and calves do most of the work.

**Joint-friendly**: Lower impact than running → easier on knees and ankles.

**Calorie burn**: At 12–14 mph, a 150-lb adult burns roughly 500–600 kcal/hr.

**Mental health**: Outdoor riding boosts serotonin and dopamine, relieves stress.

## Types of Bikes

| Type | Characteristics | Best For |
|------|----------------|---------|
| Road bike | Lightweight, drop handlebars, narrow tires | Long-distance paved roads, speed |
| Mountain bike (MTB) | Wide tires, suspension | Off-road, trails, rough terrain |
| Hybrid | Road-MTB blend | Commuting, leisure, mixed surfaces |
| City / comfort bike | Upright posture, fenders, rack | Short trips, errands |
| E-bike | Pedal-assist motor | Hills, longer commutes, reduced effort |
| Gravel bike | Road-based, wider tires | Mixed paved and unpaved terrain |

**Beginner recommendation**: Hybrid or city bike (versatile, comfortable, reasonably priced).

## Choosing the Right Bike Size

Wrong size → back, knee, and neck pain.

### Saddle Height

**Basic rule**: When your heel is on the pedal at the bottom of the stroke, your leg should be nearly straight.

**More precise calculation**: Inseam length × 0.883 = saddle height (measured from the center of the bottom bracket to the top of the saddle).

### Handlebar Height

- City/hybrid: At or above saddle height (comfortable, upright position)
- Road bike: Below saddle height (aerodynamic, performance-oriented)

### Frame Size

Sizing varies by manufacturer — always test-ride in a shop before buying if possible.

## Essential Safety Gear

### Helmet (Not Legally Required Everywhere, but Life-Saving)

- Always wear one (head injuries are the leading cause of cycling fatalities)
- Look for CPSC certification (required for US sale) or ASTM / CE markings
- Fit it snugly — no side-to-side or front-to-back movement
- Replace every 5 years or after any significant impact

### Other Gear

| Equipment | Why It Matters |
|-----------|---------------|
| Front and rear lights | Visibility at night and in low light (required by law in many states after dark) |
| U-lock | Theft prevention (pair with a cable for rear wheel) |
| Gloves | Protect palms in a fall; absorb handlebar vibration |
| Water bottle + cage | Hydration on any ride over 30 minutes |
| Flat repair kit | CO2 inflator, tire levers, and patches or a spare tube |

## Proper Riding Posture

**The goal**: Relaxed shoulders, hands, and back.

- **Elbows**: Slightly bent to absorb road vibration (straight arms transmit every bump directly)
- **Back**: Maintain a natural, relaxed curve — don't overarch or hunch
- **Head and neck**: Look ahead naturally; your helmet brim should show a strip of road about 50 feet out
- **Feet**: Position the ball of your foot over the pedal axle

**Knee alignment**: If your knees track inward while pedaling, adjust cleat angle or saddle position.

## Using Your Gears

**Front derailleur (chainring)**: Large gear changes (for terrain changes)  
**Rear derailleur (cassette)**: Fine-tuning within each chainring

| Situation | Gear |
|-----------|------|
| Climbing | Easy gear (small ring) — spin fast, lower resistance |
| Flat ground | Middle gears |
| Descending | Hard gear — maintain speed |

**Avoid cross-chaining**: big ring front + biggest cog rear, or small ring front + smallest cog rear — this stresses the chain.

**Shift timing**: Ease pedal pressure while shifting — shifting under full load risks dropping the chain.

## Bicycle Commuting

### Before You Start

- Plan a route using cycling-specific maps (Google Maps and Apple Maps both have bike routing; Komoot and Strava are also excellent)
- Scout bike parking options at your workplace
- Consider shower facilities — if none, a sink wash + fresh clothes + deodorant works fine for shorter rides
- Clothing strategy: keep a change of clothes at the office, or wear cycling kit and change on arrival

### Weather Management

- Rain: Waterproof jacket, fenders (mudguards), and a waterproof saddle cover
- Cold weather: Wind-resistant jacket, insulated gloves, toe covers or booties

### Theft Prevention

- Lock the frame and front wheel to a fixed object with a U-lock
- Use a secondary cable to secure the rear wheel
- Replace quick-release skewers with locking ones for the wheels and saddle

## Popular US Cycling Destinations and Routes

### Urban / Beginner Rides

| Route | Highlights | Distance |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Minuteman Bikeway (Boston, MA) | Paved rail trail, family-friendly | 11 miles |
| Lakefront Trail (Chicago, IL) | Scenic lakeside, mostly flat | 18 miles |
| Crissy Field / Golden Gate (San Francisco, CA) | Bay views, iconic bridge | ~10 miles loop |
| W&OD Trail (Northern Virginia / DC) | Paved rail trail, connects suburbs to city | 45 miles |

### Long-Distance / Intermediate Trails

- **Great Allegheny Passage + C&O Canal Towpath**: Pittsburgh to Washington DC, ~335 miles total — popular multi-day bikepacking route
- **Pacific Coast Route (US Route 101)**: San Francisco to Los Angeles, ~650 miles of coastal scenery
- **Katy Trail (Missouri)**: 240 miles across Missouri on a converted rail bed, relatively flat

## Basic Maintenance

### Check Weekly

- Tire pressure (road bikes: 80–100 psi; hybrids: 50–70 psi; MTBs: 25–40 psi)
- Brakes engage properly — lever should not contact the handlebar

### Check Monthly

- Clean and lubricate the chain (dry lube for dry conditions; wet lube for rain)
- Check and tighten bolts at handlebars, stem, and saddle

### Annually or Every 2,000 Miles

- Replace brake and derailleur cables
- Inspect brake pads
- Professional tune-up at your local bike shop

A good bike, properly maintained, will last decades. Start with a hybrid in the $400–700 range, figure out how you actually ride, then upgrade based on what you genuinely use. That sequence beats buying the wrong expensive bike first.
