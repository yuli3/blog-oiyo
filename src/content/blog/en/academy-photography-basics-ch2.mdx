## What is exposure?

**Exposure** is what determines how bright a photo looks. A photo that's too bright is **overexposed**; one that's too dark is **underexposed**.

The three factors that combine to produce correct exposure are known as the **exposure triangle**.

```
     Exposure Triangle
           ISO
          /    \
  Shutter Speed ─── Aperture
```

These three elements have to balance against each other to produce a photo with the brightness you want.

---

## Shutter speed

Shutter speed is the length of time the shutter stays open, which determines **how long light reaches the sensor**.

### How it's notated

```
1/4000s → 1/2000s → 1/1000s → 1/500s → 1/250s → 1/125s
Fast ←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←→→→→ Slow
Darker ←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←→→→ Brighter
```

### Shutter speed's two roles

**1. Controlling brightness**: the slower it is, the more light comes in → the brighter the photo

**2. Rendering motion**:

| Shutter speed | Effect | Good for |
|----------|------|--------------|
| 1/1000s or faster | Freezes the instant (subject stays sharp) | Sports, birds, water droplets |
| 1/250s | Freezes typical motion | Someone walking, a moving car |
| 1/60s | Slight blur possible | Portraits (the handheld limit) |
| 1/30s or slower | Motion blur | Waterfalls, light trails |
| Several seconds or more | Full light trails | Star trails, night scenes |

> **Avoiding handheld blur**: as a rule of thumb, use at least `1/(focal length)s`. With a 50mm lens, that means at least 1/50s.

---

## Aperture

The size of the light-passing opening inside the lens is expressed as an **f-number (f-stop)**.

### The counterintuitive relationship of f-numbers

```
f/1.4  f/2  f/2.8  f/4  f/5.6  f/8  f/11  f/16
←── Larger opening ───────────────────────── Smaller opening ──→
←── Brighter ─────────────────────────────────── Darker ──→
```

> **Note**: the **smaller** the f-number, the **larger** the opening — it's an inverse relationship.

### Depth of field

Aperture's second role is determining the **range that stays in focus (depth of field)**.

| f-number | Depth of field | Background | Use |
|-----|------|------|------|
| f/1.4–f/2.8 | **Shallow** | Strong blur (bokeh) | Portraits, product photography |
| f/4–f/5.6 | Moderate | Moderately blurred | Everyday snapshots |
| f/8–f/11 | **Deep** | Sharp throughout | Landscapes, architecture |
| f/16 and beyond | Very deep | Very sharp | Starburst effects |

## ISO (sensitivity)

This is how sensitive the camera's sensor is to light.

```
ISO 100 → ISO 200 → ISO 400 → ISO 800 → ISO 1600 → ISO 3200 → ISO 6400
Low sensitivity                                                High sensitivity
Darker                                                          Brighter
Less noise                                                      More noise
```

### Choosing an ISO

| Situation | Recommended ISO |
|------|----------|
| Bright outdoors | ISO 100–200 |
| Overcast/shade | ISO 400 |
| Indoors (bright lighting) | ISO 800–1600 |
| Indoors (dim lighting) | ISO 3200–6400 |
| Night/concerts | ISO 6400 or higher |

> **Rule of thumb**: keep ISO as low as possible, and handle exposure first through shutter speed and aperture.

## How the exposure triangle interacts

The three elements are all **linked** together. Change one, and you need to adjust the other two to maintain the same brightness.

### The concept of a stop

One stop means **doubling or halving the amount of light**.

```
Shutter: 1/500 → 1/250 (1 stop slower) → 2x the light
Aperture: f/5.6 → f/4 (1 stop wider) → 2x the light
ISO: ISO 200 → ISO 400 (1 stop up) → 2x the sensitivity
```

### Sample optimal settings by situation

**Portraits (outdoors, sunny)**
```
Shutter: 1/250s (freezes natural movement)
Aperture: f/2.8 (blurs the background)
ISO: 100–200 (minimizes noise)
```

**Landscapes (using a tripod)**
```
Shutter: 1/30s–several seconds (tripod required)
Aperture: f/8–f/11 (overall sharpness)
ISO: 100 (lowest noise)
```

**Sports/wildlife photography**
```
Shutter: 1/1000s or faster (freezes motion)
Aperture: f/4–f/5.6 (moderate background separation)
ISO: 800–3200, depending on the situation
```

**Night cityscapes (tripod)**
```
Shutter: 5–30 seconds (captures light trails)
Aperture: f/8 (overall sharpness plus starburst)
ISO: 100–400 (minimizes noise)
```

## Reading the histogram

A histogram is a graph showing the **brightness distribution** in a photo.

```
         ▓▓▓
      ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓
   ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓
 ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓
──────────────────────
 Dark    Mid    Bright
 0              255
```

| Histogram shape | Meaning |
|----------------|------|
| Skewed left | Underexposed (dark photo) |
| Skewed right | Overexposed (bright photo) |
| Evenly spread | Proper exposure |
| Clipped at right edge | Highlight clipping (unrecoverable) |
| Clipped at left edge | Shadow clipping |

> **Goal**: keep the histogram from clipping at either end.

## Practice quiz — 5 questions

**Q1.** What settings are needed to render a moving object sharply?
- ① Slow shutter speed + low ISO
- ② Fast shutter speed + high ISO
- ③ Low f-number + slow shutter speed
- ④ High f-number + fast shutter speed

**Answer**: ② *(a fast shutter freezes the motion; the resulting darkness is compensated with ISO)*

---

**Q2.** Between f/1.8 and f/11, which produces a more blurred background?
- ① f/1.8  ② f/11  ③ Both the same  ④ Can't tell

**Answer**: ① *(a lower f-number means a shallower depth of field → more background blur)*

---

**Q3.** What side effect appears when you raise ISO?
- ① The photo gets darker
- ② The colors fade
- ③ Noise (graininess) increases
- ④ The focus goes soft

**Answer**: ③

---

**Q4.** Which of the following correctly describes one stop?
- ① A 10x increase in shutter speed
- ② A 2x or 1/2x change in the amount of light
- ③ A 100-point increase in ISO
- ④ A 0.5 increase in aperture

**Answer**: ②

---

**Q5.** What does it mean when a histogram is clipped at the right edge?
- ① The photo is too dark
- ② There's a lot of noise
- ③ The highlights are blown out and unrecoverable
- ④ The shutter speed was too fast

**Answer**: ③
