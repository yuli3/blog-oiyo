## Lecture 1 Overview: Understanding the Universe as "Distance"

The stars in the night sky aren't just points of light — they're enormous bodies separated by distances that are hard to imagine. The first step to understanding the universe is grasping its **scale**. And the ruler we use to measure that scale is **light**.

<Callout type="info">
**Goal of this lecture**: Understand astronomical distance units (AU, light-years) and the speed of light, and grasp astronomy's core intuition — "seeing a star means seeing the past."
</Callout>

---

## 1. The Speed of Light — the Universe's Speed Limit

Light travels through a vacuum at roughly **300,000 km per second** (299,792 km/s). That's fast enough to circle Earth seven and a half times in a single second. Nothing in the universe can travel faster.

```
Speed of light c ≈ 299,792 km/s
Earth → Moon: about 1.3 seconds by light
Sun → Earth: about 8 minutes 20 seconds by light
```

If the Sun vanished right now, we wouldn't know it for **8 minutes and 20 seconds**.

---

## 2. Astronomical Distance Units

Kilometers are far too small a unit for the scale of the universe, so astronomers use bigger rulers.

| Unit | Definition | Used for |
|------|------|------|
| **AU (Astronomical Unit)** | The average Earth–Sun distance (about 150 million km) | Within the solar system |
| **Light-year (ly)** | The distance light travels in one year (about 9.46 trillion km) | Stars and galaxies |
| **Parsec (pc)** | About 3.26 light-years | Professional astronomy |

<Callout type="tip">
**A light-year measures distance, not time.** This is a common point of confusion — a light-year is the distance light travels in one year (about 9.46 trillion km), not a span of time.
</Callout>

---

## 3. Seeing a Star = Seeing the Past

Because light takes time to travel, every celestial object we see is really a view of its **past**.

```
The Sun: as it looked 8 minutes 20 seconds ago
The nearest star (Proxima Centauri): as it looked about 4.2 years ago
The Andromeda galaxy: as it looked about 2.5 million years ago
```

Looking at distant space through a telescope is really a **time machine looking into the universe's past**.

---

## 4. The Hierarchical Structure of the Universe

The universe is built in layers, from small to large.

```
Planets & moons → star systems (like the solar system) → star clusters → galaxies
→ galaxy groups & clusters → superclusters → large-scale structures (filaments) → the observable universe
```

- The **observable universe** is estimated to be about 93 billion light-years across.
- It's thought to contain **hundreds of billions** of galaxies or more.

## Key Takeaways

1. Light travels at roughly 300,000 km/s — the universe's speed limit.
2. Distance units scale up from AU (solar system) to light-years (stars, galaxies) to parsecs (professional use).
3. A light-year is a distance (about 9.46 trillion km), not a span of time.
4. Because light takes time to arrive, every celestial object appears as it was in the past — a telescope is a time machine.
5. The universe is layered: planets → star systems → galaxies → large-scale structures.
