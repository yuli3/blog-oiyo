Nut is the goddess who personifies the sky in Egyptian mythology. She is often depicted with her body arched like a bow over the earth, holding the stars within her. She was understood as the being who swallowed the sun every evening and gave birth to it again at dawn, and the dead were also believed to gain the possibility of rebirth through Nut's embrace.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Nut's representative myth is the separation of sky and earth. In the beginning, Nut the sky and Geb the earth were pressed closely together, but when Shu lifted them apart, the space of the world came into being. This scene shows the idea that the cosmos is not simply a collection of matter, but gains order through separation and distance.

In another important image, Nut swallows the sun every evening and gives birth to it again each dawn. Nut was also painted on the inside of coffins, a practice connected with the belief that the dead could enter the embrace of the sky mother and be born again. Nut shows an imagination in which the sky is not a distant ceiling, but a body that holds life.

## Reading as an Archetype — Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Nut is an archetype of the **cosmic mother** and the **embracer of night**. She surrounds everything, while also governing the cycle of swallowing and giving birth again. In modern terms, she resembles deep rest, a period of mourning, the darkness before creation, and the feeling of being protected within a larger system.

The key point when reading Nut is that darkness is not necessarily severance. The night sky is not a space that erases light, but a space that holds stars and rebirth. For that reason, the Nut archetype symbolizes rest, unseen growth, and the latent period after loss as parts of life. The power to hold may look like a pause, but it prepares the next birth.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Nut the sky itself?**

A. In Egyptian mythology, Nut is the personification of the sky as a goddess. Her representative image is a star-studded body arching over the earth.

**Q. What does it mean that Nut swallows the sun?**

A. It is a mythic expression of the cycle in which the sun sets, passes through the night, and rises again at dawn. It can also be read as a symbol of death and rebirth.
