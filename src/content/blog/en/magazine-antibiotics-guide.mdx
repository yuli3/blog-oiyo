## Taken incorrectly, antibiotics can do more harm than good

Antibiotics are effective against bacterial infections, but used the wrong way they can breed **antibiotic-resistant bacteria** or trigger serious side effects. Knowing how to take them correctly matters.

---

## The basics of how antibiotics work

### What is an antibiotic?

```
A drug that kills bacteria or stops them from growing
→ Has no effect on viruses

Viral illnesses (antibiotics NOT needed):
- Common cold (mostly viral)
- Flu (influenza virus)
- COVID-19
- Most cases of simple gastroenteritis
```

### Bacterial infections that need antibiotics

```
Situations where antibiotics are needed:
- Bacterial pneumonia
- Urinary tract infections (cystitis, pyelonephritis)
- Bacterial tonsillitis and otitis media
- Cellulitis
- Tuberculosis
- STIs (gonorrhea, syphilis)
→ Always take only after a doctor's prescription
```

## Major classes of antibiotics

| Class | Representative drugs | Main uses |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Penicillins** | Amoxicillin | Tonsillitis, otitis media, UTIs |
| **Cephalosporins** | Cefaclor, cefadroxil | Skin infections, UTIs |
| **Macrolides** | Azithromycin (Zithromax) | Pneumonia, STIs, skin infections |
| **Fluoroquinolones** | Ciprofloxacin | Severe UTIs, pneumonia |
| **Tetracyclines** | Doxycycline | Acne, malaria prevention, STIs |
| **Metronidazole** | Flagyl | Anaerobic infections, vaginal infections |

## How to take antibiotics correctly

### Rule 1: Finish the entire prescribed course

```
Keep taking antibiotics until the full course is done, even if symptoms improve
Why:
- Feeling better ≠ the bacteria are fully cleared
- Stopping early → surviving bacteria multiply → relapse + resistance

Example:
7-day course for tonsillitis
Symptoms improve after 3 days → tempted to stop
→ Never do this → complete the full 7 days
```

### Rule 2: Keep the dosing interval consistent

```
Twice-daily dosing: every 12 hours
Three-times-daily dosing: every 8 hours

→ Keeps blood drug levels steady
→ If levels drop, surviving bacteria can regrow
```

### Rule 3: How food interacts with antibiotics

```
Take on an empty stomach: ampicillin, some fluoroquinolones
Take with food: amoxicillin, cefadroxil

Foods to watch:
- Dairy: don't take with tetracyclines/fluoroquinolones (blocks absorption)
- Alcohol: never mix with metronidazole (causes severe headache, vomiting)
- Grapefruit juice: can abnormally alter blood levels of some antibiotics
```

## Antibiotic side effects and how to handle them

### Common side effects

```
1. GI upset (most common):
   Symptoms: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, stomach pain
   Management: take with food, pair with probiotics

2. Antibiotic-associated diarrhea (AAD):
   Cause: disruption of normal gut flora
   Management: take a probiotic supplement (2 hours after the antibiotic dose)

3. Vaginal or oral yeast infections (candidiasis):
   Cause: antibiotics reduce normal bacteria → yeast (Candida) overgrows
   Management: ask your doctor about adding an antifungal
```

### Serious allergic reactions (seek emergency care immediately)

```
Warning signs:
- Full-body hives + difficulty breathing → anaphylaxis (emergency)
- Rash spreading across the whole body
- Swelling around the mouth or eyes
- Fever accompanied by rash

Emergency steps:
→ Call emergency services or go to the ER immediately
→ Stop taking the drug
→ Bring the prescription packaging with you
```

## Antibiotic resistance: why it's such a serious problem

### How resistance develops

```
Antibiotic use
→ Bacteria that happen to tolerate the drug survive
→ Resistant bacteria multiply
→ The same antibiotic no longer works
→ A stronger antibiotic is needed → repeat → superbugs emerge
```

### Rules for preventing resistance

```
Do:
- Complete the full prescribed course
- Never take antibiotics without a doctor's direction
- Don't save leftover antibiotics for later use

Don't:
- Borrow antibiotics from friends or family
- Ask for antibiotics for a cold "just in case"
- Buy antibiotics online without a prescription
```

## Precautions for children

```
Weight-based dosing matters:
→ Never cut an adult tablet in half for a child
→ Always use a pediatric liquid formulation or a pediatrician's prescription

Rash after taking an antibiotic:
→ Contact a doctor immediately (need to distinguish an allergy from a drug rash)

Don't push for unnecessary antibiotics:
→ Requesting antibiotics "just in case" for a cold does more harm than good
```

## Antibiotics during pregnancy

```
Antibiotics generally considered safe in pregnancy (Category B):
- Amoxicillin
- Most cephalosporins
- Erythromycin (some formulations)

Antibiotics to avoid during pregnancy:
- Tetracyclines: affect fetal bone and tooth development
- Fluoroquinolones: affect fetal joint development
- Metronidazole: contraindicated in early pregnancy (2nd/3rd trimester at a doctor's discretion)

→ Always tell your doctor you're pregnant before getting a prescription
```

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I ask for antibiotics for a cold?**
A. A common cold is viral, so antibiotics won't help. Taking them anyway only raises your risk of resistant bacteria and side effects. Follow your doctor's judgment.

**Q. What happens if I drink alcohol while on antibiotics?**
A. Metronidazole reacts with alcohol and can cause severe headaches and vomiting. Even with other antibiotics, drinking is best avoided since it weakens immunity and slows recovery.

**Q. Can I use leftover antibiotics later?**
A. No. Antibiotic prescriptions depend on the type of infection and the specific bacteria involved. A drug prescribed for a past infection may not be right for a new one, and reusing it raises the risk of resistance.
