## What Is Loneliness?

Loneliness is a painful emotional state that comes from a mismatch between the social connection you want and the connection you actually have. It's different from solitude — the simple state of being alone. You can feel lonely surrounded by people, and you can be alone without feeling lonely at all.

Loneliness has become such a significant public health issue that countries including the UK and Japan have appointed a formal "Minister for Loneliness."

---

## Two Types of Loneliness

### Social Loneliness

This comes from an absence of a social network — friends, colleagues, a community. It's the feeling that "I have no one to spend time with."

### Emotional Loneliness

This comes from an absence of deep emotional bonds. It can happen even when you have plenty of acquaintances, if none of them make you feel truly understood.

---

## How Loneliness Affects Your Health

Research from the British Psychological Society and Harvard University has found that chronic loneliness raises mortality risk by roughly as much as smoking 15 cigarettes a day.

| Area | Effect |
|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | Higher risk of hypertension and heart disease |
| Immune system | Increased inflammatory response |
| Cognitive | Faster cognitive decline |
| Mental health | 2–3x higher risk of depression and anxiety |

<Callout type="note">
Feeling lonely is a natural human emotion. It's not something to be ashamed of — it's a signal that you need social connection.
</Callout>

## Ways to Ease Loneliness

### Increase Small Social Contact

Start with one comment, one moment of eye contact, one short message. Even a brief exchange with a barista can boost your sense of connection.

### Join Groups Built Around Shared Interests

Hobby clubs, book clubs, and fitness groups create relationships naturally around a shared purpose.

### Volunteer

Helping others provides both meaning and connection at the same time, and it's an especially effective way to ease chronic loneliness.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do extroverts feel less lonely?**
A: Not necessarily. Extroverts can still feel lonely if they lack deep emotional connection — and because they're often surrounded by social activity, they can find it harder to recognize their own emotional loneliness.

**Q: Does social media relieve loneliness?**
A: The research is mixed. Passive scrolling tends to deepen loneliness, while active engagement — commenting, messaging — can modestly ease it.
