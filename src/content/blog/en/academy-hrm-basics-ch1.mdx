## 1. What Is HRM?

**HRM — Human Resource Management**

- The process of acquiring, developing, deploying, maintaining, and separating human resources in pursuit of organizational goals.

**Distinctive Characteristics of Human Resources**

- People can interpret goals, exercise agency and respond differently to the same conditions.
- Capable of learning and growing
- Possesses emotions, drives, and intrinsic needs

**Core HR Functions**

- **Acquisition**: Workforce planning, recruiting, selection
- **Development**: Training, education, career development
- **Utilization**: Placement, transfers, job design
- **Maintenance**: Compensation, benefits, safety, labor relations
- **Separation**: Terminations, retirement, outplacement support

## 2. Evolution of HRM

Administration, personnel management, development and strategic HRM describe different emphases, not a universal timetable followed by every country or organization. Payroll accuracy and employment records remain necessary when an HR function develops strategic responsibilities.

| Emphasis | Continuing question |
|---|---|
| Administration | Are records, pay and processes reliable? |
| Personnel management | How are people recruited, assigned, supported and evaluated? |
| Development | Which capabilities need learning and opportunities to apply it? |
| Strategic HRM | How do people practices and organizational direction inform one another? |

These distinctions do not rank organizations by the title of their HR department. A small employer may make strategic decisions without a separate HR team; a large employer may have a strategy document but inadequate operational support.

## 3. Strategic HRM

**Characteristics of Strategic HRM**

- HR as a strategic business partner
- Alignment between business strategy and HR strategy

**Horizontal Alignment**

- Consistency among HR functions
- Recruiting, appraisal, and compensation linked together

**Vertical Alignment**

- Business strategy ↔ HR strategy fit
- Differentiation strategy → innovation-focused HR
- Cost-leadership strategy → efficiency-focused HR

**HR Roles (Ulrich Framework)**

- Strategic Partner · Administrative Expert
- Employee Champion · Change Agent

## 4. Strategic Fit Is a Claim to Test

CIPD describes strategic HRM as a coherent framework connecting people practices and long-term goals, with no single strategy that succeeds in every context. People capabilities should inform business strategy rather than merely follow it. [CIPD, Strategic HRM](https://www.cipd.org/uk/knowledge/factsheets/strategic-hrm-factsheet/).

Vertical alignment asks whether capabilities and working arrangements support the chosen direction. Horizontal alignment asks whether recruitment, job design, development, appraisal and rewards reinforce compatible expectations. Neither means maximizing one metric across every function.

In a fictional service business, management wants faster responses without reduced accuracy. Recruiting experienced staff may help, but rewarding case closures alone could discourage investigation. Training staff to escalate difficult cases may conflict with supervisors penalizing every escalation. Investigate these system tensions instead of assuming employees lack motivation.

### Capacity Is Not Just Headcount

Assume monthly demand of 6,000 cases and 12 minutes of direct work per case: required capacity is 1,200 hours. Ten employees with 120 usable hours each supply 1,200 hours. If the redesigned service takes 15 minutes, demand becomes 1,500 hours: a gap of 300 hours or 2.5 fully productive employee equivalents at the same capacity assumption.

This does not prove that hiring three people is the best answer. Demand fluctuates, new hires need ramp-up time and 120 usable hours must be justified. The redesigned work may improve quality. Compare process changes, work allocation, training and service priorities before choosing a response. An average forecast does not guarantee peak coverage.

Assign responsibility: HR may support workforce analysis and recruitment; operational managers define and observe work; finance reviews costs; employees identify obstacles. Calling something a “people issue” does not transfer all responsibility to HR.

Evaluate activity, capability and business outcomes separately. More training hours show delivery, not learning. Lower turnover may reflect workforce mix or fewer outside opportunities rather than improved retention practices. Record the baseline, denominator, period, alternative explanations and employee consequences before declaring success.

## 5. Practice Quiz

**Q. How do human resources differ from other factors of production (capital, land)?**
**Worked answer.** People have agency, relationships and interests that may differ from organizational objectives. Learning can develop capabilities, but employers do not own people as they own equipment. Employment decisions require attention to rights, fairness and applicable jurisdiction. Human-capital language does not erase employee interests or make every investment effective. Legal protections depend on location, coverage and the decision, not a universal list of US statutes.

**Q. Why is vertical alignment critical in Strategic HRM?**
**Worked answer.** Alignment can expose missing capabilities and contradictory incentives. It does not guarantee execution, nor does misalignment make every result impossible. The case above needs 1,500 rather than 1,200 hours. A hiring target does not resolve that gap immediately or establish whether the added work creates value. Compare options and check whether appraisal and rewards support quality. Markets, technology, finance and operational design also affect outcomes.
