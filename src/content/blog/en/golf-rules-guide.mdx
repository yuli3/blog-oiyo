## Golf, starting with how scoring works

Golf is a game about "getting the ball into the hole in as few strokes as possible." The official rules are jointly maintained by **the R&A and the USGA**. Once you know the scoring and a handful of basic terms, a round becomes much easier to follow.

---

## The basics — holes, par, and score

```
Layout:  A round is usually 18 holes. Each hole has a target
         stroke count called "par" (3, 4, or 5).
Stroke:  Every swing that makes contact with the ball = 1 stroke.
Goal:    The fewer total strokes across the round, the better.
```

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Par** | Finishing the hole in exactly the target stroke count |
| **Birdie** | One stroke under par |
| **Eagle** | Two strokes under par |
| **Bogey** | One stroke over par |
| **Double bogey** | Two strokes over par |
| **Hole-in-one** | Sinking the ball with your very first stroke |

## Parts of the course

```
Teeing area: where each hole begins
Fairway:     the closely mown strip down the middle (easiest to play from)
Rough:       the longer grass bordering the fairway (harder to play from)
Green:       the short grass surrounding the hole/cup — where you putt
Bunker:      a sand trap / Hazard: water, ponds, and similar features
```

---

## Penalty rules worth knowing

<a id="penalty"></a>

```
Out of bounds (OB): outside the course boundary (white stakes)
  → 1-stroke penalty, replay from the original spot
Penalty areas (water, etc. — red/yellow stakes):
  1-stroke penalty, then drop at a designated point
Lost ball: not found within 3 minutes = treated the same as
  OB (1-stroke penalty)
Bunkers: touching the sand with your club before your swing
  used to be a violation; the rule has since been relaxed,
  but caution is still advised
Unplayable lie: you may declare it yourself,
  1-stroke penalty, then drop
```

## Formats of play

```
Stroke play: ranked by total strokes (most tournaments use this)
Match play:  each hole is won or lost individually; whoever
             wins the most holes wins the match
Handicap:    a scoring adjustment that levels the playing field
             between players of different skill — lets beginners
             play alongside experienced golfers
```

## On-course etiquette (just as important as the rules)

Golf places as much weight on **etiquette** as it does on the rules themselves.

```
- Safety: only swing once the group ahead is out of range.
  Shout "Fore!" to warn others if your shot goes astray.
- Pace: don't hold up play — whoever is ready hits first.
- The green: never step on another player's putting line.
- Divots & bunkers: replace divots you take from the turf,
  and rake the sand smooth after playing from a bunker.
- Quiet: stay still and silent while someone else is swinging.
```

---

## Key takeaways

1. The goal is to sink the ball in the fewest strokes, typically over 18 holes.
2. Scores are described relative to par — birdie (-1), bogey (+1), and so on.
3. Out-of-bounds shots, penalty areas, and lost balls all carry a 1-stroke penalty.
4. There are two main formats: stroke play (total strokes) and match play (holes won).
5. Etiquette — safety, pace of play, the green, and staying quiet — matters just as much as the rules.

> This guide follows the official Rules of Golf as maintained by the R&A and the USGA.
