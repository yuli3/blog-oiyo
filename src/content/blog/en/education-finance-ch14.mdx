# Ch 14. Portfolio Theory and Diversification

"Can we increase returns while reducing risk?" In 1952, Harry Markowitz mathematically proved that by exploiting the **correlations** between assets, you can dramatically cut risk without sacrificing overall returns.

---

## 1. The Core of the Portfolio Effect

The expected return of a two-asset portfolio is simply the weighted average of each asset's return. But the **risk (standard deviation)** of the portfolio will be *lower* than the sum of the individual risks — as long as the correlation between the two assets is less than 1.

## 3. The Efficient Frontier

In the simulation graph, the set of portfolios that offer the highest return for a given level of risk — or the lowest risk for a given level of return — forms what is called the **Efficient Frontier**. Any rational investor should only choose portfolios that sit on this frontier.

<Callout type="tip">
**Why Correlation Matters**: Two assets with a correlation of -1 can, in theory, reduce unsystematic risk to zero. This is the rationale behind asset allocation strategies that mix stocks with gold, foreign currencies, and other uncorrelated assets.
</Callout>
