## 1. Learning objectives

1. Distinguish what the Framework Act on National Taxes and the National Tax Collection Act each govern.
2. State the order of self-assessed payment, payment notice and demand, and the statutory deadline at each stage.
3. Explain when collection before the due date is possible and what it does.
4. Explain why a demand is a precondition for seizure.

## 2. The boundary between the two Acts

The Framework Act deals with how **the amount owed** is determined: establishment, finalisation, exclusion periods and appeals. The National Tax Collection Act deals with **actually collecting** the tax so determined.

## 3. Where payment starts — self-assessed payment and payment notices

**Self-assessed payment** (Article 5) is when the taxpayer pays together with the return under the tax laws. Income tax, corporate tax and VAT work this way.

A **payment notice** (Article 6) is when the head of the competent tax office sends a notice stating the tax payable and the deadline. This applies when the government has determined or corrected the tax, or when a return was filed but not paid. Collecting from a secondary taxpayer requires a separate payment notice (Article 7).

<Callout type="info">
A payment notice is not mere information but **a disposition**, so it can itself be appealed (objection, request for review or adjudication). The day the notice is received starts the appeal period, so record the date of receipt.
</Callout>

## 4. Collection before the due date (Article 9)

The rule is to wait until the set deadline. But if waiting would make collection impossible, tax can be collected earlier. After the tax is finalised, the deadline can be shortened in the notice; before finalisation, property can be seized up to the amount expected to be finalised (Article 31(2), protective seizure before finalisation).

Protective seizure before finalisation requires **prior approval from the commissioner of the regional tax office**, and the taxpayer must be notified in writing after the seizure (Article 31(3)). If, for example, the tax is still not finalised three months after the seizure, **the seizure must be lifted immediately** (Article 31(4)).

## 5. Demand (Article 10)

If payment is not made in full by the designated deadline, the head of the competent tax office must **issue a demand within 10 days after the deadline passes**. The deadline in the demand is set **within 20 days of the date of the demand**.

A demand matters because it is **a requirement for seizure**. Article 31(1) lists as a ground for seizure "having received a demand and failing to pay in full by the deadline stated in it". A seizure that skips the demand does not meet the requirements (exceptions such as collection before the due date aside).

## 6. Fact-finding (Article 10-2)

This stage was introduced by the 2021 complete revision. To understand **the causes of arrears and ability to pay** of a taxpayer who has not paid despite a demand, the office may carry out **simple factual acts** such as requesting documents, asking questions, confirming willingness to pay, and consulting by phone or in person.

It is not a coercive measure. Think of it as a step to check the circumstances before moving straight to seizure.

## 7. Worked example of deadlines

Suppose tax under a notice with a designated deadline of 31 May 2026 is not paid.

| Stage | Date | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Designated payment deadline | 2026-05-31 | Payment notice |
| Last day to issue the demand | By 2026-06-10 | Within 10 days after the deadline (Article 10(1)) |
| Payment deadline in the demand | Within 20 days of issue | Article 10(2) |
| Seizure possible | From the day after the demand deadline | Article 31(1)1 |

## 8. Check your understanding

<Quiz>

**Q1.** By when must the head of the tax office issue a demand, and by when can the demand's payment deadline be set?

> The demand must be issued within 10 days after the designated payment deadline passes (Article 10(1)). The payment deadline in the demand is set within 20 days of the date of the demand (Article 10(2)).

**Q2.** A tax office seized a taxpayer's bank deposit straight away without a demand. Is this lawful?

> As a rule, no. Article 31(1) makes it a requirement for seizure that the taxpayer received a demand and did not pay in full by the deadline stated in it. However, failure to pay by a deadline shortened on grounds of collection before the due date (item 2 of paragraph 1) and protective seizure before finalisation (paragraph 2) follow separate requirements.

**Q3.** What procedure is needed to seize property before finalisation, and when must the seizure be lifted?

> Prior approval from the commissioner of the regional tax office is needed, and the taxpayer must be notified in writing after the seizure (Article 31(3)). It must be lifted immediately if, for example, the taxpayer provides tax security and requests release, or the tax is not finalised within three months of the seizure (paragraph 4).

**Q4.** Which Act — the Framework Act or the National Tax Collection Act — sets the assessment exclusion period? Explain using the roles of the two Acts.

> The Framework Act (Article 26-2). The exclusion period concerns how long tax can be **assessed**, whereas the National Tax Collection Act sets the procedure for **collecting** tax that has been finalised.

</Quiz>
