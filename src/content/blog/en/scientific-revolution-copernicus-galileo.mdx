## Fifteen Hundred Years of Getting It Wrong

Around 150 CE, Greek astronomer Ptolemy completed an Earth-centered model of the cosmos. Every planet, the Sun, and the stars orbited the Earth in perfect circles. This model matched Church doctrine and served as the official cosmology of the Western world for **1,500 years**.

Then in 1543, a dying Polish clergyman received the first printed copy of his book. It would change the world.

---

## Copernicus: The Reluctant Revolutionary

**Nicolaus Copernicus (1473–1543)** proposed that Earth and all other planets orbit the Sun — **heliocentrism**. He spent 30 years refining the idea but delayed publication, fearing the Church's reaction.

He finally published *On the Revolutions of the Celestial Spheres* on his deathbed. The book contained a preface (not written by him) calling it "merely a mathematical hypothesis" — a face-saving disclaimer. The Church didn't add it to the Index of Forbidden Books until 73 years later.

---

## Tycho and Kepler: Data Meets Mathematics

Danish astronomer **Tycho Brahe (1546–1601)** compiled the most precise naked-eye planetary observations ever made — but personally favored a hybrid model where the Sun orbited Earth.

His assistant **Johannes Kepler (1571–1630)** inherited the data and applied mathematical rigor. Conclusion: planets orbit the Sun in **ellipses**, not circles. **Kepler's Three Laws of Planetary Motion** emerged.

<Callout type="tip">
**Data over theory**: Kepler couldn't fit Copernicus's perfect circular orbits to Tycho's data within 8 arc-minutes of accuracy. Rather than fudge the numbers, he abandoned the circle. "These 8 minutes will lead us to a reformation of all astronomy." A model of scientific integrity.
</Callout>

## Galileo: Observation Against Authority

**Galileo Galilei (1564–1642)** pointed an improved telescope at the sky and found decisive evidence:

- Jupiter's four moons — proof not everything orbits Earth
- Venus's phases — only explicable if Venus orbits the Sun
- Mountains on the Moon — the heavens were not perfect and unchanging

Galileo publicly championed heliocentrism. In 1633, the Inquisition forced him to recant. Legend says he muttered: "And yet it moves." He died under house arrest. The Vatican formally lifted his conviction in 1992.

---

## Newton: The Synthesis

**Isaac Newton (1643–1727)** published *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica* in 1687 — perhaps the most important scientific work ever written.

He unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics with **universal gravitation**: every object attracts every other with force proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of distance. The same law that governs falling apples governs orbiting moons. Heaven and Earth became one physics.

---

## Why Then, Why There?

The Scientific Revolution happened in 16th–17th century Europe for interconnected reasons:

- **Printing press** (1450s): Knowledge spread rapidly across borders
- **Age of Exploration**: New continents challenged classical authority
- **Renaissance humanism**: Renewed faith in human reason
- **Universities and patronage**: Institutional support for natural philosophy

---

## The Method Is the Legacy

The revolution's true gift wasn't heliocentrism itself. It was **scientific method** — observation, hypothesis, mathematical modeling, experimental verification. This process, not any particular discovery, became the engine of all subsequent progress: the Industrial Revolution, medicine, technology, and everything that defines the modern world.
