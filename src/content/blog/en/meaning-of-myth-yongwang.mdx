Yongwang (龍王, Dragon King) is known in Korean and East Asian folklore, Buddhism, and folk religion as the king who governs the seas, rivers, and the underwater world. The image of him residing in the Dragon Palace (Yonggung, 龍宮) is widely recognized, and he is associated with fish, seafood, rain, bountiful catches, and underwater treasures. In Korean folktales, he sometimes appears as a sickly king, such as in the *Rabbit Tale* (*Tokkijeon*), while in the origins of fishing villages and the traditions of divers (*haenyeo*), he appears as the master of the waters. Yongwang provides abundance but also embodies the inherent dangers of water.

## Core Myth — Representative Anecdotes

One of the most familiar stories featuring Yongwang is the narrative cycle of the *Rabbit Tale*. In this story, the Dragon King falls ill, and his subjects attempt to obtain a rabbit's liver to save him, but the rabbit escapes the crisis through its wit. In this tale, Yongwang is an absolute authority figure who is nonetheless susceptible to illness, and the Dragon Palace is depicted as a place where splendor, deception, and fear coexist.

In folk religion, Yongwang was the object of prayers for the safety of the sea and a bountiful catch. The sea provides a livelihood but is also a dangerous world capable of swallowing people whole. Rituals like the *Yongwangje* or *Pungeoje* can be seen as ceremonies that acknowledge this duality and pay respect to the master of the waters. Depending on the tradition, Yongwang is also linked to the deities of the sea, rivers, wells, and ponds.

In Buddhism, Yongwang appears as a guardian of the Dharma or a being who brings rain. Thus, Yongwang is not merely a single folkloric character but a broad deity representing the authority of water through the image of a king. His palace is an unseen, deep world, and when humans are invited or dragged there, they are forced to confront their own limitations.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Yongwang is the archetype of the **King of the Depths** and the **Father of Waters**. He governs emotions, the unconscious, vitality, and danger simultaneously. In a modern context, he resembles scenarios where one faces vast, sea-like markets, the deep layers of emotion, or the invisible power structures within families and organizations.

The Yongwang archetype demonstrates that abundance and terror originate from the same source. Water gives life, and water takes it away. Therefore, Yongwang is a symbol that to obtain resources, one must respect the rules of that world. He demands a human who negotiates with the sea rather than one who seeks to conquer it.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Does Yongwang only exist in the sea?**

A. While he is most familiar as the king of the sea, depending on the tradition, he is also connected to other watery worlds such as rivers, ponds, and wells.

**Q. Are Yongwang and a dragon the same being?**

A. Yongwang is understood as the king of water who possesses the form and authority of a dragon. He carries a stronger characterization as a ruler and deity than a general dragon.
