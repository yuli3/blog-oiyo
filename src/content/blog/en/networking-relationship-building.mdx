"Your network is your net worth." — Porter Gale

Research suggests that 70–80% of hires come through personal connections rather than public job postings. But networking rarely works when you think of it as "trading business cards" or "self-promotion." Real connection starts by **giving value first**.

## The Psychology of Networking

### The Strength of Weak Ties

Sociologist Mark Granovetter's landmark 1973 research found that new opportunities more often come from "weak ties" — acquaintances — than from close friends.

**Why**: Close friends tend to share the same information you already have. Acquaintances give you access to entirely different networks.

**How to use it**: maintaining a wide circle of acquaintances, not just close friends, is what actually creates opportunity.

### The Principle of Reciprocity

Give first, and you tend to receive in return. Networking isn't really about "what can I get out of this" — it's about "how can I help?"

**Givers vs. takers**: Adam Grant's research found that the most successful people tend to be "givers" — people who help others.

## Five Common Networking Myths

| Myth | Reality |
|------|------|
| "Only extroverts are good at it" | Introverts often excel at deep one-on-one conversations |
| "The goal is collecting business cards" | Five genuine relationships beat 500 business cards |
| "You have to seem outgoing" | Authenticity is remembered longer than polish |
| "There has to be an immediate payoff" | Relationships are a long-term investment — don't expect instant returns |
| "Networking only happens at networking events" | Every interaction is a networking opportunity |

## How to Build Authentic Relationships

### 1. Lead With Curiosity

People love talking about themselves. Focus on the other person with genuine curiosity.

**Good opening questions**:
- "What's something you're really excited about lately?"
- "How did you get started in this field?"
- "What are you focused on right now?"

### 2. Offer Specific Help

"Let me know if I can help with anything" is weaker than "I know someone in this space I could connect you with."

**Ways to add concrete value**:
- Share a useful article or piece of information
- Connect two people ("You two really need to meet")
- Offer feedback or advice
- Amplify their work or project

### 3. The Golden Rule of Follow-Up

If you don't follow up within 24–48 hours of meeting someone, you'll likely fade from memory.

**What effective follow-up looks like**:
- Reference something memorable from your conversation
- Suggest a concrete next step
- Share a helpful resource or contact

### 4. Build a "Relationship CRM"

Keep track of the people who matter.

| Information | What to record |
|------|---------|
| Basics | Name, title, company, contact info |
| Interests | Professional and personal interests |
| Last contact | Date and what you discussed |
| Next steps | What to share, and when |

## Digital Networking

### Using LinkedIn Well

**Optimizing your profile**:
- A clear photo — professional but approachable
- A headline that includes relevant keywords
- Work history with concrete accomplishments
- A genuine summary that tells your story

**Content strategy**:
- Post insights drawn from real experience
- Leave meaningful comments on others' posts
- Post consistently, once or twice a week

**A sample connection request message**:
"Hi [name] — [what you have in common / why you'd like to connect]. Would love to connect."

### Using Communities

- Industry Slack groups
- In-person meetups and conferences
- Online forums, Reddit
- Study groups

## A Practical Guide to Networking Events

### Before the Event

- Review the attendee list in advance, if available
- Set a goal of having 3–5 meaningful conversations
- Prepare three questions to keep the conversation going

### During the Event

- Stand near the center of the room, not the edges
- Approach people standing alone first
- Ask "what are you focused on lately?" instead of "what do you do?"
- Use a LinkedIn QR code instead of a business card

### Continuing the Conversation and Wrapping Up

- Find common ground
- Make a concrete follow-up plan ("Want to grab coffee next week?")
- Exit the conversation at the right moment

## Maintaining Your Network

A "networking habit" for staying in touch with the people who matter:

| Frequency | Action |
|------|------|
| Weekly | Share something valuable with 1–2 people |
| Monthly | Check in casually with 5 people |
| Quarterly | Meet or call the people who matter most |
| Annually | Review and refresh your entire network |
