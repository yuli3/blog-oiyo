## IELTS Speaking Overview

**Duration**: 11–14 minutes, live 1:1 with a certified examiner.

Unlike TOEFL, IELTS Speaking tests **genuine conversational ability** — natural rhythm, responsiveness, and the ability to extend ideas spontaneously.

---

## Scoring Criteria

| Criterion | What's Evaluated |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Fluency & Coherence | Speaking smoothly without long pauses; logical flow |
| Lexical Resource | Range and accuracy of vocabulary |
| Grammatical Range | Variety and accuracy of sentence structures |
| Pronunciation | Clear enough to understand without effort (accent not penalized) |

---

## Part 1: Interview (4–5 min)

**Content**: Familiar topics — family, hometown, hobbies, work, daily routines

**Strategy**: Never give one-word or yes/no answers. Always expand with reason + example.

**IERI Response Framework**:
- **I**nitial answer: Direct response
- **E**xplain: Give a reason
- **R**elevant example: Add a specific detail
- **I**nterest: Show genuine engagement

**Example**:  
Q: "Do you enjoy cooking?"  
A: "Yes, I actually really enjoy it. [I] I find cooking quite relaxing — [E] it lets me focus on a task without thinking about work. [R] For example, I learned to make pasta from scratch last year and it became a weekly thing for me. [I] I find it's a great way to unwind."

---

## Part 2: Long Turn (3–4 min)

**Content**: Speak for 1–2 minutes on a topic card. 1 minute to prepare.

**Sample card**: "Describe a time when you had to learn something new quickly."

### 1-Minute Preparation Template

```
Who/What: 
When/Where:
What happened:
Why it was meaningful/how you felt:
```

### Response Structure

```
"I'd like to talk about [topic]."
→ Set the scene/background (30 sec)
→ Core experience/narrative (45 sec)
→ Reflection/outcome (30 sec)
→ "That's why it was particularly memorable for me."
```

---

## Part 3: Discussion (4–5 min)

**Content**: Abstract, societal-level questions related to Part 2's topic

**Key difference from Parts 1–2**: Requires analytical thinking, not just personal experience.

**Example**: "Do you think technology has made learning new skills easier or harder?"

### Part 3 Answer Structure

```
Opinion → Reason 1 → Reason 2 or contrast → Conclusion

"In my view, [position]. On one hand, [reason 1]. 
Moreover, [reason 2 or: However, one could argue that... although...].
On balance, I think [conclusion]."
```

---

## Pronunciation Tips

IELTS does NOT require a British or American accent. **Clarity is the goal**.

- **Word stress**: em-PHAS-ize the stressed syllable
- **Linking**: "going out" → "goingout"; "it is" → "itis"
- **Intonation**: Rise for questions; fall at end of statements
