## The CAT Algorithm: How GMAT Scores You

GMAT uses **Computer Adaptive Testing (CAT)**: each question's difficulty adjusts based on your running performance. You're not competing against other test-takers — you're navigating a question pool calibrated to your ability level in real time.

### Core CAT Principles

- **Early questions matter more**: The algorithm's estimate of your ability is most uncertain early; accurate answers on initial questions create a more favorable trajectory
- **Correct on hard questions > Correct on easy questions**: Answering a difficult question correctly is worth more than answering an easy one
- **Never leave questions blank**: Unanswered questions (running out of time) carry a severe penalty

## Section Pacing Guide

### Quantitative Reasoning (21 questions / 45 min)

| Difficulty | Target Time |
|-----------|-------------|
| Easy | 1.5–2 min |
| Medium | 2–2.5 min |
| Hard | 2.5–3 min |
| **Average** | **~2 min/question** |

**Pacing checkpoints**:
- After question 7: ~30 minutes remaining (on pace)
- After question 14: ~15 minutes remaining

### Verbal Reasoning (23 questions / 45 min)
- Average: ~2 min/question
- CR: 1.5–2 min; RC: 2 min for passage + 1 min per question

### Data Insights (20 questions / 45 min)
- Average: ~2.25 min/question
- DS: 2 min; Multi-Source Reasoning: 2.5–3 min

## Handling Difficult Questions

### The 2-Minute Rule
If you're stuck after 2 minutes with no clear path forward:
1. Make your best educated guess
2. Move to the next question
3. Don't look back — trust your instinct and maintain pace

### Elimination Strategy
On 5-choice questions, eliminating 2–3 clearly wrong choices improves guessing odds from 20% to 33–50%.

### Smart Guessing by Question Type
- DS with complex algebra → guess if both conditions seem needed but relationship unclear
- RC detail questions → only worth returning to if you marked the paragraph
