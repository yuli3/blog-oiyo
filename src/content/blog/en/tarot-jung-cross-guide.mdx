Treat tarot purely as a tool for predicting the future, and you miss its most interesting layer. Since the 20th century, many psychologists and counselors have read the tarot's **22 Major Arcana cards** as illustrations of what Carl Jung called **archetypes**. Drawing a card isn't about determining the future — it's closer to naming **whatever psychological phase is active in you right now** and opening a conversation with it.

<Callout type="note">
This piece offers one way of reading tarot as a language of psychological self-reflection. The connection between Jung's archetype theory and tarot isn't an academically proven causal link — it's an **interpretive framework for working with symbols**. ## Three Core Jungian Concepts

- **Archetype:** a universal image shared in humanity's collective unconscious — the mother, the hero, the shadow, and so on. The Major Arcana reads almost like a picture-book of these archetypes.
- **Shadow:** the part of yourself you don't want to admit to and have pushed down. Cards like the Devil and the Tower often touch this territory.
- **Individuation:** the lifelong process of integrating conscious and unconscious into "a more whole self." This is exactly what the overall arc of the Major Arcana resembles.

## The Fool's Journey = The Journey of Individuation

The Major Arcana begins with **The Fool** and ends with **The World**. From a Jungian lens, this sequence of 22 cards can be read as **the journey from an immature ego at the starting point to an integrated Self**.

| Phase | Representative card | Reading it through Jungian psychology |
|---|---|---|
| Departure | The Fool | An undifferentiated ego full of possibility — a pure beginning |
| Will | The Magician | The stage where the conscious ego becomes aware of its own capability |
| Inwardness | The High Priestess | The first encounter with the unconscious and intuition |
| Trial | The Tower | The collapse of a false ego structure — confronting the shadow |
| Turning point | Death | The end of an old identity, and rebirth |
| Integration | The World | The integration of conscious and unconscious — individuation complete |

<Callout type="tip">
When you draw a card, instead of asking "is this a future event?" try asking **"why has this archetype come up in me right now?"** If you draw the Tower, look at what false sense of security is due to collapse. If you draw Death, look at what identity is ready to end.
</Callout>

## Don't Fear the Shadow Cards

The most misunderstood cards in tarot (Death, the Tower, the Devil) actually read, through a Jungian lens, as **signals of growth**.

- **Death** = not a literal death, but the end of one phase. A sign that you have to let something go before the next thing arrives.
- **The Tower** = the collapse of a false foundation. Painful, but a turning point toward rebuilding on something real.
- **The Devil** = shadow, addiction, obsession. An invitation to bring a desire you've been avoiding into conscious awareness.

Facing the shadow is the core of individuation. Once your conscious mind accepts what it had suppressed, that energy finally becomes an ally.
