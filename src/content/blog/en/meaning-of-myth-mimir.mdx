Mimir is a figure in Norse mythology who symbolizes deep wisdom and memory. His name is tied to Mimir's well beside the roots of the World Tree, and Odin sacrifices one of his eyes in order to drink from its water. Mimir remains more powerfully as a source of wisdom, a counseling head, and a storehouse of memory than as a living body.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

The most famous scene is the story of Odin seeking Mimir's well. The well contains deep wisdom, but it does not grant knowledge without a price. By sacrificing one eye and drinking the water, Odin comes to understand the future and the deep structure of the world.

In another tradition, Mimir is killed after the hostage exchange between the Aesir and the Vanir, and only his severed head is returned to Odin. Odin preserves the head with herbs and spells and receives counsel from it. The image is striking: the body disappears, but wisdom remains as a speaking head.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian View

Mimir is the archetype of the **sage** and the **head of memory**. He symbolizes wisdom that remains even after experience has been separated from the body, or the deep record of life. In modern terms, he resembles a mentor, a classical text, old research notes, or the memories we return to in times of crisis. Mimir shows that knowledge is not merely the accumulation of information, but something deepened through price, loss, and long preservation.

The Mimir archetype stands opposite the "quick answer." He asks for a long time, remembers for a long time, and gives counsel when needed. In modern terms, he is closer to a person who remembers context and recognizes repeated patterns in events than to someone who simply possesses a large amount of data. Odin seeks Mimir not because of the quantity of knowledge, but because of the depth that sees through the world.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Mimir a god?**

A. His classification is not simple across every tradition, but it is better to understand him as a being older than the gods and possessed of deeper wisdom.

**Q. Why did Odin sacrifice his eye?**

A. It was the price required to gain wisdom from Mimir's well. This is a central symbol in Norse mythology: knowledge comes with loss.
