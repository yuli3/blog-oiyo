## The Axis of Growth Is Shifting

The 20th century belonged to America. Whose century is the 21st?

That question is directly tied to the future of dollar investing. Where the world's growth axis shifts determines the relative appeal of dollar assets.

### The New Growth Axes

**India:** 1.4 billion people, a young population, and rapid digital infrastructure buildout — the fastest-growing major economy in the world.

**Southeast Asia:** Vietnam, Indonesia, the Philippines. Steady, high growth driven by manufacturing relocation and a rising middle class.

**The Middle East:** Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030, the UAE's push to become a fintech hub. Oil money moving into diversified investments.

### The Evolution of Dollar Investing

This shift doesn't mean the end of dollar investing. It means **raising the weight of non-dollar assets to complement it.**

**A long-term portfolio framework:**
- Dollar assets (U.S. stocks and bonds): 40–50%
- Non-dollar developed-market assets (Europe, Japan): 20–25%
- Emerging-market assets (India, Southeast Asia): 15–20%
- Alternative assets (gold, REITs, commodities): 10–15%

The dollar will remain the world's reserve currency for decades to come. But the shift from dollar-only concentration to global diversification is a trend that can't be reversed.

> "The next decade won't be an era in which the dollar weakens — it will be an era in which non-dollar assets strengthen."
