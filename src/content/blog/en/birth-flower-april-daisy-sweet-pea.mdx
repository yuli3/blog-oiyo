April is full spring — everything blooming, everything possible. Its birth flowers match this energy exactly: the **daisy**, the universal symbol of innocence and simplicity, and the **sweet pea**, one of the most delicately fragrant flowers in existence, representing blissful pleasure and the beauty of goodbye.

## Daisy (Bellis perennis)

### The Most Honest Flower
The daisy has no pretense. Its white petals and yellow center are exactly what they appear to be — unperfumed, unelaborate, and somehow more beautiful for it. The name comes from the Old English *dæges ēage* — "day's eye" — because daisies open with the sun and close at night.

### The "He Loves Me, He Loves Me Not" Oracle
The tradition of pulling daisy petals one by one while alternating "he loves me / he loves me not" dates back to at least medieval France. The daisy was believed to be a reliable oracle of love's truth — appropriate for a flower associated with innocence and directness.

### Meanings
- **Innocence and purity**: The daisy's untouched appearance has always symbolized the uncomplicated heart
- **New beginnings**: As a spring flower, it announces fresh starts
- **True love**: The oracle tradition links it to sincere, unmanipulated feeling
- **Cheerfulness**: The bright yellow center radiates simple joy
- **Loyal love**: In Norse mythology, the daisy was sacred to Freya, goddess of love — Freya's flower was used in baby blessings and childhood protection

### Spiritual Connection
**Chakra**: Solar Plexus and Crown — the daisy's yellow center activates confidence; its white petals reach toward purity and divine connection.

The daisy is one of the few flowers that genuinely crosses cultural barriers in its meaning. From Celtic tradition to Japanese hanakotoba to Victorian flower language, daisy consistently represents the same things: innocence, cheerfulness, loyal love.

## Sweet Pea (Lathyrus odoratus)

### Blissful Departure
The sweet pea is the flower of **blissful pleasure and delicate parting**. In the Victorian language of flowers, it was primarily given as a thank-you — a way of saying "I have had a wonderful time, and I am grateful" — making it the classic goodbye flower of gracious farewell rather than sad departure.

### Origins
Native to Sicily and the southern Italian islands, sweet peas were first cultivated in the 17th century and quickly became one of the most coveted flowers in Europe for their extraordinary fragrance — a honey-and-orange blossom scent that is simultaneously simple and complex.

They became the fashionable flower of the Edwardian era (early 1900s England), when growing sweet peas was a competitive hobby among aristocratic gardeners.

### Meanings
- **Blissful pleasure**: The overwhelming sweetness of the scent associates it with pure sensory delight
- **Gentle goodbye**: "Thank you for a lovely time"
- **Departure**: Not sad leaving, but graceful transition
- **Tenderness and remembrance**: Used in memorial gardens for their soft, nostalgic quality

**Chakra**: Heart (Anahata) — sweet pea's colors range from white to deep pink and purple, all activating the heart's capacity for gratitude and tender connection.

## April's Message

April children bloom with effortless, unguarded openness. The daisy does not try to be anything other than what it is — and its beauty lies entirely in that honesty. The sweet pea does not cling to what is leaving — it fills the parting with fragrance.

For those born in April, or anyone navigating a season of new beginnings: your gift is the ability to be fully present in simple beauty, and to let endings be fragrant rather than bitter.
