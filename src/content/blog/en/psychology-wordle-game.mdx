### Introduction: When a Game Becomes a Lesson

You likely remember Wordle — the simple word game that swept the internet in early 2022. Guess a five-letter word in six tries, get color-coded feedback, share your result. The rules couldn't be simpler, yet it captured millions of people's daily attention for months.

There's a reason for that. One of the most effective ways to acquire language is through **repeated exposure combined with a reward signal** — exactly what a well-designed guessing game provides.

Today we've built a special version using the vocabulary of psychology. As you try to guess the word, you'll also encounter a short explanation of what that word means — and why it matters in everyday life.

## 2. Words Worth Knowing: Core Psychology Concepts

Here's a preview of some key terms you might encounter in the game — along with why they matter.

### ① Emotion
The most powerful driver of human behavior. Psychologists increasingly emphasize that **suppressing emotions is less healthy than observing them and labeling them** — a practice called affect labeling, which has been shown to reduce the intensity of difficult feelings.

### ② The Self / Ego
Since Freud's foundational work, the self has been one of psychology's most central subjects of study. A healthy ego functions as a mediator between inner drives and external reality — maintaining balance without collapsing under the weight of either.

### ③ Memory
Memory is the database that defines who we are. But modern psychology's most striking finding is that memory is not a fixed archive — **every time we recall something, we subtly reconstruct it**. Memory is a living, flexible system, not a recording.

## 3. What Word Games Do for Your Brain

There's more going on than entertainment:

1. **Stimulating neuroplasticity**: combining and reasoning about letters and words activates and strengthens neural pathways
2. **Dopamine release**: the satisfaction of a correct guess activates the brain's reward circuit — reinforcing the motivation to keep learning
3. **Building cognitive flexibility**: the process of exploring multiple possibilities trains the mind to think beyond habitual patterns
