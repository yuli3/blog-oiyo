## TOEIC Scoring Overview

LC and RC are each scored 5–495. Combined score: 10–990.

| Score Band | Level | Description |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| 900–990 | Advanced | Near-native fluency; understands nuance and humor |
| 800–895 | Upper-intermediate | Fully functional in professional English settings |
| 700–795 | Intermediate-high | Handles routine business tasks; some difficulty with complexity |
| 600–695 | Intermediate | Basic business communication; struggles with professional documents |
| 500–595 | Lower-intermediate | Understands simple expressions; limited in complex contexts |
| Below 500 | Elementary | Recognizes only basic phrases and expressions |

## Score-Band Strategies

### Target: 600 (Current: 400–500)

**Focus:** Part 1 and 2 perfection + Part 5 core grammar stability

**LC priorities:**
- Part 1: memorize essential action verbs + spot sound-alike distractors
- Part 2: master WH-question patterns + basic response phrases
- Parts 3 & 4: begin pre-reading practice; strengthen vocabulary

**RC priorities:**
- Part 5: parts of speech + tense + subject-verb agreement
- Part 7: focus on short single passages (emails, notices)

**6-week schedule:**
- Weeks 1–2: Part-by-part LC basics + 50 high-frequency expressions
- Weeks 3–4: 20 core grammar patterns (Ch2)
- Weeks 5–6: Mini-tests + error analysis

---

### Target: 700 (Current: 550–650)

**Focus:** Parts 3 & 4 accuracy + Part 7 single-passage mastery

**LC priorities:**
- Part 2: eliminate association traps and misleading paraphrase answers
- Part 3: consistent pre-reading + catch specific details
- Part 4: know the six talk types; focus hard on first two sentences

**RC priorities:**
- Part 5: prepositions, conjunctions vs. prepositions, vocabulary
- Part 7: increase skimming speed for single passages
- Part 7 question types: detail + inference questions

**8-week schedule:**
- Weeks 1–2: Parts 3 & 4 intensive
- Weeks 3–4: Part 5 vocabulary expansion + synonyms
- Weeks 5–6: Part 7 single-passage skills
- Weeks 7–8: Full practice sets + error analysis

---

### Target: 800 (Current: 680–750)

**Focus:** Minimize LC errors + conquer Part 7 double passages

**LC priorities:**
- Parts 3 & 4: visual-integration questions (charts, maps) — dedicated drill
- Speed adaptation: listen at 1.1×–1.2× speed
- Zero tolerance for sound-alike and association traps

**RC priorities:**
- Part 7: double-passage cross-reference questions
- Part 5 & 6: aim for full marks
- Vocabulary: advanced business and academic words

---

### Target: 900+ (Current: 800–870)

**Focus:** Eliminate ALL careless errors + triple-passage precision

**Priorities:**
- Reduce answer time per question (speed + accuracy)
- Triple-passage: systematically locate cross-reference information
- Build advanced vocabulary and idiomatic expression knowledge
- Deep error analysis: categorize every wrong answer by type

## Error Analysis Framework

Systematic error analysis accelerates score improvement more than simply doing more practice tests.

```
After each practice test, categorize every error:

Category A — Knowledge gap:
  → Don't know the word / grammar rule
  → Fix: study the rule / learn the word

Category B — Careless error:
  → Knew the answer but misread or rushed
  → Fix: slow down; underline key words while reading

Category C — Trap fell for:
  → Chose a distractor designed to mislead
  → Fix: identify the trap type and drill that pattern

Category D — Time pressure:
  → Ran out of time; guessed without reading
  → Fix: adjust time allocation; practice skimming
```

## Test-Day Checklist

```
Night before:
□ Review your personal error categories (not new material)
□ Go to sleep at a normal time

Morning of the test:
□ Eat a proper meal (blood sugar affects concentration)
□ Arrive at the test center 30 minutes early
□ Bring ID, test admission ticket, pencils

During the test:
□ LC: Pre-read questions during the directions; never wait passively
□ RC Part 5: 20–30 seconds per question; skip and return if stuck
□ RC Part 7: Read questions before passages
□ Never leave blanks — there is no penalty for guessing
□ If you fall behind in Part 7, use process of elimination and move on
```

### LC Practice (Questions 1–10)

**Questions 1–3** refer to the following conversation:

*Man: "I can't get this printer to work. Do you know if there's a technician on call today?"*  
*Woman: "I'm not sure, but you might want to check with the front desk. They usually have a list of available maintenance staff."*  
*Man: "That's a good idea. I'll head over there now."*

(A) He cannot find the front desk.  
(B) A piece of equipment is not functioning.  
(C) He does not know the maintenance staff.

**2.** What does the woman suggest?
(A) Calling a technician directly  
(B) Contacting the front desk for information  
(C) Waiting for maintenance staff to arrive  
(D) Fixing the printer himself

**3.** What will the man most likely do next?
(A) Return to his desk  
(B) Call the woman's supervisor  
(C) Go to the front desk  
(D) Purchase a new printer
