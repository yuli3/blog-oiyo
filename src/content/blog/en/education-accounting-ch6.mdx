Equity is the residual interest in assets after deducting liabilities. So the first question in equity accounting is "is this financial instrument really equity?", and the second is "which box of equity does a transaction with owners move?". The first is answered by K-IFRS 1032 (IAS 32), the second by the Korean Commercial Act and local practice. The basics of equity components were covered in [Principles of Accounting chapter 5](/en/academy-accounting-basics-ch2/) and [chapter 10](/en/academy-accounting-principles-ch10/). Earnings per share is covered in [chapter 17](/en/academy-intermediate-accounting-ch17/).

## 1. Distinguishing liabilities from equity

If a financial instrument contains any **contractual obligation to deliver cash or another financial asset**, that part is a financial liability, even if its legal form is a share.

A contract settled in the entity's own equity instruments is equity only if it **exchanges a fixed number of shares for a fixed amount**. A contract under which the number of shares varies uses the shares like currency and is a financial liability.

## 2. Share issues

### A. Rights issues

Issuing 10,000 shares with a par value of ₩5,000 at ₩9,000 each, with issue costs of ₩3 million, gives share capital of ₩50 million and share premium of ₩90 million − ₩50 million − ₩3 million = ₩37 million. Issue costs are deducted from equity, not expensed. If shares are issued below par, the difference is recorded as a discount on share issue (capital adjustment) and first offset against share premium.

In all three, total equity and shareholders' percentage interests are unchanged. A bonus issue turns legal reserves into share capital, a stock dividend turns distributable profit into share capital, and a share split moves no box of equity at all.

## 3. Treasury shares (cost method)

Treasury shares are recorded at cost and deducted from equity. On disposal, the difference between proceeds and cost is dealt with inside equity.

<LectureTable
  title="Treasury share transactions (unit: ₩10,000)"

  rows={[
    ["Buy 1,000 shares at ₩10,000", "—", "Treasury shares 1,000"],

  ]}
/>

A loss on disposal is first offset against existing gains on disposal of treasury shares; any remainder is held as a capital adjustment and later written off through appropriation of retained earnings. Gains and losses are settled only within equity because of the principle that no profit or loss is recognized on transactions with owners.

## 4. Capital reductions

- **Paid capital reduction:** shares are retired for consideration paid to shareholders. If less than par is paid, the difference is a **gain on capital reduction** (capital surplus); if more, a **loss on capital reduction** (capital adjustment).
- **Deficit-covering capital reduction (without consideration):** share capital is reduced without payment to eliminate an accumulated deficit. Consolidating 20,000 shares of ₩5,000 par (share capital ₩100 million) at five into one reduces share capital to ₩20 million, and the ₩80 million reduction covers the deficit. If the reduction exceeds the deficit, the excess is a gain on capital reduction.

## 5. Appropriation of retained earnings and covering deficits

Retained earnings are divided into the legal reserve, voluntary reserves and unappropriated retained earnings. A deficit is usually covered first by unappropriated retained earnings and voluntary reserves, and then by legal reserves. Before the 2011 amendment of the Commercial Act, the earned legal reserve had to be used before the capital reserve, but since the amendment there is no order between the two (Commercial Act, Article 460). If the capital and earned legal reserves together exceed 1.5 times share capital, the excess may be reduced by a shareholders' resolution and used, for example, for dividends (Article 461-2).

<Callout type="warning">
The boxes of equity differ by country. The body of K-IFRS requires little more than paid-in capital, retained earnings and other components, so the split into capital surplus and capital adjustments is a product of Korean practice and the Commercial Act. When comparing with foreign financial statements, it is safer to reconcile only total equity and accumulated other comprehensive income. Redeemable convertible preference shares (RCPS) are common in start-up investment; if holders have a redemption right, they are classified as liabilities and can push the debt ratio up sharply.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

Company AA (par ₩5,000) bought 2,000 treasury shares at ₩8,000 each. It then sold 800 shares at ₩9,500 and 700 shares at ₩6,000, and retired the remaining 500. There was no opening balance of gains on disposal of treasury shares. How do the equity items change at each step, and by how much has total equity fallen overall?

The purchase deducts treasury shares of ₩16 million from equity. Selling 800 shares brings in ₩7.6 million of cash; the ₩1.2 million difference from their cost of ₩6.4 million is a gain on disposal. Selling 700 shares brings in ₩4.2 million against a cost of ₩5.6 million, a loss of ₩1.4 million; it is first offset against the gain of ₩1.2 million, and the remaining ₩200,000 is a loss on disposal. Retiring 500 shares eliminates share capital of ₩2.5 million and treasury shares of ₩4 million, with the ₩1.5 million difference recorded as a loss on capital reduction. Total equity changes only by the cash flows: $-1{,}600 + 760 + 420 = -420$ (₩10,000), a fall of ₩4.2 million. The retirement does not change total equity.
