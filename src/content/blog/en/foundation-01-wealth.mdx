## Ⅰ. Assets vs Liabilities

In his book *Rich Dad Poor Dad*, Robert Kiyosaki offered a very clear definition.

- **Assets**: Things that **put money into your pocket** (dividends, rent, interest, stocks, and so on)
- **Liabilities**: Things that **take money out of your pocket** (car installments, credit-card installments, depreciating materials, and so on)

Even a place we commonly call “my home,” if it requires loan interest and taxes, is closer to a liability in cash-flow terms, even if it is classified as an asset in accounting.

---

## Ⅱ. The Rule of 72 (the Magic of Compound Interest) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This is the easiest way to calculate how long it takes for your principal to **double**.

> **72 ÷ rate of return = years until the principal doubles**

- 10% annual return: 7.2 years
- 6% annual return: 12 years
- 2% annual return: 36 years

Compound interest rises gradually at first, but once it passes a certain threshold, it grows exponentially. This is called the “snowball effect.”

---

## Ⅲ. Inflation: The Invisible Thief ⭐⭐⭐⭐

If the actual inflation rate is 4%, the purchasing power of your bank balance is disappearing by 4% each year. Holding only deposits instead of investing is not risk-free; it is like choosing **“a certain path to becoming poorer.”**
