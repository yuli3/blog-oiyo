## What Is a Tipping Point?

If you keep filling a glass with water, at some point it suddenly overflows. Climate systems have analogous thresholds: when a critical temperature is crossed, the system **abruptly and irreversibly shifts** into a new state. This is a **climate tipping point**.

Even if humanity stopped all greenhouse gas emissions today, some tipping points may already have been crossed — or may be crossed very soon.

---

## Key Climate Tipping Points

### 1. West Antarctic Ice Sheet (WAIS) Collapse
If the western Antarctic ice sheet melts, global sea levels rise 3–5 meters. Recent research warns this process may already be unstoppable. Once begun, it plays out over centuries to millennia — but cannot be reversed.

### 2. Greenland Ice Sheet Melt
Complete Greenland ice melt would raise sea levels ~7 meters. Scientists estimate the tipping point lies somewhere between 1.5–2°C of warming above pre-industrial temperatures.

### 3. Amazon Rainforest Dieback
The Amazon generates its own rainfall — a self-sustaining "flying river." If deforestation exceeds 20–25% of the forest, rainfall declines, triggering a drying feedback loop that converts rainforest to savanna. Currently, 17–20% has already been cleared.

<Callout type="tip">
**The Amazon reversal**: The Amazon historically absorbed carbon from the atmosphere. A 2021 study found that the eastern Amazon now emits more carbon than it absorbs — the forest switching from a carbon sink to a carbon source. This is one of the most alarming signals in climate science.
</Callout>

### 4. Permafrost Thaw
Permafrost in Siberia, Canada, and Alaska stores roughly twice the carbon currently in the atmosphere — locked in frozen organic matter. As it thaws, it releases methane and CO₂, accelerating warming in a **positive feedback loop** beyond human control.

---

## The Domino Effect

More alarming than individual tipping points is that they're **interconnected**. One falling can trigger others.

Example: Ice sheets melt → ocean circulation changes → Amazon rainfall patterns shift → Amazon dries out → more carbon released → temperatures rise further → more permafrost thaws...

A landmark 2018 *Nature* paper warned that cascading tipping points could push Earth toward a "Hothouse Earth" state — 4–5°C warmer than today, with sea levels tens of meters higher — regardless of subsequent human action.

---

## How Much Time Is Left?

The IPCC states that to limit warming to 1.5°C, global emissions must fall 45% by 2030 and reach net-zero by 2050.

Even if all current national pledges are fully delivered, end-of-century warming is projected at 2.5–3°C.

This is not a counsel of despair — it's a call for urgency. Tipping points aren't cliff edges but probability zones; every tenth of a degree matters. The question isn't "can we still act?" but "how fast can we move?"
