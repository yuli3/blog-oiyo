Sansin is a deity who guards mountains in Korean mountain belief. The mountain was both the backdrop surrounding a village and a sacred space containing water, trees, medicinal herbs, game, and ancestral graves. For that reason, people believed that a mountain had an owner, and that treating that owner carelessly could bring disaster. Sansin appears in many forms: an old man with a white beard, a deity accompanied by a tiger, a female mountain spirit, or a being understood as an ancestral figure of a particular region. The details vary by tradition, but Sansin is a being who guards the boundary between nature and humans.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

In Sansin tales, many stories describe a person lost in the mountains who meets a mysterious old man and receives either help or a test. Sansin may reveal the location of medicinal herbs, or punish a greedy person. These stories show that the mountain gives resources to humans, but becomes dangerous when approached with greed.

The Sansingak inside Buddhist temples also shows a distinctive feature of Korean Sansin belief. Even after Buddhism entered Korea, the mountain deity did not disappear, but continued to be enshrined within temple space. In Sansin paintings, Sansin is often shown seated together with a tiger. Here, the tiger is a companion that represents the fear and authority of the mountain.

Rather than belonging to one central myth, Sansin is joined to different memories attached to each local mountain. The Sansin of one mountain may be understood like a general, the Sansin of another like a grandmother, and in some places even like Dangun. For that reason, it is more accurate to see Sansin as a layered deity formed by Korea's geography and local communities.

## Reading as an Archetype — Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Sansin is an archetype of the **Wise Old Man** and the **Guardian of the Wild**. He shows the way, but reveals the danger of the mountain to those who are not prepared. In modern terms, he resembles nature conservation, hiking ethics, a strict teacher in a specialized field, or a guide encountered in deep inner work.

The Sansin archetype says that humans are not owners of nature, but visitors. Entering the mountain requires permission and humility. Sansin is therefore a symbol of the old wisdom that anyone who wants protection must first respect boundaries.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Sansin a Buddhist deity?**

A. Sansin originally has strong roots in mountain belief and folk religion. However, as Sansin came to be enshrined in the Sansingak of Korean Buddhist temples, Sansin also became an important deity within Buddhist space.

**Q. Why is there a tiger beside Sansin?**

A. The tiger symbolizes the mountain's authority and fear, as well as the power of the wild. It shows both Sansin's protective force and the danger of the mountain.
