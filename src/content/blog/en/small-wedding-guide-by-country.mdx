## Why small weddings are on the rise

Fatigue with oversized venues, ballooning costs, and formal guest-list obligations is pushing more couples toward **a smaller, more meaningful wedding.** Small weddings typically run 20-50 guests, and the whole point is cutting cost while raising authenticity. Here's how the format and cost play out by country.

---

## South Korea

```
Format: house weddings, restaurant weddings, outdoor (garden)
      weddings, small chapel weddings, self-planned weddings.
Guests: family and close friends, roughly 30-60 people.
Cost: can be meaningfully cheaper than a large wedding hall,
      though the per-person catering rate and venue still swing it a lot.
Notes: emcee and officiant duties are often skipped or handled by
      a friend, the run of show is flexible, and DIY touches
      (self-made bouquets, décor) are common.
```

- The per-person catering rate tends to be the single biggest lever on total cost — controlling headcount is key.
- Even a "small" wedding usually still budgets separately for studio photos, the dress, and hair/makeup.

---

## Japan

```
Shoninzu-kon (少人数婚, "small-headcount wedding") / kazoku-kon (家族婚, "family wedding"):
  - Family-centered, 10-30 guests, in a restaurant or small hotel banquet room.
Photo wedding (フォトウェディング):
  - No ceremony at all — just photos. Minimizes cost.
Nashi-kon (ナシ婚, "no-ceremony wedding"):
  - Just the legal registration, with a trip or a meal in place of a ceremony.
```

- Japan has a well-developed "kaihi-sei" (会費制) culture, where guests pay a fixed attendance fee, which caps their financial burden.
- The spectrum runs wide, from photo-only weddings to full family weddings.

---

## United States

```
Micro Wedding: roughly 20 or fewer guests.
Elopement: just the couple, or a tiny handful of witnesses,
  often at a courthouse or outdoors. Frequently combined with travel.
Courthouse Wedding: a simple legal marriage at
  a courthouse or city hall, plus a small celebration.
```

- The emphasis is on "experience" — a chef's dinner, a scenic natural setting, a freeform run of show.
- Elopement is the option for couples who want to cut cost to the bone.

---

## Europe

```
Town Hall / Mairie wedding:
  - The legal marriage happens simply at a town hall (common in France, Germany, etc.).
Intimate Wedding:
  - A small circle of family and friends, often at a countryside farmhouse, castle, or garden.
```

- Many European countries formally separate the legal (civil) marriage from any religious or symbolic ceremony.

## Small wedding prep checklist (works across countries)

```
1. Lock the concept and scale: guest count, mood (casual/formal)
2. Allocate budget: venue / catering / hair-makeup-dress / photography / styling
3. Book the venue: factor in headcount, season, indoor vs. outdoor
4. Design the run of show: emcee, officiant, toasts, ceremony
5. Send invitations personally and thoughtfully — it matters more at a small scale
6. Document it: photo and video matter even more the smaller the wedding gets
```

| Category | Cost-saving tip |
|------|-------------|
| Venue | Weekdays, mornings, off-season, renting out a restaurant |
| Catering | Control headcount; a buffet or family-style meal over a plated course |
| Styling | Rent, do your own makeup, separate the photo session from the ceremony |
| Production | DIY touches, a self-made bouquet, a friend as emcee |

## Who a small wedding is right for

1. Couples who value **what the day means to the two of them** more than formality.
2. Couples who want **real connection** with the people closest to them.
3. Couples who'd rather put the money toward **the honeymoon or their first home.**

The essence of a small wedding isn't "small" — it's **"unmistakably us."** The smaller the scale, the more freely a couple's own style can come through.
