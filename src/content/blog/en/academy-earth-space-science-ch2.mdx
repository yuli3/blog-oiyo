## Lecture 2 Overview: Our Neighborhood, the Solar System

Now that we've grasped the scale of the universe (Lecture 1), let's tour our own home: the solar system. It's a single star (the Sun) and the family of planets, moons, and small bodies bound to it by gravity.

<Callout type="info">
**Goal of this lecture**: Understand the structure of the solar system centered on the Sun, the difference between rocky and giant gas planets, and the role small bodies play.
</Callout>

---

## 1. The Sun — 99.8% of the Solar System's Mass

The Sun is a star that accounts for about **99.86%** of the solar system's total mass. It generates energy through **nuclear fusion**, converting hydrogen into helium.

- **Composition**: about 74% hydrogen + about 24% helium
- **Surface temperature**: about 5,500°C / core: about 15 million °C
- **Energy source**: hydrogen fusion (about 600 million tons of hydrogen fused every second)

The Sun's overwhelming gravity is what keeps every planet locked in orbit. For more on the mechanics behind this, see [Intro to Gravity and Orbits](/en/science-gravity-orbit-guide/).

## 2. Two Types of Planets

| Category | Rocky (terrestrial) | Giant gas/ice (Jovian) |
|------|--------------|----------------------|
| Planets | Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune |
| Size | Smaller, generally denser | Larger, generally less dense |
| Surface | Solid rock | No solid surface (gas, ice) |
| Moons/rings | Few | Many (well-developed rings) |

<Callout type="tip">
Between the two groups lies the **asteroid belt** (between Mars and Jupiter). The inner solar system was too hot for anything but rock to remain, while the cooler outer region let gas and ice accumulate into giant planets. For a closer look, see [Intro to the Rocky Planets](/en/science-rocky-planets-guide/) and [Intro to the Giant Planets](/en/science-giant-planets-guide/).
</Callout>

## 3. The Planets at a Glance

From the Sun outward: **Mercury → Venus → Earth → Mars → Jupiter → Saturn → Uranus → Neptune**. The asteroid belt lies mainly between Mars and Jupiter; the Kuiper Belt extends beyond Neptune. This ordering is not a scale drawing.

| Planet | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| Mercury | Smallest planet and closest to the Sun |
| Venus | Thick carbon dioxide atmosphere; hottest planetary surface, about 460°C |
| Earth | Liquid water and life; axial tilt produces seasons |
| Mars | Red surface and evidence of past water |
| Jupiter | Largest planet; the Great Red Spot is a long-lived storm |
| Saturn | Prominent ring system |
| Uranus | Strongly tilted rotation axis |
| Neptune | Extremely fast atmospheric winds |

## 4. Moons and Small Bodies

- **Moons**: bodies orbiting a planet. Earth's Moon drives tides and helps stabilize Earth's axial tilt.
- **Asteroids**: found mainly in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
- **Comets**: "dirty snowballs" of ice and dust that grow a tail as they approach the Sun.
- **Dwarf planets**: Pluto, Ceres, and others. Pluto was reclassified from planet to dwarf planet in 2006.

---

## 5. Why Pluto Is No Longer a Planet

In 2006, the International Astronomical Union (IAU) formally defined what counts as a "planet."

The IAU's 2006 definition applies to bodies in **our solar system**, excluding satellites:

1. The body orbits the Sun.
2. Its self-gravity is sufficient to produce a nearly round shape.
3. It has cleared the neighborhood around its orbit.

Pluto satisfies the first two criteria but not the third, so it is classified as a dwarf planet. "Cleared" means gravitational dominance, not a perfectly empty orbit. See the [definition reproduced by NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/what-is-a-planet/).

<Callout type="note">
Scientific definitions change as new discoveries are made. Pluto's reclassification wasn't a "demotion" — it was a reorganization of the classification system after several similar objects were found in the Kuiper Belt.
</Callout>

## Key Takeaways

1. The Sun is a hydrogen-fusing star that makes up about 99.86% of the solar system's mass.
2. Planets split into rocky worlds (the inner four) and giant gas/ice worlds (the outer four).
3. Small bodies cluster in the asteroid belt at the boundary and the Kuiper Belt further out.
4. Moons, asteroids, comets, and dwarf planets fill out the rest of the solar system.
5. Pluto became a dwarf planet because it fails the third condition of the planet definition — clearing its orbital neighborhood.
