## What is a destination wedding?

A destination wedding is a ceremony held **somewhere other than where the couple lives.** The appeal is combining the wedding with the honeymoon, and celebrating with a small circle of close guests in a special location. It's sometimes called a "honeymoon wedding."

---

## Popular destination wedding locations

| Location | What it's known for |
|------|------|
| **Hawaii** | The classic beach wedding — chapels, sunsets. US territory, so the legal process is straightforward |
| **Bali (Indonesia)** | Cliffside and resort weddings, good value, striking scenery |
| **Cancún / the Caribbean** | Close to North America, well-developed all-inclusive resort packages |
| **the Maldives** | Luxury resorts, maximum privacy |
| **Europe (Italy, France)** | Castles, villas, and the Tuscan countryside — a classic look |
| **Local countryside alternatives** | Domestic escapes that feel like a getaway without the travel burden |

## The legal side — the most important checkpoint

```
Key point: the "ceremony" and the "legal marriage" can be two separate things.

Type A) Symbolic ceremony:
  - Hold the ceremony abroad, and file the
    legal marriage registration separately at home later. (Most common)

Type B) Legally binding marriage abroad:
  - Requires meeting local legal requirements — paperwork,
    residency periods, notarization. Requirements vary widely by country.
```

- Most couples choose **a symbolic ceremony abroad + legal registration back home.**
- If you want the marriage to be legally binding on location, check that country's requirements well in advance (documents, residency, translation, apostille).

## Cost and guest considerations

```
Cost breakdown:
  - Wedding package (venue, officiant, flowers, hair and makeup)
  - Flights and accommodation (for the couple + guests attending)
  - Photo/video, dress, local coordination
Guests:
  - Keep the list small (immediate family, close friends). Factor in guests' travel and lodging costs.
  - Be upfront about dates and costs when you send invitations.
```

- Guests take on real travel expense, so be thoughtful about headcount and timing.
- Read the vendor's package carefully for what's included, refund policy, and rain contingency.

## A taste of wedding culture by country

```
United States: rehearsal dinner → ceremony → reception (first dance, toasts, cake cutting).
Italy: a church ceremony plus a villa reception, with confetti (sugared almonds) as favors.
India: a multi-day celebration (mehndi, sangeet, baraat).
Japan: couples choose between Shinto, church-style, or secular ceremonies.
Mexico: the lazo (cord) and arras (13 coins) rituals.
```

- Weaving in a bit of local tradition can make a destination wedding feel even more special.

---

## Destination wedding planning timeline

```
6-12 months out: choose location, date, and vendor; confirm legal requirements
4-6 months out: finalize guest list, book flights/lodging, dress shopping
2-3 months out: local coordination and detailed scheduling, prepare documents
1 month out: final confirmations (rain plan, reservation checks)
After you're home: file the legal marriage registration if needed
```

---

## Who a destination wedding is right for

1. Couples who want the wedding and honeymoon to be **one experience.**
2. Couples who care more about **the setting and mood** than tradition.
3. Couples who want an **intimate day** with the people closest to them.

The heart of a destination wedding isn't extravagance — it's **a story only that place could give you.** Just make sure you've nailed down the legal side of the marriage well ahead of time.
