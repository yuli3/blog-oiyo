# The Full MBTI Test (Likert Scale Version)

Most MBTI tests you'll find online force you to pick "A or B" for every question. But in real life, nobody is 100% E or 100% I. Most of us sit somewhere on a continuous spectrum — say, 60% E and 40% I.

This test uses a **5-point Likert scale** (not at all true → very true) so you can express exactly how much you agree with each statement. Your result isn't a flat four-letter type — it's a percentage breakdown across each dimension.

## The 4 Dimensions of MBTI

| Dimension | What It Measures | Extreme Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **E / I** | Where you direct your energy | E: recharges at a party ↔ I: recharges alone |
| **S / N** | How you process information | S: facts and experience ↔ N: patterns and possibilities |
| **T / F** | How you make decisions | T: logic and principle ↔ F: values and feeling |
| **J / P** | How you approach life | J: plans and deadlines ↔ P: flexibility and spontaneity |

MBTI was developed by Isabel Briggs Myers and Katherine Cook Briggs, building on Carl Jung's theory of psychological types (1921). Since its first publication in 1962, an estimated 1.5 to 2 million people worldwide use it every year for career counseling, team building, and personal development.

## How to Read Your Results

### Reading MBTI as a Percentage

For example, 70% E / 30% I points to a clear extrovert, while 52% E / 48% I is closer to an ambivert. That subtle difference can look very different in day-to-day life.

<Callout type="tip">
**MBTI is a tool, not an identity.** Your type doesn't cap what you're capable of. "I'm an INTJ, so I can't express emotion" is a much less useful frame than "I lean INTJ, and I'm practicing expressing emotion anyway."
</Callout>
