A provision is **a liability of uncertain timing or amount**. Unlike ordinary liabilities it involves estimation, so the heart of the standard is when to recognize it as a liability and at what amount. The standard is K-IFRS 1037 *Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets* (IAS 37). Financial liabilities such as bonds were covered in [chapter 7](/en/academy-intermediate-accounting-ch7/).

## 1. Recognition criteria

A provision is recognized when all three of the following are met.

1. The entity has a **present obligation** (legal or constructive) as a result of a past event.
2. It is **probable** (more likely than not) that an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation.
3. A **reliable estimate** can be made of the amount.

A **constructive obligation** arises when past practice or published policies create a valid expectation in others that the entity will discharge the responsibility. Even without a legal obligation, consistently honouring a policy of "free refunds within one year of purchase" creates a constructive obligation.

### A. It must not depend on future actions

A present obligation must be one the entity cannot avoid whatever it does in future. An obligation to clean up pollution a factory has already caused is a present obligation. But if a change in the law requires filters to be fitted to the factory within three years, the entity can avoid this by closing the factory or changing how it operates, so there is no present obligation. **Future operating losses** are not provisions either.

Contingent assets are not recognized so as not to bring forward unrealized gains. This is a typical area where liabilities and assets are treated asymmetrically.

## 3. Measurement: the best estimate

A provision is measured at the **best estimate** of the expenditure required to settle the obligation at the end of the reporting period.

### A. A large population: expected value

Company X sold 1,000 appliances this year. Experience shows that 20% have minor defects costing ₩50,000 each to repair, 5% have major defects costing ₩400,000 each, and the remaining 75% have none.

### B. A single obligation: the most likely outcome

For a single obligation, such as one lawsuit, the most likely outcome is the starting point. But if the other possible outcomes are mostly higher or lower, the estimate is adjusted in that direction.

### C. Present value and interest expense

Where the time value of money is material, the provision is measured at present value. At the beginning of 20X1, company Y took on an obligation to dismantle and restore equipment in three years' time; the estimated cost is ₩10 million and the discount rate 5%. The initial provision is $1{,}000 / 1.05^3 ≈ 863.8$ (₩10,000), i.e. about ₩8.638 million, and the same amount is added to the cost of the equipment. Each year the opening provision multiplied by the discount rate is recognized as **interest expense**: ₩432,000 in 20X1, ₩453,000 in 20X2 and ₩476,000 in 20X3, so that the provision reaches ₩10 million at the end of year 3.

## 4. Special cases

- **Onerous contracts:** if the unavoidable costs of meeting a contract exceed the expected benefits, the net cost (the lower of the cost of fulfilling it and the penalty for exiting) is recognized as a provision. Under the 2022 amendment, the cost of fulfilling includes both incremental costs and an allocation of other directly related costs.
- **Restructuring:** a constructive obligation arises when there is a detailed formal plan and the entity has started to implement it or announced its main features to those affected, raising a valid expectation. A board decision alone is not enough. Retraining or relocating continuing staff and investment in new systems are not included.
- **Reimbursements:** amounts to be reimbursed by third parties, such as insurance, are recognized as a separate asset only when **virtually certain** and are not offset against the provision.

<Callout type="warning">
Provisions are easily used to manage earnings. Building large provisions in a good year and releasing them in a bad one makes profit look smooth. That is why the standard allows a provision to be used only for the expenditure for which it was originally recognized and requires it to be reviewed at each reporting date and adjusted to the best estimate. Litigation provisions and contingent liability notes can change significantly, so compare the movements and their basis year by year.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

At the end of 20X1 company Z faces two lawsuits. In lawsuit A, the probability of losing is 70%, and if it loses, damages will be ₩5 million (60%) or ₩8 million (40%). In lawsuit B, the probability of losing is 30%, with damages of ₩10 million if it loses. It is also virtually certain that an insurer will reimburse half of the damages in lawsuit A. How is this reflected in the financial statements?

For lawsuit A an outflow is probable, so a provision is recognized. As a single obligation, the most likely outcome of ₩5 million is the starting point. But the other outcome (₩8 million, 40%) is higher, so the standard requires an amount above ₩5 million as the best estimate; the probability-weighted amount $500 \times 0.6 + 800 \times 0.4 = 620$ (₩10,000), i.e. ₩6.2 million, is a reference for that judgement. The insurance reimbursement is virtually certain and is recognized as a separate asset, not offset against the provision; in the income statement the provision expense may be presented net of the reimbursement. For lawsuit B an outflow is not probable, so nothing is recognized and it is disclosed as a contingent liability.
