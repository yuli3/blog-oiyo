### Introduction: Responsibility Running on Tracks: All About Driving Licenses

Just as a license is needed to drive a car, a more stringent **'Railway Driving License'** is required to control railway vehicles weighing hundreds of tons. This is not just about manipulation skills; it is a process of legally recognizing expertise in understanding complex railway systems and dealing with emergency situations.

For those interested in the South Korean rail industry, understanding the types of licenses, eligible ages, grounds for disqualification, and administrative disposal criteria for license cancellation is essential. Today, we summarize the driving license and training system, which is a gateway that prospective engineers must pass.

---

### 1. Types of Railway Driving Licenses (Article 10)

Railway driving licenses are divided into six types according to their power source and structure.

1.  **High-speed Railway Vehicle Driving License:** High-speed trains (such as KTX) and railway equipment.
2.  **First-class Electric Vehicle Driving License:** Electric locomotives and railway equipment.
3.  **Second-class Electric Vehicle Driving License:** Electric multiple units (urban and commuter trains) and railway equipment.
4.  **Diesel Vehicle Driving License:** Diesel locomotives, diesel multiple units, steam locomotives and railway equipment.
5.  **Railway Equipment Driving License:** Track construction and maintenance machines, inspection vehicles, accident-recovery cranes, etc.
6.  **Streetcar Driving License:** Trams.

The list is set by Article 11 of the Enforcement Decree of the Railroad Safety Act. Holders of the higher licenses may also drive railway equipment. ([KRIC license guide](https://kric.go.kr/jsp/board/portal/sub04/lic/certifiDetail.jsp?board_seq=24))

### 2. Disqualifications for a License (Article 11(1))

Not everyone can get a license. Anyone who falls under any of the following cannot.

1. Anyone **under 19**. (One of the most common number questions in railway law.)
2. People with **mental illness or epilepsy** that may cause danger in driving, as set by Presidential Decree.
3. People **addicted to drugs (narcotics, hallucinogens) or alcohol** that may cause danger in driving, as set by Presidential Decree.
4. People who have **completely lost hearing in both ears or sight in both eyes**.
5. People for whom **2 years** have not passed since their license was revoked, or who are **within a suspension period**.

### 3. Physical Exam, Aptitude Test, Training and Practical Apprenticeship (Articles 12, 15, 16, 21)

Applicants must pass the physical exam run by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure and Transport (Article 12) and the driving aptitude test, complete driver training (Articles 15 and 16), and then take the license exam. The length and method of training are set by ministerial ordinance (Article 16(2)).

- **Training:** Builds the knowledge and skills needed to drive, taken at a training institution designated by the Minister.
- **Theory:** Railway Safety Act, driving theory, vehicle structure, etc.
- **Skills:** Real vehicle operation and simulator training.
- **Practical apprenticeship (Article 21):** After getting a license, anyone who wants to work as a driver must complete an apprenticeship under ministerial ordinance to learn the route and real operation before driving alone.

### 4. Revocation and Suspension of a License (Article 20(1))

The Minister may revoke a license or suspend it for **up to 1 year**.

- **Mandatory revocation (items 1–4):** License obtained by fraud / becoming subject to disqualifications 2–4 (illness, addiction, hearing or sight loss) / **driving during a suspension** / **lending the license to someone else**.
- **Discretionary revocation or suspension (item 5 onward):** Causing a railway accident intentionally or by gross negligence while driving, driving after drinking or using drugs (violating Article 41(1)), refusing an alcohol or drug check, violating orders under this Act, etc.
- **Alcohol threshold (Article 41(3)):** For drivers, a blood alcohol concentration of **0.02% or more** counts as having drunk.
- **Penalty standards:** Suspension periods grow with repeat violations, ending in revocation (Enforcement Rule appendix).

### Wrapping Up: The Weight Beyond the License Card

A railway vehicle driving license is not just a plastic card. It is a 'promise with the state' to safely transport thousands of passengers to their destinations.

**A Solid Foundation: Create a chart of driving ranges by license type and engrave the numbers '19 years old' and '2 years' in your mind. Especially, the alcohol threshold for drivers (0.02%) and the fact that lending a license is a mandatory revocation ground must be remembered in conjunction with the personnel regulations discussed in previous articles. Laws are logical; if you read while thinking about why these regulations were created, complex legal provisions will become reliable allies helping your understanding of the industry.**
