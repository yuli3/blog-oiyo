## Stock Returns Are More Than "How Much Did It Go Up?"

A 30% price increase sounds great — but your actual take-home return depends on **commissions, taxes, and dividends**. The real formula:

```
Total Return = (Sale Price + Dividends Received − Purchase Price − Commissions − Taxes) / Purchase Price × 100
```

<Callout type="info" title="Tax-Loss Harvesting in Taxable Accounts">
At year-end, you can sell losing positions to realize losses that offset capital gains elsewhere in your portfolio. Key rules:
- The wash-sale rule disallows the loss if you repurchase the same or substantially identical security within 30 days
- Losses first offset gains of the same type (short/long-term), then can offset up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year
- Unused losses carry forward indefinitely
</Callout>

## 4. US Brokerage Commission Comparison

| Broker | Stock/ETF Trade | Options per Contract | Notable Feature |
|--------|---------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Fidelity | $0 | $0.65 | No account minimums; fractional shares |
| Charles Schwab | $0 | $0.65 | Strong research tools; no minimums |
| Robinhood | $0 | $0 | Commission-free options; beginner-friendly |
| TD Ameritrade / Schwab | $0 | $0.65 | thinkorswim platform for active traders |
| Interactive Brokers | $0 (IBKR Lite) | $0.65 | Best for international and margin trading |

<Callout type="tip" title="$0 Commission Still Has Hidden Costs">
Commission-free brokers generate revenue through payment for order flow (PFOF), which may mean slightly worse execution prices. For most buy-and-hold investors the impact is negligible, but active traders may get better fills at non-PFOF brokers.
</Callout>
