# Introduction to Economics — A Choice Is Already a Cost Before Any Price Is Attached

This course does not introduce economics for the first time. It is for people who have seen scarcity and the PPF at university, and it re-fixes the boundaries so that "cost" is not confused with accounting outlays in interviews, work or exams. Consumer and firm optimization continue in [Chapters 2–4](/en/academy-economics-basics-ch2/) and in microeconomics, and national income in [Chapter 7](/en/academy-economics-basics-ch7/).

## 1. Opportunity cost is the single best alternative forgone

Explicit costs are money actually paid out. Implicit costs are the value of the next-best use of the same resources. Economic cost is the sum of the two, and opportunity cost is **the single most valuable alternative forgone** within that sum. Adding up everything forgone is double counting.

Suppose a year at university costs 5 million won in tuition and 500,000 won in textbooks, the after-tax wage you could have earned in that time is 24 million won, and a room at home that you used for free could have been let for 500,000 won a month. The economic cost of the year is $5+0.5+24+6=35.5$ million won. "I've already paid the tuition, so I have to keep going" is the sunk cost fallacy of the next section.

<FormulaBox
  label="Marginal rate of transformation"
  formula="MRT = |\Delta Y / \Delta X|"
  description="The amount of Y given up to produce one more unit of X. On the PPF, this value is the opportunity cost at that point."
/>

Moving from 20 chips and 0 wheat to 18 chips and 6 wheat gives up 2 chips for 6 wheat, so the opportunity cost of one chip is 3 units of wheat. Moving the same resources from 10 chips and 24 wheat to 8 chips and 28 wheat makes the opportunity cost of a chip 2 units of wheat. When the starting point changes, the opportunity cost changes. Memorizing "the opportunity cost of a chip is 3" as a constant explains only a linear PPF.

## 2. Sunk costs are not compared with future marginal benefits

10 million won that can no longer be recovered is not an input into the decision to continue. If putting in another 2 million won recovers 4 million won, the net benefit is +2 million won. The moment the cumulative loss so far of 5 million won is put into the denominator, the question stops being "should we save this project?" and becomes "should we save our pride?"

<Callout type="warning">
The rule "go ahead if marginal benefit exceeds marginal cost" holds only when benefits and costs appear **to the same decision-maker in the same units**. With social external costs, option value or credit constraints, private and social margins diverge. That gap is [market failure in Chapter 6](/en/academy-economics-basics-ch6/).
</Callout>

## 3. The PPF puts three statements into one picture

The curve shows the maximum combinations that can be produced with given resources and technology. Being on the curve is technical efficiency (no resources wasted), not allocative efficiency (the combination people want). Inside the curve, resources are not fully used, as with unemployment or idle capital; outside it is impossible with current technology.

A PPF that is bowed out (concave to the origin) means resources are specialized for particular goods, so the marginal opportunity cost rises as you switch. A straight-line PPF is the special case in which the two goods use resources in the same proportions. Growth is an outward shift of the curve; a move along the curve is not growth but reallocation.

A market economy is an institution in which prices try to match this MRT with consumers' marginal rate of substitution; it does not mean there is no inequality. A planned economy replaces that matching with quantity orders. Real mixed economies are not an average of the two extremes but a division of labour in which government takes on what markets cannot do, such as public goods and externalities.

## Check your understanding

A piece of equipment has already cost 30 million won. Putting in another 8 million won is expected to bring in 10 million won of future cash flow, and the equipment could be sold now for 2 million won. Should you continue? Leave out the sunk 30 million won. The net benefit of continuing is $10-8=2$ million won, and selling brings 2 million won. If they are equal you are indifferent; if the timing and risk of the returns differ, discount them. "We've already spent 30 million won" is not a reason.
