## What is a chronotype?

A chronotype describes your preferred sleep-wake pattern based on your biological clock (circadian rhythm). Sleep expert Dr. Michael Breus classifies people into four animal-based types.

Chronotype is strongly influenced by genetics. Rather than forcing yourself into an early-riser schedule that doesn't fit, understanding your type and building a schedule around it tends to work much better.

---

## The 4 chronotypes

### Lion — the early bird

Wakes up naturally around 5-6am. Peak energy in the morning, and typically sleepy by 9-10pm. Best suited to strategic and planning work in the morning.

### Bear — the sun-follower

The most common type, at roughly 55% of the population. Wakes around 7-8am, dips in energy around 2-3pm, and picks back up around 4-6pm.

### Wolf — the night owl

Doesn't really activate until 11am-noon. Creative work peaks in the afternoon and evening. Struggles most with the social demands of mornings.

### Dolphin — irregular / light sleeper

About 10% of the population. Characterized by light sleep, frequent waking, and daytime fatigue. A high proportion have some form of insomnia. Focus tends to be relatively good mid-morning (9-11am).

## Optimal schedule by chronotype

| Activity | Lion | Bear | Wolf | Dolphin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Demanding focus work | 8-10am | 10am-2pm | 5-9pm | 10am-noon |
| Exercise | 5-6pm | 5-7pm | 6-8pm | 7-9am |
| Bedtime | 10-11pm | Midnight | 1-2am | Midnight-1am |

<Callout type="note">
Social schedules (school, work) are largely built around the Bear type, which puts Wolves and Dolphins at a structural disadvantage. This mismatch is sometimes called "social jetlag."
</Callout>

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Can you change your chronotype?**
A: A full change is difficult, but shifting by 1-2 hours is possible. Keeping a fixed wake time and increasing morning light exposure nudges you toward an earlier type.

**Q: Does chronotype change with age?**
A: Yes. It shifts toward a later (Wolf-like) type during adolescence, then gradually shifts back toward an earlier (Lion-like) type in adulthood. That's part of why many people naturally wake up earlier after their 50s and 60s.
