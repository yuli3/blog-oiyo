### Introduction: Do You Live Among “Facts,” or in a “World”?

We often think of life as a series of objective facts: an accident happened, money was lost, someone criticized us, and so on. But Adlerian psychology poses a startling question: **“We do not live in an objective world; we live in a subjective world seen through the glasses of ‘meaning’ that we assign.”**

Today, I would like to discuss how the many “facts” that trouble us can be transformed by our “interpretations,” and how the power of interpretation can rescue our lives.

---

### 1. Facts Do Not Change, but Meaning Does

Growing up poor is a “fact.” But what meaning we give that fact belongs entirely to us. One person may remain in hurt, interpreting it as “I can only be unhappy because of poverty,” while another may interpret it as “Because of that poverty, I learned independence and urgency” and use it as a stepping stone to success.

What matters is not what happened in the past, but how we interpret it “here and now.” Adler called this “teleology.” Perhaps we cling to past pain because we have a “purpose” of using it as an excuse to avoid present challenges. Changing an interpretation does not change the past; it prevents the past from ruling us.

### 2. The Magic of the Subjective World: Is the Glass Still Half Full?

It is a familiar example, but the difference between seeing water in a glass and thinking, “Only half is left,” or “As much as half remains,” is a “reconstruction of the world” that goes beyond the simple power of positivity.

The happiness and unhappiness we feel are determined not by external conditions, but by our subjective evaluation of those conditions. Beyond prison bars, one person sees mud while another sees stars. What exists within us, in the end, is made up of the pieces of interpretation we choose. When we reclaim sovereignty over interpretation, the world is no longer a prison that confines us but a space of infinite possibility.

### 3. The Alchemy of Turning Wounds into Experience

“Post-traumatic growth (PTG)” in psychology is one of the most dramatic examples of the power of interpretation. Even after the same tragedy, some people collapse, while others use it as nourishment and grow into deeper people.

The difference comes from changing the resentful question, “Why did this happen to me?” into the question of meaning, “What meaning could this give my life?” A person who knows how to work the alchemy of “interpretation” before painful facts can draw up their own light from any darkness in life.

### 4. Practicing Interpretation for a Better Life

- **Change “because of” to “thanks to”:** Simply replacing a negative predicate with a positive one begins to change the brain’s cognitive structure.
- **Shift your perspective (Reframing):** Redefine a “sensitive” personality as a “delicate” one, and a “slow” pace as a “careful” pace.
- **Choose a future-oriented interpretation:** Ask yourself, “Will this break me, or will it teach me?”

---

### Conclusion: Inside You Is a World You Have Made

If life feels difficult, try cleaning your “glasses of interpretation” rather than trying to change the facts. Facts are neutral. You alone are responsible for coloring them and giving them warmth.

To say that what exists within you is not facts but only interpretations is, conversely, a hopeful message that **you can rewrite your world whenever you wish**. Interpret your life in the most beautiful and radiant way. You have every right and enough strength to do so.

**What color is your world being painted today? I wish you a day filled with your own beautiful interpretations.**
