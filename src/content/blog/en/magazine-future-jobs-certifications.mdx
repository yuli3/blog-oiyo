# A Great Upheaval in the AI-Era Labor Market: Are Your Job and Credentials Safe?

As of 2026, the explosive growth of generative AI is no longer a story from a film; it has become a reality that separates companies’ quarterly results. As the evolution of technology shakes the foundations of the labor-market landscape, what work will we do five years from now, and what abilities will we need to demonstrate?

Based on the **World Economic Forum’s latest Future of Jobs Report 2025** and job-statistics data from global consulting firms, this article closely examines trends in promising and discouraged careers, as well as the changing trajectory of employers’ preference for certifications.

---

The total number of jobs will grow. The question, however, is **which jobs will grow and which will disappear**. The AI era does not create growth without employment, but it does thoroughly disrupt “mid-level repetitive knowledge work.”

### 💡 Additional Insight
- **What recommended careers share:** They are not simply for people who code well; they are for people who gather scattered information and interpret it as **business value (BI)**, as well as roles that build the company’s **security and trust**, areas AI cannot simply take over.
- **What declining careers share:** Administration, entry-level accounting, and jobs that overcome language barriers—once symbols of white-collar work—have entered the automation track first as LLMs (large language models) have developed.

## 3. Changing Hiring Standards: A Tectonic Shift in the Certification Ecosystem

The most striking change is in companies’ **way of validating talent**. Certifications filling a résumé no longer inspire human-resources managers with the same trust they once did.

<Callout type="warning">
**The End of Knowledge-Accumulation Certifications** <br/>
Certifications that ask “Who has perfectly memorized regulations?” or “Who has memorized Excel functions?” (for example, basic office-computing certificates or the first stage of multiple-choice legal-foundations credentials) are losing utility. One AI prompt can produce a solution and Excel macro code in five seconds.
</Callout>

So which credentials can represent a career that is distinct from machines?

### Characteristics of Certifications That Will Survive and Those That Will Decline

**The certification scorecard of the future**

| Certification type | Representative examples | Employer preference | Outlook for change |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| Computing / cloud / security | AWS Certified, CISA, CISSP, Information Security Engineer | Very high (core preference) | Demand for credentials that control security and infrastructure costs will surge as cloud infrastructure migrates |
| Data interpretation / analysis | SQLD and ADsP (South Korean credentials); analytics training relevant to the target role | Role-dependent | Evaluate recognition in your target country and demonstrate skills through practical analysis projects |
| Advanced professions / licenses | CPA, attorney, labor attorney, patent attorney | Retained, with changing roles | AI replaces routine search and bookkeeping; consulting, negotiation, risk management, and soft skills become essential |
| Routine office and input-based skills | Word processor, basic computerized tax accounting | Rapid decline (at risk) | Reduced to résumé decoration as employers shift to directly testing AI-tool fluency |

## 4. What It Takes to Be a “Hybrid Talent” Who Can Thrive in the Future

In the WEF report, CHROs (Chief Human Resources Officers) at numerous global companies agree that they will focus most closely on **one thing** when hiring employees in the future.

That is **analytical thinking**.

> *“Machines produce the fastest answer. But if the question is wrong, machines will faithfully race down the wrong path. We seek people with analytical thinking: people who can ask AI the right questions and critically break down whether AI’s answer is logically valid.”*

1. **Technological Literacy:** Even without coding directly, understanding mechanisms and seeing how AI can be applied to one’s work.
2. **Complex Problem-Solving Ability:** The ability to connect fragmented knowledge and plan new business models.
3. **Resilience:** A lifelong-learning attitude that can immediately learn new tools again, even when today’s skill becomes useless tomorrow.

---

## 5. Wrapping Up: How Should You Act?

An enormous opportunity lies ahead. If you are absorbed in memorization at a library to obtain a certificate, you should immediately reconsider the direction of that study.

- **If you are preparing for civil-service or public-enterprise employment:** Check the actual recruitment requirements in your country before choosing a credential. SQLD and ADsP are South Korean examples, not universal recommendations. Combine any required qualification with evidence of skills relevant to the role; no credential guarantees an interview advantage.
- **If you are already employed:** Leave behind excuses such as “I’m from the humanities” or “I’m not a developer,” and become the early adopter who tests AI solutions that can be introduced to your role first. If your technical ability is limited, become a prompt designer who combines your domain knowledge with AI to produce deep outputs that others cannot create.

The future contest for jobs is not a contest between humans and artificial intelligence.  
It is a contest between **“people who can freely wield AI”** and **“people who remain in traditional ways of working.”** Stop collecting meaningless certificates; it is time to design your own distinctive hybrid skills.
