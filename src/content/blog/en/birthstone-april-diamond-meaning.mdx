## What Is a Diamond?

Diamond is pure **carbon (C)** in crystalline form, created deep within the Earth's mantle (approximately 140–190 km below the surface) under extreme heat (~1,200°C) and pressure (~50 kbar) over billions of years. Volcanic activity (kimberlite pipes) carries them toward the surface.

**Mohs hardness: 10** — the absolute maximum on the hardness scale. No natural material can scratch a diamond. The name itself comes from the ancient Greek **'adamas'** — "unconquerable."

Major sources: Russia (ALROSA), Botswana, Canada, South Africa, and Australia. The world's largest recorded diamond, the **2,492-carat Sethuana**, was discovered in Botswana in 2025.

## History

### India: The Original Diamond Kingdom
Diamonds were first used in India around the 4th century BCE. The **Golconda region** was the world's only source of diamonds until the 18th century, producing legendary stones like the **Hope Diamond** and **Koh-i-Noor** that still carry historical weight today.

Early Indians believed diamonds were formed where **lightning struck the earth** — making them symbols of divine power and the fury of the gods. Warriors embedded diamonds in their armor for protection.

### Ancient Greece and Rome
Greeks believed diamonds were **tears of the gods** or fragments of fallen stars. Some traditions held that Cupid's arrows were tipped with diamonds, which is why diamonds became associated with love's power.

### The Engagement Ring Tradition
In 1477, Archduke Maximilian of Austria gave **Mary of Burgundy** a diamond engagement ring — the first recorded diamond proposal in history. This launched the modern tradition of diamond engagement rings.

The 19th-century discovery of South African diamond mines, combined with De Beers' legendary marketing campaign — **"A Diamond is Forever"** (1947) — cemented the diamond as the ultimate symbol of enduring love.

## Understanding the 4Cs

When evaluating or purchasing a diamond, these four factors determine quality:

| Criterion | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Carat** | Weight (1 carat = 0.2g); larger diamonds are exponentially rarer and more valuable |
| **Cut** | The most important factor; determines how light reflects and creates sparkle |
| **Color** | D (colorless, highest) through Z (yellow tint); D-F is colorless, G-J near colorless |
| **Clarity** | Freedom from internal inclusions; FL (Flawless) is the highest grade |

**Practical tip**: For most buyers, **G-H color with VS2–SI1 clarity** offers beautiful diamond beauty at a significantly better value than flawless stones.

## Lab-Grown Diamonds

Laboratory-grown diamonds are rapidly changing the industry. They are **chemically and physically identical** to natural diamonds (pure crystallized carbon), typically priced at 1/3 to 1/5 of equivalent natural diamonds.

From an energetic perspective, opinions differ: some say the absence of billions of years of natural compression creates a different energy; others argue chemical identity means energetic identity. What matters most is your intention and connection to the stone.
