# Freelancer Contract Guide

The most common freelance crisis: "I did the work, they won't pay, and there's no contract." A contract isn't a trust document — it's a **legally enforceable evidence document**. Every freelance engagement, no matter how small, deserves one.

---

## 1. Why Contracts Are Non-Negotiable

| Without a contract | With a contract |
|---|---|
| Scope disputes: can't prove what was agreed | Defined scope creates basis for out-of-scope billing |
| Unpaid work: hard to pursue legally | Payment terms give you clear legal standing |
| IP ownership unclear | Explicit IP assignment clause protects both parties |
| Tax evidence missing | Formal contract supports expense deductions |

<Callout type="warning">
**Verbal contracts are legally valid** in most jurisdictions, but almost impossible to prove. Email and message threads serve as supplementary evidence, but a written contract is always the strongest protection.
</Callout>

## 2. Eight Essential Contract Clauses

### ① Parties
- Full legal names (or business names), addresses, and contact details of both parties
- Business registration number if the client is a company

### ② Scope of Work
The single most important clause. Vague language creates disputes.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Build a website" | "5-page responsive website; 3 rounds of Figma design revision included" |
| "Write marketing content" | "12 Instagram feed posts/month + 4 blog articles (2,000 words each)" |
| "Translation work" | "English → Spanish technical document translation, 15,000 words" |

### ③ Deadlines and Milestones
- Final deadline + intermediate review dates
- Built-in buffer: "If client feedback is delayed, delivery date extends by equal time"

### ④ Payment Terms
```
Fee: $X,XXX USD (VAT excluded / included)
Terms: 30% deposit on signing, 70% balance within 7 business days of approval
Method: Bank transfer (account details attached)
```

<Callout type="tip">
**Deposit upfront is the #1 protection against non-payment.** Standard practice: 30% minimum, 50% for large projects. "Pay when it's done" is a red flag — don't start work without a deposit.
</Callout>

### ⑤ Revision Limit
- "2 rounds of revisions included; additional revisions billed at $XX/hour"
- Unlimited revisions is an invisible pay cut

### ⑥ Intellectual Property (IP) Clause

| Model | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Full transfer on payment** | Copyright transfers to client upon full payment |
| **License grant** | Freelancer retains copyright; grants client usage rights |
| **Retain portfolio rights** | "Freelancer may display work in portfolio" |

<Callout type="note">
In most common law jurisdictions (US, UK, Australia), work created by an independent contractor **does not automatically become "work for hire."** Without a written IP transfer clause, copyright stays with the creator. Clients who assume "we paid for it, it's ours" may be wrong.
</Callout>

### ⑦ Confidentiality (NDA)
- Client's unpublished business information stays private
- Specify portfolio usage rights explicitly

### ⑧ Termination and Dispute Resolution
- Notice period (e.g., "14 days written notice")
- Compensation for completed work if terminated early
- Governing law and jurisdiction

## 3. Tax Treatment for Freelancers

### Self-Employment Tax Basics

| Country | Tax Model | Self-Employment Rate |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | Sole proprietor / S-Corp | 15.3% SE tax + income tax |
| **United Kingdom** | Self-employed / Ltd company | Class 4 NICs + income tax |
| **Germany** | Freiberufler / Gewerbetreibender | Trade tax + income tax |
| **Australia** | Sole trader / Company | Income tax + GST (if > AUD 75K) |
| **Canada** | Sole proprietor | CPP contributions + income tax |

<Callout type="tip">
**Deductible expenses** (globally): laptop/equipment, software subscriptions (Figma, Adobe, GitHub), professional development, home office (proportional), phone and internet (business portion), accounting fees, professional insurance.
</Callout>

## 4. Non-Payment Prevention Checklist

Before starting any project:

- [ ] Verify client's business registration (public business registry in your jurisdiction)
- [ ] Collect deposit before starting
- [ ] Bill per milestone — don't hand over full deliverable until final payment
- [ ] Include late payment interest clause (5–15% annual rate)
- [ ] Reserve copyright until full payment received
- [ ] Use invoicing software with automatic payment reminders

<Callout type="warning">
**If payment is withheld, escalate in order**: ① Formal demand letter → ② Small claims court (usually handles up to $10K–$25K without a lawyer) → ③ Civil lawsuit. Don't wait months — act within 30 days of the due date.
</Callout>
