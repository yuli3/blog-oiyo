## What Is Value-Added Tax (VAT)?

**Value-Added Tax (VAT)** is an indirect tax levied on the **value added** at each stage of production and distribution. The final consumer ultimately bears the cost, but the tax is collected and remitted by businesses at every step in the supply chain.

"Value added" means the difference between what a business pays for its inputs and what it charges for its outputs. A freelance designer who pays \$110 (tax-inclusive) for software tools and charges a client \$550 for design services has added \$440 in value — and owes tax on that \$440, not on the full \$550.

This mechanism is called the **invoice method**: businesses collect output VAT from customers, deduct input VAT already paid on purchases, and remit only the difference to the government.

### Key Characteristics of VAT

- **Indirect tax**: The entity that pays the tax (the business) is different from the entity that ultimately bears it (the final consumer).
- **Multi-stage with credit system**: Tax is collected at every stage, but businesses can reclaim the tax they paid on inputs — so only the final consumption is taxed in practice.
- **Regressive by nature**: A flat percentage hits lower-income households proportionally harder. Most VAT systems reduce this through exemptions on essentials like food, medicine, and education.

| Situation | Formula |
|-----------|---------|
| Net price → VAT amount | VAT = Net Price × VAT Rate |
| Net price → Gross price | Gross = Net × (1 + VAT Rate) |
| Gross price → Net price | Net = Gross ÷ (1 + VAT Rate) |
| Gross price → VAT amount | VAT = Gross − (Gross ÷ (1 + VAT Rate)) |

When issuing invoices, the net price and VAT must be shown separately.

### Commonly Exempt Items (varies by country)

<Callout type="info">
**Typical VAT Exemptions Across Major Economies**

1. **Unprocessed food** — fresh produce, meat, fish (processed/packaged food often taxed)
2. **Healthcare services** — doctor visits, hospital care, prescription drugs
3. **Education** — schools, colleges, recognized training programs
4. **Financial services** — banking, insurance (though rules vary)
5. **Residential rental** — letting a home for habitation (commercial leases are typically taxable)
6. **Books and newspapers** — widely exempt or zero-rated in many countries
7. **Children's clothing** — zero-rated in the UK and Ireland, for example
</Callout>

## 4. Who Must Register for VAT?

VAT registration thresholds vary by country. Businesses with turnover above the threshold must register, collect VAT, and file returns.

| Country | Registration Threshold | Standard Rate |
|---------|----------------------|---------------|
| United Kingdom | £90,000/year | 20% |
| Australia | AUD $75,000/year | 10% (GST) |
| Canada | CAD $30,000/year | 5% (GST) + provincial |
| Germany | €22,000/year | 19% |
| France | €85,800/year (goods) | 20% |
| Singapore | SGD $1 million/year | 9% (GST) |

*Thresholds and rates as of 2024–2025. Always verify with local tax authority.*

**Total VAT remitted**: \$20 + \$40 + \$40 + \$40 = **\$140** — exactly what the consumer paid. The input credit mechanism ensures that only consumption is taxed, regardless of how many steps the product passes through.

### Non-Deductible Input Tax — Common Situations

<Callout type="warning">
**Input VAT You Typically Cannot Reclaim**

1. **No valid VAT invoice** — you must hold a proper tax invoice to claim a credit
2. **Business entertainment** — meals and entertainment for clients are often blocked or partially blocked
3. **Private use items** — goods or services with mixed business/personal use require apportionment
4. **Exempt activities** — VAT on inputs directly related to exempt supplies cannot be recovered
5. **Personal vehicles** — private cars are typically blocked even when purchased by a business
</Callout>

## 6. VAT Filing — How It Works

Most VAT-registered businesses file returns periodically (monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on turnover and country). The return reports:

- **Output VAT**: total VAT charged on sales
- **Input VAT**: total VAT paid on eligible purchases
- **Net payable or refundable**: the difference

<Callout type="info">
**VAT Refunds for Exporters**
Businesses that zero-rate most of their supplies (e.g., exporters) will often have more input VAT than output VAT, generating a refund. This is a deliberate policy to keep exports price-competitive — exports leave the country VAT-free while the exporter recovers all input costs.
</Callout>

## 8. VAT vs. US Sales Tax — Key Differences

| Feature | VAT (most countries) | US Sales Tax |
|---------|----------------------|--------------|
| Who pays at each stage | Every business in the chain; each claims credit | Only the final consumer; businesses are exempt |
| Tax visibility | Included in or shown alongside price | Usually added at checkout |
| Invoice requirements | Detailed tax invoices at every stage | No universal invoice mandate |
| Uniformity | Single national rate (with exemptions) | Varies by state, county, and city |
| Collection | Businesses collect and remit | Businesses collect and remit to state |
| Cross-border exports | Zero-rated; inputs refunded | Typically exempt for export |
