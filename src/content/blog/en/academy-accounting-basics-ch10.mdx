[Chapter 2](/en/academy-financial-accounting-advanced-ch2/) showed that FA's ROE is about 20.2%. ROE alone cannot tell whether that return comes from high margins, fast asset turnover or heavy debt. DuPont analysis splits ROE into a product of factors to reveal its sources.

## 1. Three-step DuPont analysis

<FormulaBox
  label="Three-step DuPont decomposition"
  formula="\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{Net profit}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{Net margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average total assets}}}_{\text{Asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Average total assets}}{\text{Average equity}}}_{\text{Financial leverage}}"
  description="FA in 20X2: 8% × 1.263 × 2.0 ≈ 20.2%. The product of the first two terms is ROA (10.1%); multiplying by leverage gives ROE."
/>

## 2. Same ROE, different routes

Compare FA with FB, a company in the same industry (unit: ₩100 million).

The two companies' ROEs are almost identical. FA makes up for low margins with fast turnover and high debt; FB achieves the same return with high margins and little debt. If the economy weakens and sales fall, FA's heavily indebted profit swings more. The same ROE can carry different risk.

## 3. Five-step DuPont analysis

Splitting net margin into three further parts separates the effects of tax and interest.

<FormulaBox
  label="Five-step DuPont decomposition"
  formula="\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{Net profit}}{\text{Pre-tax profit}}}_{\text{Tax burden}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Pre-tax profit}}{\text{Operating profit}}}_{\text{Interest burden}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Operating profit}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{Operating margin}} \times \text{Asset turnover} \times \text{Financial leverage}"
  description="FA: 0.800 × 0.833 × 12.0% × 1.263 × 2.00 ≈ 20.2%. A tax burden of 0.8 means an effective tax rate of 20%; an interest burden of 0.833 means 16.7% of operating profit goes on interest."
/>

More debt raises the leverage term but lowers the interest burden term. For leverage to raise ROE, the operating profit earned with the extra borrowing must exceed its interest.

## 4. Separating operating and financing performance

The traditional DuPont analysis does not distinguish operating liabilities such as trade payables from financial liabilities such as borrowings. Penman's reformulation splits the balance sheet into **net operating assets** (operating assets − operating liabilities) and **net financial obligations** (financial liabilities − financial assets). ROE can then be divided into the return earned from operations (RNOA) and the effect of financial leverage.

$$
\text{ROE} = \text{RNOA} + \frac{\text{Net financial obligations}}{\text{Equity}} \times (\text{RNOA} - \text{After-tax net borrowing cost})
$$

If RNOA exceeds the borrowing rate, debt lifts ROE; if it falls short, debt drags ROE down. Operating competitiveness and financing decisions can then be assessed separately.

<Callout type="warning">
ROE grows as its denominator, equity, shrinks. Large share buybacks or heavy dividends reduce equity and raise ROE without the business improving. A company whose equity has shrunk through accumulated losses can also show a high ROE. Always check with a DuPont decomposition whether an ROE improvement comes from margin and turnover or from leverage.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

FC's net margin is 5%, asset turnover 2.0, average total assets ₩200 billion and average equity ₩80 billion. What is its ROE? Which raises ROE more: increasing the net margin to 6% without adding debt-financed assets, or keeping the margin unchanged and using debt to raise assets to three times equity?

Financial leverage is $2{,}000 / 800 = 2.5$, so ROE is $5\% \times 2.0 \times 2.5 = 25\%$. Raising the net margin to 6% gives an ROE of 30%; raising leverage to 3 (all else equal) also gives 30%. The two routes give the same ROE, but the leverage route adds interest expense that may lower the actual net margin, and it increases financial risk. On the same assumptions, improving the margin is the higher-quality rise in ROE.
