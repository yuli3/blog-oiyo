# Macroeconomics — Short-Run Output Is Set by the Interest Rate That Clears Both the Goods and Money Markets

If prices are fixed in the short run, output is determined by demand. But part of demand, investment, depends on the interest rate; the interest rate is set in the money market; and money demand depends in turn on output. Looking at the goods and money markets separately breaks this loop. **IS-LM** puts both markets on one plane and solves them together. This chapter builds IS and LM with the same numbers and compares two policies. The general formula for the simultaneous solution is in [Chapter 23](/en/education-economics-ch39/).

## 1. IS: equilibrium in the goods market

Consider an economy with consumption $C=20+0.8(Y-T)$, taxes $T=10$, investment $I=40-2r$ (r in %) and government spending $G=30$ (all in trillions of won). Substituting into the goods-market equilibrium $Y=C+I+G$:

$$Y=20+0.8(Y-10)+40-2r+30 \quad\Rightarrow\quad Y=410-10r$$

When the interest rate rises by 1 percentage point, investment falls by 2 trillion won and, through the multiplier $1/(1-0.8)=5$, output falls by 10 trillion won. That is why IS slopes downward.

## 2. LM: equilibrium in the money market

Let real money demand be $L=0.5Y-20r$ and real balances $M/P=180$. In equilibrium $M/P=L$:

$$180=0.5Y-20r \quad\Rightarrow\quad r=0.025Y-9$$

When output rises, demand for transactions money rises, and since real balances are fixed, the interest rate must rise to reduce the demand to hold money instead of bonds. That is why LM slopes upward. The more sensitive money demand is to the interest rate (the larger the 20), the flatter LM is. A larger money supply or a lower price level shifts LM to the right.

## 3. Equilibrium and two policies

Solving IS and LM together, $Y=410-10(0.025Y-9)$ gives $Y=400$ and $r=1\%$.

**Fiscal expansion**: raising $G$ by 10 trillion won shifts IS to $Y=460-10r$. If the interest rate stayed put, output would rise by the full multiplier, 50 trillion won, to 450. But as output rises, money demand rises and the interest rate goes up. The new equilibrium is $Y=440$, $r=2\%$. The higher interest rate cuts investment by 2 trillion won, which through the multiplier pushes out 10 trillion won of output. This is the **crowding-out effect**.

**Monetary expansion**: raising real balances from 180 to 200 shifts LM to $r=0.025Y-10$. The new equilibrium is $Y=408$, $r=0.2\%$. The lower interest rate raises investment, which raises output.

<Callout type="warning">
IS-LM is a benchmark model that assumes a closed economy, fixed prices and unchanging expectations. The open economy is covered in [International Finance, Chapter 3](/en/education-international-finance-ch3/), price adjustment in Chapters 8 and 15, and expectations in Chapter 9. And since many central banks target interest rates rather than the money supply, in practice it is closer to how they operate to draw LM as "a horizontal line at the interest rate the central bank chooses". Then the crowding out from a fiscal expansion depends on whether the central bank raises its rate.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

In this economy, what is the new equilibrium if taxes are cut by 10 trillion won (T = 0)? A tax cut initially raises consumption by only $0.8\times 10=8$ trillion won, so IS becomes $0.2Y=90-2r$, i.e. $Y=450-10r$. Solving with LM, $Y=450-0.25Y+90$ gives $Y=432$ and $r=1.8\%$. The same 10 trillion won has a smaller effect as a tax cut (32 trillion) than as government spending (40 trillion), because part of the tax cut is saved in the first round.
