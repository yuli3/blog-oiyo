# The Empathy Precision Test

Empathy is one of the most complex social abilities in psychology. We tend to simplify it to "understanding how someone else feels," but modern psychology actually splits empathy into three independent dimensions.

## A Three-Dimensional Model of Empathy

**1. Cognitive Empathy (CE)**
"Why did that person act that way?" This is the ability to understand another person's thoughts and perspective — analyzing their position through logical reasoning. High CE is a core skill for negotiators, therapists, and leaders.

**2. Affective Empathy (AE)**
This is the ability to feel another person's emotion as your own — tearing up during a sad film, or feeling genuinely happy when a friend is happy. It's linked to the activity of mirror neurons.

**3. Empathic Action (EA)**
This is the ability to turn empathy into actual behavior — moving past understanding and feeling into "so what can I actually do?" It shows up strongly in volunteers, doctors, and social workers.

These three dimensions are independent of each other. You can find high cognitive empathy paired with low affective empathy (the analytical-leader type), high affective empathy that never turns into action (the sensitive-but-inward type), and many patterns in between.

## How to Read Your Results

### What Each Axis Means

**High CE, low AE (the analytical empathizer)**
You understand someone else's situation well but don't connect with it emotionally at a deep level. You give solid, objective advice, but people may see you as "cold."

**High AE, low CE (the emotional empathizer)**
You connect deeply on an emotional level but may struggle to analyze the situation logically. Watch out for empathy fatigue.

**Low EA (understands and feels, but doesn't act)**
You understand and feel, but struggle to turn that into action. This can come from a lack of confidence or from not knowing how to read the situation.

<Callout type="tip">
**Empathy is a skill you can train.** To build CE: read books and watch films from unfamiliar perspectives. To build AE: keep an emotion journal, practice mindfulness. To build EA: start with small acts of kindness.
</Callout>

### The Risk of Too Much Empathy: Empathy Fatigue

More empathy isn't automatically better. Excess AE can lead to emotional burnout, sometimes called empathy fatigue — a common issue for people in healthcare, counseling, and education. Boundary-setting and self-care need to grow alongside empathy itself.

This test is a self-understanding tool built with reference to Simon Baron-Cohen and Sally Wheelwright's Empathy Quotient (EQ) research (2004) and Mark Davis's Interpersonal Reactivity Index (IRI, 1983).
