[Chapter 4](/en/education-accounting-ch2/) showed that profit and the change in cash are different numbers. The statement of cash flows unpacks that difference by activity. The standard is K-IFRS 1007 *Statement of Cash Flows* (IAS 7). This chapter covers the classification of activities and indirect-method adjustments; preparing the statement when investing and financing are complex continues in [Intermediate Accounting chapter 18](/en/academy-intermediate-accounting-ch18/).

## 1. Distinguishing the three activities

Operating activities are the principal revenue-producing activities and everything else that is not investing or financing. Investing activities concern long-term assets and investments not included in cash equivalents; financing activities change the size and composition of equity and borrowings.

### A. Classification choices for interest and dividends

The current standard allows a choice of activity for interest and dividends, to be applied consistently from period to period.

- Interest paid: operating or financing
- Interest and dividends received: operating or investing
- Dividends paid: financing or operating
- Income taxes: operating in principle, or investing/financing if specifically identifiable with those activities

Korean companies choose differently, so align these classifications first when comparing operating cash flows across companies. K-IFRS 1118 (IFRS 18), due to apply from 2027, narrows the choice for general entities: interest paid goes to financing, and interest and dividends received go to investing.

## 2. Direct and indirect methods

Operating cash flow can be presented in two ways.

- **Direct method:** gross receipts and payments such as receipts from customers and payments to suppliers. The standard encourages it.
- **Indirect method:** starts from profit and adjusts for items that involved no cash and for changes in working capital. It is what most companies use.

Both methods give the same total operating cash flow.

## 3. The logic of indirect-method adjustments

<FormulaBox
  label="Structure of the indirect method"
  formula="\text{Operating CF} = \text{Profit} + \text{Non-cash expenses} - \text{Non-cash income} - \text{Investing/financing gains} + \text{Investing/financing losses} \pm \Delta\text{Operating working capital}"
  description="Increases in operating assets (receivables, inventory, prepayments) are deducted and decreases added. Increases in operating liabilities (payables, accrued expenses) are added and decreases deducted."
/>

Remember the direction of working capital adjustments like this: a ₩2 million increase in receivables means ₩2 million of sales has not yet been received in cash, so it is deducted from profit. An ₩800,000 increase in payables means ₩800,000 of expenses has not yet been paid in cash, so it is added.

## 4. Preparing it with numbers

Company F's figures for the year (unit: ₩10,000): profit 500, depreciation 120, gain on disposal of PP&E 30. Receivables up 200, inventory down 50, payables up 80, interest payable down 10. Equipment bought for 400; equipment with a carrying amount of 60 sold for 90. New long-term borrowings of 300 and dividends paid of 100. Opening cash is 250. Interest paid is classified as operating and dividends paid as financing.

The gain on disposal of 30 is deducted in operating, and the full 90 of cash received on disposal goes to investing. Without deducting the gain, the same 30 would be counted twice, in operating and investing.

## 5. How to read it, and its limits

Operating cash flow of 510 is close to profit of 500, but its make-up differs: depreciation of 120 is added back and the 200 increase in receivables is deducted. If receivables keep growing like this each year, profit is earned but cash does not follow. **Free cash flow** — operating cash flow less capital expenditure (510 − 400 = 110) — is used as a gauge of what is available for dividends and repayments.

<Callout type="warning">
The cash flow statement also leaves room for management. Delaying payments to suppliers past the reporting date or factoring receivables temporarily improves operating cash flow. Disclosures on supplier finance arrangements have been strengthened from 2024. The indirect method, moreover, shows only how profit is adjusted, not how much was actually received from customers. Look at trends over several years rather than one year's figure, and at whether the gap between profit and operating cash flow keeps widening.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

Company G's profit is ₩8 million. There were depreciation of ₩1.5 million, a ₩1.2 million increase in inventory, a ₩600,000 decrease in receivables, a ₩400,000 decrease in payables, a ₩100,000 increase in prepaid expenses and a ₩250,000 loss on disposal of PP&E. What is operating cash flow under the indirect method?

$800 + 150 + 25 - 120 + 60 - 40 - 10 = 865$ (₩10,000), i.e. ₩8.65 million. The loss on disposal relates to investing and is added back; increases in inventory and prepayments are deducted; the decrease in receivables is added; and the decrease in payables is deducted.
