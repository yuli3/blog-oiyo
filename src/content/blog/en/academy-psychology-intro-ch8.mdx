## We Don't Judge Things Alone

Social psychology studies how other people, groups, and social situations shape an individual's thoughts, feelings, and behavior. People can act differently when they're alone versus when they're around others — not because their willpower is weaker, but because social context shapes judgment.

Social psychology covers topics like prejudice, conformity, persuasion, helping behavior, group decision-making, and interpersonal attraction.

## Core Concepts in Social Psychology

| Concept | Description | Example |
|------|------|------|
| Conformity | The tendency to align with a group's standard | Staying quiet because everyone else is silent |
| Attribution | The process of interpreting the cause of a behavior | Blaming a person's personality for them being late |
| Prejudice | Evaluating a group without sufficient evidence | Making assumptions based on job or age |
| Persuasion | The process of influencing attitudes or behavior | Advertising, presentations, recommendations |

These concepts show up constantly in everyday life. Learning social psychology gives you a more three-dimensional view of relationships.

## Principles for Everyday Application

Applying psychology to daily life calls for some caution. Don't use a single concept to sum up a person — consider the situation and individual differences together. "That's just how they are" is less psychologically sound than asking, "what situation produced that behavior?"

Psychology should also function as a tool for understanding yourself and others more accurately and with less bias — not a technique for manipulating people.

## Example: Silence in a Meeting

If no one speaks up in a meeting, that doesn't necessarily mean everyone agrees. A manager's reaction, pressure from the majority opinion, or the expectation that speaking up won't change anything can all produce silence. Anonymous input or speaking in a fixed order can reduce that pressure.

## Key Takeaways

- Social psychology studies how social situations shape an individual's thoughts and behavior.
- Conformity, attribution, prejudice, and persuasion are social psychology concepts that show up constantly in daily life.
- Psychological concepts should be used to consider situation and individual differences, not to label people.
- The goal of everyday application is more accurate, fairer understanding — not manipulation.

To sharpen your psychological thinking, try connecting the brain, learning, memory, motivation, development, personality, and social psychology concepts from this series to real situations you observe.
