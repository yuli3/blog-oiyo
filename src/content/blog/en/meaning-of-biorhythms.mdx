## 1. Genesis: The Berlin Doctors

In the late 19th century, Wilhelm Fliess (a friend of Freud) and Alfred Teltscher observed cyclical patterns in their patients that didn't align with the 7-day week. They identified strict mathematical periodicities in human vitality.

Though often dismissed by strict materialist science, the concept gained massive popularity in the 1970s and 80s as a tool for industrial safety (predicting accident-prone days for workers) and sports performance optimization.

## 2. Core Logic: The Three Waves

Biorhythms are based on the interference patterns of three distinct cycles.

### ① Physical Cycle (23 Days)

- **Focus:** Coordination, strength, immunity, endurance.
- **High Phase:** You feel energetic, robust, and ready for gym PRs.
- **Low Phase:** You need rest; prone to fatigue and minor illness.

### ② Emotional Cycle (28 Days)

- **Focus:** Mood, creativity, empathy, nervousness.
- **High Phase:** Optimistic, cooperative, resilient to stress.
- **Low Phase:** Irritable, withdrawn, prone to "blues."

### ③ Intellectual Cycle (33 Days)

- **Focus:** Logic, memory, focus, analytical ability.
- **High Phase:** Sharp mental acuity, easy learning.
- **Low Phase:** Mental fog, difficulty concentrating.

## 3. The Critical Day (Zero Point)

The most dangerous days are not the "Low" days, but the **Critical Days**—the moments when the wave crosses the zero line, switching from High to Low (or vice versa).

Imagine a pendulum swinging. It is fastest and most unstable at the center point. On a Critical Day, your system is in flux. Accident statistics often show a spike on these transition days.

## 4. Modern Relevance: Energy Management

In the age of burnout, Biorhythms offer a framework for **Energy Management**. Instead of demanding 100% output every day (which is impossible), we can plan our schedule around our waves.

- **Double Critical Day:** When two curves cross zero simultaneously. Take extreme care.
- **High-High-High:** A "Super Day." Schedule your most important presentation or competition.

## 5. Conclusion: Riding the Wave

We are not machines; we are waves. By acknowledging that energy is not constant, we grant ourselves permission to have "off" days without guilt. Biorhythms teach us that "down" is not a failure—it is simply the gathering of momentum for the next "up."

### FAQ

**Q: Is Biorhythm the same as the Horoscope?**
A: No. Horoscopes are based on planetary positions. Biorhythms are based purely on the number of days you have been alive. It is a biological calculation, not an astronomical one.

**Q: Does it work for everyone?**
A: Anecdotally, many find strong correlations. Scientifically, it is controversial. However, as a tool for tracking personal energy patterns, many users find it invaluable for planning.
