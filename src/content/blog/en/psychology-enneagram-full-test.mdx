## What Is the Enneagram?

The **Enneagram** is a personality theory that classifies human character into nine fundamental types. Rooted in ancient Sufi traditions, it was introduced to the West in the early 20th century and systematized by psychologists after the 1970s.

Unlike MBTI, which describes behavioral patterns, the Enneagram digs beneath the surface to reveal the **core motivations and fears** driving those behaviors. It's a deep psychological system that answers the question: *"Why do I act the way I do?"*

Each type is defined by a **core desire** (what it most deeply wants) and a **core fear** (what it most wants to avoid). These two forces shape our automatic patterns — the way we react under stress, how we build relationships, and where we grow.

<Callout type="note">
**Enneagram vs. MBTI**: MBTI maps how you process information and make decisions. The Enneagram maps *why* — the hidden motivations, defenses, and fears that drive your behavior. Used together, they give a far more complete picture of who you are.
</Callout>

## The 9 Enneagram Types at a Glance

| Type | Name | Core Desire | Core Fear |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** ⚖️ | The Reformer | To be good, ethical, and right | Being corrupt or defective |
| **2** 🤗 | The Helper | To be loved and appreciated | Being unloved or unwanted |
| **3** 🏆 | The Achiever | To be valuable and successful | Being worthless or a failure |
| **4** 🎨 | The Individualist | To find a unique identity | Being ordinary, without identity |
| **5** 🔬 | The Investigator | To be capable and knowledgeable | Being incompetent or helpless |
| **6** 🛡️ | The Loyalist | Safety and a supportive environment | Being without support, alone |
| **7** ✨ | The Enthusiast | To be happy and satisfied | Being trapped in pain or deprivation |
| **8** 🦁 | The Challenger | To control one's own life | Being controlled or violated by others |
| **9** ☮️ | The Peacemaker | Inner and outer peace and harmony | Losing peace through conflict |

## Wings and Integration / Disintegration Directions

### Wings

Every type is influenced by one of its two neighboring types more strongly. This "wing" adds nuance and texture to your core type.

**Example — Type 4:**
- **4w3** (Four with a Three wing): achievement-oriented artist, more image-conscious
- **4w5** (Four with a Five wing): introspective, intellectual, more withdrawn

People of the same type can look quite different depending on their wing.

### Stress and Growth Directions

One of the Enneagram's most powerful insights is that under **stress** (disintegration) and during **growth** (integration), we take on characteristics of a different type:

| Type | Disintegration (stress) | Integration (growth) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | → 4 (self-critical, moody) | → 7 (spontaneous, joyful) |
| 2 | → 8 (domineering, explosive) | → 4 (self-aware, creative) |
| 3 | → 9 (disengaged, numb) | → 6 (loyal, committed) |
| 4 | → 2 (clingy, needy) | → 1 (principled, purposeful) |
| 5 | → 7 (scattered, impulsive) | → 8 (confident, decisive) |
| 6 | → 3 (competitive, vain) | → 9 (peaceful, receptive) |
| 7 | → 1 (critical, perfectionistic) | → 5 (focused, deep) |
| 8 | → 5 (withdrawn, isolated) | → 2 (caring, generous) |
| 9 | → 6 (anxious, suspicious) | → 3 (goal-oriented, energized) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: I see myself in several types. How do I find my real type?**  
A: Everyone has qualities of all nine types. The key is which core fear and desire most consistently *drives* you — not just occasionally, but as a recurring pattern. Look at your conflict patterns and defensive reactions.

**Q: Can my Enneagram type change over time?**  
A: Your core type stays the same, but your **health level** within that type can shift significantly. Growth through self-awareness, therapy, and practice moves you to healthier expressions of your type.

**Q: Can I use the Enneagram with MBTI?**  
A: Absolutely. The two systems address different dimensions. MBTI explains cognitive style; the Enneagram explains core motivation. Together they create a far richer map of personality.
