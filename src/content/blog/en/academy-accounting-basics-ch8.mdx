An audit's conclusion is summed up in one line: the opinion in the auditor's report. To reach it, the auditor accumulates and evaluates the misstatements found and judges whether sufficient evidence has been obtained. This chapter covers the types of opinion, how to choose among them and how the auditor's report is structured. Materiality was set in [chapter 2](/en/academy-auditing-ch2/).

## 1. Evaluating uncorrected misstatements

All misstatements found during the audit above the clearly trivial threshold are accumulated (KSA 450). Management is asked to correct them, and the total of uncorrected misstatements is compared with materiality.

<FormulaBox
  label="Judging uncorrected misstatements"
  formula="\sum \text{Uncorrected misstatements (factual + judgemental + projected)} \;\text{vs}\; \text{Overall materiality}"
  description="Factual misstatements are those where the facts are clear, judgemental misstatements are differences between management's and the auditor's estimates, and projected misstatements are those extrapolated from a sample to the population."
/>

AM's overall materiality is ₩100 million. At the end of the audit, a cut-off error of ₩40 million (factual), a difference in the estimate of the loss allowance of ₩35 million (judgemental) and a misstatement of ₩30 million projected from an inventory sample (projected) remain. The total of ₩105 million exceeds materiality. If management corrects only the cut-off error, the remaining ₩65 million is below materiality and an unmodified opinion can be issued. Projected misstatements carry sampling risk, however, so the judgement should leave a margin.

## 2. Types of audit opinion

  title="Choosing a modified opinion"
  headers={["Cause", "Material but not pervasive", "Material and pervasive"]}
  rows={[

    ["Inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence (scope limitation)", "Qualified opinion", "Disclaimer of opinion"],
  ]}
/>

- **Unmodified opinion:** the financial statements are presented fairly, in all material respects.
- **Qualified opinion:** fair except for a specific matter; the effect is material but does not pervade the financial statements.
- **Adverse opinion:** misstatements are so pervasive that they affect the financial statements as a whole.
- **Disclaimer of opinion:** the auditor cannot express an opinion for lack of evidence, and the possible effects could be pervasive.

**Pervasive** effects are those not confined to specific items, or, if confined, representing a substantial part of the financial statements, or, for disclosures, fundamental to users' understanding. For example, being unable to attend the inventory count at one subsidiary whose inventory is 3% of assets leads to a qualified opinion; if most accounting records were destroyed by fire and no alternative procedures are possible, the result is a disclaimer.

A listed company receiving a non-clean opinion (qualified, adverse or disclaimer) may be designated as an administrative issue or face delisting, so the consequences are serious.

## 3. Structure of the auditor's report

An auditor's report under KSA 700 puts the **Opinion** section first, followed by the **Basis for Opinion**, (where applicable) a material uncertainty related to going concern, **Key Audit Matters**, other matter, the responsibilities of management and those charged with governance for the financial statements, and the auditor's responsibilities.

### A. Key audit matters (KAM)

These describe, in the auditor's professional judgement, **the matters of most significance** in the audit of the current period (KSA 701): why each is a key audit matter and how it was addressed in the audit. Korea phased them in for listed companies, and they now appear in the reports of all listed companies. Revenue recognition, goodwill impairment, capitalization of development costs and inventory valuation are common topics. Key audit matters do not modify the opinion.

### B. Emphasis of matter and other matter

- **Emphasis of matter:** draws attention to a matter already appropriately disclosed in the financial statements that is fundamental to users' understanding (uncertainty over a major lawsuit, a major catastrophe and so on).
- **Other matter:** refers to a matter not disclosed in the financial statements that is relevant to understanding the audit or the auditor's responsibilities (such as the prior-period statements having been audited by another auditor).

An emphasis of matter does not modify the opinion either. A going-concern uncertainty is reported in a separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section ([chapter 8](/en/academy-auditing-ch8/)).

<Callout type="warning">
An unmodified opinion and key audit matters do not mean "the company is safe". Management sometimes bargains with the auditor over correction amounts to secure a clean opinion. If uncorrected misstatements pile up close to materiality and the auditor moves on without further procedures, audit failure follows. Users should read the key audit matters and emphasis of matter paragraphs to see where the auditor saw risk.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

AN's overall materiality is ₩500 million and its total assets ₩100 billion. The auditor (1) could not obtain sufficient evidence on a ₩3 billion investment in an overseas subsidiary because the local auditor's information was not provided, and (2) found that management had not recognized lease liabilities of ₩8 billion and right-of-use assets of ₩8 billion. Management refused to correct (2). What opinion should be issued?

(1) is a scope limitation. ₩3 billion exceeds materiality, but at 3% of assets it can hardly be said to pervade the financial statements, so on its own it would warrant a qualified opinion. (2) is a misstatement. ₩8 billion is 16 times materiality and affects the debt ratio and several notes, so the extent of its effect on users' judgements must be weighed. If the effect is judged confined to specific items in the statement of financial position, the opinion is qualified; if it is judged pervasive, distorting the whole debt structure, the opinion is adverse. When both causes are present, the opinion is based on the more serious one and both are described in the basis section.
