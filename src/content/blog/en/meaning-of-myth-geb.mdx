Geb is the male god who personifies the earth in Egyptian mythology. He is paired with the sky goddess Nut, and when the two are separated, the space of the world opens. Geb is not merely soil, but the living earth that holds grain, minerals, tombs, and the foundation of kingship. In Egypt, the earth was a place that contained both life and death.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Geb's representative myth is his separation from Nut. When sky and earth are pressed tightly together, the world has not yet gained the space where humans and living beings can exist. As Shu lifts Nut upward and Geb remains below, an ordered world unfolds between heaven and earth.

Geb is also connected with kingship. The Egyptian king was understood as one who inherited "the throne of Geb," and the earth was both the physical basis of rule and the ground of sacred right. At the same time, the dead were buried in the earth and returned to Geb's embrace. For this reason, Geb contains both fertile fields and the silence of the tomb.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Geb is the archetype of the **earth father** and the **foundation**. He does not appear as visibly as the dazzling sky, but he provides the reality on which every movement stands. In modern terms, he resembles the conditions that support life even when they are not glamorous: a family's means of living, an organization's infrastructure, finances and space, and the health of the body.

The key to reading Geb is that a foundation becomes more important precisely when it is less visible. A vision of the sky cannot last without the support of the earth. For this reason, the Geb archetype can be read as a symbol that asks us to check the actual ground, resources, body, and place on which we can stand before speaking of great ideals.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Geb a male god?**

A. Yes. In Egyptian mythology, the sky is expressed as the goddess Nut and the earth as the male god Geb, which differs from Greek mythology.

**Q. Why is Geb related to kingship?**

A. Because the king is the one who governs order upon the earth. Geb symbolized the sacred foundation of the throne and the territory.
