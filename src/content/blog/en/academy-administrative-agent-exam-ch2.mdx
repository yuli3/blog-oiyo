## Structure and Nature of the Written Exam

The advanced written portion of the public administration exam differs fundamentally from multiple-choice testing. Rather than recalling rules from a list, you must apply legal doctrine to factual scenarios in a structured, persuasive essay. The length and logical organization of your answer directly affect your score.

**Subject Structure for the Written Exam**

| Subject | Time | Points | Question Format |
|---------|------|--------|-----------------|
| Civil and Contract Law | 90 min | 100 | 3–4 scenario-based questions |
| Administrative Procedure | 90 min | 100 | Procedural description + applied scenarios |
| Document Drafting and Administrative Management | 90 min | 100 | Official document drafting + organizational theory |

<Callout>
The most common mistake on this exam is stating the legal rule without applying it to the facts. The grader's core question is: "How does this doctrine apply to the specific facts presented?" High scores come from clearly connecting the concrete facts of the scenario to the legal rule — not from a recitation of doctrine in the abstract.
</Callout>

## Contract Law Scenarios — Common Dispute Patterns

### Sale of Goods and Product Defects

**Warranty and defect scenarios**: "A buyer discovers a defect in goods after purchase. Describe the legal remedies available to the buyer" is a classic question format.

Scenario answer structure:
1. Applicable warranty — was an express warranty made? Does an implied warranty of merchantability apply (UCC § 2-314)?
2. Buyer's remedies — rejection of non-conforming goods (UCC § 2-601), revocation of acceptance (§ 2-608), damages for breach of warranty (§ 2-714), or cancellation (§ 2-711)
3. Statute of limitations — four years from the date the cause of action accrues (UCC § 2-725)

**Title and ownership transfer**: Questions on when title passes (UCC § 2-401), the rights of a bona fide purchaser for value, and risk of loss allocation.

### Lease Agreements

**Tenant protection disputes**: The key elements — whether the tenant has a valid lease, the landlord's duty to maintain habitable conditions (implied warranty of habitability), remedies for breach (rent withholding, repair-and-deduct, lease termination), and retaliatory eviction protections.

**Lease renewal and rent increases**: Notice requirements for non-renewal, lease holdover rules, and applicable rent-stabilization or rent-control provisions in the jurisdiction.

### Construction and Service Contracts

**Contractor's warranty of workmanship**: When a completed project has defects, the client's rights include demand for repair, reduction in contract price, and damages. Note: courts generally will not order rescission of a construction contract for a completed building — the remedy is damages.

**Negligence and tort liability**: The four elements — duty, breach, causation, and damages. Categories of damages (economic and non-economic), comparative negligence, and contributory negligence.

## Administrative Procedure — Complete Understanding of the Process

### Procedural Requirements for a Lawful Agency Action

An agency action must satisfy not only substantive requirements (valid legal basis) but also procedural requirements. Procedural violations can render an agency action unlawful and subject to reversal.

**Notice Stage**
Before imposing a burden on an individual, the agency must provide adequate notice of:
- The proposed action and its legal basis
- The facts and circumstances on which the proposed action is based
- The deadline and method for submitting a response or objection

Exceptions to notice: Actions necessary to prevent imminent harm to public health or safety; actions where the opportunity to comment would be "impracticable, unnecessary, or contrary to the public interest" (APA § 553(b)(B)).

**Opportunity to Be Heard**
- **Formal Hearing (Adjudication)**: Required when a statute calls for a decision "on the record after opportunity for an agency hearing." Parties may present evidence, call witnesses, and receive a written decision with reasons.
- **Informal Comment**: For most rulemaking, written comments are accepted and must be considered. The agency must respond meaningfully to significant comments in the final rule's preamble.
- **Public Hearing**: Used for major policy, planning, or regulatory decisions with broad public impact.

**Reasoned Explanation (Statement of Basis and Purpose)**
When issuing a rule or decision, the agency must provide its reasoning. An inadequate explanation can make the action "arbitrary and capricious" under the APA (§ 706(2)(A)). Whether an agency can supplement its reasoning after the fact (post-hoc rationalization) is also a tested issue.

### Advance Notice of Rulemaking and Negotiated Rulemaking

**Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM)**: Used when an agency is exploring a new policy area and wants early public input before drafting a proposed rule. No fixed minimum notice period, but it is published in the Federal Register.

**Final Rule Publication**: The final rule is published in the Federal Register with at least a 30-day period before the effective date (unless the agency finds good cause for a shorter period).

### Guidance Documents and Informal Agency Action

Guidance documents (interpretive rules, policy statements, staff manuals) are agency communications that explain existing law or policy but do not themselves have the force of law. They do not require notice-and-comment and bind neither the agency nor the public — though in practice agencies often follow them closely. Distinguishing guidance from binding rules is a tested distinction.

## Document Drafting and Administrative Management

### Standards for Government Document Drafting

Effective and compliant document drafting is the administrative agent's core service. Federal and state standards govern official correspondence to government agencies.

**Categories of Official Documents**: Regulatory documents (statutes, rules, regulations); directive documents (agency guidance, policy memos); public notice documents (public notices, announcements); internal management documents (forms, logs, registers); public petition documents (applications, complaints, requests filed by individuals); and general correspondence.

**Core Components of an Official Document**
- Header: Submitting party, recipient agency and office, document title and subject
- Body: Statement of purpose, factual background, legal basis, requested action
- Attachments: List of supporting documents
- Signature block: Name, title, contact information, date

**Numbering conventions**: Use the standard hierarchical structure: 1, 2, 3 → a, b, c → (1), (2), (3) → (a), (b), (c)

**Numbers in official documents**: Spell out numbers in words for legal documents (e.g., "one thousand dollars ($1,000)") except where pure numerals are conventional.

### Approval and Delegation Systems

**Types of Approval Authority**: Standard approval by the designated authority; delegated approval (decision-maker formally delegates authority to a subordinate for defined categories); acting approval (a designated official acts in the place of the authority holder who is unavailable). The scope and limits of delegated authority are tested.

**Document transmittal and receipt**: Documents should be transmitted on the date they are finalized. Incoming documents are date-stamped and routed to the responsible unit.

### Records Management

**Federal records retention schedules**: The National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) sets General Records Schedules (GRS) that govern retention for most federal records. Permanent, 75-year, 25-year, 10-year, 7-year, 3-year, and 1-year schedules exist for different categories.

**Electronic records**: The Federal Records Act requires that electronic records, including email, be managed under the same retention requirements as paper records. Know when electronic records are considered official records.

## Authorized Scope of Practice

The authorized scope of an administrative agent's practice is a core tested subject — both for professional licensing exams and for ethical compliance in practice.

**What a licensed administrative agent may do**:
1. Prepare documents to be submitted to administrative agencies on behalf of clients
2. File and track submitted documents on behalf of clients
3. Provide consultation and advice on administrative law and agency procedures
4. Represent clients at administrative hearings (informal agency proceedings)

**What is reserved for licensed attorneys**: Filing documents in courts, representing clients in formal court proceedings, providing legal advice on criminal matters, and work that constitutes the practice of law as defined by state bar rules.

## Written Exam Answer Structure and Partial Credit Strategy

### Standard Answer Framework

The IRAC method is the universal structure for scenario-based legal analysis:

**Issue** → **Rule** → **Application** → **Conclusion**

### Maximizing Partial Credit

Partial credit scoring is explicitly used on most professional exam written sections. Even if your final conclusion is wrong, a well-reasoned analysis of the issue will earn substantial credit.

- **Issue identification**: Accurately identify the legal question the facts present
- **Rule statement**: State the applicable statute, regulation, or case doctrine precisely
- **Application**: Connect the specific facts of the scenario to each element of the rule
- **Conclusion**: State a clear, justified conclusion
