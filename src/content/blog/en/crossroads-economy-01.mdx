## Wars Don't End. The World Gets Rebuilt.

When geopolitical conflict drags on, the question investors ask most often is simple: "When will it end?"

But that's the wrong question. The better one is: **"What will be left standing once this conflict is over?"**

### Conflict Reshapes Structure

The 2022 Russia-Ukraine war rewired global energy supply chains. Europe scrambled to cut its reliance on Russian gas, and investment in LNG infrastructure surged. The semiconductor supply chain shifted toward "de-risking from China" and reshoring amid the U.S.-China rivalry. The Israel-Hamas conflict shook the security architecture of the Middle East.

You can't predict when a conflict will end. But the **structural change** it produces tends to move in a consistent direction.

### Three Shifts Investors Should Watch

**1. Supply chain realignment:** As geopolitical risk rises, companies move away from the lowest-cost supply chain toward the "safest" one. That's why India, Southeast Asia, and Mexico stand to benefit.

**2. The rise of the defense industry:** NATO members' pledges to raise defense spending create long-term demand for the defense sector — demand driven by policy, not the business cycle.

**3. Energy independence:** Conflict strengthens the political will toward energy self-sufficiency. The acceleration of renewable energy investment isn't just about economics; there's a security logic behind it too.

### Ask the Right Question

Don't ask "when will the war end?" Ask "what new industries and regions benefit from this conflict?" Don't react to short-term noise — position for structural change.

> Geopolitical conflict always redraws the map of the global economy. Whoever reads the new map first captures the opportunity.
