In the Dangun myth, Hwanung is the son of Hwanin, a being who descends from heaven with the wish to govern and benefit the human world. He is not simply the son of a heavenly deity, but a mediator who connects the principles of heaven with human life. In the record of the *Samguk Yusa*, Hwanung descends beneath the sacred tree on Taebaeksan, opens Sinsi, and commands the powers that govern wind, rain, and clouds. For that reason, Hwanung is read as a figure who prepares the order before the founding of a state: the environment and norms by which a community can live.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Hwanung's central scene is the passage in which he asks to descend because he desires the human world. Here, the word "desires" is better read not as simple greed, but as an expression of interest in and responsibility toward the human world. Hwanin understands his son's intention, gives him three heavenly seals, and sends him down. Hwanung then settles beneath the sacred tree on Taebaeksan with a retinue of three thousand.

Hwanung commands Pungbaek, Usa, and Unsa, and governs many affairs of human life, including grain, lifespan, illness, punishment, good, and evil. This is the myth's way of explaining political institutions, agricultural conditions, and moral order all at once. The name Sinsi is also less a city than the first scene in which the human world receives heavenly standards and becomes ordered.

When the bear and the tiger ask to become human, Hwanung gives them conditions: mugwort and garlic, and a period without seeing sunlight. Details vary by tradition and interpretation, but Hwanung is not a magician who grants transformation immediately. He is a being who presents the rules of a rite of passage. Through his union with Ungnyeo, Dangun is born, and Hwanung's role continues as the heavenly lineage that gives birth to the founding ancestor.

## Reading as an Archetype — From a Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Hwanung is an archetype of the **descending father** and the **giver of order**. He brings the possibilities of a higher place down into the reality of a lower place, giving names and rules to chaos. In modern terms, he resembles a leader, educator, or designer who turns a vague vision into institutions and everyday rules.

At the same time, the Hwanung archetype also contains the danger that arises when authority descends only from above. The will of heaven must meet the patience of the earth before it can be completed as the new order called Dangun. Hwanung is therefore a symbol of the power that tries to apply ideals to reality, while also revealing the condition that this power must respect human time and the human body.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Hwanung the same being as Hwanin?**

A. No. In the tradition, Hwanin is a high divine being of heaven, while Hwanung is his son, who descends to the human world and opens Sinsi.

**Q. What are Hwanung's heavenly seals?**

A. The *Samguk Yusa* says that he received three heavenly seals, but it does not clearly explain what specific objects they were. They are often interpreted as symbols of heavenly authority.
