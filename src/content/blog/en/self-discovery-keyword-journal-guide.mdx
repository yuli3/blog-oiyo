# Your Favorite Keywords Are a Practical Self-Portrait

Personality tests are helpful, but ordinary favorites often describe you more clearly. Speaking, persuading, listening, assembling, observing. A sweet drink, fresh air after a shower, the smell of warm laundry, a small sensory comfort at home. These details are not trivial. They show what kind of attention makes you feel alive.

## 1. Start With Verbs

Nouns say what you like. Verbs reveal how you move.

| Keyword | Possible meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Speaking | You organize thoughts by bringing them outside |
| Persuading | You enjoy reading another person's logic and needs |
| Listening | You value context, warmth, and relational timing |
| Assembling | You feel satisfaction when abstract ideas become touchable |
| Observing | You prefer noticing patterns before judging |

Write three moments when each verb felt natural. Do not force a career answer. Begin with real scenes.

## 2. Collect Small Joys

Happiness becomes easier to repeat when it is small enough to schedule.

- A drink you like
- Wind on your skin after a shower
- Warm laundry
- A familiar home scent
- A quiet corner that feels arranged for you

Ask: what condition made this pleasant? Temperature, texture, smell, privacy, memory, rhythm, or someone nearby?

## 3. People You Want to Meet Again

The people you miss often point to values: safety, learning, gratitude, repair, or unfinished conversation. Write the person's name, what they gave you, and one sentence you would want to say now.

## 4. Fill One Part of the Day With Favorites

Do not redesign your whole life at once. Choose one favorite keyword and give it a small place today.

Example:

- Verb: observing
- Action: walk for ten minutes and notice colors
- Space: clean one corner of the desk
- Reward: drink something you like

This becomes a living profile. Tendencies change over time, so write "lately I am drawn to..." instead of "I am always..."
