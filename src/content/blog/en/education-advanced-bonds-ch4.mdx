# Credit Analysis and Spreads: The Price of Risk

Credit risk is the risk that contractual interest or principal is not paid. Government debt also differs by sovereign, currency and contract; it is not universally free of default, currency or rate risk. A spread is a difference from a stated benchmark. Align currency, maturity, options and liquidity before comparing.

## 1. The Language of Credit: Ratings

A rating is an agency’s opinion about relative creditworthiness, not a fixed numerical default probability or a principal guarantee. Interpretation depends on agency, sector and assessment date.

## 3. Mastering the Spreads

Wider spreads can reflect expected losses, risk compensation, liquidity, options and market supply or demand. An observed yield gap is neither a default probability nor an expected excess return by itself.

- **OAS**: A model-based spread adjusted for embedded options. Non-credit factors remain, and volatility and exercise assumptions matter. It is not pure default risk.
- **TED spread**: A historical gap between three-month USD LIBOR and Treasury rates. FRED discontinued TEDRATE after removing its LIBOR data on January 31, 2022. Do not present it as a current real-time risk series.

### A. Separate Expected Loss from the Market Spread

Take a hypothetical one-year default probability PD=2%, recovery R=40% and fixed exposure at default EAD=100,000,000 currency units:

<FormulaBox formula="EL = EAD \times PD \times (1-R)" />

EL=100,000,000×0.02×0.60=1,200,000, or 1.2% of exposure. This simplified expected loss omits discounting, dependence, recovery timing and changing exposure. It is not a certain annual loss of 1.2%. The 120bp is not the entire bond spread, and a spread alone cannot identify PD.

<Callout type="warning">
A high quoted yield does not guarantee a high realized return. Diversification leaves common shocks and correlated defaults. Recovery depends on collateral, seniority and legal proceedings.
</Callout>

## 4. Wrapping Up: Pricing the Uncertainty

Review cash flows and maturity payments, refinancing, collateral and guarantees, creditor priority, stress scenarios and pricing assumptions. The 5 Cs are a checklist, not a valuation model producing a fair price from a score. Operating cash flow is not EBITDA; ratings and spreads alone do not prove undervaluation.
