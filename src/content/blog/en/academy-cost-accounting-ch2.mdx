A merchandiser has one kind of inventory: merchandise. A manufacturer has inventory at three stages: **raw materials** not yet put into production, **work in process** being produced, and **finished goods**. Costs pass through these three inventories in turn before becoming cost of sales. This chapter uses accounts and schedules to show where the cost classifications of [chapter 1](/en/academy-cost-accounting-ch1/) fit in that flow.

## 2. The schedule of cost of goods manufactured

The schedule of cost of goods manufactured sets out the work in process account as a table and shows **cost of goods manufactured**. The income statement takes this amount to calculate cost of sales.

Company MM's figures for the period (unit: ₩10,000): opening raw materials 50, purchases 300, closing raw materials 40, with all raw materials used as direct materials. Direct labor is 200 and manufacturing overhead 150. Opening WIP is 80, closing WIP 90, opening finished goods 120 and closing finished goods 100.

**Total manufacturing cost** (660) is the cost newly put into production this period; **cost of goods manufactured** (650) is the cost of goods completed this period. They differ by the 10 increase in WIP over the period. The company's prime cost is $310 + 200 = 510$ and its conversion cost $200 + 150 = 350$ (₩10,000).

## 3. Indirect materials and indirect labor

In practice, some raw materials, such as screws and lubricants, are used as **indirect materials** that are hard to trace to products. The wages of factory cleaners and maintenance staff are **indirect labor**. Both go into **manufacturing overhead**, not direct materials or direct labor. If ₩300,000 of the ₩3.1 million of raw materials issued were indirect materials, direct materials would be ₩2.8 million and manufacturing overhead ₩1.8 million, while total manufacturing cost stays at ₩6.6 million.

## 4. The flow in the accounts and journal entries

When raw materials are issued to production: Dr Work in process, Cr Raw materials. When products are completed: Dr Finished goods 650, Cr Work in process 650. On sale: Dr Cost of sales 670, Cr Finished goods 670. Manufacturing overhead is first collected in a manufacturing overhead account and then applied to work in process. Applying it with a predetermined rate before actual costs are known is the job costing of [chapter 3](/en/academy-cost-accounting-ch3/).

<Callout type="warning">
A blurred line between manufacturing costs and selling and administrative expenses changes profit. Shifting head office costs into manufacturing overhead leaves part of them in closing inventory, reducing this period's expense and raising profit. Conversely, treating factory costs as selling and administrative expenses understates inventory. For a company whose inventory grows faster than sales, look at the composition and trend of indirect costs in the schedule of cost of goods manufactured.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

Company NN's cost of sales for the period is ₩12 million, opening finished goods ₩1.8 million and closing finished goods ₩2.3 million. Opening WIP is ₩1 million and closing WIP ₩1.4 million; direct labor is ₩3.5 million and manufacturing overhead is 80% of direct labor. What are direct materials for the period?

From the finished goods account, cost of goods manufactured is $1{,}200 + 230 - 180 = 1{,}250$ (₩10,000). From the WIP account, total manufacturing cost is $1{,}250 + 140 - 100 = 1{,}290$. Manufacturing overhead is $350 \times 80\% = 280$, so direct materials are $1{,}290 - 350 - 280 = 660$, i.e. ₩6.6 million.
