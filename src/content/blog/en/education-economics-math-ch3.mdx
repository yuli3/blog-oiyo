# Mathematics for Economics — With Constraints, Derivatives Alone Are Not Enough

Without constraints, $\nabla f=0$ is the first-order condition. With a budget or resource constraint, one must move along its boundary. The Lagrangian is a technique that treats the constraint like a price. Nash equilibria and mixed strategies, where several people choose at once, belong to [game theory](/en/education-economics-ch41/) in microeconomics. This chapter deals only with the extrema of a single decision-maker.

## 1. The multiplier is the value of one unit of the constraint to the objective

$\max u=xy$ s.t. $2x+y=12$. $L=xy+\lambda(12-2x-y)$.

<FormulaBox
  label="First-order conditions"
  formula="y=2\lambda,\quad x=\lambda,\quad 2x+y=12"
  description="λ=x and y=2x. Substituting into the budget gives 2x+2x=12, so x=3, y=6 and λ=3. If the budget becomes 13 won, utility rises by about 3."
/>

At $x=3, y=6$, $u=18$. With a budget of 13 won, $x=3.25, y=6.5$ and $u=21.125$, an increase of 3.125 that matches the approximation $\lambda=3$. This is $\mathrm{MU}_x/P_x=\lambda$, the marginal utility per won of consumer theory.

## 2. With inequalities and non-negativity, corners can be the solution

KKT adds complementary slackness for non-binding constraints to the equality Lagrangian. If a good's marginal utility per won is lower than another's, consumption of that good stops at zero. "Always an interior tangency" is a special solution that assumes positive consumption.

The second-order condition checks whether the Hessian is negative definite (for a maximum). Matching only the first order and reading a saddle point as maximum profit picks output the wrong way round. It is the same as the distinction between average and margin in [chapter 1](/en/education-economics-math-ch1/).

<Callout type="warning">
Do not read the multiplier λ as the size of happiness. A monotonic transformation of the objective changes λ. With the same preferences written as $\ln u$, the multiplier takes a different value. What can be compared are relative prices within the same model. Mutual optimization by several people is not one optimization but an equilibrium.
</Callout>

Once time is added, the same first-order conditions become differential equations. The stability of their steady state is covered in [chapter 4](/en/education-economics-math-ch4/).

## Check your understanding

If the budget rises 50% from 12 won to 18 won, the linear approximation suggests that utility rises by $\Delta u \approx \lambda \Delta m = 3 × 6 = 18$. In fact $x=4.5, y=9$ and $u=40.5$, an increase of 22.5, and the new multiplier is $\lambda=x=4.5$. With this utility function λ grows with income, so the linear approximation holds only for small changes. 50% is not small.
