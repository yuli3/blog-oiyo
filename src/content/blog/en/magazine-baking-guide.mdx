## Why Baking Is Different from Cooking

Cooking is forgiving. You can improvise, taste as you go, and adjust on the fly.

**Baking is chemistry.**

If the ratios, temperature, or order of operations are off, your result will be completely different from what you intended. That's why precise measurement matters — and why understanding the underlying principles reduces failure dramatically.

---

## The Core Principles of Baking

### Gluten

Flour + water → gluten formation.

- **Bread flour (12–14% protein)**: used for bread — needs strong, elastic structure
- **All-purpose flour (10–12% protein)**: versatile (cookies, cakes)
- **Cake flour (8–10% protein)**: for cakes and delicate cookies — produces a tender crumb

**Over-mixing develops too much gluten**: bread becomes chewy and tough; cake becomes dense and rubbery.

### Leavening Agents

| Agent | How It Works | Common Uses |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Baking powder | CO₂ from heat + liquid | Muffins, cakes |
| Baking soda | Needs an acid (buttermilk, vinegar, cocoa) | Pancakes, chocolate cake |
| Yeast | Fermentation produces CO₂ + alcohol | Bread, doughnuts |

**Baking powder vs baking soda**: baking soda is 3–4× more potent. Confusing the two causes bitterness or over-rising.

### Emulsification

Creaming butter + sugar together traps air, producing a tender, light texture.

Egg yolk lecithin bonds water and fat together, creating a smooth, uniform batter.

## Measurement Principles

**Always weigh flour**: scooping with a cup can vary by 20–30% depending on how packed it is. A digital scale eliminates this entirely.

| Ingredient | How to Measure |
|-----------|---------------|
| Dry goods (flour, sugar, cocoa) | Weigh in grams or ounces |
| Liquids (milk, oil) | Scale or liquid measuring cup |
| Butter | Scale (room-temperature or cold) |
| Salt, leavening | Measuring spoons |
| Vanilla extract | Measuring spoons |

## Basic Recipes

### Butter Cookies (Best First Project)

**Ingredients (about 20 cookies)**:
- 100 g (7 tbsp) unsalted butter, room temperature
- 60 g (½ cup) powdered sugar
- 1 egg
- 200 g (1⅔ cups) all-purpose or cake flour
- 1 g (¼ tsp) salt
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

**Method**:
1. Cream butter + powdered sugar with a hand mixer on medium, about 3 minutes.
2. Add egg and mix until combined.
3. Sift in flour + salt, then fold gently with a spatula (avoid over-mixing).
4. Chill the dough in the refrigerator for 30 minutes.
5. Bake at 340°F (170°C) for 12–15 minutes.

**Common failure**: butter too cold → won't cream properly. Make sure it's soft at room temperature.

### Muffins (One-Bowl Recipe)

**Ingredients (6 muffins)**:
- 200 g (1⅔ cups) all-purpose flour
- 100 g (½ cup) sugar
- 2 tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp salt
- 1 egg
- 120 ml (½ cup) milk
- 60 ml (¼ cup) vegetable oil
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

**Method**:
1. Whisk together all dry ingredients (flour, sugar, baking powder, salt).
2. Whisk together all wet ingredients (egg, milk, oil, vanilla) separately.
3. Pour the wet into the dry and stir only 10–15 times (a few lumps are fine).
4. Fill muffin cups 80% full.
5. Bake at 350°F (180°C) for 20–22 minutes.

**Key rule**: over-mixing makes muffins tough. Follow the "just until combined" principle.

### Sandwich Bread (Introduction to Yeast)

**Ingredients (1 loaf)**:
- 250 g (2 cups) bread flour
- 20 g (1½ tbsp) sugar
- 5 g (1 tsp) salt
- 4 g (1¼ tsp) instant yeast
- 160 ml (⅔ cup) milk, warmed to 95°F (35°C)
- 20 g (1½ tbsp) unsalted butter, room temperature

**Method**:
1. Dissolve yeast in warm milk for 5 minutes.
2. Add to dry ingredients and knead for 10 minutes (gluten development).
3. Add butter and knead 5 more minutes. Windowpane test: stretch a small piece thin — if you can see light through it without tearing, it's ready.
4. First rise: let rest in a warm spot for 60 minutes or until doubled.
5. Punch down, shape, and place in a loaf pan.
6. Second rise: 30–40 minutes.
7. Bake at 350°F (180°C) for 25–30 minutes.

**Ideal fermentation temperature**: 78–86°F (26–30°C). In summer, room temperature works. In winter, use the oven's proof setting or place near a bowl of hot water.

### Basic Sponge Cake

**Ingredients (one 6-inch / 15 cm round)**:
- 3 eggs, room temperature
- 90 g (7 tbsp) sugar
- 90 g (¾ cup) cake flour
- 30 g (2 tbsp) melted butter
- 30 ml (2 tbsp) milk

**Method**:
1. Warm eggs + sugar over a double boiler to 104°F (40°C) while beating with a hand mixer on high until ribbon stage (batter falls in thick ribbons that hold their shape briefly).
2. Sift in cake flour and fold from the bottom up with a spatula (preserve the air bubbles).
3. Mix melted butter + milk with a small amount of batter first, then fold into the main batter.
4. Pour into a greased and floured pan; tap the pan firmly to release large air bubbles.
5. Bake at 340°F (170°C) for 25–30 minutes.

## Understanding Your Oven

### Temperature Guide

| Temperature | Uses |
|------------|------|
| 300–325°F (150–160°C) | Macarons, meringue, delicate cookies |
| 340–350°F (170–180°C) | Cakes, muffins, cookies |
| 375–400°F (190–200°C) | Bread, croissants, pie |
| 425°F+ (220°C+) | Baguettes, pizza |

**Preheat is non-negotiable**: at least 15–20 minutes. Putting food in a cold oven means the temperature rises too slowly, leading to dense, poorly-risen results.

### Rack Position

- **Top third**: browns the surface quickly — useful when you want color fast
- **Middle**: even heat — correct for most baking
- **Bottom third**: crisps the base — good for pies and tarts

## Troubleshooting Common Baking Failures

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Underbaked center | Oven too hot, pan too small | Lower temperature, bake longer |
| Burnt top, raw inside | Temperature too high | Cover with foil, reduce heat |
| Sunken cake | Opened oven too early, too much leavening | Don't open during baking |
| Bread didn't rise | Dead yeast (water too hot) or too cold | Use 95–100°F (35–38°C) water |
| Cookies spread too flat | Butter too soft, not enough chilling | Refrigerate dough before baking |
| Bread is dense and dry | Not enough liquid, over-kneaded, over-baked | Check hydration ratio |

## A Beginner's Roadmap

1. **Step 1**: Butter cookies, muffins — no yeast, maximum confidence
2. **Step 2**: Sponge cake, pound cake
3. **Step 3**: Yeast breads (sandwich loaf, dinner rolls)
4. **Step 4**: Croissants, macarons (advanced technique)

Start with a recipe and follow it precisely in grams. Failures are how you learn the principles. Begin with a batch of butter cookies today.
