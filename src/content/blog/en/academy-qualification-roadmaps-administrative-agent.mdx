## What Is a Paralegal / Legal Administrative Professional?

A **paralegal** (also called a legal assistant) is a trained professional who performs substantive legal work under the supervision of a licensed attorney. Paralegals handle document drafting, legal research, case management, client intake, regulatory filings, and administrative law proceedings.

In the US, paralegal work is **not licensed at the state level** (with very limited exceptions). Instead, professional credentials issued by national organizations serve as the primary quality standard and career differentiator.

**Key national bodies**:
- **NALA** (National Association of Legal Assistants) — issues the Certified Paralegal (CP) credential
- **NFPA** (National Federation of Paralegal Associations) — issues the Registered Paralegal (RP) credential
- **NALS** (formerly National Association of Legal Secretaries) — issues the PP (Professional Paralegal) and ALS designations

**Exclusive practice areas** (tasks paralegals perform that non-credentialed staff typically cannot):
- Drafting pleadings, contracts, and correspondence for attorney review
- Representing clients in certain administrative hearings (Social Security appeals, immigration matters before USCIS, IRS practice under Circular 230 for Enrolled Agents)
- Supervising legal support staff in larger firms

## Types of Credentials

Credentials cluster around two tracks: **legal/paralegal** and **public administration / government service**.

| Credential | Issuing Body | Focus |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| **CP (Certified Paralegal)** | NALA | General paralegal practice |
| **RP (Registered Paralegal)** | NFPA | General paralegal practice |
| **PP (Professional Paralegal)** | NALS | Legal office administration |
| **MPA (Master of Public Administration)** | Universities | Government management and policy |
| **ICMA-CM** | ICMA | Local government management |
| **Enrolled Agent (EA)** | IRS / NAEA | Federal tax representation |

The most widely pursued entry-level credential is the **NALA CP**, which is analogous in function to the Korean Administrative Agent (행정사) — both certify a professional who manages regulatory filings, government-facing documents, and administrative proceedings.

## Eligibility Requirements

### NALA CP

No single fixed pathway — candidates must meet **one of these combinations**:

- Graduation from an ABA-approved paralegal program; **or**
- Associate's degree + one year of paralegal experience; **or**
- Bachelor's degree (any field) + one year of paralegal experience; **or**
- High school diploma + five years of paralegal experience (with annual continuing education)

### NFPA RP

- Six years of paralegal experience **and** 12 hours of CLE credit in the preceding two years; **or**
- Four years of experience + ABA-approved paralegal certificate + 12 CLE hours

### Public Administration Credentials

The **MPA** (Master of Public Administration) is the standard professional degree for mid-career government and nonprofit professionals. Most NASPAA-accredited programs require:
- Bachelor's degree
- Statement of purpose, relevant work/internship experience (typically 1–3 years preferred)
- GRE (varies by program; many are now test-optional)

The **ICMA-CM** (Credentialed Manager) requires active ICMA membership plus documented local government management experience (5 years for non-supervisory; less for assistant/deputy roles).

## Exam Structure

### NALA CP Examination

**Format**: Computer-based, offered at Pearson VUE testing centers

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Part I | Communications (legal writing, research) |
| Part II | Ethics and professional responsibility |
| Part III | Legal research methods |
| Part IV–VII | Four specialty area sections chosen by the candidate (e.g., litigation, corporate, real estate, family law, criminal law, contracts, torts) |

**Pass standard**: Scaled score of 75 or higher on each section
**Exam duration**: Approximately 4 hours total

Candidates may retake individual failed sections without retaking passed sections.

### NFPA PACE (Paralegal Advanced Competency Exam)

**Format**: 200 multiple-choice questions, computer-based

Covers:
- Legal research and writing
- Substantive law (contracts, torts, civil procedure, evidence, ethics)
- Management and supervisory skills (for senior practitioners)

**Pass rate**: Approximately 60–70% (varies by exam cycle)

### Enrolled Agent (EA) — Special Enrollment Examination (SEE)

For those interested in tax representation before the IRS (the closest direct analog to the Korean administrative agent's tax filing and appeal functions):

- **Three-part exam**: Individual taxation; Business taxation; Representation, practices & procedures
- Offered year-round at Prometric centers
- No degree requirement — open to anyone

## Subject-by-Subject Guide and Study Strategy

### Legal Research and Writing

**Scope**: Westlaw/LexisNexis navigation, case briefing, statutory interpretation, legal memoranda, correspondence drafting

Study strategy:
- Use NALA's practice exams and the *Paralegal Today* textbook series
- Practice writing IRAC-format (Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion) analysis
- Written skills assessed in both CP and RP exams

### Substantive Law (Civil Procedure, Contracts, Torts, Evidence)

Core areas tested heavily in both CP and PACE:
- Civil procedure: pleadings, discovery, motions practice, federal vs. state court jurisdiction
- Contracts: formation, defenses, remedies
- Torts: negligence elements, strict liability, intentional torts
- Evidence: Federal Rules of Evidence fundamentals

Strategy: Use commercial bar prep outlines (e.g., Emanuel Law Outlines) as supplemental review — they are denser than paralegal textbooks and sharpen conceptual clarity.

### Ethics and Professional Responsibility

- NALA Code of Ethics and Professional Responsibility
- ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct (unauthorized practice of law, confidentiality, conflicts of interest)
- Supervision rules: paralegals work under attorney oversight; attorney retains ultimate responsibility

### Administrative Procedures (Federal)

- Administrative Procedure Act (APA) fundamentals: notice-and-comment rulemaking, adjudication, judicial review
- Practice before federal agencies: Social Security Administration appeals, USCIS filings, IRS correspondence
- Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests

This section overlaps most directly with the Korean administrative agent curriculum and is often underemphasized in paralegal programs — it is a differentiator for candidates interested in government-facing practice.

### Document Management and Office Administration

- Federal records management (NARA regulations)
- E-discovery and electronic records under FRCP Rule 26
- Docketing, case management software (Clio, MyCase, Filevine)

## Pass Rates and Statistics

| Credential | Pass Rate | Annual New Credentialed |
|-----------|----------|------------------------|
| NALA CP | ~55–65% | ~2,000–3,000 |
| NFPA PACE | ~60–70% | ~500–1,000 |
| Enrolled Agent (EA) | ~60–75% per part | ~4,000–6,000 |

Paralegal credentials are **moderately selective** — comparable in difficulty to a professional certification exam, not a bar exam. Focused preparation is rewarded.

## Study Timeline by Profile

### Intensive (4–8 months)

Best suited for recent paralegal program graduates or those with substantial legal work experience:

- Months 1–2: Legal research, writing, and ethics
- Months 3–4: Substantive law review (contracts, torts, civil procedure)
- Months 5–6: Administrative law and specialty areas
- Month 7–8: Full practice exams under timed conditions

### Working Professional (12–18 months)

3–4 study sessions per week, 2 hours each:
- Year 1: Complete an ABA-approved online paralegal certificate (if not already credentialed)
- Year 1–1.5: CP exam preparation using NALA study guides and practice tests

### Recommended Resources

- **NALA CP Handbook** (free download from nala.org)
- **The Paralegal Professional** (Moran & Landy) — widely used textbook
- **Westlaw Edge** or **Fastcase** student access for legal research practice
- **Emanuel Law Outlines** for substantive law review

## Career Paths After Credentialing

### Law Firm Practice

The most common path. Credentialed paralegals command higher billing rates and salaries.

**Practice areas**:
- **Immigration**: Prepare visa petitions, adjustment-of-status applications, deportation defense file management (USCIS, EOIR)
- **Real estate / transactional**: Title searches, closing document preparation, contract review
- **Corporate**: Entity formation filings, SEC disclosure support, M&A due diligence
- **Litigation support**: E-discovery management, deposition summaries, trial binder preparation
- **Administrative law / regulatory**: Agency filings, rulemaking comment preparation, licensing applications

**Salary range** (US, 2024):
- Entry-level (0–3 years): $45,000–$65,000
- Mid-career with CP (3–8 years): $65,000–$90,000
- Senior / specialist paralegal (8+ years, major markets): $90,000–$130,000+

### Government and Public Sector

Paralegal and public administration credentials open government roles:

- **Federal agency paralegal specialist** (GS-7 to GS-12): Legal departments of the EPA, HHS, DOJ, SEC, FTC
- **Legislative staff**: Congressional offices, state legislature legal counsel offices
- **Regulatory affairs specialist**: FDA, FCC, CPSC — managing compliance filings and licensing matters
- **Contract specialist**: DOD, GSA — federal acquisition positions (FAC-C certification pathway)

### Corporate Legal Departments (In-House)

- Contracts manager, compliance analyst, intellectual property coordinator
- In-house roles increasingly accept credentialed paralegals in lieu of attorneys for routine legal-adjacent work
- Strong growth area, especially in tech and healthcare sectors

## Credential Comparison: Paralegal vs. Related Credentials

| | CP (NALA) | EA (IRS) | MPA | JD (Attorney) |
|--|-----------|----------|-----|---------------|
| Primary function | Legal document work, agency filings | Tax representation before IRS | Government management | Full legal practice |
| Exam difficulty | Moderate | Moderate | Graduate degree | Bar exam (high) |
| Preparation time | 6 months–1.5 years | 6–12 months | 2 years (graduate) | 3 years (law school) + bar prep |
| Exclusive authority | Admin filings under supervision | Tax rep before IRS | Management roles | All legal practice |
| Entry barrier | Low–moderate | Low | Moderate | High |

For professionals who want to work in administrative law, government filings, and regulatory compliance without the time and cost of a law degree, the **CP + EA combination** offers the broadest practical authority at the most accessible entry point.
