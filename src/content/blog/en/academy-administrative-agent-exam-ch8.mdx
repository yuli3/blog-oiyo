## Principles of Official Document Drafting

```
Official Document:
Any document created or received by a government agency
in the course of official duties.

Drafting Principles:
Write concisely and clearly.
Use plain language and standard English.
Use Arabic numerals.
Dates: spell out in full (e.g., May 13, 2026).

Document Structure:
Header: recipient agency, reference number.
Body: subject line and substantive content.
Closing: issuing entity, date, authorized signature.
```

---

## Major Document Types

```
Permit / License Application:
State the purpose and statutory authority.
List attachments and supporting documents.
Signature and certification.

Administrative Appeal Petition:
Petitioner and respondent information.
Description of the agency action challenged.
Grounds for appeal (prayer for relief + legal basis).
Supporting evidence.

Request for Reconsideration / Objection Letter:
Identify the action being challenged.
State grounds for objection.
Specify the relief requested.

General Petition / Inquiry:
Nature of the request.
Applicable legal authority.
Attachments.
```

## Drafting an Administrative Appeal Petition

```
Required Elements:
Petitioner: full name and address.
Respondent: name of the agency.
Subject of appeal: precisely identify the action challenged.
Prayer for relief: what outcome is sought.
Grounds: why the action is unlawful or improper.

Attachments:
Copy of the challenged agency order/notice.
Supporting evidence and exhibits.

Where to File:
With the issuing agency or the appropriate
  administrative appeals tribunal.

Filing Deadline:
Typically 30–60 days from notice of final action
  (check governing statute; compare exam rule:
  within 90 days of notice, 180 days of action).
```

## Common Drafting Errors

```
Frequent Mistakes:
Date format: write "May 13, 2026" — do NOT use
  numeric-only formats without context.
Recipient: use the exact official agency name.
Legal authority: cite the specific statute, section,
  and subsection.
Signature block: distinguish official seal vs. signature.

Document Classification:
General / Confidential classification marking.
Reference and tracking number systems.

Corrections:
Draw a single line through incorrect text and initial.
Do not use correction fluid on official documents.
```

## Key Concept Cards

**Date Format in Official Documents** ★★★★★
: Write out the full date: "May 13, 2026." Never abbreviate in a way that creates ambiguity.
*Memory tip: Spell out the date — no ambiguous short forms.*

**Petition = Prayer for Relief + Legal Grounds** ★★★★★
: Prayer states the specific outcome sought. Grounds explain the legal and factual basis for the challenge.
*Memory tip: Petition = "what I want" + "why I'm entitled."*

**Document Corrections = Strikethrough + Initial** ★★★★☆
: Line through the error, initial (and date) next to it. Correction fluid is prohibited on official forms.
*Memory tip: Cross out + initial — no white-out.*

## Practice Quiz

**Q. How do you distinguish "prayer for relief" from "grounds for appeal" in an administrative appeal petition?**
> The prayer for relief states the specific outcome the petitioner is requesting (e.g., "Vacate and set aside the license revocation issued on [date]"). The grounds explain why the agency action was unlawful or improper — citing specific statutory violations, procedural defects, or factual errors. The prayer should be concise; the grounds should be detailed, supported by legal authority and the factual record. This mirrors the distinction between a "demand" and "claims" in a civil complaint.

**Q. Why must an administrative petition cite the precise statutory authority?**
> Agencies may only act when authorized by law (ultra vires doctrine). If a petition omits the governing legal authority, the agency may decline to process it as incomplete. Citing the exact statute and subsection also demonstrates that the applicant or petitioner understands the legal standard being applied, and allows the reviewing officer to assess the merits efficiently. For the exam, always include the full statutory citation.
