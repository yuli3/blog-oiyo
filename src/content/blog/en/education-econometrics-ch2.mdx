# Econometrics — OLS Is a Calculation That Draws a Line Through Means, Not a Cause

In the simple regression $Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X + U$, OLS chooses the $(\beta_0, \beta_1)$ that minimizes the sum of squared residuals. For that to match the ATE of chapter 1, $X$ and $U$ must be uncorrelated. This chapter pins down the calculation first and leaves what that assumption does to the next chapter.

## 1. The slope comes from the normal equations

For a sample of $n$ observations, fit so that $\sum \hat u_i = 0$ and $\sum X_i \hat u_i = 0$. This means the mean of the residuals is zero and the residuals have zero sample covariance with $X$.

<FormulaBox
  label="OLS slope"
  formula="\hat\beta_1 = \widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(X,Y) / \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(X)"
  description="How much Y deviates from its mean when X does, divided by how spread out X is. Without variation in X, the division is impossible."
/>

<FormulaBox
  label="Intercept"
  formula="\hat\beta_0 = \bar Y - \hat\beta_1 \bar X"
  description="The regression line passes through the sample means."
/>

If years of schooling are 12, 14 and 16 and wages (in units of 10,000 won) are 280, 320 and 390, then $\bar X=14$ and $\bar Y=330$. The sum of cross-deviations is $(-2)(-50)+0+(2)(60)=220$ and the sum of squared deviations of $X$ is 8, so the sample covariance is 110 and the sample variance 4 (both divided by $n-1$). Then $\hat\beta_1=220/8=27.5$ (275,000 won per year) and $\hat\beta_0=330-27.5×14=-55$. Covariance and variance must be divided by the same denominator. The intercept is negative because zero years of schooling is extrapolated outside the sample; it is better not to interpret it.

## 2. R² is a share of fit, not proof that the model is right

<FormulaBox
  label="Coefficient of determination"
  formula="R^2 = 1 - SSR/SST"
  description="SSR is the sum of squared residuals and SST the total sum of squares of Y. Adding variables never lowers R²."
/>

Saying that education explains 40% of the variation in wages means that, in this sample, the education regression line accounts for that share of the total sum of squares. If omitted ability moves wages through education, R² can rise while the causal interpretation gets worse.

## 3. Residuals are not the unobserved error U

The residual $\hat u$ is what is left over from the estimated regression. $U$ is the disturbance when the model is true. If the model is misspecified, $\hat u$ may be small while $U$ has no meaning. If the scatter plot is curved and a straight line is drawn, residuals bunch on one side. What is needed then is not more variables but a new functional form.

<Callout type="warning">
Reading a coefficient of 0.08 from a regression of log wages on schooling as "one year of schooling raises wages 8%" is an approximation. $\exp(0.08)-1≈8.3\%$, and this too is a statement about the conditional mean when $U$ and schooling are uncorrelated. Do not lump together people at an educational ceiling or years mixed up with work experience under the same slope.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

Does standardizing $X$ change $\hat\beta_1$? Yes. Dividing $X$ by its standard deviation $s_X$ turns the slope into $\hat\beta_1 s_X$. In the example of section 1, $s_X=2$, so the standardized slope is 55 (550,000 won), the change in Y per standard deviation of X. It differs from 27.5 in the original units, but it describes the same relationship in different units.
