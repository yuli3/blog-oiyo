## Trademark Law

```
Trademark:
A mark used to distinguish one party's goods/services from another's
Includes words, symbols, logos, colors, sounds, and scents
(15 USC § 1127; Lanham Act)

Registration Requirements:
Must be distinctive
Must not fall under absolute or relative bars to registration

Absolute Bars to Registration (15 USC § 1052):
Merely descriptive, generic, or deceptively misdescriptive marks
Marks comprising the flag or coat of arms of the US or any state/nation

Relative Bars to Registration:
Confusingly similar to a prior registered or used mark
Dilution of a famous mark (TDRA — 15 USC § 1125(c))
```

## Trademark Registration Procedure and Rights

```
Filing Basis (USPTO):
Use-based application (§ 1(a)): mark already in use in commerce
Intent-to-Use application (§ 1(b)): bona fide intent to use
Foreign Priority (§ 44(d)): 6-month Paris Convention window

Examination:
USPTO examines for distinctiveness and conflicting marks
Office Actions issued; applicant has 3 months to respond (extendable)

Registration:
Registration fee paid → rights arise (nationwide constructive use)
Term: 10 years from registration date (renewable indefinitely)

Non-Use Cancellation:
3 consecutive years of non-use → prima facie abandonment
Owner bears burden to rebut (15 USC § 1127)
```

## Trademark Infringement and Remedies

```
Infringement Standard:
Use of a confusingly similar mark for related goods/services
Likelihood of confusion (not actual confusion required)
Multi-factor Polaroid/DuPont test

Remedies (15 USC § 1114, § 1117):
Injunctive relief
Actual damages or disgorgement of defendant's profits
Treble damages for willful infringement
Attorney's fees in exceptional cases

Criminal Penalties (18 USC § 2320):
Trafficking in counterfeit goods: up to 10 years imprisonment / $2M fine
(Equivalent Korean penalty: ≤7 years or ≤₩100M → ~$75K)
```

## Design Patent Law

```
Design Patent (35 USC § 171):
Protects the ornamental appearance of an article of manufacture
Covers shape, configuration, or surface ornamentation

Requirements:
Novel, original, and ornamental
No prior art identical or substantially similar in overall appearance

Secrecy / Deferred Publication:
Design applications are not published before grant
Confidentiality maintained until grant (comparable to Korean secret-design system)

Term:
15 years from grant date (AIA, for applications filed after May 13, 2015)

Related Designs:
Continuation applications cover variations of the same overall design concept
```

: US design patents last 15 years from grant; utility patents last 20 years from filing.
*Memory hook: Design = 15 yrs from grant; utility = 20 yrs from filing*

## Practice Questions

**Q. What is the difference between use-based and registration-based trademark systems?**
> The US operates a use-based system: rights arise from use in commerce, not merely registration (§ 1(a) basis). Registration provides nationwide constructive notice and the right to sue in federal court, but common-law rights exist without registration. By contrast, most countries (including Korea) follow a first-to-file registration system — prior use without registration does not establish rights. Famous marks may receive protection even without registration under dilution doctrine (TDRA). Strategy: file early, use consistently.

**Q. Why is the design patent "secrecy" feature strategically valuable?**
> Design patent applications in the US are not published before grant, providing inherent confidentiality during pendency. This prevents competitors from copying the design before launch — similar to the Korean "secret design" system. Once the patent issues, the patentee gains enforceable rights retroactive to the filing date. This is especially valuable in fast-moving industries like consumer electronics or fashion where product lifecycles are short.
