# Introduction to Economics — Openness and Government Are the Leaks in the Closed Multiplier Formula

The simple Keynesian multiplier is an upper bound resting on fixed prices, fixed interest rates, a closed economy and idle resources. What exam preparation needs is not memorizing the formula but picking out **which assumption is currently broken**. The balance of payments and Mundell–Fleming are covered in full in [International Finance](/en/education-economics-ch36/), and trade models in [International Trade](/en/education-economics-ch34/).

## 1. The same 50 trillion has a different first-round effect as spending and as a tax cut

With MPC = 0.8, MPS = 0.2, the closed-economy government spending multiplier is 5 and the tax-cut multiplier is 4. Spending 50 trillion won is 50 trillion of demand at once; of a 50 trillion tax cut, only 40 trillion is spent.

<FormulaBox
  label="Simple multipliers"
  formula="k_G = 1/(1-MPC), \quad k_T = -MPC/(1-MPC)"
  description="A marginal tax rate or a propensity to import enlarges the denominator and shrinks the multiplier. Crowding out effectively reduces the numerator."
/>

When interest rates rise and private investment falls, that is crowding out. It grows the closer the economy is to full employment and the more firmly the central bank defends price stability. In a liquidity trap, rates do not move and crowding out is weak. "Fiscal policy always has a large effect" and "fiscal policy has no effect" are both statements made outside the model.

The balanced-budget multiplier is 1 in the simple model. If spending and taxes both rise by 50 trillion, first-round net demand is 50 of spending minus a 40 fall in consumption, or 10 trillion, but after the multiplier the rise in output is $5×50-4×50=50$ trillion, equal to the increase in government spending. First check whether a question is about the simple model or about crowding out.

## 2. When won/dollar rises, the won depreciates

If 1 dollar goes from 1,300 won to 1,400 won, the dollar is dearer and the won weaker. "A rising exchange rate means a strong currency" is an error that ignores the quotation convention. The real exchange rate multiplies in relative prices, so export competitiveness cannot be judged from the nominal rate alone.

Purchasing power parity is a long-run anchor. In the short run, interest rates and expectations weigh more on the exchange rate. The claim that depreciation immediately improves the current account depends on export and import elasticities and on lags (the J-curve). That calculation belongs to the exchange-rate chapter of international finance.

## 3. Comparative advantage is about opportunity cost, not absolute productivity

If Korea makes 2 chips or 1 unit of wheat per hour, and the foreign country 1 chip or 1 unit of wheat per hour, Korea has an absolute advantage in chips. The opportunity cost of a chip is 0.5 wheat in Korea and 1 wheat abroad. Korea has a comparative advantage in chips, the foreign country in wheat. The foreign country has an absolute advantage in neither good but still gains from trade.

<Callout type="warning">
Do not read a current account surplus as a report card. A surplus is the mirror of saving minus investment, not a competitiveness medal. It also appears when weak domestic demand cuts imports. The gains from trade in an introductory course are a possibility for the country as a whole, not a wage rise for every worker.
</Callout>

At 1,300 won to the dollar, a product exported at 1 dollar brings in 1,300 won; at 1,400 won, 1,400 won. Whether that 10% stays as margin depends on the invoicing currency, pass-through and whether imported intermediate inputs get dearer at the same time. The one-liner "depreciation is good news for exporters" is wrong for industries that rely heavily on imported intermediates.

## Check your understanding

In a closed model with idle resources and MPC = 0.6, which raises output more: 20 trillion of spending or a 20 trillion tax cut? $k_G=2.5$ → 50 trillion, $k_T=1.5$ → 30 trillion. Spending does. Adding openness and crowding out shrinks both numbers, but the sign of the gap may remain.
