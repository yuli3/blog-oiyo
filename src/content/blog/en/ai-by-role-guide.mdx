"I don't know where to use AI" usually means "I don't know which of my tasks it actually fits." The same tool works completely differently depending on your role. Here's a role-by-role breakdown of how to apply AI to real work.

<Callout type="note">
Want to see what AI services exist before you dive in? Start with a broader service catalog, don't paste sensitive or confidential information, and always have a human check the output.
</Callout>

## Across every role — the 4 ingredients of a good prompt

Regardless of your job, output quality comes down to the prompt.

1. **Assign a role:** "You are an expert in ___" narrows the perspective.
2. **Give context:** Include the purpose, audience, and constraints (length, tone, format).
3. **Show an example:** Give one or two samples of the result you want.
4. **Ask for steps:** Don't demand a finished product in one shot — iterate through draft → feedback → revision.

## Developers

- **Code review and refactoring:** Paste a function and ask, "Review this for bugs, edge cases, and readability."
- **Test generation:** "Write unit tests for this function, including boundary cases."
- **Error interpretation:** Share a stack trace and ask for likely causes and a debugging order.
- **Documentation:** Generate README drafts, comments, and API docs from your code.
- Caution: always run and verify generated code yourself. Check security and license implications.

## Planning / PM

- **Requirements gathering:** Turn scattered notes into "user stories + acceptance criteria."
- **Meeting summaries:** Condense long discussions into decisions, action items, and owners.
- **Prioritization:** Sort a feature list into an impact-vs-effort matrix.
- **Research drafts:** Build the skeleton of a competitor comparison table (verify facts yourself).

## Marketing / Content

- **Copy variations:** Turn one message into multiple versions by tone, length, and channel.
- **Content planning:** Go from a topic to candidate titles, an outline, and keyword clusters.
- **Repurposing:** Turn a long article into social posts, a newsletter, or a video script.
- Caution: verify facts and figures yourself, and let a human polish the brand voice.

## Design

- **Idea generation:** Brainstorm concepts, moods, and color directions.
- **Copy and UX writing:** Draft button labels, help text, and error messages.
- **Image generation and editing:** Mockups, backgrounds, and drafts (always check copyright and commercial-use terms).

## Admin, documents, and learning

- **Document drafting:** Write and polish reports, emails, and announcements.
- **Spreadsheets and data:** Explain formulas and functions, and outline data-cleanup steps.
- **Learning support:** Explain concepts through simple analogies, and generate practice questions and summaries.

<Callout type="tip">
**Where to start:** Don't chase a big automation win right away. Apply AI to one small, recurring annoyance first — a summary, a cleanup task, a first draft. Once that one habit sticks, the scope naturally expands.
</Callout>
