# Tenant Rights Complete Guide

Renting is one of the most financially significant contracts most people sign, yet tenant rights are widely misunderstood. In most countries, tenants have far more legal protection than they realize — but only if they know how to exercise those rights.

---

## 1. Before You Sign: Due Diligence

### Check the Property's Legal Status
Before handing over any money, verify:

| What to Check | How | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner verification** | Land registry / title search | Landlord ≠ registered owner |
| **Mortgage status** | Ask for statement or check registry | Mortgage exceeds 70–80% of property value |
| **Prior encumbrances** | Title search | Existing liens, tax debts |
| **Zoning compliance** | Local authority records | Illegal conversion, unlicensed HMO |

<Callout type="warning">
**Rent-to-own fraud is common**: Always confirm the person signing as landlord is the actual registered owner. For properties managed by agents, request written proof of their authority to let the property.
</Callout>

### Pre-Move-In Inspection
- Document all existing damage with **dated photos** before moving in
- Create a written inventory signed by both parties
- Without this, the landlord may claim all damage was caused by you

## 2. Security Deposit Protections

Security deposit rules vary significantly by jurisdiction, but several protections are nearly universal:

| Country / Region | Maximum Deposit | Protection Scheme Required |
|---|---|---|
| **England & Wales** | 5 weeks' rent | Yes — TDS, DPS, or MyDeposits |
| **Germany** | 3 months' cold rent | Separate bank account required |
| **United States** | 1–2 months (varies by state) | Some states require escrow accounts |
| **France** | 1 month (unfurnished) / 2 months (furnished) | Must be returned within 1–2 months of move-out |
| **Australia** | 4 weeks' rent (max) | Government-held bond scheme |

<Callout type="tip">
**UK tenants**: If your landlord fails to protect your deposit in a government-approved scheme within 30 days, you're entitled to **1–3x the deposit amount** as compensation. This is one of the most commonly overlooked tenant rights.
</Callout>

## 3. Your Right to Repairs

In virtually every jurisdiction, landlords are legally obligated to maintain:
- Structural integrity (roof, walls, floors)
- Heating and hot water systems
- Electrical safety
- Plumbing and drainage

**Your responsibility typically covers:**
- Minor consumables (light bulbs, batteries)
- Damage caused by your negligence

| Issue | Reasonable Response Time |
|---|---|
| No heating in winter | 24 hours (emergency) |
| Broken lock / security issue | 24 hours |
| Leaking roof or pipes | 2–5 days |
| General maintenance | 14–28 days |

<Callout type="note">
If your landlord refuses to repair, document everything in writing (email is ideal). After a reasonable deadline passes, you may have the right to arrange repairs yourself and deduct the cost from rent — but check local law first, as procedure varies.
</Callout>

## 4. Rent Increases: What's Legal

| Country | Rent Increase Rules |
|---|---|
| **England** | Once per year; must use Form 4 process; tenant can challenge at tribunal |
| **Germany** | Max 20% over 3 years (10% in some cities with rent cap) |
| **France** | Indexed to IRL (rent reference index); controlled in tension zones |
| **New York City** | Stabilized tenants: annual % set by city; other: 30-day written notice |
| **California** | Max 5% + local CPI (cap 10%) for covered properties (AB 1482) |

<Callout type="warning">
Verbal rent increases are not enforceable in most jurisdictions. Always require written notice. You do NOT have to accept an increase just because the landlord asks — verify your local rules before agreeing.
</Callout>

## 5. Eviction Protections

In most countries, eviction requires:
1. **Valid legal ground** (non-payment, lease breach, landlord moving in, etc.)
2. **Proper written notice** (notice period varies: 1–6+ months)
3. **Court order** — self-help eviction (changing locks, removing belongings) is **illegal everywhere** in developed countries

### Notice Periods by Country

| Country | Standard Notice Period |
|---|---|
| United States | 30–60 days (varies by state) |
| United Kingdom | 2 months (Section 21) |
| Germany | 3–9 months depending on tenancy length |
| France | 6 months (unfurnished) / 3 months (furnished) |
| Australia | 30–90 days depending on state |

<Callout type="tip">
**If you receive an eviction notice**: Don't panic and don't leave immediately. Contact a tenant advice organization first — many notices have procedural errors that make them invalid. In the UK, Shelter and Citizens Advice offer free guidance.
</Callout>
