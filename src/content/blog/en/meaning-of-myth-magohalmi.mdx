Magohalmi appears in regional Korean folktales as a vast female being who creates mountains, islands, rocks, and landforms. The name "halmi" may sound like a plain or even belittling word for an old woman, but in these tales Magohalmi is a creator who exceeds human scale. Stories tell of her carrying soil in the folds of her skirt to make mountains, or leaving landforms through her footprints and touch. Depending on the tradition, her name and character may vary as Mago, Mago Grandmother, Mago Fairy, and other forms, and Daoist imagination sometimes overlaps with indigenous giant folklore.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

The basic structure of Magohalmi stories is that a gigantic body makes the shape of the land. She is said to have formed mountains by dropping soil as she carried it, made islands by placing stones, and turned the places where she sat or lay down into landforms. These stories show an imagination that does not treat natural scenery as unfamiliar matter, but understands it as the trace of a transcendent body.

In some traditions, Magohalmi tries to build a fortress or lay a bridge, but fails. Stories are sometimes attached in which the work stops because a rooster crows or time runs out. The details vary by tradition, but these failure tales reveal a sense that even immense creative power has limits and is bound by time. The creator in myth is not so much a being who completes everything as one who leaves unfinished traces in the terrain.

It is safer to understand Magohalmi as a cluster of regional folktales rather than as a figure fixed in a single scripture. Reducing her to one standard plot can therefore distort the sources. What matters is that Magohalmi is a representative name through which Korean folk imagination has connected the forms of the earth with a gigantic female body.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Magohalmi is an archetype of the **earth mother** and the **giant creator**. She is less a nurturing mother than a primordial body that moves mountain ranges and seas. In modern terms, she calls to mind how powerfully cities, landscapes, and historical environments beyond an individual's life can act upon a person's unconscious.

The Magohalmi archetype restores the feeling of "vastness." For someone trapped in everyday problems, she reminds them that life is set upon a much wider terrain. At the same time, her unfinished tales say that even enormous plans may leave only traces before time, chance, and error. For this reason, Magohalmi is a symbol that holds both the power and the limits of creation.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Are Magohalmi and Seolmundae Halmang the same being?**

A. It is difficult to conclude that they are completely the same being. Both have the character of giant female creators, but Seolmundae Halmang is especially distinct in Jeju tradition.

**Q. Where do Magohalmi stories appear?**

A. They remain widely in regional oral folktales and folk traditions rather than in one classical text. For that reason, the details differ depending on the tradition.
