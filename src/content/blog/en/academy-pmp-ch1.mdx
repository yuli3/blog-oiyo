## What Is PMP?

**PMP (Project Management Professional)** is PMI's (Project Management Institute) globally recognized certification validating the ability to lead and manage projects.

**Exam details**:
- 180 questions (multiple choice, drag-and-drop, matching)
- 230 minutes
- Passing score: approximately 60%+ (exact threshold not published)
- Prerequisites: 36 months (bachelor's) or 60 months (high school) project experience + 35 hours PM education

---

## PMBOK 7th Edition Changes

The PMBOK Guide evolved significantly from 6th to 7th edition:

**6th Edition**: Process-focused (5 Process Groups, 49 Processes, 10 Knowledge Areas)

**7th Edition**: Principles and outcomes-focused
- 12 Project Management Principles
- 8 Performance Domains
- Approach-agnostic (covers predictive, agile, and hybrid)

<Callout type="tip">
**Exam composition**: The current PMP exam is approximately 50% predictive (traditional) and 50% agile/hybrid. You must understand both approaches to pass.
</Callout>

## The 12 PM Principles (PMBOK 7th Edition)

1. **Stewardship**: Act responsibly and ethically
2. **Team**: Build a collaborative team environment
3. **Stakeholders**: Engage and manage stakeholders
4. **Value**: Focus on delivering value
5. **Systems Thinking**: See the whole system
6. **Leadership**: Motivate and influence
7. **Tailoring**: Adapt the approach to context
8. **Quality**: Meet stakeholder expectations
9. **Complexity**: Navigate and respond to complexity
10. **Risk**: Optimize risk responses
11. **Adaptability/Resiliency**: Embrace change and recover from setbacks
12. **Change**: Enable transitions to desired future states

## The 8 Performance Domains

| Domain | Core Focus |
|--------|-----------|
| Stakeholders | Engagement, expectation management |
| Team | Team development, leadership |
| Development Approach & Life Cycle | Choose predictive/agile/hybrid |
| Planning | Schedule, budget, resource planning |
| Project Work | Execution, issue management |
| Delivery | Scope, WBS, value realization |
| Measurement | KPIs, performance tracking |
| Uncertainty | Risk and opportunity management |

## Development Approaches

### Predictive (Waterfall)
- Clear, stable requirements with minimal changes
- Detailed upfront planning, sequential execution
- Examples: Construction, manufacturing, regulatory compliance

### Agile
- Unclear or frequently changing requirements
- Iterative, incremental development in sprints
- Examples: Software development, product development

### Hybrid
- Combines elements of predictive and agile
- Example: Plan predictively, develop in agile sprints

## Key Concept Cards

**PMBOK 7th Edition Shift** ★★★★★
: Process-focused → principles and outcomes. 12 principles + 8 domains. Agile fully integrated.

**Predictive vs. Agile** ★★★★★
: Stable requirements = predictive. Changing requirements = agile. Mixed = hybrid. Context-dependent choice.

**Tailoring** ★★★★☆
: Adjusting the approach and processes to fit the organization, project, and team. Central concept in PMBOK 7th Edition.

## Practice Quiz

**Q1. A software development project has requirements that change frequently. What development approach is most appropriate?**
> Agile. Through short iterations (sprints), agile can accommodate evolving requirements. Customer feedback after each sprint is incorporated into the next — making it optimal for unclear or changing requirements.

**Q2. Why is "tailoring" so important in the PMP exam?**
> No two projects are identical. PMBOK 7th Edition emphasizes selecting appropriate methods based on context rather than applying a single approach universally. Tailoring involves combining predictive, agile, and hybrid elements based on organizational culture, project complexity, team capability, and stakeholder needs.
