## 1. HRD (Human Resource Development)

**Definition**

- Human Resource Development
- Enhancing the learning capacity of individuals, groups, and organizations.

**HRD Domains**

- Training & Development (T&D)
- Organization Development (OD)
- Career Development (CD)

**Training Needs Analysis (TNA)**

- **Organizational analysis**: Strategic direction and goals
- **Task/Job analysis**: Required competencies
- **Individual analysis**: Competency gaps per employee

A performance gap is not automatically a training need. In a fictional service team, employees may know the escalation procedure but lack permission to use it, face conflicting targets or be unable to access account records. More instruction would not by itself remove those barriers. Diagnose the required performance, current performance and causes before selecting an intervention. OPM's guidance explicitly considers non-training solutions; its agency-specific legal requirements are not universal employer obligations. [OPM, Training Needs Assessment](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/training-and-development/planning-evaluating/).

Translate a broad goal into observable work. “Improve customer handling” might become “identify the disputed item, explain the evidence and route an unresolved case to an authorized reviewer.” Specify the conditions, acceptable quality and who can verify the behavior. Then separate missing knowledge from missing resources, incentives or authority.

## 2. Training and Development Methods

**OJT (On-the-Job Training)**

- Learning while performing actual work
- Mentoring, coaching, job rotation
- Connected to actual tasks; requires supervision, safe practice and feedback rather than assuming immediate independent competence

**Off-JT (Off-the-Job Training)**

- Instruction separated from normal job execution; it can occur in a training room at the workplace or online
- Classroom lectures, workshops, seminars, e-learning
- Can support systematic practice and theory; delivery to a large audience does not prove learning or transfer

**Blended Learning**

- Combines learning formats and activities, for example online preparation with coached job practice
- Theory and practice in tandem

**Coaching vs Mentoring**

- **Coaching**: Improves specific skills (current role focus)
- **Mentoring**: Broader career guidance (long-term relationship)

These are common emphases, not fixed duration rules. Coaching can support longer development, and mentoring can address a current dilemma. Agree the purpose, confidentiality boundaries and the distinction between developmental advice and formal performance decisions. A remote employee practicing a real case with a supervisor can receive OJT; an employee attending a classroom session in the same building can receive off-the-job instruction. Physical location is not the defining boundary.

## 3. Training Evaluation — Kirkpatrick's Four Levels

**Level 1 — Reaction**

- Participant satisfaction (post-training survey)
- Perceived training quality

**Level 2 — Learning**

- Changes in knowledge, skills, and attitudes
- Measured via pre- and post-tests

**Level 3 — Behavior**

- Transfer of learning to the job
- Observed changes in on-the-job behavior

**Level 4 — Results**

- **Organizational outcomes**: cost savings, productivity, turnover
- Financial and non-financial outcomes relevant to the program's purpose; a separate ROI analysis may be appropriate when costs and attributable benefits can be supported

## 4. Career Development Management

**Career Development**

- Planning and supporting an individual's long-term career growth.

**Career Anchors (Schein)**

- A framework for reflecting on career values, motives and perceived capabilities, rather than a permanent restriction on future choices.
- **Examples**: Technical/Functional, Managerial, Security/Stability, Entrepreneurial Creativity, Service/Dedication

**CDP (Career Development Program)**

- Designing career paths
- Linking job moves and training

**Succession Planning**

- Identifying and developing candidates for key positions
- Supports continuity by preparing capability and interim coverage; it does not guarantee that a vacancy will be filled successfully.

## 5. Evaluate Different Claims with Different Evidence

The four levels organize different questions; they are not a causal staircase. Kirkpatrick Partners describes the framework as linked but non-linear and recommends planning from desired results back to behaviors and learning. [Kirkpatrick Model](https://www.kirkpatrickpartners.com/the-kirkpatrick-model/).

| Evaluation question | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|
| Reaction | Relevance and experience ratings can reveal barriers. A favorable rating does not show that the task was learned. |
| Learning | A scored demonstration or comparable test can assess learning. Reusing answers or changing test difficulty can distort a pre/post comparison. |
| Behavior | Observe the specified work after training. Check whether people had an opportunity and support to apply it. |
| Results | Compare targeted outcomes over a defined period. Other changes can produce the same pattern. |

Plan observation and performance support with line managers. A job aid, access to information and useful feedback may be necessary alongside instruction. The framework's own discussion of drivers separates knowing how from the conditions that sustain application. Do not copy its illustrative study percentages as universal success rates. [Kirkpatrick Partners, performance support](https://www.kirkpatrickpartners.com/blog/how-to-use-drivers-to-ensure-training-success/).

### A Hypothetical ROI Calculation

A pilot has USD 10,000 in instructor and material costs plus USD 5,000 in paid participant time. Assume these are the complete incremental costs for the chosen evaluation period: USD 15,000. Rework falls by 400 hours; at an assumed avoidable cost of USD 50 per hour, the candidate gross benefit is USD 20,000.

If all that reduction were attributable to the intervention and economically realizable, net benefit would be USD 5,000 and ROI would be 5,000 / 15,000, approximately 33.3%. If only half were attributable, benefit would be USD 10,000 and ROI approximately −33.3%. These are sensitivity scenarios, not measured attribution estimates.

Time freed is not automatically a cash saving: salaries may remain unchanged, and redeployment may create capacity rather than reduce expenditure. Check case mix, recording rules, other process changes, maintenance costs and the observation horizon. Do not double-count the same hours as both reduced costs and additional output. Use non-financial outcomes when monetization would conceal an important quality or safety effect.

## 6. Practice Quiz

**Q. What makes attributing organizational results to training difficult?**
**Worked answer.** Define the outcome and period first, then distinguish observing a change from attributing it to training. A product change, new system or easier case mix could affect the result. In the pilot above, the same observed 400-hour reduction produces positive or negative ROI depending on attribution and realizable benefit. Neither a favorable reaction survey nor an improved test score settles that question. A comparison group or repeated pre-intervention observations may improve the analysis, but the design and its remaining limitations must be stated.

**Q. Why is Succession Planning critical for organizations?**
**Worked answer.** It can reduce dependence on a single incumbent by identifying critical capabilities, developing candidates and preparing interim coverage. A list of names does not establish readiness, guarantee retention or promise promotion. Assess evidence of capability, availability, development needs and handover arrangements. Internal candidates bring organizational knowledge; external candidates may bring missing capabilities. Neither source is always cheaper, faster or better aligned. This chapter does not assert a general Sarbanes-Oxley succession requirement.

Career development should connect the employee's interests with credible work opportunities and learning, not just prescribe a ladder. Career anchors are a reflection framework rather than a fixed diagnosis of what someone can never value differently. Record agreed experiences, support, review dates and alternative routes. A development plan is not an employment guarantee; succession readiness and the individual's preferred career are related but distinct decisions.
