## 1. The anchoring effect

Anchoring is the phenomenon where the first number or standard mentioned acts as a reference point for every judgment that follows. In negotiation, the first offer can strongly sway expectations around price, timeline, or scope of work.

If a seller opens with $1,000, a buyer might feel like $800 is already a big concession. But if the buyer opens with $500, that same $800 can suddenly feel expensive. In other words, the first offer isn't just a number — it's the starting coordinate of the entire negotiation.

## 2. When should you make the first offer?

| Situation | First-offer strategy |
|------|--------------|
| You have enough information | Open first and set the reference point |
| You lack information | Listen to their offer first and gauge the range |
| Market benchmarks are clear | Open with objective data attached |
| The relationship matters | Favor an explained offer over an aggressive anchor |

Always going first isn't automatically the right answer. The power of a first offer grows when you're prepared and can explain the reasoning behind it.

## 3. A concession is a message

A concession isn't simply the act of backing down. Its size, speed, and stated reason all send a message to the other side. Give up too much, too fast, and your original offer starts to look flimsy. Refuse to concede at all, and the conversation can stall out completely.

A good concession comes with conditions attached. Link the give with a take: "If we move up the deadline, we'll need to cut the number of revisions," or "If we adjust the price, we'll need to extend the contract term."

## 4. Example: negotiating scope of work

A manager says, "I need both the report and the presentation deck finished by the end of this week." Rather than answering flatly with "I can't do that," you could say, "I can have the report done by this Friday, but the deck as well will need until next Tuesday." That opens up an alternative.

## 5. Key takeaways

- Anchoring is the phenomenon where the first offer becomes the reference point for everything judged afterward.
- A first offer is more effective when it's backed by information and reasoning.
- Concessions land better when they're framed as an exchange, not a free gift.
- Conceding quickly and too much can weaken your negotiating power.
