## What Are Career Values?

**Career values** are the things you most want from work — what you're optimizing for when you choose a job, company, or career path. They're not preferences ("I like interesting work") but *drivers* ("without this, I can't sustain engagement or satisfaction long-term").

Two people can have identical skills and backgrounds and still thrive in completely different environments. One might need autonomy above all else; another might need recognition; a third might prioritize stable income. Knowing your top value doesn't just help with job hunting — it explains why your current role feels draining, clarifies what you're negotiating for, and gives you language for career conversations.

Career values clarify:
1. **Career direction** — what to optimize for in your next move
2. **The real cause of burnout** — most burnout is values mismatch, not overwork
3. **Salary negotiation strategy** — what matters beyond the number
4. **Job change decisions** — whether to switch or restructure your current role

The test has 20 statements, 2 per value. Rate each from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). There are no right or wrong answers — the goal is to surface your authentic priorities, not your idealized ones.

## The 10 Core Career Values

| Value | The Central Question | Typical Strong Matches |
|---|---|---|
| 🦅 **Autonomy** | Can I work my own way and make my own calls? | Freelancer, startup, researcher, consultant |
| 🎨 **Creativity** | Can I build new things and think creatively? | Designer, marketer, product, artist |
| 🏠 **Stability** | Is my environment predictable and secure? | Government, large corporate, teaching |
| 🏆 **Recognition** | Will my contributions be seen and rewarded? | Sales, executive, politics, athletics |
| 🤝 **Relationships** | Will I work with people I respect and enjoy? | HR, counseling, education, healthcare |
| 🌍 **Social Impact** | Does my work make a real difference? | NGO, healthcare, education, civic sector |
| 📚 **Learning & Growth** | Can I keep developing skills and knowledge? | Research, consulting, tech, academia |
| 👑 **Leadership** | Can I lead, direct, and shape outcomes? | Management, military, entrepreneurship |
| ⚖️ **Work-Life Balance** | Is my personal time genuinely protected? | Remote work, public sector, certain tech roles |
| 💰 **Compensation** | Is the financial reward substantial? | Finance, investment, law, medicine |

## Burnout's Real Cause: Values Mismatch

Most professionals attribute burnout to overwork. Research consistently finds the deeper cause is a **mismatch between your core values and your current work environment**.

| Your Core Value | Mismatched Environment | Burnout Symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | Micromanagement, rigid process | Helplessness, loss of initiative |
| Social Impact | Meaningless routine tasks | Emptiness, cynicism |
| Learning & Growth | Stagnant, repetitive work | Boredom, career anxiety |
| Work-Life Balance | Late nights, weekend work | Exhaustion, physical symptoms |
| Recognition | Hidden, undervalued contributions | Resentment, disengagement |

<Callout type="tip">
**If you're currently burning out**: identify which value isn't being met. Before changing jobs, explore whether role adjustment, manager change, or project ownership could resolve it. Job change is expensive — a values conversation is free.
</Callout>

## Career Strategy by Top Value

### If Autonomy is #1
- Find intrapreneur roles within larger organizations
- Plan a path toward freelancing or consulting
- Prioritize companies with high remote-work ratios and flat hierarchies

### If Creativity is #1
- Seek companies with 20% time or innovation projects
- Build a portfolio or side project to demonstrate your capabilities
- Negotiate for creative roles rather than purely operational ones

### If Stability is #1
- Research long-term employment data before accepting offers
- Compare benefits packages carefully
- Prefer large companies and public-sector roles over startups

### If Social Impact is #1
- Explore impact investing, ESG-focused companies, and B Corps
- Look for companies with transparent social mission metrics
- Consider how your current skill set can transfer to the social sector

### If Learning & Growth is #1
- Prioritize companies with strong L&D budgets and internal mobility
- Ask about mentorship and conference attendance in interviews
- Calculate your learning rate alongside your salary offer

## When Values Conflict

Career values often pull against each other. The test reveals your *primary* value, but understanding the tensions matters:

**Autonomy vs. Stability**: Freelancers have autonomy but face income volatility. Corporate roles offer security but constrain independence. → Decide which you can live without; compromising on both makes you miserable.

**High Compensation vs. Work-Life Balance**: Finance and consulting pay well but demand significant time. → Is this a phase you can sustain for 3–5 years, or is it indefinite? Plan accordingly.

**Social Impact vs. Compensation**: Mission-driven organizations often pay less. → Social impact startups and impact investment funds exist specifically in this intersection.

<Callout type="note">
**Values shift over time**: Research suggests that in your 20s, learning and growth tends to dominate; in your 30s, recognition and leadership rise; in your 40s–50s, social impact and balance become more central. Reassess every five years.
</Callout>
