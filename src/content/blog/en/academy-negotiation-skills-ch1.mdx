## 1. Negotiation isn't a war of words you win

Negotiation is the process of using conversation and choices to reach an agreement both sides can live with, whenever your wants don't perfectly match someone else's. It isn't just haggling over price. Adjusting the scope of a project, rescheduling a deadline, dividing up chores, or planning a family trip — most of the everyday give-and-take in our lives is really a form of negotiation.

Good negotiation isn't the skill of making the other side back down. It's **the skill of structuring your goals and theirs together**. That's why preparation, information, standards, and relationship management matter more in negotiation than raw talking ability.

## 2. Distributive vs. integrative negotiation

| | Distributive negotiation | Integrative negotiation |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Basic image | Dividing a fixed pie | Growing the pie, then dividing it |
| Focus | A single price, share, or condition | Multiple interests and options |
| Relationship | Closer to a one-off transaction | Favors long-term relationships |
| Risk | Zero-sum thinking | Requires time to explore |

Distributive negotiation tends to look like a structure where whatever one side gains, the other side loses. Integrative negotiation, by contrast, tries to raise satisfaction on both sides by adjusting several conditions together — not just price, but timeline, quality, risk-sharing, and after-sale support.

## 3. An example

Imagine someone wants to sell a used laptop for $700, and a buyer wants to pay $600. Looking only at price, that's a $100 gap — a distributive negotiation. But if the seller wants a fast, no-hassle deal, and the buyer needs extra accessories, the picture changes. Settling at $650 with an immediate handoff, bag included, can end up being a better deal for both sides than a straight price fight.

## 4. The negotiator's basic questions

Before any negotiation, it helps to write down three things: What do I absolutely need to get? What am I willing to give up? What is likely to matter most to the other side? Having answers to these keeps you from being pulled around by emotion or snap judgment mid-conversation.

## 5. Key takeaways

- Negotiation isn't a war of words meant to defeat the other side — it's a process of building options both parties can agree to.
- Distributive negotiation is closer to dividing up a fixed share.
- Integrative negotiation looks for value both sides can gain by combining multiple conditions.
- Good negotiation starts with preparation and structure, not eloquence.
