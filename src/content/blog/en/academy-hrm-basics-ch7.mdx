## 1. Content Theories

**Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs:**

Physiological → Safety → Social (Belonging) → Esteem → Self-Actualization

The ordering is not a rule requiring complete satisfaction before another need matters. Maslow's original paper discusses exceptions and partial satisfaction of several needs at once. Its illustrative percentages are not population estimates. [Maslow, 1943](https://www.yorku.ca/pclassic/Maslow/motivation.htm).

**Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory:**

**Hygiene Factors**: Prevent dissatisfaction (salary, working conditions, job security)

**Motivator Factors**: Create satisfaction (achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth)

- The theory distinguishes dissatisfaction from satisfaction; it does not establish that pay can never influence effort, perceived fairness or retention.

**Alderfer's ERG Theory:**

Existence · Relatedness · Growth

**Regression possible**: frustration at higher levels can reactivate lower-level needs.

**McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y:**

**Theory X**: Managerial assumptions emphasizing avoidance of work and a need for control

**Theory Y**: Alternative assumptions emphasizing possible self-direction and responsibility under suitable conditions

- These are assumptions to examine, not fixed types of employee.

## 2. Process Theories

**Vroom's Expectancy Theory** — Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence

- **Expectancy**: Belief that effort will lead to performance
- **Instrumentality**: Belief that performance will lead to reward
- **Valence**: Personal value placed on the reward

**Adams' Equity Theory**

Compares one's own input/output ratio to a reference person's  
Perceived inequity → discomfort → behavioral adjustment  
(reduce inputs, increase outputs, change the reference, or leave)

**Goal-Setting Theory (Locke)**

Specific, challenging goals can direct effort, subject to commitment, capability, task demands and support.
Requires goal acceptance and feedback

- **SMART Goals**: Specific · Measurable · Achievable · Relevant · Time-bound

## 3. Reinforcement Theory

**Reinforcement Theory (Skinner)** — Behavior is shaped by its consequences.

**Positive Reinforcement** — Desired behavior + reward → behavior repeats

**Negative Reinforcement**

Removal of an aversive stimulus → behavior repeats  
(Example: completing work on time to avoid a manager's follow-up)

**Punishment** — Undesired behavior + aversive consequence → behavior suppressed

**Extinction** — Removing rewards → behavior fades over time

**Reinforcement Schedules**

- **Fixed-Interval**: Reinforcement of the first qualifying response after a fixed time interval; a payroll calendar alone does not demonstrate this contingency
- **Fixed-Ratio**: After a set number of behaviors (per-unit commission)
- **Variable-Interval**: After unpredictable time intervals
- **Variable-Ratio**: After unpredictable number of behaviors
Variable-ratio describes reinforcement after a varying number of responses, not a universally best workplace reward system. [APA definition](https://dictionary.apa.org/variable-ratio-schedule).

## 4. Models Generate Questions, Not Employee Diagnoses

| Perspective | Question to investigate |
|---|---|
| Needs | What does the person value, and which conditions cause concern? |
| Expectancy | Can effort improve performance, is the promised outcome credible, and is it valued? |
| Equity | Which contributions, outcomes and comparison people shape fairness judgments? |
| Goals | Are the goal, resources, capability and feedback adequate? |
| Reinforcement | Which consequence follows the behavior, and does the behavior become more likely? |

Low output does not identify its cause. Missing access, difficult cases or a defective process can also reduce output. Investigate conditions and preferences rather than assigning a fixed motivational type.

### An Index Is Not a Productivity Forecast

For a simplified single-outcome exercise, normalize E and I from 0 to 1 and use positive valence V on the same range. With E = 0.8, I = 0.5 and V = 0.75, the product is 0.30. Changing assumed I to 0.9 produces 0.54, an 80% relative increase in the constructed index.

This is not an 80% productivity forecast or a 54% probability of effort. The scale, single outcome and multiplicative form are exercise assumptions. Real choices may involve several valued or disliked outcomes, habits and constraints. Ask how inputs were elicited instead of treating a manager's impressions as measurements.

In a fictional team, public recognition for reporting errors is valued by one employee and disliked by another. Both may fear blame. The reward's name does not establish its value or effect. Observe what follows an actual report and whether information quality improves. Fewer recorded errors may reflect concealment rather than better performance.

Positive and negative reinforcement both concern increasing a response. Negative reinforcement removes, prevents or postpones an aversive condition; it is not punishment or criticism. Define the target and observe the subsequent pattern before deciding whether an intended reward functioned as reinforcement. [APA, negative reinforcement](https://dictionary.apa.org/negative-reinforcement).

## 5. Apply the Models to HR Decisions

The models direct attention to different parts of an HR system. [Performance management](/en/academy-hrm-basics-ch5/) defines what is evaluated; [compensation design](/en/academy-hrm-basics-ch6/) defines what earns pay. A motivational label does not establish that either system works as intended.

| Decision | Evidence to examine before changing the system |
|---|---|
| Performance-related bonus | Check the published eligibility rule, actual ratings and payments, timing, and whether employees understand and trust the link. Payment records are not a direct measure of instrumentality. |
| Pay compression | Compare role demands, relevant contributions, market conditions and employees' chosen reference points. Similar salaries alone do not establish identical inputs or a particular fairness judgment. |
| Challenging target | Specify quantity, quality, resources and the employee's influence over results. A target affected by unavailable tools or external demand needs a different diagnosis from a capability gap. |

In a fictional team, the bonus is advertised as performance-related, but a fixed budget produces the same payment for everyone regardless of ratings. Examine whether the rule describes a group payment, whether another outcome is genuinely contingent on individual performance and how the decision was explained. Raising the payment ceiling does not repair a missing performance–outcome link by itself. Equally, the records do not allow an analyst to assign a numerical I value to every employee: credibility is a belief, not the correlation between ratings and payments.

A second hypothetical organization pays a new hire the same salary as an experienced colleague. This may raise a pay-compression question, but their responsibilities, hours, skills and external alternatives may differ. Ask which comparison matters to each person and examine the reasons for the pay decision. Do not predict reduced effort or resignation from salary equality alone. Consistent decision procedures and an explanation are relevant, but neither automatically resolves a perceived distribution problem.

HR can also examine task support, training and job design rather than treating pay as the only lever. Providing training does not prove that effort–performance expectancy increased; a person may still lack access, authority or time to use the skill. Follow the actual conditions and behavior, using defined measures and protecting personal information. Multiple outcomes, professional commitments and constraints can matter beyond the simplified single-outcome product.

### Application Problem → Worked Answer

**Problem.** A manager doubles the bonus ceiling after staff question the performance link and declares, “Valence doubled, so motivation doubled.” What should HR check?

**Worked answer.** A ceiling is neither a received payment nor a measurement of personal value. Check the eligibility and payout rule, actual implementation, communication, whether staff value the outcome and whether they can influence the rated performance. Even if a hypothetical V doubled while other inputs stayed fixed, the arithmetic would double a constructed index, not establish a measured behavioral effect. Changing the advertised reward size cannot by itself validate the link or identify the cause of poor performance.

## 6. Practice Quiz

**Q. When does Vroom's Expectancy Theory predict that motivation equals zero?**
**Worked answer.** A zero input gives a zero product in the simplified E × I × V calculation. It does not prove that a real employee has no motivation of any kind. Another outcome or professional commitment may matter. Diagnose the relevant effort-performance link, credibility of the outcome and personal value without treating an arithmetic identity as an observed psychological fact.

**Q. Does a surprise reward establish a variable-ratio schedule or guarantee better performance?**

**Worked answer.** No. Establish how reinforcement is contingent on response counts; unpredictability alone does not identify the schedule. Specify behavior, quality limits and consequences, then observe outcomes. More rushed closures can worsen quality. Continued responding is not necessarily valuable performance, and an experimental effect is not a reason to manipulate employees through unpredictable pay.
