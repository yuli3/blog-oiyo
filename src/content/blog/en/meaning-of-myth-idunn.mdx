Idunn is the goddess in Norse mythology who keeps the apples that preserve the gods' youth. Her name evokes a sense of youth and newness, and shows that even the gods do not possess eternal youth automatically. Idunn matters less for great battles than for the value of sustaining, restoring, and caring for life force.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Idunn's representative story is the scene in which she is abducted by the giant Thiazi. Loki, under threat, lures Idunn outside Asgard. When Idunn and the apples disappear, the gods quickly begin to age and weaken. This shows that even the gods' power cannot be maintained without a continual supply of life force.

The gods pressure Loki into recovering Idunn. Loki flies off in the form of a falcon, brings Idunn back in a small nut-like form, and the pursuing Thiazi eventually dies. This story emphasizes that youth is a vulnerable resource that can be taken away, and that the whole community depends on protecting it.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

Idunn is an archetype of the **maiden of regeneration** and the **keeper of life**. She is not so much the protagonist who creates vitality as the quiet center that keeps vitality from disappearing. In modern terms, she can call to mind care, rest, healthy routines, and a creator's recovery time, all of which sustain the energy of an organization. In a culture that pursues productivity alone, Idunn symbolizes youth and life force as sacred resources that must be maintained.

The Idunn archetype lets us see recovery not as a luxury, but as a foundation. The story that even the gods age without the apples says that powerful beings also need continual replenishment. In modern terms, this resembles sleep, vacation, healthy food, and a creator's empty time: conditions that may not appear clearly on a performance chart, but that make every activity possible.

## Related Figures

Reading [Bragi](/en/meaning-of-myth-bragi/), [Loki](/en/meaning-of-myth-loki/), and [Skadi](/en/meaning-of-myth-skadi/) together helps connect the abduction incident with the compensation narrative that follows it.

When reading Idunn, it is best not to narrow youth into a matter of appearance. Her apples mean the resilience, rhythm, and replenishment of life that keep a community alive and in motion.
The fact that the gods age when she disappears tells us that the absence of care is also the absence of strength.
Idunn is quiet, but she is a central axis that holds the life rhythm of the community.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Are Idunn's apples apples of immortality?**

A. They are understood as apples that maintain the gods' youth. Rather than complete immortality, they symbolize ongoing regeneration and vitality.

**Q. Why did the gods age when Idunn disappeared?**

A. The story shows a structure in which the gods' youth is not their natural state, but depends on Idunn's apples.
