## What is emotional intelligence?

Emotional Intelligence (EQ) is the ability to recognize, understand, and effectively manage your own emotions and the emotions of others. Daniel Goleman argued that EQ plays a bigger role in career success than IQ does.

Its influence is especially visible in roles built on interpersonal skill — leadership, teamwork, negotiation, and conflict resolution.

---

## The 5 domains of EQ (Goleman's model)

### 1. Self-Awareness

The ability to recognize your own emotions in real time. Asking "why am I feeling this right now?" is where EQ starts.

### 2. Self-Regulation

Managing emotions instead of acting on them impulsively — waiting ten seconds when you're angry, or reframing a negative thought, are both examples.

### 3. Motivation

Acting from internal goals rather than external rewards.

### 4. Empathy

Understanding other people's emotions and perspectives — this includes both cognitive empathy (understanding) and emotional empathy (feeling with someone).

### 5. Social Skills

The ability to build and maintain relationships and exert influence — communication, collaboration, and conflict resolution all fall here.

---

## How to raise your EQ

| Domain | Practice |
|---|---|
| Self-awareness | Keep an emotion journal — write down 3 emotions a day and trace their source |
| Self-regulation | Identify your emotional triggers, then build a response routine |
| Empathy | Active listening — understand before you advise |
| Social skills | Practice nonviolent communication (NVC) |

<Callout type="tip">
EQ is trainable. The goal isn't suppressing or ignoring emotion — it's accurately recognizing it and putting it to use.
</Callout>

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Does high EQ always mean someone is a good person?**
A: No. EQ is separate from morality. Some people with high EQ skillfully manipulate others for their own benefit (sometimes called "dark EQ"). High EQ is a tool — what it's used for depends on values.

**Q: Which matters more, IQ or EQ?**
A: It depends on the field. Technical and analytical roles lean more on IQ, while leadership, service, and collaborative roles find EQ the stronger predictor.
