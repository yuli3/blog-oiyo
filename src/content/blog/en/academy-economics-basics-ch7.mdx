# Introduction to Economics — GDP Is Final Production Measured in Markets, Not Welfare

Macroeconomics does not start from "a country's report card." It starts from **final goods and services, valued at market prices, produced within a territory over a given period**. What is included and excluded is where interview questions set their traps.

## 1. Expenditure, production and income look at the same circular flow from three sides

<FormulaBox
  label="Expenditure approach"
  formula="Y = C + I + G + (X-M)"
  description="C is household consumption, I equipment, construction and inventory changes, G government consumption and investment (excluding transfers), and X−M net exports."
/>

If wheat is bought to make bread, the wheat is an intermediate good and is not added to GDP directly; it is already in the price of the bread. Wheat left over in stock is counted as investment (an increase in inventories). Second-hand goods were not produced in the period, so only the dealer's margin may be counted as a service. Transfers (unemployment benefits) are not G.

Nominal GDP in year 1: 10 apples × 1,000 won + 5 pears × 2,000 won = 20,000 won. Year 2: 12 × 1,200 + 5 × 2,200 = 25,400 won. Real GDP in year 2 at base-year prices is $12×1000+5×2000$ = 22,000 won. The growth rate is 10%, not 27%.

<FormulaBox
  label="GDP deflator"
  formula="\text{Deflator} = \text{Nominal GDP} / \text{Real GDP} \times 100"
  description="Here 25400/22000 × 100 ≈ 115.5. Prices rose about 15.5% and quantities 10%."
/>

The CPI is the consumer price of a fixed basket; the deflator reflects the current composition of all domestic production. Imported consumer goods are in the CPI but weigh little in the deflator. "What is inflation?" is a question of which index you choose.

## 2. What GDP does not count

Household production, environmental damage, leisure, distribution and black markets are left out or wrongly included. If pollution creates clean-up spending, GDP can rise. Do not read an announcement of growth as a statement about improved welfare.

Potential GDP is the level of output that can be produced without accelerating inflation. If actual GDP is below it, there is a (negative) output gap; if above, overheating. The cycle is fluctuation around a trend; if causes that differ each time (demand shocks, supply shocks) are all called "recession," policy goes wrong. Stimulating demand in a supply-shock recession only raises prices.

## 3. Unemployment is a separate flow, not the flip side of growth

The unemployment rate is job seekers as a share of the labour force. If people stop looking for work, the numerator and denominator both shrink and the rate can look lower. The employment rate corrects that illusion. Okun's law is an empirical correlation, not an identity.

<Callout type="warning">
Do not write "the economy is doing well" in a year of high nominal growth. If oil prices raise import prices, nominal figures swell while real output may stall. Conversely, during deflation a nominal wage freeze is a real wage rise. Always look at the price index as well.
</Callout>

Gross national income (GNI) adds net factor income received from abroad. For a country receiving large dividends from overseas factories, GNI may be more meaningful than GDP. Choosing the indicator is already part of the analysis.

## Check your understanding

Are 10 trillion won of government employees' wages and 10 trillion won of unemployment benefits both included in GDP? Wages are government consumption (production), so they are included; unemployment benefits are transfers, so they are not. If recipients spend the money, it is counted indirectly through C. Adding both 10 trillions to G is double counting and misclassification.
