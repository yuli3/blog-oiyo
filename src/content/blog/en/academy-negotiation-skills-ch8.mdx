## Real negotiation starts with small adjustments

Most people picture negotiation as something reserved for big contracts or a salary review. But real negotiation skill is built in small conversations. Rescheduling a meeting, shifting your work priorities, deciding a family budget, asking for a refund — all of these are negotiation practice too.

What matters isn't winning every single time. It's building the habit of calmly sorting out what you want, what the other side is worried about, and what terms both of you could actually agree to.

## The 5-minute prep routine

| Step | Question |
|------|------|
| Goal | What outcome do I want to get? |
| Limit | What terms are unacceptable to me? |
| Alternative | What can I do if we don't reach an agreement? |
| The other side | What's likely to matter most to them? |
| Options | What can I actually adjust — price, timing, scope, or quality? |

Even for a short negotiation, running through this table in your head makes what you say noticeably steadier. Preparation isn't about spending a lot of time — it's about not losing track of the variables that matter.

## An example at work

Saying "I have too much on my plate" can come across as a complaint. Say instead, "I won't be able to finish both A and B this week — can I get A done by Friday and hold B until next Tuesday?" and it becomes a choice you're offering. Negotiation language is the skill of turning a feeling into a request.

## Principles to hold onto in the moment

First, don't concede without a standard to justify it. Second, summarize what the other person said to cut down on misunderstanding. Third, propose a package of terms instead of a single condition. Fourth, confirm the agreement one more time at the end. Just these four habits noticeably raise the quality of your everyday negotiations.

## Key takeaways

- Negotiation skill grows more from small everyday adjustments than from massive deals.
- A quick check on your goal, limit, alternative, the other side, and your options steadies the conversation.
- Turn emotional statements into concrete requests and choices.
- Once you reach an agreement, always confirm the terms and the next steps.

For your next round of practice, try turning this series' principles into real dialogue you can rehearse — that's the fastest way to build genuine negotiation instinct.
