# FPS Aim Training Guide

In Valorant, Overwatch, Apex Legends, CS, PUBG, and other FPS games, a lot of fights still come down to **aim**. Positioning, utility, timing, and team play all matter, but when the duel starts, your crosshair has to land on the target quickly and cleanly. The good news is that aim is not just talent. It is a trainable skill. This guide breaks aim into the parts that actually matter and shows you how to practice them without wasting hours.

## Aim Is Not One Skill - It Is Four

When people say someone has "good aim," they are usually mixing several skills together. Training gets much more efficient when you separate them.

1. **Flicking** - The ability to snap your crosshair onto an enemy who appears far from your current aim point. This is the skill behind quick headshots, sudden peeks, and fast target acquisition.
2. **Tracking** - The ability to keep your crosshair on a moving opponent. It matters most for sustained fire, spray control, and heroes or weapons that reward continuous damage.
3. **Target switching / Gridshot** - The ability to move between multiple targets quickly and reset your aim after each hit. This affects speed, target priority, and how well you handle chaotic fights.
4. **Precision aiming** - The ability to make small, controlled corrections onto tiny hitboxes. This is what turns near-misses into headshots.

If you do not know which one is weakest, you may end up practicing the wrong thing. Start by measuring each skill separately.

## A 5-10 Minute Daily Warm-Up Routine

Professional players warm up before scrims and matches, but the routine does not need to be long. Consistency matters more than volume.

- **1-2 minutes of gridshot** - Wake up your eyes and hand. Focus on rhythm first, not raw speed.
- **2-3 minutes of flicks** - Hit one target at a time, keeping accuracy above 80% before increasing speed.
- **2-3 minutes of tracking** - Follow moving targets smoothly. Pay attention to relaxed arm and wrist movement.
- **1-2 minutes of precision** - Finish with small targets to sharpen micro-corrections.

The key is **short, focused practice every day**. Ten minutes a day builds muscle memory better than one exhausted two-hour session once a week.

## Mouse Sensitivity Settings

- **Consistency is everything.** If your sensitivity changes across games, devices, or practice tools, your muscle memory will not settle. Pick a setup and keep it stable.
- **Low to medium sensitivity is a strong default.** Many high-level players use larger arm movement for big turns and wrist movement for fine adjustments.
- Practice with **the same sensitivity you use in-game**. That is how aim-training gains transfer into ranked matches.

## Common Mistakes

- **Chasing speed while ignoring accuracy** - If your accuracy drops below 80%, slow down. Repeating sloppy shots teaches bad habits.
- **Queueing ranked with no warm-up** - It is better to spend five minutes warming up than to use your first two matches as practice rounds.
- **Ignoring your setup** - Desk space, chair height, monitor position, mouse grip, and stable frame rate all affect aim. Your mechanics are tied to your environment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Does aim training actually help in ranked games?**
Yes, especially for reaction time, flicking, tracking, and warm-up consistency. But aim alone will not carry every match. Crosshair placement, pre-aiming, movement, positioning, and decision-making are what turn better mechanics into better rank results.

**Q. How long should I practice each day?**
Five to ten minutes is enough for a warm-up. Short, focused sessions are easier to maintain and usually more useful than grinding until your hand is tired.

**Q. Is it okay to change mouse sensitivity often?**
It is not recommended. Every major sensitivity change forces you to rebuild your feel for distance and correction. Unless something is clearly wrong, keep one setup for a while.
