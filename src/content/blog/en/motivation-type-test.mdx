## What Is Self-Determination Theory (SDT)?

**Self-Determination Theory**, developed by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan, is one of the most influential theories of human motivation. Rather than splitting motivation simply into "intrinsic" and "extrinsic," it places motivation along a spectrum based on **degree of autonomy**.

---

## The Spectrum of Motivation

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Amotivation | No intention or motivation at all | "I don't even know why I have to do this." |
| External regulation | Driven by reward or punishment | "I only do it for the money." |
| Introjected regulation | Driven by avoiding guilt or shame | "I'd be embarrassed if I didn't." |
| Identified regulation | Driven by a sense that it's worthwhile | "I exercise because it's good for my health." |
| Integrated regulation | Aligned with one's identity | "This is just who I am." |
| Intrinsic motivation | The activity itself is enjoyable | "I do it because it's genuinely fun." |

## Building Sustainable Motivation

### Increase Autonomy

Simply having a choice raises intrinsic motivation. Try shifting your language from "I have to" to "I choose to."

### Build a Sense of Competence

An appropriate level of challenge — not too easy, not too hard — boosts both a sense of accomplishment and intrinsic motivation. It's the same principle behind a state of Flow.

### Find Relatedness

Motivation lasts longer when you're not doing it alone. Study groups, workout partners, and communities all play a powerful role in sustaining motivation.

<Callout type="note">
Extrinsic motivation isn't inherently bad. The "crowding-out effect," where external rewards undermine intrinsic motivation, mainly occurs when a reward is added to an activity you already enjoy.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: I find it really hard to feel intrinsically motivated about my job. What can I do?**
A: The work itself may not align with your values. That said, you can find meaning by reframing your role (job crafting), requesting more autonomy, or focusing on the skills you're building.

**Q: How do I raise intrinsic motivation in a child who seems unmotivated?**
A: Acknowledging effort and process — rather than just praising — combined with giving them choices tends to be more effective. Use external rewards carefully, especially for activities the child already enjoys, since they can backfire.
