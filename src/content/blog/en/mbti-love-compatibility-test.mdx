## How MBTI Compatibility Actually Works

MBTI compatibility isn't a simple matter of "same types click" or "opposites attract." What matters is how each of the four dimensions — E/I, N/S, T/F, J/P — plays out between two specific people.

### Complementary Compatibility

This pairing happens when one partner's strengths fill in the other's gaps. A classic example is INTJ (strategy, vision) and ENFP (energy, creativity) — a pairing where each partner grows by making up for what the other lacks.

### Similarity-Based Stability

Sharing similar values and lifestyles tends to reduce conflict. Pairs like ISFJ–ISTJ or ENFP–INFP, who share an axis (N/F or S/J), often experience less day-to-day friction.

## Common Type-Based Conflicts in Relationships

| Friction pattern | Root cause | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| J vs. P | Planning vs. spontaneity | Shared calendar + protected free time |
| T vs. F | Logic vs. emotion | A commitment to "I'll lead with empathy first" |
| E vs. I | Socializing vs. recharging | Respecting each other's need for space |
| N vs. S | Future-focus vs. present-focus | Setting short-term goals alongside a long-term vision |

<Callout type="note">
Any pairing can build a great relationship with effort. Treat your compatibility score as a starting point, not a verdict — real relationships run on communication and respect.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is dating hard for low-compatibility type pairs?**
A: Not necessarily. Couples who learn to understand and respect their differences often build especially strong relationships. Use your compatibility score to spot potential friction points early, not as a prediction of failure.

**Q: Does compatibility change if my MBTI type changes?**
A: MBTI can shift subtly as you grow, especially after stress or major life changes — it's worth retaking the assessment periodically.
