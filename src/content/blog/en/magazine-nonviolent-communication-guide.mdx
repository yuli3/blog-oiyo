## Every word is one of two things

The clinical psychologist **Marshall Rosenberg** left a bold insight:

> Every human utterance is ultimately one of two things — **please**, or **thank you**.

Even harsh, aggressive words carry, underneath, a clumsy request from an unmet need. Hearing them this way makes room to meet the other person with compassion: "Ah, they're asking for something right now." This one shift of perspective removes half of relationship stress. ([NVC definition →](https://wiki.oiyo.net/en/meaning-of-nonviolent-communication/))

## The four steps of NVC

NVC divides speaking into four steps.

### 1. Observation — facts, without evaluation
❌ "You're always irresponsible."
✅ "You came late to three meetings this week."

### 2. Feeling — real emotion, not a thought
❌ "I feel ignored." (that's an interpretation)
✅ "I felt anxious."

### 3. Need — the necessity behind the feeling
✅ "I want my time to be respected."

### 4. Request — concrete and doable
✅ "Next time, could you let me know in advance if you'll be late?"

Simply separating observation from evaluation, and feeling from interpretation, changes the temperature of a conversation.

## Six ways of talking that break connection

Conversely, NVC names ways of speaking that damage relationships:

**Judging · Blaming · Demanding · Comparing · Taking for granted · Rationalizing**

What they share is a focus on **the other's fault instead of their need**. Sentences that start with "Why do you always…" usually fall into this trap.

## Boundaries and compassion go together

Healthy conversation isn't endlessly accommodating the other person. As Gestalt therapist Fritz Perls put it — "I am I, and you are you." Each person owning their own life is the premise of genuine encounter. You can set boundaries and still listen with compassion. ([Gestalt prayer →](https://wiki.oiyo.net/en/meaning-of-gestalt-prayer/))

## Know your conflict style

People react differently to the same conflict. Avoiding, competing, compromising, collaborating — knowing your default pattern shows you what to adjust in conversation.
