### Introduction: The Surest Way to Build Muscles of Thought

"If you can't say it, you don't know it, and if you can't write it, your thoughts aren't organized."
Debate and Essay are high-level intellectual activities that train 'Control' and 'Regulation', the highest levels of metacognition.
Here are 11 activities to logically prove my thoughts, accept others' thoughts, and move toward better conclusions.

---

### 40. 2-Minute Pair Debate

It is a mini debate that is easiest to start.

- **Method:**
  1.  Face your partner.
  2.  Decide roles for Pros (A) and Cons (B) on the topic. (Decide by rock-paper-scissors)
  3.  A argues for 1 minute, and B refutes for 1 minute.
- **Point:** If roles are assigned, it becomes training to create logic even if it is different from one's usual thoughts.

### 41. Value Line Debate

It is an activity that visually shows one's position.

- **Method:**
  1.  Draw a long line on the classroom floor and set one end as 'Strongly Agree' and the other end as 'Strongly Disagree'.
  2.  Students stand at appropriate positions on the line according to their opinions. (Neutral is also possible)
  3.  You can move your position while talking or presenting to the person next to you why you stood at that position.
- **Point:** You can check friends with similar thoughts and friends with different thoughts at a glance.

### 42. Choice Thinker

It is a role-playing debate where you become a historical figure or philosopher.

- **Method:**
  1.  Present 4 thinkers or figures related to the topic. (e.g. Confucius, Mencius, Xunzi, Han Feizi)
  2.  Select a thinker you support and move to that group.
  3.  Interpret current problems and discuss solutions from that thinker's perspective.
- **Point:** Cultivates **[Empathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empathy)** ability to deeply understand others' perspectives.

### 43. Commenting Debate

Online comment culture was applied to offline education.

- **Method:**
  1.  Write your thoughts on the topic on a post-it and attach it to the blackboard or wall. (Original Post)
  2.  Read friends' posts, write contents to refute or empathize on a different colored post-it, and attach it below. (Comment)
  3.  Continue silent debate by attaching re-refutations (Replies) to comments.
- **Point:** Students who are afraid of speaking can also participate actively in writing.

### 44. Mini Group Debate

Share deep conversations with a select few.

- **Method:**
  1.  Form a group of 3~4 people.
  2.  Distribute roles such as Leader (Moderator), Recorder (Scribe), Praiser (Mood Maker), and Keeper (Timekeeper).
  3.  Discuss intensively for a set time and organize results.
- **Point:** Create a structure where everyone can participate without free riders.

### 45. Whole Class Debate

Everyone becomes a jury and makes a verdict.

- **Method:**
  1.  Select representative teams for Pros and Cons and hold a debate battle in the center.
  2.  The remaining students become jurors, listen to the logic of both sides, and take notes.
  3.  After the debate ends, jurors make a verdict (vote) and write the reasons.
- **Point:** Realize that logical validity is more important than simply speaking well.

### 46. Photo Standing Debate

Unfold creative arguments through images.

- **Method:**
  1.  Spread various pictures or photo cards on the desk.
  2.  Pick one photo that represents your current mood or thoughts on the topic.
  3.  Show the photo and say "The reason I chose this photo is~".
- **Point:** Intuitive images stimulate the brain to create rich stories.

### 47. Tinkerbell Discussion

Collect opinions in real time with digital tools.

- **Method:**
  1.  Upload a topic using the board function of **[Tinkerbell](https://www.tkbell.co.kr/)**.
  2.  Students upload their opinions and related materials (images, video links) with smartphones.
  3.  Evaluate and feedback on each other's opinions through 'Like' and comment functions.
- **Point:** You can verify collective intelligence accumulating like big data with your eyes.

### 48. Toulmin's 6-Step Argumentation

Use the model of **[Stephen Toulmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Toulmin)**, the standard of logical writing.

- **Method:** Write according to the following 6 steps.
  1.  **Data:** Facts that become grounds
  2.  **Claim:** My conclusion
  3.  **Warrant:** Principle connecting data and claim
  4.  **Backing:** Material supporting the warrant
  5.  **Rebuttal:** Expected opposing opinions and re-refutations
  6.  **Qualifier:** Adjusting the strength of the argument (Not necessarily ~)
- **Point:** The best training method to create a rigorous logical structure.

### 49. O-R-E-O MAP Harvard Writing

The 4-step persuasive writing formula that Harvard students use.

- **Method:**
  1.  **O (Opinion):** Argue the core opinion. ("I think ~.")
  2.  **R (Reason):** Give a reason. ("Because ~.")
  3.  **E (Example):** Give an example. ("For example ~.")
  4.  **O (Opinion/Offer):** Emphasize opinion and suggest. ("Therefore, we must ~.")
- **Point:** Becomes the basis of business writing that delivers the core briefly and powerfully.

### 50. Making an 8-Page Book

Make your own book with a piece of paper.

- **Method:**
  1.  Fold an A4 paper into 8 parts, cut the center, and make a mini-book.
  2.  Compose learned contents in storytelling format on page 1 (cover), pages 2-7 (content), and page 8 (back cover).
- **Point:** Taste the joy of creation by structuring learning contents and weaving them into a book.
