Nidhogg is described as a dragon or serpent dwelling beneath the roots of the World Tree, Yggdrasil. He is depicted gnawing at the roots of the tree and is associated with dark imagery related to the dead. If Yggdrasil symbolizes the order of the entire world, Nidhogg represents the force that incessantly corrodes that order from below.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

The core image of Nidhogg is that of him gnawing at the roots of Yggdrasil. While the World Tree is the central axis connecting gods, humans, and various realms, there is constant damage occurring beneath it. This scene demonstrates that cosmic order is not a fixed state of perfection, but a structure perpetually caught between care and threat.

Furthermore, Nidhogg is mentioned in a structure of opposition to the eagle at the top of the tree, with the squirrel Ratatoskr serving as the messenger relaying insults between the two. The scene where the top and the bottom—the noble and the subterranean resentment—exchange words reveals that the world is always in a cycle of tension, insult, and corrosion.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

Nidhogg is the archetype of the **shadow of the roots** and the **corroding dragon**. He is the image of resentment, corruption, and unresolved emotions that weaken a seemingly sound structure from below. In a modern context, this brings to mind the distrust accumulated beneath an organization's superficial achievements, unspoken wounds within a family, or long-standing anger within an individual's unconscious. Nidhogg serves as a warning that if the damage to the roots is ignored, even the greatest tree is not safe.

The Nidhogg archetype shows that problems do not arise only from above. The world of the roots is not easily visible, yet the damage there shakes the entire structure. In modern terms, he resembles things that continuously drain energy from beneath the surface, such as unrecorded debts, unresolved conflicts, or long-held shame. To see Nidhogg is to look at the root cause.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Nidhogg the same serpent as Jormungandr?**

A. No. While both possess the imagery of a serpent or dragon, Jormungandr is the serpent of the sea that encircles the world, whereas Nidhogg is the entity beneath the roots of the World Tree.

**Q. Why does Nidhogg gnaw at the roots?**

A. It can be understood as a symbolic scene demonstrating the forces of corrosion and death that operate continuously at the base of world order.
