## 1. Strategic HRM (SHRM) Overview

**SHRM (Strategic Human Resource Management)**: An approach that links human resource management with organizational strategy to create a sustainable competitive advantage.

### Traditional HRM vs SHRM

| Category | Traditional HRM | SHRM |
|----------|----------------|------|
| Focus | Administration & compliance | Strategic partnership |
| Time Horizon | Short-term & transactional | Long-term & transformational |
| Role | Administrative support | Participation in strategic decision-making |
| Control | Rule-based | Value- & commitment-based |
| Employee Relations | Cost center | Investment asset |

### Fit in SHRM
- **Vertical Fit**: Alignment between HR strategy and business strategy
- **Horizontal Fit**: Consistency among HR subsystems

## 2. Competency Model

**Competency**: A collection of behaviors, knowledge, skills, and attitudes shared by high-performing talent.

### Competency Types

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Core Competency | Common to all employees | Customer orientation, collaboration |
| Job Competency | Specialized competency by job role | Financial analysis, coding |
| Leadership Competency | For managers | Vision casting, coaching |

### Competency-Based Recruitment and Selection (CBRS)
Behavioral Event Interview (BEI) technique: "How did you resolve a team conflict in the past?" → STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

## 3. Talent Management

### Competition for Talent
McKinsey's "War for Talent": Core talent (A-players) account for a decisive share of performance.

**High Performer Management Principles**
- Differentiated compensation
- Assignment of challenging jobs
- Clear career paths provided
- Touchpoints with top leadership

### Succession Planning
An internal candidate development system to prepare for future vacancies in key positions.

**9-Box Grid**: A 9-cell matrix classifying talent by Performance (Low/Medium/High) × Potential (Low/Medium/High).

## 4. Advanced Motivation Theories

### Equity Theory (Adams)

Employees judge fairness by comparing their **input-to-output ratio** to a reference standard.

- **Under-reward**: Decrease inputs, increase outputs, or change the standard
- **Over-reward**: Increase inputs or cognitive reinterpretation

### Expectancy Theory (Vroom)

Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence

| Element | Question |
|---------|----------|
| Expectancy | Will effort lead to goal achievement? |
| Instrumentality | Will achieving the goal lead to rewards? |
| Valence | Are the rewards meaningful to me? |

### Reinforcement Theory (Skinner)

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Positive Reinforcement | Reward desirable behavior |
| Negative Reinforcement | Increase behavior by removing unpleasant stimuli |
| Extinction | Decrease behavior by stopping rewards |
| Punishment | Suppress behavior through unpleasant stimuli |

## 5. Diversity Management

Management that transforms diversity in gender, age, race, and cultural background into organizational strengths.

**Effects of Diversity Management**: Creative problem solving / Global market accessibility / Reflecting customer diversity / Attracting top talent

**Glass Ceiling**: An invisible barrier women and minorities face when advancing to senior career levels.

### Inclusive Leadership
Creating an environment where diverse members can contribute while maintaining their identities.

---

## 6. HR Outsourcing and Shared Services

**HR Outsourcing**: Contracting out non-core HR tasks such as recruitment, payroll, and training to external specialized agencies.
**Advantages of HR Outsourcing (HRO)**: Cost reduction, securing expertise, focus on core competencies.

**HR Shared Services Center (HR SSC)**: Centralizing and standardizing HR administrative tasks across multiple business units.
