## Stress relief = self-care

"Relieving stress" is vague. It becomes actionable only when you turn it into **concrete actions on three axes**. ([Self-care definition →](https://wiki.oiyo.net/en/meaning-of-self-care/))

### 1. Physical care
Sleep, food, movement, rest. A surprising share of what looks like an emotional problem is actually sleep deprivation and blood-sugar swings. If the physical signs of stress — headache, heartburn, neck and shoulder tension, sleep changes, hair loss, skin trouble — appear, bodily recovery comes first.

### 2. Mental care
This is about managing the quality of stimulation: an information diet, an absorbing hobby, learning. As the [Yerkes-Dodson law](https://wiki.oiyo.net/en/meaning-of-yerkes-dodson-law/) shows, the right amount of stimulation is itself a source of vitality.

### 3. Emotional / everyday care
Naming emotions, securing maskless time, spending time with people you love. Happiness research finds that **who you're with** weighs heavily on well-being.

## Your mind's protective response — defense mechanisms

Under stress we unconsciously use defense mechanisms to protect the self. This isn't bad — **everyone uses them**. But noticing which ones you rely on creates room to choose a more mature approach.

- **Mature** — sublimation (channeling urges into creation or exercise), humor, suppression
- **Neurotic** — rationalization, repression, reaction formation
- **Immature** — projection, regression
- **Psychotic** — denial

The more intense the stress, the easier it is to slip into immature mechanisms. When the thought "that person hates me" (projection) arises, simply knowing it may be a defense mechanism lets you step back. ([Defense mechanisms →](https://wiki.oiyo.net/en/meaning-of-defense-mechanisms/))

## Relief and avoidance are different

This is the crucial distinction. **Binge eating, heavy drinking, endless scrolling** only let you forget for a moment while worsening the signals. Real relief actually restores the state of body and mind.

## How do you know it's relieved?

The standard is less your subjective mood and more **the change in signals**.

1. Has your **sleep quality** returned?
2. Has **physical tension** eased?
3. Has your **emotional range** recovered — neither numb nor over-reactive?
4. Do you find yourself **wanting to do things again**?

The fourth matters most. The opposite of apathy isn't pleasure — it's **the desire to do**.

## Check your state now

Move past a vague feeling and start with measurement.

👉 [Holmes-Rahe stress test](https://oiyo.net/en/stress-events/test) · [Burnout test](https://oiyo.net/en/burnout/test) · [Emotion diary](https://oiyo.net/en/emotion-diary)

Stress isn't an enemy to eliminate but a lifelong companion to manage. The goal isn't zero, but a **sweet spot** — and a **rhythm of recovery** that brings you back when you fall.
