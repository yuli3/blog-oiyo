### Introduction: Is Saving the Purchase Price Enough?

Alex had been diligently saving for years, dreaming of owning a home for the first time. When he finally found the perfect $400,000 condo and was about to sign the contract, his real estate agent dropped a bombshell: "You'll need roughly $4,400 more for taxes." He had been so focused on the purchase price that he had forgotten about the **acquisition tax** lurking in the background.

Acquisition tax is a local tax paid to the municipality when you acquire real estate. The larger the purchase amount, the heavier the burden. Recently, the rates have grown increasingly complex depending on whether you already own property and the location of the home, making an accurate calculation essential. Today we will cover everything you need to know about acquisition tax — something you must master alongside your mortgage size before you sign on the dotted line.

<h2>2. Acquisition Tax Structure and Key Tax-Saving Points</h2>

<h3>① Understanding the Tiered Rate System (1–3%)</h3>
The acquisition tax rate on residential property ranges from 1% to 3% depending on the purchase price. In the past, crossing certain price thresholds caused a sudden jump in the effective rate — a sharp "cliff effect" — but the system has since been reformed to calculate tax in a smooth, proportional manner. Even a small difference in price can meaningfully change the tax amount, so keep this in mind when negotiating the purchase price.

<h3>② The 85 m² (National Housing Size) Threshold</h3>
Whether the dedicated floor area exceeds 85 m² determines whether the **Special Rural Development Tax (0.2%)** applies. If you prefer a larger unit, budget for this additional tax on top of the purchase price. Conversely, choosing an apartment under 85 m² lets you enjoy a tax exemption on the special rural development tax, delivering a tangible saving.

<h3>③ First-Time Homebuyer Tax Reduction</h3>
If your household is buying its very first home, you must claim the policy-backed tax benefit. Currently, for homes priced at $800,000 or below (equivalent threshold), first-time buyers can receive a reduction of up to $1,350 in acquisition tax. Check the eligibility requirements in advance to protect your hard-earned assets.

## 3. Key Cautions When Paying Acquisition Tax

1.  **Within 60 days of the balance payment date**: Acquisition tax must be filed and paid within 60 days of the balance payment date (or the registration date). Missing the deadline triggers heavy penalties, so be careful.
2.  **Multi-homeowner surcharges**: Depending on whether the area is a designated adjustment zone and how many homes you currently own, the acquisition tax rate can rise to 8% or even 12%. Once you are buying a second home, consulting a professional is essential.
3.  **Budget for ancillary costs**: In addition to acquisition tax, remember that attorney fees, revenue stamps, and national housing bond purchases typically add about 0.3–0.5% of the purchase price.
