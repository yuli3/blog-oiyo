In Egyptian mythology, Thoth is the god who governs writing and wisdom, measurement and calculation, and the order of the moon. He is depicted with the head of an ibis or in the form of a baboon, and appears as the scribe of the gods and a mediator of disputes. In Thoth's world, speech and writing are not merely records, but powers that organize reality and establish order.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Thoth's representative role is to record and mediate disputes among the gods. When Horus and Set contend over kingship, Thoth appears as an intellectual figure who manages the language of judgment and procedure. Rather than fighting through force, he seeks to restore order through logic, documents, testimony, and rules.

In another important tradition, Thoth appears as the god of the moon and time calculation. If the sun represents the order of the day, the moon is the marker that measures the changes and cycles of the night. Thoth is the god who captures invisible rhythms in numbers and letters, making possible the order of agriculture, ritual, and the calendar.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Thoth is the archetype of the **sage** and the **scribe**. Rather than trying to become king himself, he records the world so that it remains in an intelligible structure. In modern terms, he resembles researchers, lawyers, accountants, editors, and data analysts: people who organize complex events through language and systems.

The key to reading Thoth is that knowledge is not a neutral ornament, but an act of creating order. Records preserve memory, but they also decide what will be kept and what will be deleted. For this reason, the Thoth archetype demands both accuracy and responsibility. Good intelligence does not withdraw from reality; it is the power to transform confusion into a form that can be read.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Thoth the god who invented writing?**

A. In Egyptian tradition, Thoth was regarded as the patron of writing and wisdom. For this reason, he held an important place as the guardian of scribes, learning, and calculation.

**Q. Why is Thoth connected with the moon?**

A. The moon symbolizes cycles, measurement, and the light of night. Thoth's qualities of calculation and order fit well with the temporality of the moon, which is why they became connected.
