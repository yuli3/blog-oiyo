# Me in Social Space: Social Psychology and Group Dynamics

"Man is a social animal." — Aristotle's proposition is the most fundamental premise in psychology. We can become **completely different people** when we are alone versus when we are in a group. Today, we dissect the reasons behind this through the classic experiments of social psychology.

<Callout type="note">
  **Applying Cognitive Dissonance in Marketing and Politics**: After purchasing an expensive product, consumers search only for positive information about the product to justify their purchase decision. This is called **Post-purchase Rationalization**. Advertisers exploit this by intensively exposing messages such as "Your choice was right" immediately after purchase.
</Callout>

## 4. Morality in Groups: The Bystander Effect

<LectureBarChart
  title="Number of Witnesses and Probability of Helping Behavior (%)"

**How to Secure Help in a Crisis**: To overcome the bystander effect, you must **point out a specific person**. Saying "Someone please help" is far less effective than saying "You in the red jacket, please call 119!"

## 📖 References
- **[Eichmann in Jerusalem] by Hannah Arendt**: A masterpiece connecting the Milgram experiment and real-world history through the concept of the 'banality of evil'.
- **[Opening Skinner's Box] by Lauren Slater**: 10 experiments that changed the history of psychology, including the Milgram, Asch, and Rosenhan experiments.
- **[Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion] by Robert Cialdini**: A classic outlining the 6 laws of persuasion, such as social proof, authority, and liking.
