Valkyries are not a single person, but a group of female beings who choose those who will die on the battlefield. The name itself is understood to mean "chooser of the slain," and they are connected to Odin as they select the warriors who will go to Valhalla. They are not beautiful decorations of battle, but fearsome beings who carry out selection at the boundary between life, death, and honor.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

The core function of the Valkyries is to choose who will die and who will survive on the battlefield, and among the dead, who will become Odin's warriors. From a human perspective this choice is cruel, but within the structure of myth it is also the selection of fallen warriors in preparation for Ragnarok.

In later heroic traditions, Valkyrie-like figures such as Brynhildr become entangled in stories of love, oaths, and betrayal. In these stories, the Valkyrie appears not simply as a servant of the gods, but as a female warrior with her own judgment and emotions. Scenes where the fate of war collides with human love make the image of the Valkyrie even more complex.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

The Valkyrie is an archetype of the **messenger of death** and the **warrior goddess**. She does not stand only on the side of destruction; she is a mediator who carries death into a field of meaning. In modern terms, she resembles the moment when someone must calmly decide who will take on which responsibility in a crisis, or the psychological process of turning an ended experience into training for the next stage. The Valkyrie reveals the double nature of being chosen: it is glory, and at the same time the loss of life.

The Valkyrie archetype does not speak only of romantic heroism. To be chosen also means to be separated from a safe life. In modern terms, it resembles the moment of being called to a major project, a dangerous mission, or public responsibility. That call brings pride, but it also demands personal peace in return. The Valkyrie shows that honor and sacrifice can enter through the same door.

## Related Figures

Reading [Odin](/en/meaning-of-myth-odin/), [Freya](/en/meaning-of-myth-freya/), and [the Norns](/en/meaning-of-myth-norns/) alongside this entry connects the imagery of chosen death and fate.

When reading the Valkyries, we should look less at the romance of battle and more at the coldness of selection. They are not beings who decorate death beautifully, but beings who show how death is incorporated into an order.
In this sense, the Valkyrie is closer to a figure who reveals the arrangement of fate than one who represents victory.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Are Valkyries like angels?**

A. Their appearance was romanticized in later periods, but their original function is the selection of fallen warriors and the guidance of death. It is difficult to say they are simply the same as angels.

**Q. Is Valkyrie the name of one person?**

A. No. Valkyrie is a collective name, and it includes beings with many names and individual traditions.
