## What Is a Paralegal / Legal Document Professional?

A **Paralegal** (also called a legal assistant) works under the supervision of a licensed attorney to perform substantive legal work — drafting documents, researching law, preparing filings, and managing client matters. In most US states, paralegals cannot represent clients in court or give independent legal advice, but their work is the backbone of legal service delivery.

A closely related role is the **Legal Document Assistant (LDA)** or **Document Preparation Professional**, recognized in states like California, which allows non-attorneys to prepare legal forms for self-represented clients under strict regulatory guidelines.

**National credentialing bodies**:
- **NALA** (National Association of Legal Assistants) — Certified Paralegal (CP) credential
- **NFPA** (National Federation of Paralegal Associations) — PACE (Paralegal Advanced Competency Exam)
- **NALS** — Professional paralegal designations (PP, PLS)

**Core work areas**: Real estate document preparation, court filing, estate planning documents, corporate formations, contract drafting, litigation support

---

## Eligibility Requirements

### NALA Certified Paralegal (CP)

The most widely recognized paralegal credential in the US:

**Pathways to eligibility**:
1. Graduation from an ABA-approved paralegal program
2. Graduation from a bachelor's degree program in paralegal studies
3. Bachelor's degree in any field + 1 year paralegal experience under attorney supervision
4. Associate degree in paralegal studies + 2 years experience
5. High school diploma / GED + 7 years paralegal experience

**Disqualifying factors**:
- Criminal convictions for felonies involving moral turpitude
- Misrepresentation on the application

### NFPA PACE Exam

Requires:
- Associate's degree + 6 years paralegal experience, OR
- Bachelor's degree + 4 years paralegal experience, OR
- Paralegal certificate (ABA-approved) + 6 years experience

## Exam Structure

### NALA CP Exam

| Component | Format | Topics |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Section 1 | Multiple choice | Communications, ethics, legal research |
| Section 2 | Multiple choice | Human relations and interviewing |
| Section 3 | Multiple choice | Judgment and analytical ability |
| Section 4 | Multiple choice | Legal terminology and substantive law |
| Section 5 | Multiple choice | Paralegal skills in specific practice areas |

**Total**: Approximately 200 questions  
**Duration**: 5 hours  
**Passing score**: 70% or higher on each section

Practice areas tested include: civil litigation, real estate, corporate law, criminal law, probate and estate administration, contracts, and family law.

### NFPA PACE Exam

- 200 multiple-choice questions
- 4 hours
- Tests advanced legal knowledge across substantive law and paralegal skills
- Scaled score required (500 or above on a 200–800 scale)

## Subject Areas and Study Strategy

### Civil Litigation

The largest practice area for paralegals by employment volume. Key topics:

- Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (FRCP) — complaint, answer, motions practice
- Discovery: interrogatories, depositions, document requests, e-discovery
- Trial preparation: exhibit lists, witness summaries, trial binders
- Post-trial: appeals, enforcement of judgments

### Real Estate Document Work

This is the US functional equivalent of Japanese or Korean judicial scrivener work — the closest parallel to property registration document preparation:

- **Purchase and sale agreements** — drafting and reviewing
- **Title insurance**: title search, commitment, and policy issuance process
- **Deed preparation**: warranty deeds, quitclaim deeds, special warranty deeds
- **Mortgage documents**: promissory notes, deeds of trust / mortgages
- **Closing disclosure (CD)** and HUD-1 settlement statement preparation
- **RESPA** (Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act) compliance
- **Recording**: county recorder / register of deeds procedures for document filing

Real estate paralegals at title companies and real estate law firms handle the majority of these tasks under attorney supervision.

### Corporate Law and Business Formations

- Articles of incorporation / organization filing (Secretary of State)
- LLC operating agreements and corporate bylaws
- Annual report filings and registered agent maintenance
- Mergers and acquisitions due diligence support
- Securities compliance documentation

### Probate and Estate Administration

- Wills, trusts, powers of attorney, and advance directives
- Probate petition filing and estate administration
- Inventory and appraisal of estate assets
- Distribution to beneficiaries

### Criminal Law

- Bail motions, plea agreements, sentencing memoranda
- Public defender support work
- Post-conviction work (expungements, appeals)

### Ethics and Professional Responsibility

Heavily tested on both the CP and PACE exams:

- Unauthorized practice of law (UPL) — what paralegals can and cannot do
- Confidentiality and attorney-client privilege
- Conflicts of interest
- Supervision and competency standards (ABA Model Guidelines)

## Study Timeline by Background

### ABA-Approved Paralegal Program Graduate

**6 months to 1 year** of focused exam preparation.

- Complete all program requirements, then begin CP exam preparation
- Use NALA's online study resources and practice exams
- Focus on weak practice areas identified in program coursework

### Career-Changer with Bachelor's Degree

**1–2 years** including experience accumulation.

- Complete an ABA-approved paralegal certificate program (typically 6–12 months)
- Work under attorney supervision for 1+ years before sitting the CP exam
- Study in parallel during the experience period

### Long-Term Experience Path

Paralegals with 7+ years of experience can sit the CP exam without a formal paralegal degree. This path is common for legal secretaries and administrative staff who transition into paralegal roles.

## Career Paths After Certification

### Law Firm Paralegal

The most common path. Real estate, corporate, litigation, and family law firms employ large numbers of paralegals:

- **Income structure**: Salary-based, with billing rates (typically $100–$175/hour billed to clients, with paralegal receiving $50,000–$90,000 in salary)
- Real estate closing volume directly drives demand — active real estate markets increase paralegal employment

### Title Company and Escrow

Title insurance companies (First American, Fidelity National Title, Old Republic) employ large paralegal and title examiner teams. Work centers on title searches, commitments, and closing document preparation.

### Corporate Legal Department

Fortune 500 companies maintain in-house legal teams with paralegal support for contracts, IP, compliance, and litigation management.

### Court Administration and Public Sector

Federal and state courts, the DOJ, Legal Aid, and public defender offices hire paralegals. Pay is lower than private sector but benefits and job security are strong.

### Independent Legal Document Assistance (California Model)

In California, registered **Legal Document Assistants (LDAs)** can provide document preparation services directly to self-represented clients without attorney supervision — a unique independent practice model that partially mirrors the judicial scrivener role in Japan/Korea.

States like Arizona (document preparer program) and Florida have similar frameworks for non-attorney document preparation services.

---

## Paralegal vs. Attorney vs. Legal Document Assistant

| Role | Credential | Court Representation | Independent Client Service |
|------|-----------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| Attorney | JD + Bar | Yes — full | Yes |
| Certified Paralegal (CP) | NALA CP / NFPA PACE | No (supervised only) | No |
| LDA (California) | State registration | No | Yes — forms only |
| Legal Secretary | None required | No | No |

For document preparation (deeds, corporate filings, estate documents): the paralegal or LDA path is efficient and cost-effective. For litigation representation: only an attorney can appear in court.
