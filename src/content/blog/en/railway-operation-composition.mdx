### Introduction: Order on Tracks: The World of Operation Rules

If the Railway Safety Act is the "Law" in a broad framework, then the **'Railway Vehicle Operation Rules'** are practical **'Guidelines'** that prescribe how trains are actually made (composition), at what speed they should run, and in what order they should stop. These rules, prescribed by Ordinance of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, are the most specific promises that engineers or controllers face every moment in the field.

Today, we will summarize the essence of these operation rules, which are essential for anyone interested in the technical side of the South Korean rail industry.

---

### 1. Train Composition

Making a train is more than coupling cars. There is a strict order for safety and efficiency.

- **Power car position (Art. 11):** The power car used for driving is coupled at **the front of the train** as a rule. Exceptions include two or more locomotives controlled from the front, helper locomotives, breakdowns, and rescue, snow-clearing, works and test trains.
- **Brakes (Art. 14):** Trains have brakes that act on every car together and stop the cars automatically if they separate.
- **Braking axle ratio (Art. 15(2)):** Trains are composed so the ratio of braked axles to coupled axles is **100**. If some cars' brakes fail, speed is reduced according to that ratio (Art. 15(3)).

### 2. Train Speed (Articles 34 and 35)

The rules do not set numbers for maximum or limited speeds. **Railway operators set and apply them.**

- **Maximum speed (Art. 34):** Set by the operator for each route and vehicle, within the design limits of track and rolling stock.
- **Cases needing a separate speed limit (Art. 35):** Caution-signal sections, **propelling (pushing) operation** (except when controlled from the front), **reverse running**, facing an unlocked switch, **shunting**, pilot working, hand-signal sections, dispatcher-directed running, manned running in driverless sections, etc.
- **Exam trap:** Numbers such as 'propelling at 25 km/h' are not in these rules. Do not learn an operator's internal figures as legal ones.

### 3. Brake Test (Article 17)

Before a train departs, the key step is checking that the brakes work.

- **When:** Whenever a train is **composed** or its **composition changes**, before it runs. Detaching or attaching cars and changing locomotives count as composition changes.
- **What:** Confirm the brakes work normally. Detailed methods, such as checking service and emergency braking, pressure and actual vehicle response, are in the operator's work standards.

### 4. Reverse Running and Propelling Operation

Trains normally run forward, but sometimes they move backward (reverse running) or are pushed from behind (propelling).

- **Reverse running (Art. 26):** **Prohibited as a rule.** Exceptions are track, overhead line or vehicle failures, works, rescue and snow-clearing trains that need it, helper locomotives at the rear, and railway accidents. Even then, the train must not hinder other trains.
- **Driving position (Art. 13):** Trains are driven from the cab of the front car in the running direction, with exceptions such as driving while signaling from the front.
- **Propelling:** A brake van goes at the front (Art. 16(1)) and a separate speed limit applies (Art. 35). Because visibility is poor, someone signaling from the front is standard practice.

### Wrapping Up: Rules Become Safety Only When Followed

It is no exaggeration to say that operation rules are 'rules written in blood' created after numerous experiences of accidents and technical reviews. Familiarizing yourself with these rules is not just about knowing the law, but the process of preparing to take responsibility for the lives of passengers as a rail professional.

**A Solid Foundation: Study conditions such as 'braking axle ratio 100', 'brake test whenever the composition is made or changed', 'reverse running prohibited with exceptions' and 'cases needing a separate speed limit (Art. 35)'. Check whether any km/h figure not in the rules is an operator's internal standard. Especially, distinguishing who the approval authority is (Controller vs. Station Master) by situation is key to understanding the professional hierarchy. Persistence in reading the core provisions repeatedly until the rules become second nature will build your technical foundation for the industry.**
