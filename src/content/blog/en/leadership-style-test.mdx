## The 6 Leadership Styles

According to Daniel Goleman's research, leadership styles fall into six broad categories. The best leaders can move fluidly between several of them depending on the situation.

| Style | Core Message | Best Used When |
|---|---|---|
| Visionary | "Come with me" | A clear direction is needed during change |
| Coaching | "Try this" | Developing team members is the priority |
| Affiliative | "People come first" | Healing team conflict, building trust |
| Democratic | "What do you think?" | Consensus or a range of ideas is needed |
| Pacesetting | "Do it my way, to a high standard" | A high-performing team needs fast results |
| Commanding | "Do this right now" | A crisis or emergency situation |

---

## Switching Styles by Situation

Effective leaders don't stick to one fixed style — they read the situation and adjust.

### In a Crisis

A commanding style drives immediate action. But once the crisis passes, switching quickly to an affiliative or democratic style is what keeps team morale intact.

### As Team Members Grow

New or inexperienced team members respond best to coaching. More experienced team members do better with a democratic or delegating style that builds autonomy.

<Callout type="note">
Pacesetting and commanding styles work well for short-term results, but overused, they can hurt team morale and increase turnover over time. Treat them as backup styles wherever possible.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can you actually change your leadership style?**
A: Yes. Leadership isn't an inborn trait — it's a trainable skill. That said, it's more realistic to start from your natural strength style and gradually expand your range from there.

**Q: Is this test useful if I'm not in a formal leadership role?**
A: Yes. Leadership skills apply to everyday work life too — influencing a team, driving a project, mentoring a junior colleague, and more.
