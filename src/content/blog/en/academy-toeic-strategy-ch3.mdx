## Single Passage Strategy

### Core Principle: Read the Questions First

Reading questions before the passage lets you search selectively rather than reading everything.

**Recommended sequence:**
1. Read all 3–5 questions for the set
2. Identify the document type (email? advertisement? notice?)
3. Skim the passage quickly to locate key information
4. For each question, re-read only the relevant section

### Document Structure Templates

**Email / letter:**
```
Sender / recipient header
Date
Subject line
─────────────────
Opening: purpose of the email
Body: key content, details
Closing: request, call to action
Signature
```

**Notice / memo:**
```
Heading
Date / recipient
─────────────────
Purpose (reason for notice)
Details
Instructions / warnings
Contact information
```

**Job advertisement:**
```
Company name / job title
Main responsibilities
Requirements / qualifications
How to apply
Benefits / conditions
```

## Question Type Strategies

### Type 1: Main Purpose / Topic

*"What is the main purpose of the email?"*  
*"What is the article mainly about?"*

**Strategy:**
- The answer is almost always in the opening paragraph
- Look for "I am writing to…" or "The purpose of this letter is…"
- Eliminate options that are too specific or too broad

---

### Type 2: Detail Questions

*"What does Ms. Brown offer to do?"*  
*"When will the shipment arrive?"*  
*"How much does the service cost?"*

**Strategy:**
- Identify the question keyword (name, date, amount)
- Scan the passage for that keyword
- Read carefully around it
- Watch for **paraphrasing** — the correct answer uses different words than the passage

**Common paraphrase patterns:**

| Passage says | Answer choice says |
|-------------|--------------------|
| send an email | contact by email |
| is not available | will be absent |
| at no extra cost | free of charge |
| prior to | before |
| personnel | staff / employees |
| approximately | about / around |
| require | need / must have |

---

### Type 3: NOT / True Questions

*"Which of the following is NOT mentioned?"*  
*"What is TRUE about the company?"*

**Strategy (most time-consuming type):**
- Check all four answer choices against the passage one by one
- The correct answer (for NOT) = information absent from the passage, or contradicted by it
- Use elimination: rule out choices confirmed in the passage
- **Time warning**: if time is short, skip and return; don't get stuck here

**Common traps:**
- Similar content appears in the passage but a detail is slightly different
- Confusion between two passages in double/triple sets

---

### Type 4: Inference Questions

*"What is suggested about the company?"*  
*"What can be inferred from the letter?"*  
*"What does the writer imply?"*

**Strategy:**
- The answer is not stated directly — you must draw a logical conclusion
- Combine information from multiple sentences
- Reject options that require outside knowledge or that go beyond what the text supports

---

### Type 5: Speaker / Audience Questions

*"Who most likely is the writer?"*  
*"Who is the intended audience of this notice?"*

**Strategy:**
- Look for profession-related vocabulary, job titles, and described tasks
- Check the first and last paragraphs — they usually contain the most clues

---

### Type 6: Vocabulary in Context

*"The word 'secure' in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to ___"*

**Strategy:**
- Always go back and re-read the **sentence containing the word**
- Choose the meaning that fits the context, not the dictionary definition

- "secure a deal" → **obtain** (not "safe")
- "address a concern" → **handle / resolve** (not "a postal address")

---

### Type 7: Sentence Insertion

*"In which position [1][2][3][4] does the following sentence best belong?"*

**Strategy:**
1. Read the sentence to be inserted carefully — note pronouns and key ideas
2. Read the four candidate positions in the passage
3. The sentence must connect logically to what comes before AND after
4. Look for:
   - Pronoun referents ("it," "this," "they" — what do they refer to?)
   - Discourse connectors ("However," "Therefore," "In addition")
   - Chronological or cause-effect flow

## Double and Triple Passage Strategy

The core challenge: questions may require information from **two or three passages combined**.

```
Signal phrases for cross-passage questions:
"According to the email and the schedule…"
"Based on the advertisement and the letter…"
```

**Strategy:**
1. Read all questions first — note which ones require cross-referencing
2. Answer single-passage questions first
3. For cross-passage questions, identify which passages hold the relevant information
4. Avoid mixing up information between passages

## Time Management

```
Total RC time:   ~75 minutes

Allocation guide:
  Part 5 (30 Qs):  12–15 minutes (25–30 sec/question)
  Part 6 (16 Qs):  8–10 minutes (~3–4 min/text)
  Part 7 (54 Qs):  50–55 minutes remaining

Part 7 time per text type:
  Short single passage (2 questions): ~3 min
  Standard single passage (3–4 questions): ~5–6 min
  Double passage set: ~8 min
  Triple passage set: ~10–12 min
```

**Golden rule**: Guessing and moving on is always better than spending 5 minutes on one question.

## Practice Quiz

**Q1.** A detail question asks about a specific price. You scan the passage and find "complimentary with any purchase over $50." The answer choice says "free of charge for qualifying orders." Is this a match?

> Yes. "Complimentary" = free; "any purchase over $50" = "qualifying orders." This is a classic paraphrase. The answer is a match.

**Q2.** A NOT question has four answer choices. Three of them are confirmed in the passage. What is your answer strategy?

> Eliminate the three confirmed options by finding them in the text. The remaining option — the one you **cannot** find or that contradicts the passage — is the correct "NOT" answer.

**Q3.** In a sentence-insertion question, the sentence to be inserted begins "This decision…". What should you look for in the passage?

> A previous sentence that describes **a decision being made** — that sentence must appear immediately before the insertion point, so "This decision" has a clear referent.
