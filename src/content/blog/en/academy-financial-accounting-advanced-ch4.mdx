The last stage of financial statement analysis turns the results into **value**. The discounted cash flow (DCF) model is widely used, but there is also the **residual income model** (RIM), which starts directly from accounting numbers. With consistent assumptions, the two give the same value. This chapter uses the residual income model to see how accounting information connects to value.

## 1. Defining residual income

**Residual income** (abnormal earnings) is net profit minus the minimum return shareholders require, that is, the cost of equity.

$$
\text{RI}_t = \text{NI}_t - r \times B_{t-1} = (\text{ROE}_t - r) \times B_{t-1}
$$

$B_{t-1}$ is the opening book value of equity and $r$ the cost of equity. Residual income is positive only when ROE exceeds the cost of equity. Profit that falls short of the cost of equity destroys shareholder value.

## 2. The residual income model

Adding **clean surplus accounting** (change in equity = net profit − dividends, with no share issues) to the dividend discount model gives:

## 3. Estimating FA's value

FA's equity at the end of 20X2 is ₩50 billion and its cost of equity 10%. Assume ROE stays at 20% and residual income does not grow.

- Residual income for 20X3: $(20\% - 10\%) \times 500 = 50$ (₩5 billion)
- Value: $500 + 50 / 0.10 = 1{,}000$ (₩100 billion)
- Justified P/B: $1{,}000 / 500 = 2.0$ times

When ROE equals the cost of equity, P/B is 1: the company is worth only its book value. When ROE is below the cost of equity, P/B is below 1. That does not mean the shares are cheaper than liquidation value; it is the market's judgement that **continuing to use the capital destroys value**.

## 4. Expectations embedded in the market price

[Chapter 7](/en/academy-financial-statement-analysis-ch7/) assumed FA's market capitalization to be ₩80 billion. Working back the expected ROE embedded in this price, $800 = 500 + \text{RI} / 0.10$ gives $\text{RI} = 30$ (₩3 billion), an expected ROE of 16%. The market expects FA's ROE to fall from 20% to about 16%. [Chapter 3](/en/academy-accounting-basics-ch10/) showed that much of FA's ROE comes from financial leverage. The analyst judges whether this expectation is too pessimistic or appropriate, and makes an investment decision.

## 5. Relationship with P/E

P/E is price divided by earnings. Seen through the residual income model, P/E reflects **earnings growth** and the **persistence of abnormal returns**. When one-off gains inflate net profit, P/E looks low, but recalculated on normalized earnings ([chapter 6](/en/academy-financial-statement-analysis-ch6/)) it rises. Read P/B together with ROE, and P/E together with earnings growth.

<Callout type="warning">
Because the residual income model starts from accounting numbers, it is affected by accounting distortions. Conservative accounting (expensing R&D immediately) reduces book value and makes future ROE look higher; aggressive accounting does the opposite. The model is designed to give the same value regardless of accounting method, but with a short forecast horizon this offset does not fully occur. A 1-percentage-point difference in the cost of equity also changes value greatly, so present the result as a range under different assumptions, not a single number.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

FJ's equity is ₩200 billion and its cost of equity 8%. Next year's ROE is expected to be 12%, and residual income is assumed to grow by 2% a year thereafter. Find the equity value and justified P/B with the residual income model. If the current market capitalization is ₩240 billion, what residual income growth rate is the market assuming?

Next year's residual income is $(12\% - 8\%) \times 2{,}000 = 80$ (₩8 billion). Value is $2{,}000 + 80 / (0.08 - 0.02) ≈ 2{,}000 + 1{,}333 = 3{,}333$ (about ₩333 billion), a P/B of about 1.67. With a market value of ₩240 billion, $2{,}400 = 2{,}000 + 80 / (0.08 - g)$ gives $0.08 - g = 0.2$, so $g = -12\%$. The market expects abnormal earnings to disappear quickly.
