## What Is Narcissism?

Narcissism is a personality trait defined by excessive self-focus, a sense of superiority, and reduced empathy. The name comes from Narcissus, the figure in Greek mythology who fell in love with his own reflection.

Psychologists treat narcissism as a spectrum rather than a category — it ranges from healthy self-regard all the way to Narcissistic Personality Disorder (NPD).

---

## Three Types of Narcissism

### Grandiose Narcissism

Marked by a strong sense of superiority, a drive to dominate, and a willingness to exploit others. Common among high-achieving leaders and public figures.

### Vulnerable Narcissism

Outwardly introverted and sensitive, but harboring grandiose expectations underneath. Extremely sensitive to criticism and easily hurt.

### Communal Narcissism

Emphasizes helping and self-sacrifice, but still craves recognition for "how good a person I am." Reacts with anger when that recognition doesn't come.

---

## Healthy Self-Esteem vs. Narcissism

| Healthy self-esteem | Narcissism |
|---|---|
| Sees failure as a growth opportunity | Blames failure on others |
| Is genuinely happy for others' success | Experiences others' success as a threat |
| Has real empathy | Empathy is instrumental and surface-level |
| Accepts criticism | Reacts to criticism with anger or defensiveness |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What happens if I date a narcissist?**
A: Early on, it can feel intensely flattering — often called "love bombing." Over time, though, this pattern frequently escalates into gaslighting, dismissiveness, and exploitation. If your partner shows strong narcissistic tendencies, setting healthy boundaries becomes essential.

**Q: My score came back high — does that mean I have NPD?**
A: No. A high score doesn't equal NPD. NPD requires persistent symptoms that seriously impair someone's social functioning. Talk to a mental health professional for an accurate assessment.
