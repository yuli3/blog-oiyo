## 1. Introduction: The Sacred Architecture

A house is more than shelter. It is a spiritual organism.
In Traditional Korean belief (**Gasin-sinang**), the house is staffed by a team of invisible administrators.
If the family is healthy and wealthy, the gods are happy. If there is sickness or bankruptcy, the gods are angry or have left.

---

## 2. Seongju-sin (The House Lord)

The CEO of the house is **Seongju**. He resides in the **Main Roof Beam** (Maru).
He controls the overall fortune of the family.
When moving into a new house, a ritual (Seongju-gut) was performed to welcome him.
He is a benevolent father figure. He demands respect but gives protection.

---

## 3. Jowang-sin (The Kitchen Goddess)

The Kitchen is the domain of **Jowang**. She resides in the **water bowl** placed on the stove.
She records the good and bad deeds of the family and reports them to Heaven.
She is the goddess of Fire and Cleanliness.
Before modern plumbing, mothers would wake at dawn to draw fresh water (Jeonghwa-su) and pray to Jowang for the family's health.
**She tells us: Cooking is a sacred act.**

---

## 4. Samsin Halmeoni (The Birth Grandma)

In the warmest corner of the room resides **Samsin** (Three Spirits Grandma).
She is responsible for pregnancy, childbirth, and the health of children.
When a baby is born, seaweed soup (Miyeok-guk) is offered to her.
She reminds us that life itself is a gift from the void.

---

## 5. The Toilet God (Cheuk-sin)

Even the toilet has a god. **Cheuk-sin** is a fierce, moody goddess with long hair.
You must knock (cough) before entering, so as not to startle her.
This reflects a primitive hygiene wisdom: The latrine is a dangerous place (disease). Treat it with caution.

---

## 6. Conclusion: Re-enchanting the Home

We live in "machines for living" (Le Corbusier). Our apartments are sterile boxes.
But imagine if you treated your kitchen stove as an altar. Imagine if you treated your roof as a protector.
The House Gods teach us to treat our domestic space with **Reverence**.
When you clean your floor, you are not just removing dust; you are polishing the face of the god who lives there.
