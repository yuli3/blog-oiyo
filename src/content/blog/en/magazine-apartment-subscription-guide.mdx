## What Are First-Time Homebuyer Programs?

**First-time homebuyer programs** are government and lender-backed initiatives that help eligible buyers purchase a home at below-market terms — through reduced down payments, subsidized interest rates, or outright grants.

The more competitive the market, the harder it is to qualify for the best programs. Meeting eligibility requirements and understanding how scoring systems work gives you a real edge.

---

## The Basics: Federal and State Programs

**FHA Loans**: The most widely used first-time buyer program, backed by the Federal Housing Administration. Down payments as low as 3.5% for buyers with credit scores of 580+.

**Eligibility**: Open to US citizens and permanent residents. Income limits vary by program and county.

**Contributions**: Most programs require monthly savings or a defined down payment amount. How long you've been actively saving affects eligibility for certain grants.

---

## How Homes Are Allocated

### Open-Market Competition (Points & Lottery)

**Points/Priority System**: Many state and local programs rank applicants by a scoring formula. Higher scores = higher priority for affordable units. Typically used for units under a certain size or price threshold.

**Lottery**: Some programs — particularly for high-demand affordable developments — use random draws regardless of score. Applies to larger units and certain designated developments.

### Special Set-Asides

Before general applicants, specific groups receive reserved allocations:

| Category | Key Criteria |
|----------|-------------|
| Newly married couples | Married within past 7 years, do not own a home |
| First-generation buyers | Never previously owned a home; employed or self-employed |
| Large families | Three or more dependent children |
| Elderly caregiver households | Supporting a parent 65+ for three or more years |
| Priority referrals | Veterans, persons with disabilities, essential workers |

Set-aside slots have **a one-time use limit** per household per program type.

---

## How Priority Scores Are Calculated

In programs that use a scoring formula, maximum scores typically range from 80–100 points.

| Factor | Points |
|--------|--------|
| Time without owning a home | 0–32 pts (2 pts per year, capped at 15 years = 32 pts) |
| Number of dependents | 0–35 pts (5 pts for 0 dependents, +5 pts per additional dependent, max 35 pts at 6+) |
| Length of savings history | 1–17 pts (less than 6 months = 1 pt; 15+ years = 17 pts) |

**Key insight**: Dependents account for roughly 40% of the total score. Married applicants with children have a structural scoring advantage.

---

## How "Non-Owner" Time Is Calculated

Your non-ownership period generally starts from **age 30 or the date of marriage**, whichever comes first.

- If you sold or disposed of a home before age 30, the clock starts at your 30th birthday
- If you married at 29, the clock starts on your wedding date
- Both you and your spouse's ownership history is counted — a home in either name disqualifies the period

**Core rule**: Never acquire property (including inheriting with title) if you want this period to keep accumulating.

## Savings Strategy

The earlier you open a dedicated savings account, the better — because the **length of savings history** directly affects your score.

**Contribution strategies**:
- Public/subsidized housing under a certain size: often requires proof of a minimum deposit (varies by county/city — in high-cost areas, think $3,000–$15,000 minimum)
- Private affordable developments (scored): consistent monthly contributions matter more than lump sums; $1,000/month kept steady for years builds the best history

**Minimum deposit tiers (illustrative, by area cost level)**:

| Area Type | Units Under ~900 sq ft |
|-----------|----------------------|
| High-cost metro (e.g., NYC, SF, LA) | ~$10,000–$15,000 |
| Mid-tier metro (e.g., Denver, Austin) | ~$5,000–$8,000 |
| Lower-cost markets | ~$2,000–$3,000 |

*Actual requirements vary by specific program. Always verify with the administering agency.*

## Step-by-Step: After You're Selected

1. Confirm selection via the program's official portal (e.g., state housing authority website)
2. Submit documentation (income verification, proof of non-ownership, tax returns)
3. Sign the purchase agreement (earnest money deposit: typically 1–3% of purchase price)
4. Secure financing — arrange your mortgage (FHA, USDA, VA, or conventional)
5. Closing — pay remaining down payment + closing costs, receive keys

**Important**: Withdrawing after selection typically triggers a **waiting period or ban** from reapplying for a defined period.

## Practical Strategies

### Strategy 1: Go for Set-Aside Categories First

First-generation buyer and newly-married set-asides are less competitive than general pools. If you meet income and asset limits, your chances are significantly better than in the open lottery — even with a lower priority score.

### Strategy 2: Look at Less Competitive Markets

Major metro cores are brutally competitive. Suburban new developments and secondary cities often have far better odds — and in some cases still qualify for the same federal programs.

### Strategy 3: Target Lottery-Based Allocations

In programs where larger units or certain developments use a pure lottery, a low priority score doesn't matter. Focus on these when your score is still building.

### Strategy 4: Watch for Price-Capped Developments

When a development is subject to price controls or affordability restrictions, units sell below market rate — which means significant potential appreciation once restrictions lift. Competition is fierce, but the long-term value for patient buyers is real.

## Pre-Application Checklist

- [ ] Confirm your current savings account history and contribution record with your bank
- [ ] Verify neither you nor your spouse has ownership interest in any property
- [ ] Use an online priority score calculator to estimate your current standing
- [ ] Check whether you qualify for any set-aside category (veteran, first-gen, disability, etc.)
- [ ] Compare the listed sale price against comparable market prices in that area

---

There's no need to rush. The longer you've been without owning, and the more dependents you have, the stronger your position becomes. Even if you don't get selected today, consistent savings and a clean ownership history will put you in a genuinely competitive position in 10–15 years — or sooner if you qualify for a set-aside.
