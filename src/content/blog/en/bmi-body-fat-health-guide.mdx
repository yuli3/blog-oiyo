## Why the Scale Number Is Not the Whole Story

Consider a man in his mid-thirties who received an "obese" classification at a health checkup with a BMI of 25.3. He had been going to the gym for three years, training five times a week — and his actual body fat percentage was 16%, well within the healthy range. On the flip side, a BMI of 21 sounds perfectly healthy, but it is common among people who never exercise and have so little muscle mass that their body fat percentage reaches 30% — the so-called "skinny fat" phenomenon. Both cases produce different conclusions, but BMI alone cannot tell them apart.

BMI is a powerful screening tool, but it cannot distinguish muscle from fat. That is precisely why this calculator provides both BMI and estimated body fat percentage at the same time.

## What Is BMI?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is your weight in kilograms divided by the square of your height in meters.

**BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)²**

Example: 175 cm, 70 kg → 70 ÷ (1.75)² = **22.9**

The calculation is simple and the metric has been validated in large-scale epidemiological studies, which is why it is widely used in clinical settings. That said, BMI cannot distinguish body fat from muscle mass.

## Estimating Body Fat: The Deurenberg Formula

Body fat percentage is the proportion of your total body weight made up of fat. This calculator uses the **Deurenberg formula**:

**Body Fat (%) = (1.20 × BMI) + (0.23 × Age) − (10.8 × Sex factor) − 5.4**
- Sex factor: Male = 1, Female = 0

Example: BMI 23, 30-year-old male → (1.20 × 23) + (0.23 × 30) − (10.8 × 1) − 5.4 = **18.3%**

## The Limits of BMI and What to Use Instead

A high BMI does not necessarily mean poor health. Conversely, a normal BMI can coexist with high visceral fat.

**Cases Where BMI Alone Is Misleading**
1. **Athletes with high muscle mass**: Muscle is denser than fat, causing BMI to be overestimated.
2. **Older adults**: Loss of muscle mass may keep weight low even as body fat percentage rises.
3. **Pregnancy and nursing**: Weight changes are unrelated to body fat.

**Supplementary Metrics**
- **Waist circumference**: Abdominal obesity risk above 90 cm (men) or 80 cm (women)
- **Waist-to-height ratio**: Waist circumference ÷ height should ideally be ≤ 0.5
- **Body composition analysis (InBody)**: Direct measurement of muscle mass, body fat, and visceral fat

<Callout>
BMI is a screening tool. When setting body composition goals, consider waist circumference, body fat percentage, and blood markers (blood glucose, triglycerides, cholesterol) alongside BMI rather than relying on it alone.
</Callout>

## A Concrete Example: Same BMI, Different Health

Take two people: 170 cm tall, 73 kg. Both have a BMI of 25.3 — classified as overweight (or Obesity Class I by Asian-adjusted standards).

- **Person A (35-year-old male, 4 years of strength training)**: Using the Deurenberg formula, estimated body fat ~17%. High muscle mass accounts for the heavier weight. Waist circumference 79 cm. Low health risk.
- **Person B (35-year-old male, sedentary lifestyle)**: Same formula yields ~23% body fat. Fat concentrated in the abdomen. Waist circumference 92 cm (above the 90 cm threshold for abdominal obesity). Metabolic risk present.

BMI cannot tell these two people apart, but body fat percentage and waist circumference clearly signal different realities. Keep this context in mind when interpreting your calculator results.

## Getting the Most Out of This Calculator

This tool is most useful not as a one-time check but for **tracking change over time**. Measuring at the same time of day (morning, fasted) each month and recording both BMI and body fat percentage together shows you what is really happening beneath the scale number. If your weight stays the same but body fat percentage is dropping, that means muscle is increasing — which is a genuine sign of improving health.

If your BMI is above 23 (Asian standard) or your body fat percentage exceeds 25% (men) or 35% (women), dietary and exercise improvements are recommended. On the other hand, if your waist circumference is within normal range, focusing on maintaining muscle mass may be a more appropriate strategy than pursuing weight loss.
