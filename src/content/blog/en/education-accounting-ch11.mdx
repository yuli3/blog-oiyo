A factory has **production departments** that make products directly (machining, assembly) and **support departments** that serve them (power, maintenance, quality control). To calculate product costs, support department costs must first be moved to production departments and then applied to products with production department rates. This chapter compares three ways of doing the first step. The case for departmental rates was made in [chapter 5](/en/education-accounting-ch12/).

## 2. Direct method

Services exchanged between support departments are **ignored**, and costs are allocated only to production departments in proportion to their shares.

- S1: P1 and P2 in the ratio 50 : 30 → P1 $200 \times 5/8 = 125$, P2 75
- S2: P1 and P2 in the ratio 60 : 30 → P1 $300 \times 2/3 = 200$, P2 100

P1 receives ₩3.25 million and P2 ₩1.75 million. It is the simplest calculation but ignores the reality that maintenance uses power.

## 3. Step-down method

Support departments are allocated in a set **sequence**: a department allocated earlier allocates to later ones and receives nothing back afterwards. Usually the department that provides the most service to other support departments goes first. S1 (20% to S2) is allocated before S2 (10% to S1).

- S1's 200: 40 to S2, 100 to P1, 60 to P2
- S2's $300 + 40 = 340$: nothing goes back to S1, so P1 and P2 share 60 : 30 → P1 $340 \times 2/3 ≈ 226.7$, P2 ≈ 113.3

P1 receives about ₩3.267 million and P2 about ₩1.733 million. Changing the sequence changes the result.

## 4. Reciprocal method

**All** services exchanged between support departments are reflected. Each support department's total cost (its own cost plus costs received from other support departments) is found with simultaneous equations.

In this example the shares exchanged between support departments are small, so the differences are small, but with larger shares the direct method's distortion grows. Under every method the production departments' total is the same ₩5 million: allocation divides the total, it does not increase or reduce it.

## 5. Dual rates

Dual-rate allocation **splits support department costs into variable and fixed costs** and allocates them on different bases: variable costs on **actual usage**, fixed costs on **maximum available usage (practical capacity)**. Fixed costs arise from providing enough capacity for the maximum that production departments need, so this stops other departments from bearing those costs in months when one department uses little.

<Callout type="warning">
Allocating support department costs at actual cost passes support departments' inefficiencies on to production managers — if maintenance is wasteful, production department costs rise. For performance evaluation it is therefore better to allocate at **budgeted costs or standard rates** and leave the difference from actual as the support department's responsibility ([Managerial Accounting chapter 5](/en/academy-accounting-basics-ch6/)).
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

Company ZZ's support department A (cost ₩1 million) provides 25% of its services to B, 50% to production department X and 25% to Y; support department B (cost ₩1.5 million) provides 20% to A, 40% to X and 40% to Y. How much is allocated to X and Y under the reciprocal method?

$A = 100 + 0.2B$ and $B = 150 + 0.25A$. Substituting gives $A = 100 + 30 + 0.05A$, so $A = 130/0.95 ≈ 136.84$ and $B = 150 + 34.21 = 184.21$. X receives $0.5 \times 136.84 + 0.4 \times 184.21 ≈ 68.42 + 73.68 = 142.11$ and Y $0.25 \times 136.84 + 0.4 \times 184.21 ≈ 34.21 + 73.68 = 107.89$ (₩10,000). The total of ₩2.5 million equals the two support departments' combined costs.
