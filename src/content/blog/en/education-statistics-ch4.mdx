# Correlation and Regression: Connecting the Dots

Data points never exist in isolation. Many pairs—such as height and weight, education and income, or temperature and ice cream sales—influence or vary with each other. Statistics quantifies these 'relationships.'

## 1. Correlation

Correlation indicates the extent to which two variables change together. The **Correlation Coefficient ($r$)** ranges from -1 to +1.

- **$r > 0$ (Positive Correlation)**: Both increase or decrease together.
- **$r < 0$ (Negative Correlation)**: As one increases, the other decreases.
- **$r = 0$ (No Correlation)**: No apparent relationship between the two.

<Callout type="important">
**Correlation does not imply causation.** There is a strong positive correlation between ice cream sales and drowning incidents, but eating ice cream doesn't cause drowning. A third variable, 'Summer (High Temperature),' affects both phenomena.
</Callout>
## 2. Simple Linear Regression

While correlation shows "there is a link," regression analysis shows "how strong the link is, and how we can predict the future with it."

$$y = ax + b$$

It's the process of finding the single best-fitting line (the Regression Line) that explains the relationship between data points.

## 3. Accuracy of the Model: $R^2$

$R^2$ (R-squared) measures how much of the variation in the actual data is explained by our regression line. The closer it is to 1, the higher the predictive power of the model.
