Amun is a god in Egyptian mythology whose nature is that of "the hidden one." At first, he was an important god of the Theban region, but as political power grew during the New Kingdom, he merged with Ra as Amun-Ra and gained the status of a supreme god. Unlike visible sunlight, Amun symbolizes the hidden life force and the background of kingship: unseen, yet moving everything.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Amun's core is clearer in his historical and religious rise than in a single mythic event. As Thebes emerged as a political center, the cult of Amun also grew, and Amun was worshiped in union with Ra as Amun-Ra. This union shows how a local god expanded into an imperial supreme deity.

Amun was also deeply connected to the legitimacy of kingship. The pharaoh was understood as a ruler chosen and protected by Amun, and Karnak Temple became the center of state ritual. Amun's "hiddenness" is not weakness, but an expression of invisible authority and life force operating behind all phenomena.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Amun is the archetype of the **hidden father** and the **invisible center**. Rather than appearing directly before the eye, he moves the world from behind the scenes through institutions, authority, and something like breath. In modern terms, he resembles organizational culture, invisible rules, the beliefs behind a leader, and the tacit values that move a society.

The key to reading Amun is that what is unseen is not therefore absent. People often notice only visible results, but in reality, trust, atmosphere, and mood that are difficult to name determine action. The Amun archetype shows both sides: when the hidden center is healthy, order takes shape; when it hardens into opaque power, it can become oppression.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Are Amun and Amun-Ra the same concept?**

A. Amun is the god of Thebes, while Amun-Ra is the form in which Amun is joined with the sun god Ra. He held especially great authority during the New Kingdom.

**Q. What does it mean that Amun is "the hidden one"?**

A. It means that divine power is not directly visible, yet moves the world and kingship. He symbolizes a centrality that can be sensed like air but is difficult to grasp.
