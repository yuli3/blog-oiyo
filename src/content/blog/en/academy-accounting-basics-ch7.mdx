Tax accounting adjusts the results of financial accounting to the tax law to calculate the corporate tax base. Accounting measures profit under K-IFRS; tax measures it under the *Corporate Tax Act*. Bridging the gap between the two numbers is the work of **tax adjustments**. This course follows the Corporate Tax Act applicable to business years in 2026. The structure of the Corporate Tax Act is covered in [the corporate tax chapter of Specific Taxes](/ko/academy-tax-law-basic-ch3/) (in Korean), and the effect of tax adjustments on income tax expense in the financial statements in [Intermediate Accounting chapter 13](/en/academy-intermediate-accounting-ch13/).

## 1. The structure of tax adjustments

The Corporate Tax Act calls revenue from transactions that increase net assets **gross income (ikgeum)** and costs from transactions that decrease net assets **deductible expenses (songeum)**. Income for the business year is gross income minus deductible expenses. In practice, rather than gathering gross income and deductible expenses from scratch, only the differences from book net profit are adjusted.

## 2. Book-based and return-based adjustments

- **Book-based (closing) adjustment items:** deductible only if the company records them as expenses in its books — depreciation, additions to the bad debt allowance and retirement benefit provision, and certain write-downs of assets (such as from natural disasters). If not recorded in the books, they cannot be deducted through a tax adjustment.
- **Return-based adjustment items:** reflected through tax adjustments whether or not they are recorded in the books. Most inclusions and disallowances, and contributions to retirement pension plans, fall here.

Book-based items also mean the company can choose the timing of deductions. Recording less depreciation reduces that year's deductions and defers them ([chapter 5](/en/academy-tax-accounting-ch5/)).

## 3. Income disposition

Income increased by tax adjustments must be assigned to **whoever it accrued to**. This is income disposition. Either income tax is levied on the recipient or the difference in net assets for tax purposes is tracked.

When money has flowed out of the company and the recipient is unknown, it is disposed of as a **bonus to the representative**. The company must then withhold and pay the representative's employment income tax, which is a heavy burden.

## 4. Worked example

TA (an ordinary corporation) reports book net profit of ₩50 million for its 2026 business year. Its tax adjustments are as follows.

  rows={[

    ["Depreciation above the limit", "Disallowed expense", "+400", "Retained"],
    ["Traffic fine", "Disallowed expense", "+50", "Other outflow"],
    ["CEO's personal travel expenses", "Disallowed expense", "+200", "Bonus"],
    ["Accrued interest (interest subject to withholding)", "Excluded income", "−100", "Negative retained"],
    ["Income for the business year", "", "6,850", ""],
  ]}
/>

Income tax expense is the tax itself and is not deductible. The ₩4 million of disallowed depreciation means the asset for accounting is ₩4 million smaller than for tax, so it is tracked as retained and deducted later within the depreciation limit. Interest subject to withholding is gross income when received, so accrued interest recognized on an accrual basis is excluded and carried as negative retained.

<Callout type="warning">
Retained adjustments are a ledger tracking **timing** differences between accounting and tax. A retained disallowance this year will eventually come back as a deduction. Errors in managing retained balances lead later to double taxation or omitted tax. Other outflow, bonus and dividend dispositions, by contrast, are permanent differences. The distinction broadly corresponds to temporary and permanent differences in [Intermediate Accounting chapter 13](/en/academy-intermediate-accounting-ch13/).
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

TB's net profit is ₩80 million. Its tax adjustments are bad debt allowance above the limit of ₩6 million; ₩2.5 million of depreciation disallowed and retained in a prior year that is deductible this year; income tax expense of ₩15 million; ₩4 million of company funds taken by a salesperson, whose recipient is confirmed; and ₩300,000 of interest on a national tax refund (excluded income). What is income for the business year, and how is each item disposed of?

$8{,}000 + 600 - 250 + 1{,}500 + 400 - 30 = 10{,}220$, i.e. ₩102.2 million. The ₩6 million of bad debt allowance above the limit is a disallowed expense (retained); the ₩2.5 million depreciation reversal a deductible addition (negative retained); the ₩15 million of income tax expense a disallowed expense (other outflow); the ₩4 million taken by the salesperson a disallowed expense (bonus); and the ₩300,000 of refund interest excluded income (other).
