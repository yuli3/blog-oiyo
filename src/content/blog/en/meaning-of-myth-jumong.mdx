Jumong is remembered as Dongmyeongseongwang, the founding ruler of Goguryeo. His story survives in a form where historical record and foundation myth overlap, with different emphases appearing in the *Samguk Sagi*, the *Samguk Yusa*, and the founding narrative on the Stele of King Gwanggaeto. The name Jumong is commonly interpreted as meaning a person skilled with the bow, and his life shows the classic pattern of a heroic narrative: extraordinary birth, persecution, escape, the building of a new capital, and eventual kingship.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

Jumong's birth narrative is told as the story of Hae Mosu and Yuhwa, or as a meeting between a heavenly power and the world of water. The account in which Yuhwa receives sunlight, lays an egg, and Jumong is born from that egg emphasizes the extraordinary nature of royal authority. Although the details of genealogy and scene vary by tradition, Jumong is depicted from the beginning as a child who does not fully belong inside the ordinary human order.

Raised in Buyeo, Jumong becomes the target of envy and threat because of his exceptional skill with the bow. To escape danger to his life, he leaves for the south, and the famous scene in which fish and soft-shelled turtles form a bridge for him as he crosses a river marks this passage. This episode can be read to mean that the new king is not merely fleeing, but crossing a boundary with the approval of nature and the divine world.

When Jumong reaches Jolbon, he founds the new kingdom of Goguryeo. His narrative is the story of one who has been driven out creating a world of his own. The bow is a weapon, but it is also a symbol of the focus required to aim accurately at a distant target, while the river crossing is a rite of passage from an old political order into a new community.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Jumong is an archetype of the **hero** and the **exiled prince**. He begins as a threatened child, but accepts his talent and destiny and creates a new center. In modern terms, he resembles someone who is not recognized by an existing organization and goes on to found a new project, city, or community.

The power of the Jumong archetype lies not in the place of origin, but in direction. He does not become king through sacred lineage alone; he moves through real danger and makes choices. For that reason, Jumong symbolizes the moment when talent becomes the object of envy, and one must leave an old enclosure and aim one's own bow.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Jumong a god or a king?**

A. Tradition describes him as the founding king of Goguryeo, but his birth and escape narratives are strongly combined with mythic elements.

**Q. What does the story of fish and soft-shelled turtles forming a bridge mean?**

A. It can be read as a symbol that the natural world approves the new king's movement. The river is the boundary separating the old world from the new one.
