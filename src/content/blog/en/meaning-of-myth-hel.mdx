Hel is a being born to Loki and Angrboda, and a sibling of Fenrir and Jormungandr. Knowing that these three beings would one day become great calamities, the gods send each of them to a different place. Hel comes to rule the world of the dead, and her name is used to refer both to the place and to its ruler.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Hel's central episode comes after the death of Baldr. When Baldr descends to the underworld, the gods send a messenger to Hel in hopes of bringing him back. Hel says she will return him if every being weeps for Baldr. This condition is severe, but it is presented not as chaotic malice, but as a rule belonging to the world of death.

In the end, one being refuses to weep, and Baldr cannot return. Hel is less a villain who steals Baldr than a figure who guards the boundary that beings cannot easily cross back once they have passed the threshold of death. Her world contains deaths other than Valholl, including deaths that are not heroic.

## Reading as an Archetype — Jungian Perspective

Hel is an archetype of the **shadow queen** and the **guardian of the threshold**. She represents death, decline, illness, and loss, the things people often want to turn away from, but she also keeps them within an order. In modern scenes, she resembles the time when one must honestly face mourning, depression, and loss that does not recover. Hel symbolizes the power to name what has ended, without covering everything with positivity.

The Hel archetype does not force the pace of recovery. Some losses do not disappear simply because someone says they should pass quickly, and grief needs a place to remain. In modern terms, Hel's symbolism can be seen in funerals, farewells, the silence after failure, and the process of acknowledging the body's limits. She does not romanticize darkness, but she makes us recognize that darkness too is part of the world.

## Related Figures

When reading Hel, it is important not to define the underworld only as a place of punishment. Her world contains ordinary deaths outside heroic death, as well as losses that are difficult to name.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Hel an evil being?**

A. It is difficult to see her as simply evil. She is the being who rules the world of death and symbolizes an irreversible boundary.

**Q. How are Hel and Valholl different?**

A. Valholl is the place where Odin gathers slain warriors, while Hel is understood as a broader world of the dead. They express different kinds of death.
