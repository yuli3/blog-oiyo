Work is where we spend more of our time than almost anywhere else. Staying in the wrong job doesn't just make you unhappy — it bleeds into your health, your relationships, and your sense of identity. And yet changing jobs isn't easy. Fear, uncertainty, and a lack of information keep a lot of people stuck in place. This guide is a full roadmap for making a career change work.

## When to consider changing jobs

### Good signs it's time

- There's no more room to grow in your current role
- Your pay is significantly below market rate
- The company's direction and values no longer match yours
- Conflict with a manager that isn't getting resolved
- A clearly better opportunity exists

### Signs you should reconsider

- Your current job is just hard right now (every job has hard stretches)
- External factors (a colleague, a project) are about to change anyway
- You're deciding emotionally, without enough preparation

<Callout type="info">
**Changing jobs vs. fixing it where you are**: If the problem is genuinely structural to that company, changing jobs is the right call. But if it's a communication issue or temporary stress, try to resolve it internally first.
</Callout>

## Self-assessment before you move

### List your strengths

1. Your five proudest achievements from the last 3-5 years
2. The areas where people come to you for advice
3. What you lose track of time doing
4. What you want to do even when it's hard

### Clarify your values

| Value | Importance (1-5) | Met by current job? |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| Work-life balance | | |
| Growth opportunities | | |
| Compensation | | |
| Social impact | | |
| Creative expression | | |

### Sketch your ideal role

- The kind of work you want to do
- The team culture you want
- Your preferred work style (remote, hybrid, in-office)
- Target industry/function
- Target salary range

## Career-change prep checklist

### 6 months out

- [ ] Clarify your goal and reason for changing jobs
- [ ] Build a target list of roles/companies (20-30)
- [ ] Identify skill gaps and start learning
- [ ] Optimize your [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/) profile
- [ ] Start reactivating your network

### 3 months out

- [ ] Update your resume (lead with results, not duties)
- [ ] Prepare a portfolio (if relevant)
- [ ] Line up references
- [ ] Gather intel on target companies

### 1 month out

- [ ] Start submitting applications
- [ ] Prep for interviews (STAR method, mock interviews)
- [ ] Build your salary negotiation strategy

## Writing a resume that gets read

### Getting past the ATS (Applicant Tracking System)

Most large companies use an ATS to filter resumes by keyword.

**How to get past it**:
- Mirror the key terms from the job posting in your resume
- Keep the format simple (minimize tables and graphics)
- Some ATS platforms parse Word files more reliably than PDFs — check what the employer prefers

### Lead with results, not duties

**Weak**: "Handled customer service"
**Strong**: "Handled 1,000+ inquiries a month, hit a 92% satisfaction score, and cut response time from 48 to 24 hours"

**Formula**: [Action] + [Method] + [Result] + [Number]

### One page vs. two

- Under 5 years of experience: one page
- 5-10 years: one to two pages
- 10+ years: two pages

## Interview strategy

### The STAR method

A structure for answering experience-based questions:
- **S**ituation: set the scene
- **T**ask: your role or responsibility
- **A**ction: what you specifically did
- **R**esult: the outcome (with numbers where possible)

### Common interview questions to prepare for

| Question | Key point |
|------|-----------|
| "Tell me about yourself" | A career story that connects to this specific role |
| "Why are you leaving your current job?" | Frame it positively (new challenge) — never trash your old employer |
| "What's your biggest weakness?" | A real weakness, plus the work you're doing on it |
| "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" | A concrete growth goal tied to this role |
| "What are your salary expectations?" | A reasonable range backed by market research |

### Interview them back

- "What does success in this role look like?"
- "How would you describe the team culture in one word?"
- "What's the biggest challenge in this role right now?"
- "Why is this position open?"

## Salary negotiation

### How to research the market

- Salary comparison sites and platforms for your industry (Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, Levels.fyi, Payscale, or local equivalents)
- Your professional network — ask people in similar roles
- Recruiters who specialize in your industry

### Negotiation strategy

**1. Don't go first**
"Could you share the budgeted range first?" avoids anchoring yourself too low.

**2. Give a range, not a number**
A range is more flexible than a single figure: "I'm looking at something in the $X-$Y range."

**3. Think total compensation**
Base pay + bonus + equity + benefits + flexible work + growth opportunities all count.

**4. Say thank you, then ask for time**
"Thank you — could I take a day to review and get back to you?" This buys you room to negotiate.

## Managing the gap after you leave

**How to handle a gap without burning out**:
1. Build an emergency fund covering 3-6 months of expenses beforehand
2. Treat the gap as a chance to build skills
3. List what you did during the gap on your resume (courses, projects)
4. Protect your mental health: keep a routine, exercise, stay socially connected

<Callout type="tip">
**Timing tip**: Job hunting while still employed gives you the strongest position. You negotiate as a "candidate with options," not a "desperate applicant."
</Callout>
