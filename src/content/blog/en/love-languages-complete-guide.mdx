"I try so hard, so why does my partner say they don't feel loved?" — Many couples run into this exact conflict, and the cause is usually **mismatched love languages**. After decades of marriage counseling, Dr. Gary Chapman found that people express and receive love in fundamentally different ways. That insight became the foundation of his book *The 5 Love Languages*.

## The Five Love Languages

### 1. Words of Affirmation

This type expresses love through words — and feels loved through words.

**Traits**:
- Phrases like "I love you," "thank you," and "you make me happy" carry enormous weight
- Compliments, encouragement, and gratitude recharge their emotional batteries
- Critical or negative words wound them deeply

**How to speak this language**:
- Offer one sincere compliment every day
- Express appreciation through texts, notes, or letters
- "You worked hard today — you did great."
- Praise them in front of others

**Note**: specific, sincere words matter far more than routine or generic ones.

---

### 2. Acts of Service

This type expresses love through action — and feels loved through help.

**Traits**:
- Values "actions over words"
- Deeply moved when a partner takes care of something before being asked
- Unprompted help means the most

**How to speak this language**:
- Do the dishes when your partner looks tired, without being asked
- Help out with their workload
- Have coffee ready in the morning
- Just do it — instead of asking "what can I help with?"

**Note**: service done grudgingly or with complaints tends to backfire.

---

### 3. Receiving Gifts

This type expresses love through gifts — and feels loved through gifts.

<Callout type="info">
**A common misconception**: this love language isn't about materialism. What matters is the message behind the gift — "I was thinking of you." Meaningful beats expensive, every time.
</Callout>

**Traits**:
- A gift is tangible proof that "I was thinking about you"
- Even a small gift on an ordinary day can mean a great deal
- Forgetting or overlooking gifts causes real hurt

**How to speak this language**:
- Pick up a small item during a walk that you think they'd like
- Remember their favorite food and make it for them
- Never miss a meaningful date
- Send a package that says, "This reminded me of you."

---

### 4. Quality Time

This type expresses and feels love through time spent together.

**Traits**:
- "Being together" matters more than almost anything else
- Undivided, phone-down attention carries deep meaning
- What matters is presence itself, more than the conversation

**How to speak this language**:
- Put the phone away during dinner
- Suggest "let's carve out just-us time this weekend"
- Give your full attention when they're talking
- Create new shared experiences (a new restaurant, a trip, a hobby)

**Note**: physical presence with your attention on your phone feels, to this type, the same as being alone.

---

### 5. Physical Touch

This type expresses and feels love through physical connection.

**Traits**:
- Not just sexual touch — hand-holding, hugs, a pat on the shoulder, all count
- Meaningful touch creates a sense of safety and connection
- Being pushed away physically feels like rejection

**How to speak this language**:
- A light touch on the shoulder or back as you pass by
- Holding hands or sitting close together
- A hug first, when they've had a hard day
- Regular physical affection, separate from anything sexual

## Finding Your Love Language

### Self-Reflection Questions

Ask yourself:

1. What does your partner do that makes you feel most loved?
2. What does your partner do that hurts you the most?
3. How do you usually express love to your partner?
4. What do you most often ask your partner for?

### A Quick Diagnostic

| Situation | Likely love language |
|------|----------------|
| You're deeply hurt when your partner is glued to their phone | Quality time |
| Forgetting your birthday devastates you | Receiving gifts or words of affirmation |
| Broken promises make you angriest of all | Acts of service or quality time |
| Falling asleep without a hug feels wrong | Physical touch |

## Understanding a Partner With a Different Love Language

**The most common conflict pattern**:
- Partner A (acts of service) expresses love by working hard around the house → Partner B doesn't register it as love
- Partner B (words of affirmation) frequently asks "Do I look pretty?" → Partner A finds this puzzling

**How to fix it**:
1. Identify each other's love languages
2. Express love in your partner's language, not just your own
3. Ask your partner directly what they want

<Callout type="tip">
**The golden rule**: express love the way your partner wants to receive it — not the way you'd want to receive it yourself.
</Callout>
