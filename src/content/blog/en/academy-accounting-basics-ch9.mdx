The final chapter of principles of accounting looks at where the rules used so far come from. The same transaction can produce different numbers depending on the standard applied. When reading or preparing financial statements, the first thing to check is **which standards this company uses**.

## 1. Who sets the standards, and their force

International standards are issued by the **International Accounting Standards Board (IASB)** under the IFRS Foundation. In Korea, under Article 5 of the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies, the Financial Services Commission sets accounting standards and delegates the work to the **Korea Accounting Standards Board (KASB)**. Standards take effect when the KASB's Accounting Standards Committee resolves them and the Financial Services Commission approves them.

## 2. Three frameworks and who applies them

Korea has required listed companies to apply K-IFRS since 2011. K-IFRS numbers IAS-series standards 10xx and IFRS-series standards 11xx. For example, IAS 16 (property, plant and equipment) is K-IFRS 1016, IFRS 15 (revenue) is K-IFRS 1115 and IFRS 18 (presentation and disclosure) is K-IFRS 1118.

### A. Principles-based and rules-based

K-IFRS is a **principles-based** standard that sets principles and leaves judgement to the entity, in exchange requiring detailed disclosure of the basis for those judgements in the notes. K-GAAP has relatively specific rules that make it easier to apply but leave less room to interpret new kinds of transactions.

## 3. Examples where the numbers differ by standard

### A. Goodwill

A company acquires another company and pays ₩10 million more than the fair value of its net assets. That ₩10 million is goodwill.

For the same acquisition, the cumulative profit difference over five years is ₩5 million. When comparing the profits of acquisitive companies, this difference must be adjusted for.

### B. The centre of consolidation

A parent using K-IFRS presents consolidated financial statements as its primary statements. K-GAAP also requires consolidation, but Korean disclosure practice has centred on separate statements. For a group with many subsidiaries, separate and consolidated debt ratios can differ widely.

### C. Other differences

Differences also arise in the criteria for recognizing development costs as assets, the scope for revaluing property, plant and equipment, the classification of financial instruments and lease accounting (K-IFRS recognizes most leases as right-of-use assets and lease liabilities). Each item is covered under K-IFRS in Intermediate Accounting.

## 4. Choosing and switching standards

A company using K-GAAP that prepares to list switches to K-IFRS. It restates its statement of financial position at the transition date under the new standards and must present comparative figures under them too (K-IFRS 1101 *First-time Adoption*). Exemptions from retrospective application can be chosen for some items.

<Callout type="warning">
When pulling numbers for many companies from a financial database at once, K-IFRS consolidated, K-IFRS separate and K-GAAP separate figures easily get mixed. The same "profit" cannot be compared if the standard and scope differ. Check first the basis-of-preparation paragraph at the start of the audit report and whether statements are consolidated or separate. Standards also keep changing: when presentation changes, as with the introduction of K-IFRS 1118 in 2027, even the same company's past and present figures must be reclassified to be compared.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

Company H (K-GAAP, goodwill useful life five years) and company I (K-IFRS) each acquire another company on the same day and recognize goodwill of ₩6 million. What are each company's total goodwill-related expenses over the three years after acquisition and the goodwill carrying amount at the end of year 3? What happens if I's impairment test in year 3 shows that the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit including goodwill is ₩1.5 million below its carrying amount?

H amortizes $600 / 5 = 120$ (₩10,000) each year, so total expense over three years is ₩3.6 million and the carrying amount at the end of year 3 is ₩2.4 million. I does not amortize, so without impairment the expense is 0 and the carrying amount ₩6 million. If an impairment of ₩1.5 million is identified in year 3, I first reduces goodwill by ₩1.5 million, for total expense of ₩1.5 million and a carrying amount of ₩4.5 million. A goodwill impairment loss is not reversed even if the recoverable amount later recovers.
