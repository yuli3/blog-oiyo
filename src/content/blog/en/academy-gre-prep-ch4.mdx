## GRE Analytical Writing Overview

**1 task, 30 minutes** (reduced from 2 tasks in the 2024 revision)

Scoring: 0–6 in 0.5-point increments, by ETS raters + AI

**Possible task types**:
- **Issue Task**: Defend or argue against a given position with reasons and examples
- **Argument Task**: Analyze the logical flaws in a given argument

---

## Scoring Criteria (5 Elements)

| Criterion | What Raters Look For |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Analysis of complex ideas | Depth and insight on the topic |
| Relevant reasons & examples | Concrete, well-chosen support |
| Essay organization | Clear intro, body, conclusion |
| Vocabulary & grammar | Variety, accuracy, fluency |
| Transitions | Smooth logical connections between paragraphs |

---

## Issue Essay Structure

**Task format**: Given a claim or policy statement, develop a position with supporting reasoning and examples.

### Issue Essay Template

```
Paragraph 1 (Introduction):
"The question of whether [topic] is both complex and consequential.
While some argue [opposing view], I contend that [your position],
particularly when one considers [reason 1] and [reason 2]."

Paragraph 2 (Reason 1 + Example):
"[Topic sentence]. [Concrete example/evidence]. [Link to thesis]."

Paragraph 3 (Reason 2 + Example):
"[Topic sentence]. [Example]. [Connection]."

Paragraph 4 (Counterargument + Rebuttal):
"Admittedly, one might argue that [opposing view]. 
However, this perspective overlooks [your rebuttal reason]."

Paragraph 5 (Conclusion):
"In conclusion, [restate position]. [Summarize key reasons]. [Broader insight]."
```

## Argument Essay Structure

**Task format**: A short argument passage is given. Analyze its logical weaknesses — do NOT argue for or against the conclusion.

### Common Logical Flaws to Identify

| Flaw | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Causation/Correlation error | Treating co-occurrence as causation |
| Hasty generalization | Small sample → sweeping conclusion |
| False analogy | Assuming two situations are comparable |
| False dichotomy | Presenting only two options when more exist |
| Unwarranted assumption | Treating an unproven premise as fact |

### Argument Essay Template

```
"The argument that [conclusion] contains several logical flaws 
that undermine its persuasiveness.

First, the argument assumes [assumption 1] without evidence. 
[Explain why this is problematic with a counterexample or alternative explanation.]

Second, the claim that [premise] constitutes a [name the flaw]. 
[Explain the flaw.]

Finally, [third flaw + explanation].

To strengthen the argument, the author would need to provide 
[specific evidence type]. Without these, the conclusion remains unsupported."
```

---

## Characteristics of a 5–6 Score Essay

- **Specific examples**: Historical, scientific, or social examples (not vague generalities)
- **Conceded counterarguments**: Acknowledge the other side and refute it
- **Varied vocabulary**: No repetition of the same high-frequency words
- **Sentence variety**: Balance simple, compound, and complex sentences
