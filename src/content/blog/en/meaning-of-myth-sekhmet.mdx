Sekhmet is a powerful lion-headed goddess in Egyptian mythology. Her name is associated with the meaning "The Powerful One," and she symbolizes war, plague, and the scorching heat of the sun that destroys the enemies of the Pharaoh. At the same time, she was worshipped as a goddess of healing and medicine. The power of Sekhmet reveals that destruction and healing are not entirely separate forces.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

The most famous episode involving Sekhmet is the story of the "Eye of Ra," sent by Ra to punish humanity. Sekhmet attacks humanity mercilessly and continues her destruction as if intoxicated by blood. Realizing that her power could completely annihilate the world, Ra tricks her by dyeing beer red to resemble blood, causing Sekhmet to drink it, become intoxicated, and eventually calm down.

This story illustrates that Sekhmet is not merely a goddess of war, but a being who represents the dangers of unchecked justice and rage. Simultaneously, Sekhmet’s priests were responsible for rituals related to disease and healing. They believed that the power capable of bringing illness was the same power capable of dispelling it.

## Reading the Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Sekhmet is the archetype of the **enraged goddess** and the **healer who cauterizes wounds**. She symbolizes the moment when repressed anger erupts, but this energy, when handled correctly, becomes a force that cuts through injustice and purifies what is diseased. In a modern context, she resembles situations requiring painful interventions, such as burnout, social outrage, or surgery and medical treatment.

The key to understanding Sekhmet is not the elimination of power, but its regulation. Healing without destructive force sometimes fails to address diseased structures, while anger without restraint burns the world. The Sekhmet archetype demonstrates that when strong emotions meet ethics and procedure, they can be transformed into a path of healing.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Sekhmet a goddess of war?**

A. Yes. She is a goddess of war and destruction, but she is also connected to the power to prevent plague and provide healing.

**Q. Are Sekhmet and Hathor the same goddess?**

A. They cannot be viewed as the same goddess. However, they are often interpreted as being connected as different aspects of the "Eye of Ra" and feminine solar divinity.
