## Why Home Prices and the Economy Are Moving on Different Tracks

Since COVID-19, Korea's economy has shown an unusual pattern. Even as the real economy slowed, Seoul apartment prices surged. Then, once interest rates rose, home prices fell sharply — while exporters rebounded.

Real estate and the real economy moving in **opposite directions** is a textbook case of a K-shaped economy.

### The Bank of Korea's Dilemma

The Bank of Korea has to hit two conflicting goals with a single lever: the policy rate.

- **Stabilizing home prices** calls for raising rates
- **Supporting the real economy** calls for cutting rates

This dilemma isn't unique to Korea. Most developed economies face the same structural problem as the gap between asset prices and the real economy widens.

### Why the K-Shape Keeps Deepening

In a low-rate environment, those who held assets (real estate, stocks) saw their wealth grow, while those without assets got by on wage growth alone. That gap widened dramatically after COVID — that's the K-shaped economy.

Rate hikes temporarily narrowed the gap. But once rate cuts resume, asset prices are likely to rebound and the gap is likely to widen again.

### An Investor's View

When real estate and the real economy diverge, analyze each cycle separately. Real estate is more sensitive to the rate cycle; the real economy is more sensitive to the export cycle.
