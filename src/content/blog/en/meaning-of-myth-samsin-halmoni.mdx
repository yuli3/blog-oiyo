Samsin Halmoni is remembered in Korean folk belief as a deity who grants children and watches over mothers and babies. "Samsin" can carry different meanings depending on region and interpretation, but in everyday life she is familiar as a grandmother goddess who governs childbirth and the fortune of life. She is also connected to customs such as avoiding outside visits for the first twenty-one days after a baby is born, or hanging a geumjul, a taboo rope placed at the gate. Rather than a vast creator of the cosmos, Samsin Halmoni is a life deity of daily living, close to the room, beside the kitchen, and near the mother's body.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

Samsin Halmoni was regarded as a being who observes whether a child will be conceived, what kind of child will come, and whether the child will grow well after birth. In many households, people prayed to Samsin when a child was ill or during childbirth, and in shamanic rites Samsin was also honored as an important deity connected with life. This belief was also a religious way of caring for the anxieties of an age when medicine was not yet sufficient.

The Samsin table prepared for Samsin Halmoni varies by region and household, but it usually appears as a simple offering of clean food, water, and rice, set out to pray for the safety of mother and child. Depending on the transmission, Samsin may be depicted as one grandmother, or understood as the union of three deities. For this reason, Samsin Halmoni is clearer as a figure within daily rites than within a single standardized mythic plot.

The custom of samchil-il is also closely connected to Samsin belief. Because a newborn life was considered still weak and standing on a threshold, people guarded against impure outside forces and acted carefully for a fixed period. Samsin Halmoni is the name that protects this anxious first stage, and she turns childbirth from a private event into a sacred transition for the family and community.

## Reading as Archetype — Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Samsin Halmoni is the caring aspect of the **Great Mother** and the archetype of the **guardian of birth**. She protects the vulnerability of beginnings. In modern terms, she resembles postpartum care, neonatal intensive care, and the way a family cautiously changes its rhythm of life before a new life.

The Samsin Halmoni archetype reminds us that life is not born because it is strong, but must be protected because it is weak. She speaks of the power of quiet care rather than the brilliance of a hero. Samsin Halmoni is therefore a symbol that protects what is still fragile and unnamed, such as birth, recovery, or the earliest stage of a new project.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Samsin Halmoni one figure or three?**

A. It depends on the tradition. In everyday belief, she is often spoken of as one grandmother goddess, but because of the name Samsin, she is also interpreted as three deities.

**Q. Does belief in Samsin Halmoni still remain today?**

A. Traditional rites have weakened, but samchil-il, the idea of carefully caring for mother and baby, and the expression Samsin Halmoni remain in cultural memory.
