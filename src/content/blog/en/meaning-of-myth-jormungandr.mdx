Jormungandr is the child of Loki and Angrboda, and the world-serpent that encircles Midgard. The gods cast him into the sea, and he grew so vast that he wrapped around the world and bit his own tail. This immense serpent is a monster of chaos, yet also a being that forms the world's boundary.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Jormungandr's best-known episode is Thor's fishing trip. Thor goes out to sea with the giant Hymir, uses an ox head as bait, and pulls up the world-serpent. Thor tries to strike the serpent with his hammer, but Hymir, overcome by fear, cuts the fishing line, leaving the confrontation unfinished.

This unfinished confrontation is completed at Ragnarok. Jormungandr rises from the sea and spews poison, and Thor kills him. Yet Thor falls after taking nine steps because of the poison. This ending shows that even heroic victory is not free from venom and cost.

## Reading as Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

Jormungandr calls to mind the archetypes of the **ouroboros** and the **shadow of the abyss**. The world-serpent biting its own tail creates an image of cycle and enclosure, where end and beginning interlock. In a modern setting, it resembles a long-neglected problem that surrounds an entire system before surfacing at a certain moment. Jormungandr asks whether the threat we seek to remove may in fact be part of the world's structure.

Jormungandr looks like an external enemy, but he is also the boundary already surrounding the world. For that reason, eliminating him completely means reorganizing the world itself. In modern terms, he resembles environmental problems, organizational debt, or recurring family conflicts: risks that have surrounded us for so long that they become familiar. His confrontation with Thor shows that solving a problem can sometimes change or consume the solver as well.

When reading Jormungandr, it is important to ask whether the serpent is merely an evil monster or a being that forms the boundary of the world. His body is both a threat and the circumference of order.
That means touching him is touching the balance of the world itself.
Jormungandr's poison also symbolizes the aftereffects left by the collapse of that balance.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Why is Jormungandr called the world-serpent?**

A. Because he is said to have grown so enormous in the sea that he could encircle Midgard and bite his own tail.

**Q. Does Thor defeat Jormungandr?**

A. He succeeds in killing him, but he also dies from the poison. It is a characteristically Norse ending of mutual destruction.
