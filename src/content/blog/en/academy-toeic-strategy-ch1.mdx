## TOEIC LC Overview

**TOEIC (Test of English for International Communication)** measures English communication ability in international business settings. The Listening Comprehension (LC) section contains 100 of the 200 total questions, scored 5–495.

### LC Structure

| Part | Type | Questions | Time |
|------|------|-----------|------|
| Part 1 | Photographs | 6 | ~5 min |
| Part 2 | Question-Response | 25 | ~15 min |
| Part 3 | Conversations | 39 (13 sets) | ~25 min |
| Part 4 | Talks | 30 (10 sets) | ~20 min |
| **Total** | | **100** | **~45 min** |

**Key rules:**
- Audio plays once — no replays
- Pre-reading questions before each set is the single most important strategy
- Parts 3 and 4 carry more points than 1 and 2
- Some sets in Parts 3 and 4 include visual information (charts, maps, coupons)

## Part 1: Photographs

### Format

Look at a photo, then choose the statement (A–D) that best describes it.

**Photo types**: people / objects and scenes / people + objects

### Core Strategy

**1. Analyze the photo before the audio (5–10 seconds)**
- Who is in the photo? What are they doing? Where?
- Check for people, actions, and background objects
- Note the position of prominent items

**2. Know the distractor types**

| Distractor Type | Description | Example |
|----------------|-------------|---------|
| Similar-sound trap | Confuses words that sound alike | writing / riding; stairs / chairs |
| Partially correct | Some element is right but the whole is wrong | "reading a newspaper" when they hold documents |
| Association trap | Related word, wrong description | "swimming in the ocean" for a beach photo |
| Tense error | Completed vs. in-progress confusion | "has been placed" vs. "is being placed" |

### High-Frequency Part 1 Patterns

**People in action:**
- A man is **carrying** boxes.
- A woman is **examining** documents.
- People are **gathered** around a table.
- He is **reaching for** a book on the shelf.
- She is **facing** the camera.
- They are **seated across from** each other.

**Objects and scenes:**
- Chairs are **lined up** along the wall.
- A laptop is **placed on** the desk.
- Trees are **reflected in** the water.
- The shelves are **stocked with** merchandise.
- The road is **under construction**.

## Part 2: Question-Response

### Format

Hear one question or statement; choose the most natural response (A–C). No photo.

### Question Type Strategies

**WH questions** — listen for the exact question word:

| Question word | Expected response |
|--------------|-----------------|
| When | Time, date, duration |
| Where | Place, location |
| Who | Person, title |
| What | Object, content, type |
| Why | Reason, cause |
| How | Method, means, condition |
| How long | Duration |
| How often | Frequency |

**Yes/No questions** — the answer doesn't have to start with "Yes" or "No":
> "Is the meeting scheduled for Monday?"  
> → "I think it's been moved to Tuesday." ✓

**Choice questions** (A or B?) — a third option is also valid:
> "Would you prefer coffee or tea?"  
> → "Either is fine." ✓ / "I don't mind." ✓

### Common Traps

- **Sound-alike trap**: "Where is the meeting?" → "It was very exciting." (where/were)
- **Association trap**: "Who ordered the supplies?" → "The office supply store is nearby."
- **Idiom misread**: "Could you give me a hand?" → "I'm right-handed." (give a hand = help)

### High-Scoring Response Phrases

```
Agreeing / accepting:
  "Sure, no problem." / "That works for me."
  "I'd be happy to." / "Of course."

Declining / refusing:
  "I'm afraid I can't." / "That doesn't work for me."
  "I have other plans."

Uncertain / delegating:
  "I'm not sure." / "I don't know off the top of my head."
  "You might want to check with [person]."
  "I'll have to get back to you on that."
```

## Part 3: Short Conversations

### Format

Two or three speakers; three questions per set (13 sets × 3 = 39 questions). Some sets include visual information.

### Core Strategy

**Step 1: Read the questions BEFORE the audio starts (most important)**

Use the gap between sets to read all three questions for the next set:
- Predict who is speaking, where, and about what
- Know what information to listen for

**Step 2: Understand conversation structure**

Most conversations follow:
1. First turn: situation setup (problem, request, information)
2. Middle turn: reaction, question, additional detail
3. Final turn: solution, agreement, next action

**Step 3: Focus on the final turn for "next action" questions**

"What will the man do next?" → the answer is almost always in the last lines.

### Question Types and Tactics

| Question type | Key location | Watch out for |
|--------------|-------------|---------------|
| Setting / situation | First lines | Direct statement vs. inference |
| Speaker's profession | Early lines | Job titles, work vocabulary |
| Topic | Early lines | Paraphrased wording |
| Request / problem | Middle | "Could you…", "I need…" |
| Next action | Final turn | Focus on verbs |
| Visual integration | Whole conversation | Match dialogue to chart/map |

### High-Frequency Situations

**Office / workplace:**
- Scheduling a meeting
- Document request / revision
- Business trip booking
- Project status update

**Service situations:**
- Ordering / reservations
- Inquiry / complaint handling
- Delivery / repair request

## Part 4: Short Talks

### Format

One speaker; three questions per set (10 sets × 3 = 30 questions).

**Talk types**: voice messages, announcements, advertisements, news reports, speeches/talks, tour guides

### Talk Type Strategies

| Talk type | Opening signal | Listen for |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| Voice message | "Hi, this is [name] from…" | Reason for call, callback request |
| Announcement | "Attention, please." / "Ladies and gentlemen…" | Change, warning, instructions |
| Advertisement | Product/service description | Discount, CTA, contact info |
| News report | Headline statement | Statistics, dates, locations |
| Speech / talk | Speaker introduction | Key points, next steps |
| Tour guide | Welcome + location | Rules, facts, upcoming stops |

### Universal Part 4 Strategy

1. **Pre-read all three questions before the talk begins**
2. **Focus on the first 1–2 sentences** — they almost always establish the location, speaker identity, and purpose
3. "Next action" questions → focus on the final sentences

## Practice Quiz

**Q1.** What is the "sound-alike trap" in Part 2, and why is it dangerous?

> Test-makers insert a word in the response that sounds similar to a key word in the question but is used in an unrelated way. For example, if the question uses "where," the wrong answer might use "were" (similar sound). Because you're listening — not reading — familiar sounds create false familiarity that tempts you to choose the wrong answer.

**Q2.** In a Part 3 conversation, what strategy finds the answer to "What will the man do next?"

> Focus on the **final turn** of the conversation. Answers typically contain phrases like "I'll…", "I'm going to…", or "Let me…". Pre-reading the questions lets you know in advance that you should be listening especially carefully to the man's final statement.

**Q3.** List the six talk types in Part 4.

> ① Voice message ② Announcement ③ Advertisement ④ News report ⑤ Speech / talk ⑥ Tour guide
