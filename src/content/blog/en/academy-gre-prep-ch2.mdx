## Why GRE Vocabulary Is Different

GRE vocabulary operates at a distinctly elevated level — primarily featuring:
- Latinate/Greek-rooted academic words rarely used in everyday speech
- Words with multiple meanings requiring precise contextual interpretation
- Synonyms with subtle but test-critical distinctions

**Examples**: *sycophant* (flatterer), *equivocal* (ambiguous), *laconic* (brief), *precipitate* (to trigger/cause hastily)

---

## Text Completion Strategy

**Format**: 1–3 blanks in a sentence; select the word(s) that best complete it.

### Step-by-Step Approach

1. **Read the full sentence first** — understand the overall logic
2. **Notice logical signals**: *although, despite, however, indeed, not only...but also*
3. **Predict your own word before looking at choices**
4. **Match your prediction to the answer choices**

**Trap**: Don't let an impressive-sounding word distract you. The right answer fits the sentence's precise logic, not just the general topic.

---

## Sentence Equivalence Strategy

**Format**: 6 choices; select TWO words that both complete the sentence correctly with equivalent meaning.

### Core Principle

- The two correct words must be near-synonyms in the context of the sentence
- The sentence's meaning must be essentially the same with either word
- Watch for near-synonym pairs that DON'T fit the blank's required tone or meaning

## Reading Comprehension Strategy

GRE RC passages are more abstract and philosophically dense than TOEFL — they often involve contested scholarly arguments.

### Question Types

**Main Idea**: What is the author's primary purpose? (First and last sentence of passage are key)

**Inference**: What MUST be true based on the passage? (Not "could be true")

**Purpose**: Why does the author mention X? (Function within the argument)

**Weaken/Support**: Analyze the logical structure of the argument (similar to GMAT CR)
