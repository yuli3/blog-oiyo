## TOEFL Writing Overview

| Task | Time | Type |
|------|------|------|
| Task 1: Integrated | 20 min | Read + Listen → Compare/summarize |
| Task 2: Academic Discussion | 10 min | Add your opinion to an online discussion |
| **Total** | **29 min** | Score: 0–30 |

---

## Task 1: Integrated Writing

**Format**: Read 250–300 word passage (3 min) → Listen to lecture (lecture ALWAYS contradicts or qualifies the reading) → Write comparison summary (150–225 words, 20 min)

**Key rules**:
- Center on the lecture's counterarguments (not the reading)
- Do NOT include your own opinion
- Do NOT copy sentences verbatim from the reading

### High-Score Template

```
Paragraph 1 (Introduction, 2–3 sentences):
"The reading passage discusses [topic], arguing that [main reading claim].
The professor in the lecture, however, challenges/qualifies these points, 
contending that [main lecture claim]."

Paragraph 2 (First point, 4–5 sentences):
"First, the reading claims that [reading point 1].
The professor, however, argues that [lecture counterpoint 1].
According to the lecture, [specific explanation/example]."

Paragraph 3 (Second point):
"Second, the reading states that [reading point 2].
The professor challenges this by noting that [lecture counterpoint 2]."

Paragraph 4 (Third point):
"Finally, the reading argues that [reading point 3].
The lecture suggests, on the other hand, that [lecture counterpoint 3]."
```

## Task 2: Academic Discussion

**Format**: Two students have already posted opinions; add your own unique contribution (10 min, 100+ words minimum)

**Scoring priorities**:
- Your own **new idea** (don't just agree/disagree with existing posts)
- Specific reasons and examples
- Minimum 100 words (aim for 150+)

### High-Score Template

```
Opening (1–2 sentences — your position):
"In my view, [position]. While both [Student1] and [Student2] raise valid points, 
I believe [your core argument]."

Reasoning + Example (4–5 sentences):
"One key reason is that [reason]. For instance, [concrete example]. 
This suggests that [implication]."

Conclusion or additional angle (2–3 sentences):
"Furthermore, [additional idea]. Therefore, [final conclusion]."
```

## Academic Vocabulary for Writing

### Reading vs. Lecture Comparison Phrases

| Phrase | Context |
|--------|---------|
| contradicts | Lecture directly opposes reading claim |
| challenges | Lecture questions reading's validity |
| casts doubt on | Lecture weakens reading's credibility |
| supports/confirms | Lecture adds evidence for reading |
| elaborates on | Lecture expands on reading's point |

### Academic Discussion Advanced Phrases

- "It stands to reason that..."
- "One could argue that..."
- "Empirical evidence suggests..."
- "Drawing on personal experience..."

---

## Common Errors & Corrections

| Error | Fix |
|-------|-----|
| Copy-pasting reading sentences | Always paraphrase |
| Omitting the lecture's arguments | Lecture is the PRIMARY focus |
| Response too short | Task 1: min 150 words; Task 2: min 100 words |
| Inserting personal opinion in Task 1 | Strictly prohibited in Integrated Writing |
