# Mathematics for Economics — Integration Adds the Margins Back Together

If [chapter 1](/en/education-economics-math-ch1/) read the margin through derivatives, this chapter adds those margins up to recover totals and surplus. Matrices that solve inter-industry dependence in one go are in [chapter 5](/en/education-economics-math-ch5/), and constrained optimization in [chapter 3](/en/education-economics-math-ch3/).

## 1. A definite integral is an area, and that area is surplus

With demand $P=100-Q$ and a price of 40 won, the quantity traded is 60. Consumer surplus is $\int_0^{60} (100-Q-40)\,dQ = [60Q - Q^2/2]_0^{60} = 1800$ won.

If supply is $P=10+Q$, producer surplus at the same price is $\int_0^{60} (40-(10+Q))\,dQ = 900$ won, and total surplus is 2,700 won. When a tax is imposed, the deadweight loss is taken out of this area. The taxation chapter of microeconomics uses that picture.

## 2. The constant of the indefinite integral is fixed cost in economics

The fundamental theorem of calculus tells us that $\frac{d}{dQ}\int_0^Q \mathrm{MC}(q)\,dq = \mathrm{MC}(Q)$. We go from the margin to the total and back to the margin. But if $+C$ is not observed, the **level** of total cost is missing. If the data contain only the marginal cost curve, fixed cost has to be brought in separately from accounts or experiments.

<Callout type="warning">
Do not conclude that a larger area means greater welfare. Do not add surpluses from two markets measured in different units. If inverse demand shifts with income, Marshallian surplus diverges from the compensating and equivalent variations. An integral is only the area under a given curve; it does not identify the curve itself.
</Callout>

Log-linearization, $d \ln Y = \varepsilon\, d \ln X$, turns elasticities into sums. It is the starting point of growth accounting. Where those elasticities meet the multipliers of constrained optimization is [chapter 3](/en/education-economics-math-ch3/).

## Check your understanding

With demand $P=80-2Q$ and a price of 20 won, what is consumer surplus? $Q=30$, and $\int_0^{30} (80-2q-20)\,dq = [60q - q^2]_0^{30} = 1800-900 = 900$ won. If the price falls to 10 won, $80-2Q=10$ gives $Q=35$, and $\int_0^{35} (70-2q)\,dq = 70×35 - 35^2 = 2450-1225 = 1225$ won. The area captures both the price fall and the rise in quantity.
