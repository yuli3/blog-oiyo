## Learn the rules, and the game gets 10x more fun

Soccer's official rulebook is the **17 Laws of the Game**, written and maintained by the **International Football Association Board (IFAB)**. You don't need to memorize all 17 — just the core ideas below are enough to change how you watch a match.

---

## The basics — players, time, and scoring

```
Players: 11 per side (including 1 goalkeeper). A team can't
         continue with fewer than 7 players on the pitch.
Time:    Two 45-minute halves (90 minutes total) plus stoppage
         time. 15-minute halftime break. A drawn league match
         stays a draw; a knockout match goes to two 15-minute
         extra-time periods, then a penalty shootout.
Scoring: A goal counts once the ball fully crosses the goal
         line between the posts and under the crossbar. Most
         goals wins.
Subs:    Official matches typically allow up to 5 substitutions
         (varies by competition).
```

## The most confusing rule — offside

<a id="offside"></a>

Offside exists to stop a player from camping out near the opponent's goal, waiting for a pass.

```
A player is in an offside POSITION the moment the ball is
played to them if they are:
- in the opponent's half, AND
- ahead of the ball, AND
- closer to the goal line than both the ball and the
  second-to-last opponent (usually the last defender)

→ Being in that position alone is NOT a foul.
  It only becomes an offside OFFENSE if the player then
  actively gets involved in the play.
```

<small>* There is no offside from a player's own half, or when the ball comes from a throw-in, goal kick, or corner kick.</small>

## Fouls and how play restarts

| Situation | Restart |
|------|------|
| Regular foul | Opponent's **free kick** (direct or indirect) |
| Defensive foul inside the penalty box | **Penalty kick** |
| Ball goes out over the sideline | **Throw-in** (thrown in by hand) |
| Attacker knocks the ball out over the goal line | **Goal kick** |
| Defender knocks the ball out over the goal line | **Corner kick** |

## Warnings and dismissals — cards

```
Yellow card (caution): rough tackles, delaying the game,
dissent, etc. Two yellows in one match = red card.
Red card (sending-off): serious foul play, violent conduct,
or denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity (DOGSO).
→ A sent-off player cannot be replaced — their team simply
  plays the rest of the match a player short.
```

## VAR — video review

The Video Assistant Referee (VAR) only steps in to correct 4 categories of "clear and obvious errors":

```
1. Goals (checking for offside or a foul in the build-up)
2. Penalty kick decisions — awarded or overturned
3. Direct red cards
4. Mistaken identity (the wrong player was cautioned or sent off)
```

## Positions at a glance

```
GK (Goalkeeper): the only player allowed to use hands, and
                 only inside their own penalty box
DF (Defender):   stops the opponent from scoring
MF (Midfielder): links defense and attack, controls tempo
FW (Forward):    responsible for scoring goals
```

---

## Key takeaways

1. 11 players, 90 minutes — a goal only counts once the ball fully crosses the goal line.
2. Offside requires both conditions: being in an offside position AND getting involved in the play.
3. Fouls restart with a free kick or penalty kick; the ball going out restarts with a throw-in, goal kick, or corner kick.
4. Two yellow cards or one red card means an early exit for that player.
5. VAR only reviews four things: goals, penalties, red cards, and mistaken identity.

> This guide follows IFAB's official "Laws of the Game." Specific tournaments and leagues may apply their own supplementary regulations.
