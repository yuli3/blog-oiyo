Dreams are messages from your unconscious mind. In East Asian tradition, they were long read as omens of the future or guides for important decisions; in Western psychology, they're treated as a window into the unconscious. This guide draws on both perspectives to give you a richer way of interpreting your dreams.

<Callout type="info">
**Dreams and psychology**: Freud read dreams as "the disguised expression of repressed desire," while Jung saw them as "a compensatory message the unconscious sends to consciousness." Both agree that dreams are a key to understanding your inner world.
</Callout>

## Interpreting the Dreams You Have Most Often

### 1. Flying Dreams

**What it looks like**: flying freely, struggling to stay airborne, or a fear of falling

**Traditional reading**:
- Flying high and easily: an omen of achieving a goal, a promotion, success
- Flying with difficulty: a sign of effort to overcome a current struggle

**Psychological reading**:
- A desire for freedom from your current situation
- A state of elevated self-efficacy
- An inner attempt to view your circumstances from a new angle

### 2. Losing Teeth

**What it looks like**: teeth falling out one by one, breaking, or all falling out at once

**Traditional reading**:
- Separation from someone close, a shift in a relationship
- A signal to pay attention to your health
- The start of a significant change

**Psychological reading**:
- Anxiety about your self-image
- Fear of losing control
- Common during major transitions or turning points

<Callout type="tip">
**When tooth dreams recur**: they tend to show up around career changes, relationship shifts, or big decisions. It's your inner world adjusting to change.
</Callout>

### 3. Being Chased

**What it looks like**: fleeing from something or someone

**Traditional reading**:
- There's a situation in your waking life you want to escape
- You're avoiding an important responsibility or decision

**Psychological reading**:
- An unfaced problem or fear
- Jung's "Shadow" — the part of yourself you don't want to acknowledge
- Often shows up during periods of high stress or anxiety

**What to do**: imagine stopping in the dream and turning to face whatever is chasing you. Facing it, not avoiding it, tends to be the real solution.

### 4. Water Dreams

**What water symbolizes**: across East and West alike, water represents **the unconscious, emotion, and change**.

| State of the Water | Interpretation |
|--------|------|
| **Clear, calm water** | Inner peace, emotional stability |
| **Murky, turbid water** | Emotional turmoil, a need to clear something up |
| **Rough waves** | Strong emotion, a challenging situation |
| **Flood/overflow** | Emotions that feel out of control |
| **The ocean** | The vastness of the unconscious, infinite possibility |
| **A flowing river** | The flow of life, natural change |

**Traditional reading**:
- Clear water: good luck, prosperity, a sign of good things
- Murky water: gossip or rumor, a health warning
- Falling into the sea: the start of a major change

### 5. Fire Dreams

| State of the Fire | Interpretation |
|--------|------|
| **A bright, warm fire** | Passion, energy, growth |
| **An out-of-control fire** | Anger, an eruption of repressed feeling |
| **A house on fire** | Major change, the start of rebuilding |
| **A candle's flame** | Inner light, spiritual awakening |

**Traditional reading**:
- Seeing fire: an omen of wealth or success
- A house burning down: change within the family, a new beginning

### 6. Dreams About Death

This is one of the **most misunderstood dreams** of all.

**Traditional reading**: dreaming about death isn't necessarily a bad sign. It's actually more often read as a symbol of **rebirth, transformation, and new beginnings**.

**Psychological reading**:
- The end of one thing and the start of another
- Part of the self going through a transformation
- A season in life where you need to let something go

### 7. Exam or School Dreams

**What it looks like**: feeling unprepared for a test, or finding yourself back in school

**Psychological reading**:
- Facing evaluation or a challenge in waking life
- Anxiety around the question, "Am I ready enough?"
- An unconscious desire to learn from the past

**Adults who dream about school**: if you're still having school dreams as an adult, it's worth checking what "test" or evaluation you might currently be facing in your life.

### 8. Romance Dreams

| Dream Content | Interpretation |
|--------|------|
| Falling for a stranger | Projection of your ideal self onto the anima/animus within |
| Dreaming of an ex | Unprocessed feelings from a past relationship |
| A failed romance in the dream | Anxiety around self-expression |
| A happy romance | An unmet emotional need |

## East Asian Traditional Dream Symbols

### Auspicious Dreams

| Dream | Meaning |
|---|------|
| A dragon appears | Great success, or the birth of an exceptional child |
| Picking up gold | Wealth, good fortune |
| Flying through a clear sky | Achieving a goal, a promotion |
| Picking fruit | The fruits of your labor |
| Getting married | A new agreement, a merger, an achievement |
| Slaughtering a pig | Wealth, abundance |

### Inauspicious Dreams

| Dream | Meaning |
|---|------|
| Losing teeth | A warning about separating from someone close |
| A mirror breaking | A warning about a rift in a relationship |
| Missing a train or car | A chance you might miss out on |
| A flood | A situation that may be hard to handle |

<Callout type="warning">
**The limits of dream interpretation**: what a dream means can vary enormously depending on your personal situation and emotional state. Treat the interpretations above as general guidance, and read them together with your own feelings and circumstances.
</Callout>

## How to Keep a Dream Journal

To understand your dreams more deeply, consider keeping a **dream journal**.

1. **Keep a notebook by your bed**: write things down the moment you wake up
2. **Record the details**: colors, emotions, people, places
3. **Focus on the feeling**: the emotion you felt in the dream matters most
4. **Look for patterns**: note recurring dreams or themes over time
