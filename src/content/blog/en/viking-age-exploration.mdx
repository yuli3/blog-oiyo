## Two Myths, Immediately Dispelled

When you hear "Viking," you picture horned helmets and savage warriors. Both images are wrong.

First: **Viking helmets had no horns**. That image was invented by 19th-century Romanticism.

Second: "Viking" originally referred to the act of raiding voyages, not to all Scandinavian people. Most Norse people were farmers, fishers, and craftspeople. Those who went on raiding expeditions were a minority — and raiding was only one of their activities.

---

## The Viking Age: 793–1066 CE

By convention, the Viking Age begins with the raid on Lindisfarne monastery in northeast England in 793 CE — a sudden, devastating seaborne attack that shocked Christian Europe.

Why did Norse expansion begin then? Scholars point to population growth and farmland shortage, improvements in longship technology, inheritance laws that left younger sons landless, and the relative weakness of divided Frankish and Anglo-Saxon kingdoms.

---

## Three Directions at Once

Vikings expanded simultaneously in three directions.

**West (Norsemen)**: Raided and eventually settled the British Isles and northern France (Normandy). The **Norman Conquest of England in 1066** — led by William the Conqueror, himself of Viking descent — permanently transformed the English language and culture.

**South (Danes)**: Penetrated the Mediterranean, eventually establishing a Norman kingdom in Sicily.

**East (Swedish Varangians)**: Traveled Russian rivers to trade with Byzantium and Baghdad. They established the **Kievan Rus** — the political origin of both Russia and Ukraine.

<Callout type="tip">
**The origin of "Russia"**: Kievan Rus was founded by Swedish Vikings (Varangians) who unified Slavic tribes along the Dnieper river. The name "Rus" — which gives Russia its name — traces to these Norse founders. Russia's origins are partly Scandinavian.
</Callout>

## America: 500 Years Before Columbus

Around 1000 CE, **Leif Erikson** sailed west from Greenland and landed in North America, which he called "Vinland" — almost certainly modern Newfoundland, Canada.

This isn't legend. Archaeological excavation at **L'Anse aux Meadows** in Newfoundland confirmed a Norse settlement dating to approximately 1000 CE — 500 years before Columbus. It is the only confirmed pre-Columbian European site in the Americas.

Why didn't this become a permanent settlement? Indigenous resistance, the distance from Scandinavia, and insufficient colonizing population are the main explanations. The Norse came, looked, and left.

## The Viking Legacy

The Viking Age ended around 1066 with the waning of Norse power in England. Their legacy is more pervasive than most people realize:

- **Hundreds of English words** of Norse origin: sky, knife, window, husband, egg, ugly, wrong, anger, trust
- **Days of the week**: Thursday = Thor's day, Wednesday = Woden's day
- **Russia and Ukraine**: Kievan Rus as political origin
- **Democratic traditions**: Norse society made major decisions at the **Thing** (þing) — an assembly of free men voting on law and justice. Some historians trace Nordic democratic traditions to this institution.

Beyond the raiding, the Vikings were medieval Europe's most dynamic explorers, merchants, and state-builders. The history is richer than the myth.
