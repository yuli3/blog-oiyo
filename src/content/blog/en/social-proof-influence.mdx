## The Asch Conformity Experiments

In 1951, Solomon Asch conducted a series of laboratory experiments that revealed the terrifying power of conformity. Subjects were shown a line and asked to match it to one of three reference lines. The answer was obvious.
However, the subject was placed in a room with seven confederates (actors) who all agreed beforehand to give the _wrong_ answer.

- **Result**: 75% of subjects gave the wrong answer at least once to conform with the group.
- **Conclusion**: People will deny the evidence of their own senses to avoid social friction or the feeling of being an outlier.

## Informational vs. Normative Influence

Social proof operates through two distinct cognitive pathways:

1.  **Informational Social Influence**: "They must know something I don't." This occurs in ambiguous situations (e.g., choosing a restaurant in a new city). If everyone is eating at Restaurant A and Restaurant B is empty, we assume A is better. It is a heuristic for correctness.
2.  **Normative Social Influence**: "I want to be liked/accepted." This occurs even when the situation is unambiguous (like the Asch experiment). We conform to avoid ridicule or exclusion.

## Cialdini's "Influence"

Robert Cialdini, in his seminal book _Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion_, codified Social Proof as one of the six key principles of influence. He famously demonstrated it with towel reuse in hotels.

- **Standard Sign**: "Help save the environment." -> 35% reuse.
- **Social Proof Sign**: "75% of guests in this room reuse their towels." -> **44% reuse**.

## The Werther Effect

The World Health Organization describes evidence that the way suicide is reported, and the prominence and repetition of that coverage, can affect risk among vulnerable people. This is called suicide contagion or the **Werther effect**. It does not mean that one social cue explains an individual's actions. The public-health concern is narrower: sensational or repeated coverage, explicit descriptions of methods, and romanticizing a death can increase risk, while responsible communication that emphasizes recovery and help-seeking can be protective.

> **If you need support:** If you are thinking about suicide or self-harm, contact your local emergency or crisis service now. In the United States and its territories, call or text **988**.

> **Key takeaway:** People use others' behavior as a cue, but social influence is not a command that fully determines action. Media and content creators have a responsibility to consider that influence when communicating about high-risk topics.
