## The Largest Contiguous Empire in History

By area, the largest contiguous land empire in history was the **Mongol Empire (1206–1368)**. At its peak around 1270, it covered approximately 24 million km² — larger than the entire continent of Africa.

The man who built it was born in 1162 on the Mongolian steppe as a boy named Temujin.

---

## From Slave to World Conqueror

Temujin's early life was defined by hardship. His father was poisoned by rivals when Temujin was nine. He was briefly captured and enslaved. Through extraordinary will and talent for both warfare and diplomacy, he methodically united the fragmented Mongol tribes.

In 1206, a grand kurultai (tribal assembly) declared him **Genghis Khan** — "Universal Ruler."

<Callout type="tip">
**Meritocracy on the steppe**: Genghis Khan promoted based on ability, not birth. Enemy commanders who showed skill were incorporated into his army — sometimes given high command. This was revolutionary in an era dominated by aristocratic hierarchy.
</Callout>

---

## Why the Mongol Army Was Unstoppable

**Speed**: Mongol cavalry covered over 100 km per day. Enemies often found themselves encircled before they could respond.

**Tactical sophistication**: Feigned retreats luring enemies into killing zones, coordinated multi-column attacks, and devastating psychological warfare — cities that surrendered were treated leniently; those that resisted were annihilated.

**Intelligence**: A vast network of merchants and spies provided detailed knowledge of enemy territories, political divisions, and internal weaknesses — often weaponized before armies even arrived.

**Technology absorption**: The Mongols adopted Chinese siege engineers, Persian administrators, and other specialists from conquered peoples, using each conquest to prepare for the next.

---

## The Scale of Conquest

Key conquests by Genghis Khan and his successors:

- **Jin Dynasty (northern China)**: 1211–1234
- **Khwarezmian Empire (Central Asia, Iran)**: 1219–1221 — Samarkand, Bukhara, and other Islamic cultural centers destroyed
- **Russia and Eastern Europe**: 1237–1241 — Kievan Rus destroyed, Poland and Hungary invaded
- **Abbasid Caliphate (Baghdad)**: 1258 — capital sacked, caliph killed, effectively ending the Islamic golden age
- **Song Dynasty (southern China)**: 1271–1279

---

## Pax Mongolica and Legacy

Alongside unprecedented destruction, the Mongol Empire created **Pax Mongolica** — a period when the entire Eurasian landmass was safe for travel and trade. For roughly a century, merchants, diplomats, and scholars moved freely from China to the Black Sea.

The transfer of Chinese gunpowder, printing, and papermaking to the West happened through Mongol networks. Marco Polo's journey was possible only because of this peace.

---

## Why It Fell

After Genghis Khan's death (1227), the empire was divided among his sons. By the late 13th century, it had fragmented into four successor khanates, which gradually adopted the cultures of the peoples they ruled — Yuan China became Chinese, Ilkhanate Iran became Persian-Islamic.

In 1368, Chinese rebels expelled the Mongols and founded the Ming Dynasty. The Mongol empire was gone. But its impact on Eurasian history — the trade routes opened, the cultural transfers, the political maps redrawn — persists to the present.
