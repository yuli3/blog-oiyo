## Chapter 4 Overview: Every Being Can Become a Buddha

The *Lotus Sūtra* is one of the most influential scriptures in Mahāyāna Buddhism. Revered across Chinese, Korean, and Japanese Buddhism as the "king of scriptures," it is the foundational text of the Tiantai and Nichiren schools in particular.

The Lotus Sūtra is revolutionary because of a single declaration.

> **"All sentient beings, without exception, can attain Buddhahood."**

We'll explore why this declaration is so revolutionary, and what the bodhisattva way of life means for people today.

<Callout type="info">
**Goal of this lecture**: understand the Lotus Sūtra's core teaching of the One Vehicle and the parables in the Chapter on Skillful Means, and grasp the philosophical significance of the claim that "every being can attain Buddhahood." We'll also reinterpret the bodhisattva path as a modern way of living.
</Callout>

---

## 1. King of the Scriptures — Why the Lotus Sūtra Is Special

The Lotus Sūtra's full title is the ***Sūtra of the Lotus Blossom of the Wondrous Dharma***.

A lotus blooms out of mud yet is never stained by it. This is the sūtra's central metaphor: even within a defiled world (the mud), one's pure Buddha-nature remains untouched.

The sūtra is believed to have taken shape in India around the 1st–2nd century CE. In China, the translation completed by Kumārajīva in 406 CE became by far the most widely read version. It is a vast text comprising 28 chapters.

## 2. The One Vehicle — Uniting Three Vehicles Into One

Early and sectarian Buddhism taught that there were three paths, matched to a practitioner's capacity.

| Vehicle | Meaning | Goal |
|--------|------|------|
| **Śrāvakayāna (Vehicle of the Hearers)** | Practicing by directly hearing the Buddha's teaching | Arhatship (individual liberation) |
| **Pratyekabuddhayāna (Vehicle of the Solitary Realizers)** | Awakening alone by insight into dependent origination | The solitary, self-awakened one |
| **Bodhisattvayāna (Vehicle of the Bodhisattva)** | Working toward Buddhahood while saving other beings | The bodhisattva, then Buddhahood |

The Lotus Sūtra declares that these three vehicles are, in truth, a single Buddha Vehicle.

> **"I (the Buddha) spoke of three vehicles as skillful means, but in reality there is only one great vehicle."**
>
> — Lotus Sūtra, Chapter on Skillful Means

*In modern terms*: it's as if there are separate textbooks for beginners, intermediate, and advanced students, but in the end they're all one path leading to the same destination. Stopping once you become an arhat isn't true completion — you can advance further, toward full Buddhahood.

## 3. Two Great Parables from the Chapter on Skillful Means

The Chapter on Skillful Means contains two parables considered among the finest in Buddhist literature.

### The Parable of the Burning House

A wealthy man's house catches fire. His children, unaware it's burning, keep playing inside. Even when he shouts "Fire!" they don't come out.

So the father uses skillful means: "Outside there are carts pulled by sheep, by deer, by oxen!" The children come running out. But in fact, there was only a single great cart, drawn by a white ox (the One Vehicle), waiting outside.

*In modern terms*: to save beings within this world (the burning house), the Buddha used various skillful means (the three carts). But the ultimate truth (the One Vehicle) is singular. Skillful means aren't a lie — they're wise guidance suited to the listener's level.

### The Parable of the Prodigal Son

A wealthy man had a son. The son left home and lived in poverty for many years. One day he comes looking for work at his father's gate. The father recognizes his son, but the son doesn't recognize his father.

The father doesn't immediately say, "You are my son." He first hires him as a servant, gradually builds trust, and only then finally declares, "All of this wealth is yours."

*In modern terms*: we are all children of the Buddha — we're born with Buddha-nature, but we live having forgotten it. The Buddha reveals this truth gradually, meeting us at our own level.

## 4. The Devadatta Chapter — "Even the Wicked Can Attain Buddhahood"

One of the most shocking declarations in the Lotus Sūtra appears in the **Devadatta Chapter**.

In Buddhist tradition, Devadatta is the notorious figure who tried three times to kill the Buddha. The Lotus Sūtra declares that even he will attain Buddhahood in the future.

Another shock is **the Buddhahood of the Dragon King's daughter** — an eight-year-old, non-human being who instantly attains Buddhahood. The Buddhist common sense of the time held that a woman had to be reborn as a man before she could attain Buddhahood.

<Callout type="warning">
**A revolutionary declaration**: "even the wicked can attain Buddhahood," "even a woman can attain Buddhahood in her present body" — the Lotus Sūtra directly overturned the common assumptions of the Buddhist world of its time. This is why it's called a "revolutionary scripture."
</Callout>

## 5. The Bodhisattva Path — A Way Traveled Together

Another core teaching of the Lotus Sūtra is the way of being called the **bodhisattva**.

A bodhisattva has the capacity to attain Buddhahood, but postpones complete personal liberation, choosing instead to remain in the world until all beings can awaken together.

> **"I will not enter nirvana first. I will not attain Buddhahood until hell itself is emptied."**
>
> — the vow of Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva

There is a set of bodhisattva vows known as the **Four Great Vows**:

1. **However innumerable sentient beings are, I vow to save them all.**
2. **However inexhaustible afflictions are, I vow to end them all.**
3. **However immeasurable the teachings are, I vow to learn them all.**
4. **However supreme the Buddha way is, I vow to attain it.**

---

## 6. What Does the Bodhisattva Path Mean for Us Today?

You don't need to take the bodhisattva path literally. Reinterpreted for today, it might be put this way:

**"Don't wait until you're fully ready."**

We often think: *once I've succeeded enough, once I'm wise enough, then I'll help others.* The bodhisattva path inverts this logic.

Even before you attain full awakening, you can contribute to others right now. In fact, contributing to others is itself part of the path to awakening.

<Callout type="tip">
**A modern reading of the bodhisattva path**: the bodhisattva path isn't self-sacrifice. It's a way of living that pursues your own growth and your contribution to others at the same time. It resonates deeply with what psychologist Martin Seligman called "the meaningful life" — a life devoted to something larger than yourself.
</Callout>

## 7. Famous Passages from the Lotus Sūtra, in Modern Terms

> **"The Tathāgata appears in the world for one great purpose."**
>
> — Chapter on Skillful Means

*In modern terms*: there's a single great reason the Buddha came into this world — to open the path of awakening to every being.

> **"When I first sat beneath the bodhi tree and attained awakening, in truth I had already attained Buddhahood countless ages before."**
>
> — Chapter on the Life Span of the Tathāgata

*In modern terms*: the Buddha isn't confined to a single historical figure — he is eternal truth itself.

> **"Even one who, upon hearing this sūtra, gives rise to a single moment of joy has already planted the seed of Buddhahood."**

*In modern terms*: a small act of kindness, a brief moment of openness — these, too, are already a step toward awakening.

## Key Takeaways

1. The Lotus Sūtra's One Vehicle teaching — all three vehicles (hearer, solitary realizer, bodhisattva) ultimately converge into a single Buddha Vehicle.
2. The Parable of the Burning House and the Parable of the Prodigal Son — skillful means aren't lies, but wise guidance.
3. The declaration that even the wicked and women can attain Buddhahood was revolutionary for its time.
4. The bodhisattva path doesn't wait for perfection — it's a way of walking alongside others, right now.
5. The Lotus Sūtra's teaching becomes a philosophical foundation for what we'd today call "a meaningful life."
