# Chapter 6. The Blood of the Business: Financial Management

If Operations is the "Heart" and Marketing is the "Face," then Finance is the **"Blood"** of the organization. It carries the energy (capital) needed for the business to move, grow, and survive. No matter how great a product is, a company cannot exist without proper capital allocation.

Today, we dive into the core of **Corporate Finance**—how companies raise money and how they choose to spend it.

---

## 1. The Golden Rule: Time Value of Money (TVM)

The most fundamental concept in finance is that <mark><strong>"A dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow."</strong></mark> Why? Because you can invest today's dollar to earn interest.

## 3. The Cost of Growth: WACC

Money isn't free. Companies must pay interest to lenders and provide returns to shareholders. The average cost of all these sources is the **WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital).**

- **The Objective**: To find projects that earn a return _higher_ than the WACC. This is how a company creates true "Economic Value Add" (EVA).

<Callout type="important">
  **The Risk-Return Trade-off**: You cannot get higher returns without taking more risk. Financial
  management is about finding the "Efficient Frontier" where the company gets the maximum return for
  the level of risk it can handle.
</Callout>

---

## 4. Conclusion: Financing the Future

Financial management is about more than just accounting; it’s about <mark><strong>"Maximizing Firm Value."</strong></mark> By choosing the right investments and the right mix of debt and equity, a manager ensures the long-term health and growth of the corporation.
