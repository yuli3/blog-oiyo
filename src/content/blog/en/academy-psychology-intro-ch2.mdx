## The Biological Basis of Behavior

Thinking, feeling, and moving are never separate from the body. The brain and nervous system take in sensory information, support judgment, and regulate the body's responses. That's why psychology looks at biological foundations, not just the mind alone.

Still, it would be too simple to say "every behavior is caused by the brain." The brain is constantly interacting with the environment, experience, learning, and social relationships.

## Basic Structures

| Concept | Role |
|------|------|
| Neuron | The basic information-transmitting cell of the nervous system |
| Synapse | The junction where information passes between neurons |
| Central nervous system | Brain and spinal cord — integrates and regulates information |
| Peripheral nervous system | Connects the central nervous system to the rest of the body |
| Hormones | Chemical signals that act more slowly but more broadly |

Neurons pass information through electrical and chemical signals. This process is tied to learning, emotion, movement, and memory.

## A Caution About Brain Regions

Introductory texts often link specific brain regions to specific functions — the prefrontal cortex to planning and control, the amygdala to emotional response, the hippocampus to memory. But the real brain is a network of regions working together.

So it's worth being cautious about oversimplified claims like "happiness lives in one specific spot."

## Example: The Stress Response

When something feels threatening, the body may respond with a faster heartbeat, muscle tension, and heightened attention. This system helps with survival. But when it keeps firing in situations that aren't actually dangerous, it can lead to fatigue and anxiety over time.

## Key Takeaways

- Behavior and mental life are connected to the brain, nervous system, and hormones.
- Neurons and synapses are the basic units of neural communication.
- Brain function tends to emerge from networks of regions rather than a single area working alone.
- Psychological experiences like the stress response need to be understood together with bodily responses.
