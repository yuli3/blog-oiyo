## Does U.S. Pessimism Have a Case?

Pessimism about the U.S. economy isn't just vague anxiety — there are concrete cases behind it. Three deserve a clear look.

### Case 1: A Fiscal Crisis Scenario

U.S. federal debt has passed 120% of GDP, and interest costs now exceed defense spending. If debt keeps rising, Treasury yields could eventually spike and trigger a fiscal crisis.

Plausibility: **Low to moderate.** A country that can print its own currency is unlikely to face a technical default. But dollar debasement and high inflation are realistic risks.

### Case 2: Weakening Dollar Dominance

China, Russia, and Middle Eastern oil producers are reducing dollar settlement in favor of their own currencies or the yuan. If dollar demand keeps falling over the long run, dollar dominance could weaken.

Plausibility: **Valid long-term, overstated short-term.** With no real substitute for the dollar, rapid change is unlikely.

### Case 3: Political Fragmentation

Deepening political division inside the U.S. makes effective economic policymaking harder. Continued congressional gridlock would weaken America's ability to respond to economic challenges.

Plausibility: **Moderate.** Already underway, and a negative for long-term growth potential.

### Responding to Pessimism: Diversify and Hedge

Even without fully endorsing U.S. pessimism, it's worth holding positions that hedge against these three cases. Gold, non-dollar assets, and TIPS are the tools.
