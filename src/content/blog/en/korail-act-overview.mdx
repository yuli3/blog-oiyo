### Introduction: Finding the Legal Roots of KORAIL

The **'Korea Railroad Corporation Act'** (KORAIL Act) is a document that explains why KORAIL exists, what it can do, and how it is operated. For those looking to work or study the South Korean rail industry, understanding the legal basis of its primary operator is essential.

Today, we will analyze the core contents of the KORAIL Act that define its identity.

---

### 1. Purpose of Establishment (Article 1)

KORAIL is different from general companies that aim only for profit.

> **Article 1 (Purpose):** The purpose of this Act is to contribute to the development of the railroad industry and the development of the national economy by establishing the Korea Railroad Corporation to enhance the professionalism and efficiency of railroad operation.

- **Key Objectives:** ① Enhancing professionalism/efficiency of railroad operation, ② Development of the railroad industry, ③ Development of the national economy.
- **Insight:** "Publicness," which helps the economic interests of the entire nation by efficiently operating the national key transportation network, is emphasized.

### 2. Capital and Contribution (Article 4)

How large is KORAIL?

- **Capital:** **22 trillion KRW**, **all contributed by the government** (Article 4(1)). This number shows up often in exams. An answer choice saying the government contributes only part of it is wrong.
- **Contribution in kind:** The state contributes the **operating assets** split under Article 22 of the Framework Act to the corporation in kind (Article 4(3)).

### 3. Major Business Scope (Article 9) - The Most Important Part

KORAIL does more than run trains. Article 9(1) lists ten lines of business, and Article 9(2) says it may also carry them out **abroad**.

### 4. Officers and the CEO — Governed by the Public Institutions Act

The Korea Railroad Corporation Act has no article on the number of officers or how the CEO is appointed. As a public corporation, KORAIL follows the Act on the Management of Public Institutions.

- **CEO:** Recommended by the officer recommendation committee, proposed by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure and Transport as the competent minister, and appointed by **the President** (Public Institutions Act Article 25, for large public corporations).
- **Directors and auditor:** Their number and appointment also follow that Act and the corporation's articles of incorporation.

### 5. Guidance and Supervision by the Government (Article 16)

The Minister of Land, Infrastructure and Transport guides and supervises these parts of the corporation's work (Article 16):

1. Annual business plans and budgets
2. Improving railway service quality
3. Carrying out railway business plans
4. Securing railway safety, including facilities, rolling stock and train operation
5. Other matters set by other laws

### Wrapping Up: Act Containing KORAIL's Present and Future

Studying the KORAIL Act reveals that KORAIL is more than just a train-operating organization; it is a giant platform company that develops cities, creates culture, and expands globally.
