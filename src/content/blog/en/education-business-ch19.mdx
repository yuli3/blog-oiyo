## 1. Drivers of Globalization

**Globalization**: A phenomenon where the integration of trade, investment, culture, and technology across countries intensifies.

### Four Drivers of Globalization (Yip)

| Driver | Content |
|------|------|
| Market Drivers | Consumer homogenization, increase in global customers |
| Cost Drivers | Economies of scale, low-wage production locations |
| Government Drivers | Tariff removal, intellectual property treaties |
| Competitive Drivers | Emergence of global competitors, first-mover pressure |

## 2. Foreign Entry Strategies

### Relationship Between Entry Modes and Control, Risk, and Return

| Mode | Control | Risk | Resource Commitment |
|------|--------|------|----------|
| Exporting | Low | Low | Low |
| Licensing | Low | Low | Low |
| Franchising | Moderate | Low | Moderate |
| Joint Venture (JV) | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate |
| Strategic Alliance | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate |
| Wholly Owned Subsidiary (WOS) | High | High | High |
| Merger & Acquisition (M&A) | High | High | Very High |

**Greenfield Investment**: Establishing a new factory or office locally (Long time, High control).
**Brownfield Investment**: Acquiring and renovating existing facilities (Short time, Hidden liability risk).

## 3. Multinational Enterprise Theories

### Eclectic Paradigm (OLI Paradigm, Dunning)

Explains the reasons why firms choose foreign direct investment (FDI) through three advantages.

| Advantage | Content |
|------|------|
| O (Ownership) Advantage | Firm-specific assets such as brands, technology, and managerial know-how |
| L (Location) Advantage | Access to local cheap labor, resources, and markets |
| I (Internalization) Advantage | Direct operation is more advantageous than contracting (preventing technology leakage) |

### Types of Global Strategies (Bartlett & Ghoshal)

| Strategy | Global Integration | Local Responsiveness |
|------|------------|------------|
| Global Strategy | High | Low (Standardization) |
| Multidomestic Strategy | Low | High (Localization) |
| International Strategy | Low | Low (Home-country centered) |
| Transnational Strategy | High | High (Balanced) |

## 4. Managing Cultural Differences

### Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions (6 Types)

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Power Distance | Degree of acceptance of power inequality |
| Uncertainty Avoidance | Degree of anxiety regarding uncertainty |
| Individualism vs. Collectivism | Individual vs. collective identity |
| Masculinity vs. Femininity | Performance/competition vs. quality of life/cooperation |
| Long-term vs. Short-term Orientation | Future investment vs. current tradition |
| Indulgence vs. Restraint | Degree of permissiveness in satisfying desires |

### Culture Shock U-Curve
Local adaptation through 4 stages: Honeymoon → Distress → Adjustment → Integration.

### Cultural Management Strategies
- **Ethnocentrism** (Home-country orientation): Transplanting home culture and practices as they are
- **Polycentrism** (Host-country orientation): Autonomous operations in each country
- **Geocentrism** (Global integration): Sharing best practices globally

## 5. Exchange Rates and Global Finance

### Types of Exchange Rate Risk

| Type | Content |
|------|------|
| Transaction Risk | Exchange rate fluctuations during settlement of foreign currency contracts |
| Translation Risk | Exchange rate differences when consolidating financial statements |
| Economic Risk | Impact of exchange rates on long-term competitiveness |

### Hedging Instruments
- **Forward Contract**: Fixing future exchange rates
- **Currency Swap**: Exchanging interest and principal between two currencies
- **Currency Option**: Protection against unfavorable exchange rate movements

## 6. Global Supply Chain and Trade

**Comparative Advantage (Ricardo)**: Mutual benefits arise when countries specialize in what they do relatively best.

**National Diamond Model (Porter)**: Four determinants of national competitive advantage.
- Factor Conditions (Quality and quantity of production factors)
- Demand Conditions (Quality of the domestic market)
- Related and Supporting Industries (Competitiveness of suppliers and related industries)
- Firm Strategy, Structure, and Rivalry
