## What Is a Stock?

A **stock (share/equity)** represents partial ownership of a company. When you buy a stock, you become a **shareholder** with a proportional claim on the company's assets, earnings, and growth.

**Shareholder rights**:
- Receive dividend payments (if the company pays them)
- Vote at shareholder meetings
- Claim on residual assets in liquidation (after creditors)

**Why stock prices rise**:
- Company earnings increase → stock price typically rises
- Future growth expectations improve → P/E multiple expands
- Broad market sentiment improves (bull market)

## Core Valuation Metrics

### Market Capitalization
**Market Cap = Share Price × Shares Outstanding**

Represents the total value the market assigns to a company.

### P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)
**P/E = Share Price / Earnings Per Share (EPS)**

- Lower = potentially undervalued (relative to peers)
- Higher P/E = market pricing in higher future growth
- S&P 500 historical average: ~16–17x earnings

### P/B Ratio (Price-to-Book)
**P/B = Share Price / Book Value Per Share**

- P/B < 1: Trading below liquidation value
- Particularly relevant for banks, financials, industrial companies

### ROE (Return on Equity)
**ROE = Net Income / Shareholders' Equity × 100**

- Higher = more efficient use of shareholder capital
- Warren Buffett's key metric; target 15%+ consistently
