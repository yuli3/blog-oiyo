## 1. The Wobbling Axis: Precession of Earth's Rotational Axis

Earth is not a perfect sphere, and its rotational axis is tilted by about **23.5 degrees**. Yet this axis is not fixed; like a spinning top, it slowly traces a circle. This is called **precession**.

- **Cycle**: About 25,800 years (the Platonic Year)
- **Effect**: Over tens of thousands of years, this interaction changes Earth's climate and the positions of constellations. The “turning of the age (Aeon)” described in ancient astrology is based on this astronomical phenomenon.

## 2. The Aesthetics of Orbits: Multiple Layers of Orbital Cycles

We simply call one trip of Earth around the Sun “a year,” but cycles from a cosmic perspective are much more complex.

### 2.1 Earth's Orbit

- **Average speed**: About 107,000 km/h.
- **Philosophical implication**: We seem to be standing still, but at every moment we move at tremendous speed toward the Sun and onward with it. From a cosmic perspective, “stagnation” is an impossible concept.

### 2.2 The Solar System's Galactic Orbit (Galactic Year)

The Sun orbits around the center of the Milky Way (Sagittarius A\*).

- **Cycle**: About 225 million to 250 million years.
- **Significance**: Since humanity emerged, the Sun has not completed even **0.0001 of an orbit** around the galaxy. This shows how momentary our hardships and adversities are in cosmic time.

## 3. The Weight of Destiny: Gravity and Periodicity

Every orbit is sustained by gravity. When planets with different periods—from Mercury to Neptune—align (a conjunction), they create subtle gravitational fluctuations on Earth.

Empirical systems such as “Four Pillars of Destiny” or “astrology” can be seen as ancient attempts to analyze how this vast **Cosmic Rhythm** affects human biological hormones and the formation of fetal neural networks.
