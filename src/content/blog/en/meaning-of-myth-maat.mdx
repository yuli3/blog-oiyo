Maat is both a goddess and a cosmic principle in Egyptian mythology. She represents truth, justice, balance, rightful order, and social trust, and was regarded as the standard that gods, kings, and humans should follow. When Maat collapses, the world leans toward chaos, and the king's duty was to establish and maintain Maat.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Maat's most representative scene is the judgment of the dead person's heart. In the afterlife, the heart of the deceased is weighed against Maat's feather on a scale. If the heart balances with the feather, the person is recognized as having lived in accordance with rightful order and can proceed into the afterlife.

Maat was also a central principle of kingship. The pharaoh was depicted offering Maat to the gods, meaning that the king bore responsibility for maintaining the order of the world. Maat was not abstract morality alone, but a practical order that included taxation, judgment, ritual, truthfulness in speech, and social balance.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Maat is the archetype of the **inner scale** and **just order**. This archetype does not deny emotion or desire, but keeps them centered so they do not break the balance of the whole. In modern terms, it resembles law and ethics, transparency in accounting, honesty in research, and trust in relationships.

The key to reading Maat is that truth goes beyond simple fact-checking and becomes the balance of life itself. A lie is not merely a problem in one sentence; it can bring down relationships and institutions. The Maat archetype therefore shows that individual conscience and social systems cannot be separated from each other. When the scale of the heart collapses, the scale of the world also trembles.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Maat a goddess or a concept?**

A. Both. Maat is personified as a goddess, while also being the core principle of truth and cosmic order.

**Q. What does Maat's feather mean?**

A. It is the standard of truth and balance compared with the heart in posthumous judgment. It symbolically reveals whether a life was aligned with order.
