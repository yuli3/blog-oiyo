## What Is Take-Home Pay?

If you've accepted a $75,000 job offer, the amount that hits your bank account each month is around **$4,900 to $5,200** — not $6,250. The gap of roughly $1,050 to $1,350 per month (17–21%) goes to taxes and mandatory deductions. Without understanding this gap, budget plans break down from day one.

**Take-home pay (net pay)** is your gross salary minus federal income tax, state income tax (varies by state), Social Security (6.2%), and Medicare (1.45%). Optional deductions like 401(k) contributions and health insurance premiums reduce your taxable income but also lower your monthly take-home.

Pre-tax benefit elections — such as contributing to a 401(k), HSA, or FSA — are the primary lever for legally increasing what you keep each month. A $200/month 401(k) contribution saves you roughly $44/month in federal taxes (at the 22% bracket), meaning your take-home only drops by $156, not $200.

### Deduction Structure at a Glance

```
Gross Salary
  − Social Security Tax (6.2%, up to $168,600 wage base in 2024)
  − Medicare Tax (1.45%, no cap)
  − Federal Income Tax (marginal rates 10%–37%)
  − State Income Tax (0% to 13.3% depending on state)
  − Pre-tax deductions (401k, HSA, FSA, health insurance)
= Monthly Take-Home Pay
```

## 3. FICA Taxes: Social Security and Medicare (2024)

| Tax | Total Rate | Employee Share | Employer Share | Notes |
|-----|-----------|----------------|----------------|-------|
| Social Security | 12.4% | 6.2% | 6.2% | Applies up to $168,600 in wages |
| Medicare | 2.9% | 1.45% | 1.45% | No wage cap |
| Additional Medicare | 0.9% | 0.9% | — | Applies above $200,000 (single) |

### The Social Security Wage Cap in Practice

Once your wages exceed $168,600 (2024), you stop paying the 6.2% Social Security tax on additional earnings. This creates an effective paycheck increase mid-year for high earners. A $300,000 earner saves $8,153 on Social Security compared to a world with no cap.

<Callout type="tip" title="HSA: The Best Pre-Tax Account Most People Underuse">
A Health Savings Account (HSA) paired with a High-Deductible Health Plan offers a triple tax advantage: contributions are pre-tax, growth is tax-free, and qualified withdrawals are tax-free. The 2024 contribution limit is $4,150 (individual) or $8,300 (family). Unlike FSAs, HSA balances roll over indefinitely — making them an effective retirement savings vehicle for healthcare costs.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="A Common Negotiation Mistake">
Focusing only on base salary means missing the employer 401(k) match, benefits value, and equity upside. A job offering $90,000 with a 6% 401(k) match ($5,400/year) and fully paid health insurance can be worth more than a $95,000 offer with no match and $300/month health premiums. **Compare total compensation, not just base salary.**
</Callout>
