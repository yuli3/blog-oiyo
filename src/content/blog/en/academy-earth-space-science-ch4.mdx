## Lecture 4 Overview: From Stars to the Whole Universe

Stars (Lecture 3) gather into galaxies, and galaxies together make up the universe. This lecture tackles the biggest question of all — how did the universe begin, and where is it headed?

<Callout type="info">
**Goal of this lecture**: Understand the structure of galaxies, the evidence for the Big Bang and cosmic expansion, and the dark matter and dark energy that make up most of the universe.
</Callout>

---

## 1. Galaxies — Cities of Stars

A galaxy is a massive structure of hundreds of billions of stars, gas, and dust, bound together by gravity.

```
Classified by shape:
- Spiral galaxies: with arm structures (the Milky Way, Andromeda)
- Elliptical galaxies: round or oval, dominated by older stars
- Irregular galaxies: no clearly defined shape

The Milky Way:
- A barred spiral galaxy, about 100,000 light-years across
- The Sun sits in the outer suburbs, about 26,000 light-years from the center
```

---

## 2. The Big Bang — the Universe's Beginning

The **Big Bang theory** holds that the universe began about **13.8 billion years ago** from a single point and rapidly expanded from there.

```
Key evidence for the Big Bang:
1. Cosmic expansion — galaxies are moving apart from each other (Hubble's law)
2. The cosmic microwave background (CMB) — the "afterglow" of the Big Bang (about 2.7K)
3. The ratio of light elements — the observed ratio of hydrogen to helium matches predictions
```

<Callout type="tip">
**The Big Bang wasn't an "explosion at a point" — it was the expansion of space itself.** Like dots drawn on the surface of a balloon that spread apart as it inflates, the universe isn't expanding outward from any single center — all of space is stretching at once.
</Callout>

---

## 3. Cosmic Expansion and Redshift

More distant galaxies recede faster (**Hubble's law**). Light from a receding object gets stretched to longer wavelengths and appears redder — this is **redshift**.

```
Nearby galaxy → small redshift → recedes slowly
Distant galaxy → large redshift → recedes quickly
→ direct evidence that the universe is expanding
```

---

## 4. Dark Matter and Dark Energy — 95% of the Universe

Remarkably, the ordinary matter we know — stars, planets, our own bodies — makes up only about **5%** of the universe.

| Component | Share | What we know |
|------|------|------|
| Ordinary matter | About 5% | Stars, gas, and everything we're familiar with |
| **Dark matter** | About 27% | Doesn't interact with light; detected only through gravity |
| **Dark energy** | About 68% | An unknown force accelerating the universe's expansion |

<Callout type="warning">
Dark matter and dark energy are "dark" only in name — their true nature is still **unknown**. They're required to explain galaxy rotation speeds and the accelerating expansion of the universe, but what they actually are remains one of the biggest unsolved problems in modern physics. For more, see [95% of the Universe Is Unknown](/en/dark-matter-dark-energy-universe).
</Callout>

---

## 5. The Future of the Universe

Because dark energy is accelerating the expansion, the leading scenario is that the universe will expand and cool forever (the "Big Freeze"). But since we don't know what dark energy actually is, that future isn't set in stone.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Galaxies come in spiral, elliptical, and irregular forms; the Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy.
2. The evidence for the Big Bang (about 13.8 billion years ago) includes cosmic expansion, the cosmic microwave background, and the observed ratio of elements.
3. The Big Bang wasn't an explosion — it was the expansion of space itself.
4. Redshift is direct evidence of cosmic expansion.
5. About 95% of the universe is made up of the still-mysterious dark matter and dark energy.
