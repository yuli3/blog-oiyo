## A Century of Wrong Thinking

Through most of the 20th century, the received wisdom in neuroscience was stark: once you reach adulthood, your brain is fixed. No new neurons, no recovery from damage, no significant structural change.

From the 1960s onward, cracks appeared in this view. By now, one of neuroscience's most important findings is firmly established:

**The brain continuously remodels itself in response to experience.** This is **neuroplasticity**.

---

## Hebb's Rule: Neurons That Fire Together, Wire Together

In 1949, neuropsychologist Donald Hebb proposed the key mechanism:

> "Neurons that fire together, wire together."

Repeated experience or practice strengthens the synaptic connections between the neurons involved — the biological basis of learning and memory. Conversely, unused connections weaken and eventually disappear.

"Use it or lose it" is not a metaphor. It's cellular biology.

---

## London Taxi Drivers and the Hippocampus

In 2000, Eleanor Maguire's team scanned the brains of London black cab drivers. The results were striking.

The drivers' **posterior hippocampus** — the region responsible for spatial navigation — was significantly larger than in controls. Crucially, the longer the driving career, the larger the hippocampus.

Memorizing thousands of London streets (the legendary "Knowledge" exam) had physically restructured the brain.

<Callout type="tip">
**The reversal**: A follow-up study found that taxi drivers who switched to using GPS navigation saw their hippocampi shrink. The brain builds up what it uses and prunes what it doesn't — continuously.
</Callout>

## Stroke Recovery: New Hope

Stroke was once considered permanent damage. Neuroplasticity research transformed rehabilitation.

Through intensive targeted training, **other brain regions can take over functions lost to stroke damage**. Constraint-induced movement therapy — forcing patients to use a paralyzed hand by restraining the healthy one — induces cortical reorganization and produces measurable functional recovery.

The brain can reroute. It just needs the right stimulus.

---

## Meditation Changes the Brain

Harvard neuroscientist Sara Lazar's team scanned long-term meditators. Cortical thickness was greater in regions related to attention, sensory processing, and interoception. 50-year-old meditators had cortical thickness comparable to 25-year-old non-meditators in these regions.

Even 8 weeks of mindfulness meditation produced measurable changes: the amygdala (fear/stress response center) shrank; gray matter in the prefrontal cortex (rational decision-making) increased.

---

## Are New Neurons Born in Adults?

Until the 1990s, adult neurogenesis was considered impossible. Research has now shown that new neurons are generated in the **dentate gyrus** of the hippocampus throughout adult life. Regular aerobic exercise is one of the strongest known promoters of this process.

---

## Practical Implications

1. **Learn something genuinely new**: An instrument, a language, a sport — novel challenges build new neural patterns
2. **Exercise**: Promotes neurogenesis and increases BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor)
3. **Sleep**: Memory consolidation happens during sleep
4. **Meditate**: Strengthens attention and emotional regulation circuits
5. **Stay socially connected**: Complex social interaction provides diverse cognitive stimulation

Your brain is not the hardware you were born with. It is living tissue that builds, prunes, and rebuilds itself — as long as you live.
