The haetae is an imaginary animal known in Korea and East Asia as a symbol of justice and protection from disaster. It is connected to the Chinese xiezhi tradition, and in Korea it is familiar as a symbolic figure placed at palaces, government offices, and in city imagery. The haetae was understood as a being that could distinguish right from wrong and gore the guilty, and as one that blocked the force of fire. For that reason, the haetae is not merely a decorative animal, but a mythic symbol that carries the meaning of both public order and disaster prevention.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

The haetae is transmitted as an animal that judges disputes and strikes the guilty with its horn. This image contains the hope that the law should not merely listen to what people say, but should possess a sacred sense that recognizes the essence of a matter. This is also why the haetae was used as a symbol of law and inspection.

In Korea, the haetae was also widely accepted as a symbol that prevents fire. The placement of haetae statues in front of Joseon palaces is explained as a symbolic device meant to suppress the disaster of fire in a city with many wooden buildings. The haetae's appearance and function vary slightly by tradition, but its basic image of blocking disaster and establishing order remains consistent.

The haetae has survived powerfully as a symbol rather than through one specific long narrative. It has continued to transform through sculptures in front of buildings, city branding, and images of law and justice. When a mythic animal is placed in a real public space, people feel that an invisible order has appeared before their eyes in visible form.

## Reading as an Archetype — Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, the haetae is the archetype of the **discerning guardian**. It is not a being that protects unconditionally; it protects by distinguishing right from wrong. In modern terms, it resembles oversight institutions, ethical standards, whistleblowing, and safety systems that prevent danger in advance.

The haetae archetype says that justice should not remain an abstract slogan, but should stand at actual boundaries. It recognizes wrongdoing, suppresses the force of fire, and endures at the gate of the community. For that reason, the haetae remains a persuasive symbol in an age that needs both law and safety.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Are haetae and haechi the same being?**

A. The imaginary animal called haetae in Korea is connected to the Chinese xiezhi tradition. Its name and appearance changed across regions and periods.

**Q. Why did people say the haetae prevents fire?**

A. Because it was understood as a guardian object that symbolically suppressed the disaster of fire. This meaning was especially emphasized in wooden architecture and palace spaces.
