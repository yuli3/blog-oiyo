## Overview: The Starting Point of Every Relationship

*How to Win Friends* contains some thirty principles, but at the very base sit just three pillars — the basics of basics in handling people. Internalize these three and the rest follow naturally.

<Callout type="info">
**Goal of this lesson**: Understand and apply the three fundamental principles — don't criticize, give honest appreciation, and arouse the other person's desire.
</Callout>

---

## 1. Principle — Don't Criticize, Condemn, or Complain

Carnegie opens his book with stories of hardened criminals. Even men on death row believed "I did nothing wrong." **Almost no one blames themselves.** Criticism puts people on the defensive, wounds their pride, and ultimately breeds only resentment.

> **"Criticism is like a homing pigeon. It always returns home."**

<Callout type="tip">
When you want to change someone, instead of criticizing, try first to **understand the reasons from their side.** Not "why can they only do this much?" but "how would I have acted in their situation?" Understanding always changes people more than blame.
</Callout>

---

## 2. Principle — Give Honest, Sincere Appreciation

The deepest human craving is **to be recognized as important.** Philosopher John Dewey and psychologist William James both named it the core of human nature. Yet we are stingy in filling that craving.

Sincere appreciation brings people to life. The key, though, is **"sincere."**

<Callout type="warning">
**Flattery and appreciation are different.** Flattery is fake, selfish, and from the lips. Appreciation is genuine, unselfish, and from the heart. People sense the difference instinctively. Empty praise actually erodes trust.
</Callout>

- Appreciate specifically ("you did well" → "the way you summarized that report's conclusion was clear")
- Recognize not just results but effort and attitude
- The habit of looking for something to appreciate itself transforms relationships

---

## 3. Principle — Talk About What the Other Person Wants

The only way to move people is **to give them what they want.** Saying what *you* want is useless. You must connect the other's desire to your proposal.

> **"I like strawberries and cream, but when I fish I bait the hook with worms."**

<Callout type="note">
Carnegie called this "arouse in the other person an eager want." Instead of forcing a child to study, show how studying connects to the future they want — that's the essence of persuasion. **The ability to see the world through the other's eyes** is the foundation of all relationship skills.
</Callout>

---

## 4. The One Thread Through All Three

The three principles point to the same place — **from self-centered to other-centered.** Not criticizing, appreciating, and starting from the other's desire are all about shifting your gaze from "me" to "you."

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Criticism breeds only resentment — to change someone, understand them first.
2. The deepest human craving is to feel important.
3. Sincere appreciation is powerful, but fake flattery erodes trust.
4. To move people, start from what they want, not from your own desire.
5. The shared root of all three principles is "from self-centered to other-centered."
