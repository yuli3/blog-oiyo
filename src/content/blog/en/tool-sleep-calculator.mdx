# Sleep Calculator

You sleep 8 hours but still wake up exhausted. Here's why: sleep moves through **90-minute cycles (REM + non-REM)**. If an alarm fires in the middle of a cycle — especially during deep sleep — you're yanked out of the most restorative phase and feel groggy for hours.

The key is not the total number of hours, but **completing full cycles**.

## What Is a Sleep Cycle?

Sleep repeats approximately **every 90 minutes** through 4 stages:

| Stage | Type | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Light sleep | Easily woken, 5–10 min |
| Stage 2 | Light sleep | Body temperature drops, heart rate slows |
| Stage 3 | Deep sleep (slow-wave) | Physical repair, growth hormone release |
| Stage 4 | REM sleep | Dreaming, memory consolidation, emotional processing |

<Callout type="tip">
**Ideal sleep**: Completing 4–6 cycles (6–9 hours). 6 full cycles (9 hours) maximizes physical recovery, but 4 clean cycles (6 hours) is better than 7 interrupted hours.
</Callout>

## Chronotype — Are You a Morning Lark or Night Owl?

| Type | Characteristics | Ideal Bedtime | Ideal Wake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning lark | High focus early in the day | 9–10 PM | 5–6 AM |
| Intermediate | Most common type | 10–11 PM | 7–8 AM |
| Night owl | Peak productivity late at night | 12–1 AM | 8–9 AM |

Chronotype is largely genetic but can be shifted moderately with consistent sleep habits.

## Sleep Debt

Falling 1 hour short each night accumulates **7 hours of sleep debt** in a week — the cognitive equivalent of a blood alcohol concentration of 0.1%. Weekend catch-up sleep helps but doesn't fully restore performance.

This calculator does not diagnose sleep disorders (sleep apnea, insomnia, etc.). If you experience persistent sleep problems, consult a healthcare professional.
