In the Dangun myth, Ungnyeo is the being who begins as a bear, keeps a taboo, and becomes a human woman. She is remembered as Dangun's mother, but her meaning is not limited to simple motherhood. At the center of Ungnyeo are a cave-like time without sunlight, the rough foods of mugwort and garlic, and the body's rite of passage through long endurance. For that reason, Ungnyeo is a symbol that holds the vitality of the earth, patience, transformation, and the pain paid in order to become human.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

The bear and the tiger ask Hwanung to make them human. Hwanung gives them a handful of mugwort and twenty cloves of garlic, and tells them that if they endure for the appointed period without seeing sunlight, they can become human. The tiger cannot endure and leaves, but the bear keeps the taboo and becomes a woman. This passage shows a mythic way of thinking in which humanity is not only something one is born with, but something acquired through restraint and waiting.

After becoming Ungnyeo, she has no one to marry, so she prays beneath the Sindansu for a child. Hwanung briefly transforms into a human, marries Ungnyeo, and Dangun Wanggeom is born between them. The wording and emphasis differ by transmission, but Ungnyeo is both the biological mother of the founding ancestor and the subject who completes the mythic transformation.

When reading this story by today's standards, care is needed not to reduce a woman only to the function of childbirth. Ungnyeo's core lies less in "she gave birth" than in "she changed." She is a being who passes between wilderness and humanity, darkness and community, desire and restraint, and she carries the strongest inner transformation in the Dangun myth.

## Reading as an Archetype — From Jung's Perspective

From Jung's perspective, Ungnyeo is an archetype in which the **Earth Mother** overlaps with the **initiate of transformation**. In darkness, she sheds an old form and gains a new identity. In modern terms, she resembles scenes of passing through a time no one else can endure on one's behalf: long training, recovery, migration, or reconstruction after loss.

The Ungnyeo archetype is not only a gentle symbol of motherhood, but also a symbol of strong patience. She changes not on a shining stage, but through unseen inward time. Reading Ungnyeo therefore reminds us that change does not come only as an external blessing, but is made within the time endured by the body and its habits.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Does Ungnyeo represent bear totem belief?**

A. Some views interpret her that way, but it cannot be asserted with certainty. In a myth dictionary, she is read carefully as a symbol where bear, woman, earth, and transformation overlap.

**Q. Why did the tiger fail to become human?**

A. Within the story, it is because the tiger could not keep the taboo to the end. Symbolically, it can be seen as a contrast showing the difference between wild impulse and patience.
