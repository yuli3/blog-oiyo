## 20 Core Grammar Patterns

### Pattern 1: Parts of Speech

The most frequently tested pattern. Look at the blank's position to decide whether a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb is needed.

**Position rules:**
- Noun slot: article/possessive + ___ ; or preposition + ___
- Adjective slot: be verb + ___ ; or ___ + noun
- Adverb slot: before/after a verb; before an adjective or adverb; at the start of a sentence
- Verb slot: subject + ___ + object/complement

**Memorize word families in sets of four:**

| Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
|------|------|-----------|--------|
| decision | decide | decisive | decisively |
| consideration | consider | considerable | considerably |
| management | manage | managerial | — |
| approval | approve | approved | — |

---

### Pattern 2: Verb Tense

Choose the tense using time-signal words in the sentence.

| Signal word | Tense |
|-------------|-------|
| yesterday, last year, ago | Simple past |
| tomorrow, next week, soon | Future |
| by + future time | Future perfect |
| since, for + period, recently, already | Present perfect |
| when + past, while + past progressive | Past / past progressive |

- "Sales ___ steadily since last year." → **have increased** (present perfect)
- "The project ___ by next Friday." → **will have been completed** (future perfect)

---

### Pattern 3: Active vs. Passive

**Choose passive when:**
- No object follows the blank (the action receiver is the subject)
- The agent is unknown or unimportant

- "The document ___ by the director." → **was signed**
- "The new policy ___ next month." → **will be implemented**

If a direct object follows the blank → active voice.

---

### Pattern 4: Subject-Verb Agreement

Match verb number to the subject, ignoring intervening phrases.

**Watch out for:**

1. Modifying phrase between subject and verb:  
   "The manager *of all departments* **is**..." (subject = manager)

2. Collective nouns (staff, committee, team) → singular verb:  
   "The staff **is** meeting tomorrow."

3. Either A or B / Neither A nor B → verb agrees with B (closer noun):  
   "Either the manager or the employees **are** responsible."

4. Indefinite pronouns:  
   - Singular: each, every, either, neither, anyone, everyone  
   - Plural: both, several, many, few

---

### Pattern 5: Relative Pronouns and Adverbs

| Antecedent | Subject | Object | Possessive |
|------------|---------|--------|-----------|
| Person | who | whom | whose |
| Thing/animal | which | which | whose / of which |
| Either | that | that | — |

**How to choose:**
- Blank followed by a verb → subject relative pronoun (who, which, that)
- Blank followed by subject + verb → object relative pronoun (whom, which, that)
- Blank followed by a noun → possessive (whose)

**Relative adverbs:**

| Antecedent | Adverb |
|------------|--------|
| Place (city, office…) | where |
| Time (day, year…) | when |
| Reason | why |
| Way / manner | how |

---

### Pattern 6: Prepositions

**Time prepositions:**

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| at | specific time | at 3 p.m., at noon |
| on | date / day | on Monday, on June 5 |
| in | month / year / season / morning/afternoon | in March, in the morning |
| by | deadline (completion) | by Friday |
| until | continuation to a point | until midnight |
| for | duration | for two weeks |
| since | from a past point until now | since 2020 |
| during | throughout an event | during the meeting |

**High-frequency preposition phrases:**
- **as of** [date]: effective from / as at
- **due to / owing to / because of**: before a noun phrase
- **in spite of / despite**: although (before a noun phrase)
- **in addition to**: besides, as well as
- **with regard to / regarding**: concerning
- **prior to / before**: earlier than
- **following / after**: subsequent to

---

### Pattern 7: Conjunction vs. Preposition

Conjunction + **clause (subject + verb)**; preposition + **noun (phrase)**.

| Conjunction | Preposition equivalent |
|-------------|----------------------|
| because + clause | because of + noun |
| although/though + clause | despite / in spite of + noun |
| while + clause | during + noun |
| when + clause | upon / at the time of + noun |
| so that + clause | for the purpose of + noun |

- "Although it rained, the event continued." ✓
- "Despite the rain, the event continued." ✓
- "Despite it rained…" ✗

---

### Pattern 8: Conditional Sentences

| Type | Structure | Meaning |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Real (Type 1) | If + present, will + base | likely future condition |
| Unreal (Type 2) | If + past, would + base | hypothetical present |
| Past unreal (Type 3) | If + past perfect, would have + p.p. | hypothetical past |

- "If we **receive** your order by noon, we **will ship** it today." (Type 1)
- "If she **were** available, she **would attend**." (Type 2)

### Patterns 9–20: Quick Reference

| Pattern | Key rule |
|---------|---------|
| 9. Articles (a/an/the) | Specific/known → the; first mention or countable → a/an |
| 10. Gerund vs. infinitive | "enjoy/avoid/finish + -ing"; "want/agree/decide + to" |
| 11. Participle phrases | Dangling modifier: subject of main clause = doer of participle |
| 12. Comparative/superlative | Two items → -er / more; three or more → -est / most |
| 13. Parallel structure | "A, B, and C" — all the same grammatical form |
| 14. Pronoun agreement | Pronoun matches antecedent in number and gender |
| 15. Causative verbs | have/make/let + object + base; get + object + to-inf |
| 16. Reported speech | "He said he **was** tired." (backshift in tense) |
| 17. Negation | barely/hardly/seldom = semi-negative; avoid double negatives |
| 18. Inversion | "Not until…" / "No sooner…" → inverted auxiliary + subject |
| 19. Noun clause | "I know **that** he left." / "whether" for yes/no indirect questions |
| 20. Vocabulary in context | Choose by meaning in sentence, not dictionary definition |

## Part 6 Strategy

Part 6 adds one unique question type not found in Part 5: **sentence-insertion questions**. Otherwise, the grammar rules above apply.

**Sentence-insertion approach:**
1. Read the full paragraph, not just the immediate context
2. The inserted sentence must flow logically from the preceding sentence and into the following one
3. Look for pronoun referents and discourse connectors ("However," "In addition," "As a result")

**Part 6 vocabulary questions:** the correct word must fit both grammatically and contextually with the whole passage — not just the blank's immediate surroundings.

## Practice Quiz

**Q1.** Which word correctly fills the blank? "The ___ of the new product line will be announced next week."

(A) launch (B) launching (C) launched (D) launches

> **(A) launch** — noun slot (the + ___ + of); "launch" is the noun form.

**Q2.** Choose the correct form: "The manager as well as the employees ___ in the training session."

> **is** — "as well as" is a parenthetical phrase, not a compound subject; the subject remains "the manager" (singular).

**Q3.** Choose the correct connector: "___ the heavy rain, the outdoor event was not cancelled."

(A) Although (B) Because (C) Despite (D) While

> **(C) Despite** — the blank is followed by a noun phrase ("the heavy rain"), so use the preposition *despite*, not the conjunction *although*.
