## The best way to understand the universe

Studying the universe isn't really about memorizing facts — it's about getting the order of the right questions straight. This hub walks through 10 big questions about the cosmos so you can build a mental map of astronomy and cosmology in one pass.

---

## 1. Where exactly is our place in the universe?

The first question is always one of position.

- Is Earth even a planet?
- How big is the solar system, really?
- Parallax and measuring distance
- Henrietta Leavitt and standard candles
- Galaxies and the scale of the universe

Core intuition: **We are not at the center of the universe — we're standing in just one of many reference frames.**

---

## 2. How did we learn what we now know?

The history of cosmic knowledge is also the history of the telescope.

- Naked-eye astronomy
- Galileo and the telescope
- The electromagnetic spectrum
- Radio astronomy
- Space telescopes and modern observatories

Core intuition: **Our picture of the universe doesn't change through philosophy alone — it changes when our instruments do.**

---

## 3. Why has the universe evolved the way it has?

- Atom formation after the Big Bang
- The nebular hypothesis and the formation of the solar system
- The frost line and planetary composition
- Planetary migration and collisions

Core intuition: **The universe we see today isn't a random snapshot — it's the outcome of a long evolutionary process.**

---

## 4. How old is the universe?

- The cosmic microwave background
- Cosmic inflation
- The distance ladder
- Dark energy and the Hubble tension
- Dark matter and structure formation

Core intuition: **The age of the universe isn't a single number — it's a consensus built from multiple independent lines of evidence.**

---

## 5. What is the universe made of?

- The history of chemistry and atomic theory
- Nucleosynthesis inside stars
- Particle physics and quarks
- Particle accelerators and fundamental particles

Core intuition: **Understanding the universe means looking at the very largest and very smallest things at once.**

---

## 6. What is life, exactly?

- Origin-of-life experiments
- The RNA world hypothesis
- Natural selection and complexity
- Extremophiles
- The possibility of artificial life

Core intuition: **Life looks like a miracle, yet it can also be read as an organizing principle of matter and information.**

---

## 7. Are we alone in the universe?

- The Drake equation
- SETI
- The habitable zone
- The Fermi paradox
- Kardashev civilization types

Core intuition: **"Is there alien life?" is actually an easier question than "why is it so quiet out there?"**

---

## 8. How did the universe begin?

- The basics of quantum mechanics
- Unification of the forces
- The matter-antimatter problem
- Quark confinement
- Scenarios for the early universe

Core intuition: **The moment you ask how the universe began, astronomy runs straight into particle physics — and philosophy.**

---

## 9. How will the universe end?

- Earth's long-term fate
- Galaxy collisions
- Open, closed, and flat universes
- The Big Freeze, the Big Crunch, and the Big Rip

Core intuition: **Scenarios for the end of the universe come down to reading its current composition and expansion.**

---

## 10. What is the relationship between everything and nothing?

- How the concept of "nothing" has changed
- Quantum fluctuations and cosmic origins
- The problem of "before the Big Bang"
- The multiverse
- The fine-tuning problem

Core intuition: **Cosmology always eventually meets philosophy at its edges.**
