# Consumer Rights Complete Guide

"No refunds" is not always legally enforceable. Consumer protection law in most developed countries grants you rights that override retailer policies — but you have to know them to use them.

---

## 1. Cooling-Off Periods: Your Right to Change Your Mind

Most countries give consumers a statutory period to cancel distance purchases (online, phone, mail order) without giving a reason.

| Country | Cooling-Off Period | Applies To |
|---|---|---|
| **EU / EEA** | **14 days** | All distance contracts (B2C) |
| **UK** | **14 days** | Online, phone, door-to-door sales |
| **Australia** | 10 business days (door-to-door only) | In-store: no statutory right |
| **United States** | 3 business days (FTC Rule) | In-home sales only; no federal online right |
| **Canada** | 7–10 days | Varies by province |

<Callout type="warning">
**The US has no federal cooling-off period for online purchases.** Your rights depend on the retailer's own return policy. However, credit card chargebacks provide a separate layer of protection for fraudulent or misrepresented items.
</Callout>

### Exceptions to Cooling-Off Rights (EU/UK)

Even within the cooling-off period, vendors can refuse returns for:
- Perishable goods
- Sealed goods opened after delivery (hygiene/health reasons)
- Digital downloads once accessed
- Bespoke/personalised items
- Airline tickets, hotel bookings, event tickets (time-sensitive)

## 2. Statutory Warranty Rights

Unlike voluntary "manufacturer warranties," **statutory warranty rights** are set by law and cannot be waived by a retailer's terms.

### By Country

| Country | Statutory Period | Key Provision |
|---|---|---|
| **EU** | 2 years minimum | Seller liable for defects existing at delivery |
| **UK** | 6 years (England) | Products must be of "satisfactory quality" (SOGA) |
| **Australia** | Varies (ACL) | Consumer guarantees cannot be excluded |
| **United States** | UCC implied warranty | "Merchantability" and "fitness for purpose" implied |

<Callout type="tip">
**EU/UK tip**: If a defect appears within **12 months** of purchase, it's legally presumed to have existed at the time of sale — the seller must prove otherwise. After 12 months, you must prove the defect was pre-existing (harder, but still possible).
</Callout>

### Your Remedies (EU Consumer Sales Directive)

1. **Repair** (free of charge)
2. **Replacement** (if repair impossible or takes too long)
3. **Price reduction** (proportional to defect severity)
4. **Full refund** (if repair and replacement fail, or significant non-conformity)

## 3. Misleading Advertising: Your Rights

If you were misled into a purchase, you may be entitled to rescind the contract and seek damages.

### What Counts as Misleading?

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| False claims | "Clinically proven" without evidence |
| Material omissions | Hiding significant fees or limitations |
| False origin | Claiming "made in Italy" when manufactured elsewhere |
| Fake reviews | Paying for positive reviews without disclosure |
| Comparison advertising | Unfair or false product comparisons |

<Callout type="note">
**FTC (US)**: Requires truthful, non-deceptive advertising. Influencers must disclose paid partnerships. File complaints at ftc.gov/complaint.

**UK ASA**: The Advertising Standards Authority accepts free consumer complaints at asa.org.uk.

**EU**: Each member state has a national consumer protection authority under the CPC Network.
</Callout>

---

## 4. Subscription Traps and Recurring Billing

Subscriptions are a major source of consumer complaints. Key rights:

| Issue | Your Right |
|---|---|
| Free trial converts to paid | Must be clearly disclosed; cancel before billing date |
| Cancellation obstacles | EU/UK: Must offer same ease of cancellation as sign-up |
| Hidden auto-renewal | Many US states require explicit consent |
| App store purchases | Google Play: 48 hours; Apple: up to 90 days for certain issues |

<Callout type="tip">
**Stop recurring charges**: Use your card issuer's "block merchant" or "manage subscriptions" feature. In the US, the Fair Credit Billing Act lets you dispute unauthorized charges within 60 days. In the EU, you can instruct your bank to reverse unauthorized direct debits (SEPA).
</Callout>

---

## 5. Chargeback: Your Credit Card Protection

If a merchant refuses to honor your legal rights, your credit card issuer may reverse the charge.

| Scenario | Chargeback Eligible? |
|---|---|
| Item not received | ✅ Yes |
| Significantly not as described | ✅ Yes |
| Service not rendered | ✅ Yes |
| Merchant refused valid return | ✅ Possibly (Visa/Mastercard rules vary) |
| Change of mind (no legal right) | ❌ Usually not |
| Fraud / unauthorized charge | ✅ Yes (always) |

**File within**: 60–120 days of statement date (varies by card network).

## 6. Dispute Resolution Resources

| Country | Resource | How to Access |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | FTC Consumer Sentinel | reportfraud.ftc.gov |
| **UK** | Citizens Advice | 0800 144 8848 |
| **EU** | European Consumer Centres | ec.europa.eu/consumers/ecc |
| **Australia** | ACCC | accc.gov.au/consumers |
| **Global** | eConsumer.gov | International scam reporting |

<Callout type="warning">
**Time limits matter.** Chargeback windows close (typically 60–120 days). Statutory warranty claims must be made within the limitation period (1–6 years depending on country). Don't delay — gather evidence and act promptly.
</Callout>
