## Lesson 4 Overview: Not All Bullish Crosses Are Equal

With theory (wave, time, price) covered, we return to the lines. We'll take the bullish/bearish crosses and cloud breakouts from the beginner series and read them one level deeper — by **where they happen.**

<Callout type="info">
**Goal of this lesson**: Understand how the Tenkan-sen, Kijun-sen, Chikou Span, and Senkou Span divide their roles, and learn to grade bullish and bearish crosses by their location relative to the Cloud and Kijun-sen.
</Callout>

---

## 1. Re-mapping Each Line's Role

| Line | Timeframe | Character |
|----|------|------|
| Tenkan-sen (9) | Present | Short-term balance and direction |
| Kijun-sen (26) | Present | The mid-term trend's "spine" |
| Chikou Span | 26 periods back | Contrasts current strength against the past (confirmation) |
| Senkou Span A/B (Cloud) | 26 periods ahead | Future support/resistance |

<Callout type="tip">
**The Kijun-sen's slope is the true measure of the trend.** No matter how sharply the Tenkan-sen turns up, if the Kijun-sen is flat, it's not a "real trend" yet. Only once the Kijun-sen turns upward has the trend actually stood up.
</Callout>

---

## 2. Grading Bullish and Bearish Crosses by Location

Even when the Tenkan-sen crosses above the Kijun-sen (a bullish cross), its reliability depends heavily on **where** it happens.

```
Strong bullish cross: occurs ABOVE the Cloud (with the trend)
Moderate bullish cross: occurs INSIDE the Cloud (direction unclear — wait)
Weak bullish cross: occurs BELOW the Cloud (a counter-trend bounce — be cautious)
```

A bearish cross (downward) is symmetric — a bearish cross below the Cloud is the strongest sell signal.

---

## 3. The Chikou Span — The Most Underrated Confirmation Line

The Chikou Span is nothing more than "today's close, plotted 26 periods back" — but Ichimoku Sanjin treated it as critical.

```
- Chikou Span breaks ABOVE the candle from 26 periods ago → bullish confirmation
- Chikou Span drops BELOW the candle from 26 periods ago → bearish confirmation
- Chikou Span gets "stuck" against a past candle and stalls → trend hesitation
```

<Callout type="note">
**The Chikou Span isn't a lagging afterthought — it's a filter.** Even if the Tenkan-sen and Cloud signals look good, if the Chikou Span is still blocked by a past candle, that breakout is incomplete. The signal is only complete once the Chikou Span confirms its "clear space" breakout.
</Callout>

---

## 4. Cloud Thickness and Twists, in Depth

```
- A thick Cloud: strong support/resistance (a breakout needs a lot of energy)
- A thin Cloud: weak support/resistance (easily passed through — watch for false breakouts)
- A Cloud twist (Senkou Span A/B crossing): foreshadows a trend change 26 periods ahead
  → flag it as a future signal on your watchlist in advance
```

A zone where the Cloud thins out is a signal that a breakout may be approaching — watch it especially closely when it overlaps with a change day (Time Theory).

---

## 5. Reading the Lines Together

```
Ideal bullish alignment:
price > Cloud / Tenkan-sen > Kijun-sen (rising) / Chikou Span > past candle
→ When all three line up, trend confidence is at its highest (next lesson: three-role reversal)
```

<Callout type="warning">
Don't judge on a single line. A bullish Tenkan-sen cross, a position above the Cloud, Chikou Span confirmation, and a rising Kijun-sen — only commit when these elements **all point the same direction.** If they disagree, wait.
</Callout>

---

## This Lesson's Key Takeaways

1. The Kijun-sen's slope is the measure of a "real trend" — the Tenkan-sen alone isn't enough.
2. Bullish and bearish crosses are graded by where they occur (above/inside/below the Cloud).
3. The Chikou Span's "clear space breakout" past old candles is what completes a signal.
4. Cloud thickness and twists reveal support/resistance strength and foreshadow future turns.
5. Confidence is highest when the Tenkan-sen cross, Cloud position, Chikou Span, and Kijun-sen all agree.
