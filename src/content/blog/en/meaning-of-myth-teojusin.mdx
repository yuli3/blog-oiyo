Teojusin is a household deity in Korean folk belief who protects the site of a home. Because a house was understood not merely as a building but as a living world established on a particular piece of land, that ground was believed to have its own guardian. Teojusin is understood as a being who protects household peace and prosperity together with other household deities such as Seongjusin, Jowangsin, and Eopsin. The ways of enshrining Teojusin vary by region and household, and the deity is often associated with a teojugari, a jar, the backyard, or the area near the jangdokdae.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Teojusin is not so much the protagonist of a long narrative as a deity whose presence appears through everyday rituals. When people built a house or moved, they prayed for the site to be peaceful, and when trouble arose in the household, they sometimes considered whether the energy of the site had become disturbed. This reflects a way of thinking in which the stability of life comes not only from inside the building but also from one's relationship with the land.

The methods for honoring Teojusin differed by region and household. In some places, people kept a jar filled with rice or a straw-made teojugari and offered food during holidays or gosa rites. Traditions vary, but Teojusin is generally connected with the rear of the house, the area around the jangdokdae, and stable spaces in the yard. Teojusin is not a conspicuous chief deity, but the deity who supports the floor of the home.

Belief in Teojusin is also connected with wealth. People believed that only when the site was stable could the family remain together and the household prosper. Yet the core of Teojusin is less about becoming rich than about "settling into one's place." Choosing a place to live, avoiding conflict with it, and turning unfamiliar land into a home all belong to Teojusin's domain.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Teojusin is the **guardian of place** and the **archetype of foundation**. Teojusin symbolizes the ground that supports life rather than a dazzling goal. In modern terms, this resembles scenes that are not conspicuous but form the basis of every activity, such as securing residential stability, organizing a studio, or establishing the basic rules of an organization.

The Teojusin archetype asks, "Where are you standing?" Even a good plan cannot last long if its ground is unstable. For this reason, Teojusin has even greater meaning in an age of frequent movement. Teojusin is a symbol of forming a relationship with the land, respecting the place where one stays, and slowly building the center of daily life.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Teojusin the same deity as Seongjusin?**

A. Both are household deities, but their roles are distinct. If Seongjusin is regarded as the great presiding deity of the household, Teojusin is closer to the stability of the land and site on which the house stands.

**Q. Should belief in Teojusin be seen only as superstition?**

A. In modern terms, apart from whether one holds the belief, Teojusin can be read as a symbol of an ethic of daily life that does not treat residential space and land carelessly.
