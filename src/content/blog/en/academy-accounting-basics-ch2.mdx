[Chapter 4](/en/education-accounting-ch2/) showed how the financial statements interlock. From this chapter on, each statement is examined in detail. The statement of financial position shows assets, liabilities and equity at a single point, the end of the reporting period. **Classification** matters as much as the amounts: whether the same borrowing is a current or a non-current liability changes the short-term solvency that creditors and investors read.

## 1. Presentation: current/non-current or order of liquidity

K-IFRS 1001 (IAS 1) in principle requires assets and liabilities to be presented **classified as current and non-current**. Where a presentation based on liquidity provides more reliable and relevant information, as for banks, all assets and liabilities may be presented **in order of liquidity**. Either way, the amounts expected to be recovered or settled within and after twelve months must be identifiable from the notes.

## 2. Criteria for current assets and current liabilities

### A. Current assets

An asset is current if any of the following applies.

- It is expected to be realized, or is intended to be sold or consumed, within the normal operating cycle.
- It is held primarily for trading.
- It is expected to be realized within twelve months after the reporting period.
- It is cash or a cash equivalent that is not restricted.

The normal operating cycle is the time from buying raw materials to selling products and collecting cash. Where the cycle is longer than twelve months, as in shipbuilding, receivables and inventory recovered within the cycle are current even beyond twelve months.

### B. Current liabilities

A liability is current if it is expected to be settled within the normal operating cycle, is held for trading, or is due to be settled within twelve months after the reporting period. There is one more important criterion: a liability is current if, **at the end of the reporting period, the entity does not have the right to defer settlement for at least twelve months**.

### C. Covenants and liability classification

The amendments effective from 2024 (IAS 1, *Classification of Liabilities as Current or Non-current* and *Non-current Liabilities with Covenants*) clarified how to judge this right. Where a loan agreement carries financial covenants such as a debt ratio, **only covenants with which the entity must comply on or before the reporting date** affect classification at that date. Covenants to be complied with after the reporting period do not change classification, but the risk of breach is disclosed in the notes. Management's intention to repay early does not affect classification.

## 3. Reclassifying long-term borrowings to current

A non-current liability is moved to current when, with the passage of time, its repayment date falls within twelve months.

## 5. What the statement of financial position cannot show

<Callout type="warning">
Most amounts in the statement of financial position are historical cost or amortized cost. Land bought 20 years ago may differ greatly from today's market price, and internally generated value such as brands or people is not recognized as an asset. The carrying amount of equity is therefore not the entity's market value. It is also a balance on a single day: repaying borrowings briefly at year-end and borrowing again early in the new year can make liabilities look small. Read quarterly balances and the cash flow statement together.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

At the end of 20X1, company A has current assets of ₩24 million and current liabilities of ₩12 million. Of its non-current long-term borrowings of ₩15 million, ₩5 million is repayable in September 20X2. Another loan of ₩8 million carries a covenant of "debt ratio no more than 200% at every reporting date"; A breached it at the end of 20X1 and had not obtained a waiver from the bank by the reporting date. What is the current ratio after reclassification?

The ₩5 million repayable within twelve months moves to the current portion of long-term debt. The ₩8 million loan is current because A breached a covenant it had to meet at the reporting date and so has no right to defer settlement for at least twelve months. A waiver obtained after the reporting period does not change the classification at the end of 20X1. Current liabilities are $1{,}200 + 500 + 800 = 2{,}500$ (₩10,000), and the current ratio is $2{,}400 / 2{,}500 = 96\%$ — less than half the 200% before reclassification.
