## 1. The Science of Dreams: REM Sleep and How Dreaming Works

Why do we dream at all? Dreams were mysterious territory for most of human history, but modern neuroscience is steadily uncovering how they actually work.

### What is REM sleep

Dreams occur mainly during **REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep.** REM cycles recur roughly every 90 minutes and make up about 20-25% of total sleep time. During REM, the eyes move rapidly, the brain is nearly as active as when awake, but the body's muscles go into a state of paralysis — so you don't physically act out what's happening in the dream.

### Why we dream: current theories

**Memory consolidation theory**: During sleep, the brain integrates the day's incoming information into long-term memory. Dreaming may be part of filtering out irrelevant information while reinforcing important memories.

**Emotional processing theory**: According to sleep researcher Matthew Walker, the brain reprocesses emotionally intense memories during REM sleep, reducing their emotional charge. This is supported by the fact that people with PTSD frequently show disrupted REM sleep.

**Threat simulation theory**: Proposed by Finnish neuroscientist Antti Revonsuo, this theory holds that dreaming is an evolutionary mechanism where the brain rehearses potential threats to prepare for them.

## 2. Jung's Psychology of Dream Interpretation

The foundation of modern psychological dream interpretation was laid by **Carl Gustav Jung (1875-1961).**

### The unconscious and the collective unconscious

Jung divided the human psyche into the **conscious, the personal unconscious, and the collective unconscious.** He saw dreams as messages the unconscious sends to the conscious mind.

The **collective unconscious**, in particular, holds archetypal images and themes shared across humanity — which is why people around the world tend to report similar dream themes (falling, flying, being chased).

### Dream archetypes

In Jung's framework, the figures that appear in dreams often represent psychological archetypes:
- **Anima**: the feminine aspect within the male unconscious
- **Animus**: the masculine aspect within the female unconscious
- **Shadow**: the parts of ourselves we don't want to acknowledge
- **The Wise Old Man/Woman**: inner wisdom and guidance

## 3. 30 Common Dreams, Interpreted

### Fear and anxiety dreams

**1. Falling dreams**
Falling from an apartment or a great height is one of the most common dreams of all.

*Psychological interpretation*: Reflects anxiety about losing control in real life — instability around your career, relationships, or status. People often wake up right before hitting the ground, which is linked to a muscle spasm known as the hypnic jerk.

*Traditional folk interpretation*: In many folk traditions, falling dreams are read as an omen of losing status or failing — though psychologically, they're more often reinterpreted as a signal that change and a fresh start are needed.

**2. Being chased**
Being chased and fleeing from someone or something — the most common of all fear dreams.

*Psychological interpretation*: Symbolizes a problem or emotion you're avoiding in waking life. If the pursuer is a person, it often points to conflict in a specific relationship; if it's a monster or an unclear figure, it points to vague, unnamed anxiety. Jung's advice — "if you dream you're being chased, stop and turn around" — means facing the real-life issue head-on.

**3. Teeth falling out**
Teeth loosening or falling out is considered an especially ominous dream in Korean and other East Asian traditions.

*Psychological interpretation*: Reflects a loss of confidence, anxiety about appearance, or difficulty expressing yourself. Teeth symbolize both the ability to "bite into" things assertively and your smile as a social image. Psychologists often read it as anxiety about not being able to fully express yourself at work or in social relationships.

*Traditional folk interpretation*: In Korean folk tradition, this dream is sometimes read as foretelling a death or major change in the family. (There's no scientific basis for this.)

**4. Dying**
Dreaming that you die, or witnessing someone else's death.

*Psychological interpretation*: Death dreams symbolize **an ending and a new beginning.** In Jung's framework, the death of the self in a dream is a metaphor for transformation. Dreaming of your own death often reflects a turning point in life, or a wish to shed an old identity — it is not a literal premonition.

**5. Taking an exam**
Sitting for an exam unprepared, or being unable to find the exam room.

*Psychological interpretation*: One of the most common dreams well into adulthood. It reflects anxiety about being evaluated, self-doubt about your abilities, and often shows up ahead of a real decision or challenge.

**6. Flying**
Soaring freely through the sky.

*Psychological interpretation*: One of the most positive dream types. It symbolizes freedom, liberation, and a sense of accomplishment. It often occurs after overcoming a difficult problem, or when a new perspective has opened up. It's especially common in lucid dreams.

### Fortune and misfortune: animal dreams

**7. Snakes**
A dream given special weight in Korean tradition.

*Traditional folk interpretation*: The bigger and more vivid the snake, the more it's read as a sign of coming wealth. Catching a large snake, or being chased by one, is read as a sign of financial gain; being bitten by a snake is read as money coming your way.

*Psychological interpretation*: For Jung, snakes symbolize the depths of the unconscious, primal energy, and transformation (as in shedding skin). They're also sometimes interpreted as repressed sexual energy or vitality.

**8. Pigs**
One of the most commonly cited "lucky" dreams in Korean folk tradition, often mentioned around lottery purchases.

*Traditional folk interpretation*: Pigs symbolize wealth and abundance. A fat pig entering your home, catching a pig, or eating pork in a dream are all read as signs of coming wealth. A pig-related prenatal dream is said to foretell a child blessed with fortune and abundance.

*Psychological interpretation*: Symbolizes abundance, satisfaction, and sensory pleasure.

**9. Tigers**
*Traditional folk interpretation*: Riding a tiger is read as a sign of major success and power. A tiger entering your home is read as an omen of an important guest or good news.

**10. Dragons (as a prenatal dream)**
*Traditional folk interpretation*: Considered one of the most auspicious prenatal dreams — a dragon ascending to the sky is read as foretelling a child destined for great success. An ascending dragon symbolizes achievement and advancement.

**11. Fish**
*Traditional folk interpretation*: Catching a large fish is read as a sign of wealth and success. A carp leaping is read as an omen of advancement or passing an important exam.

### Dreams of nature and the elements

**12. Fire**
*Traditional folk interpretation*: A large fire burning brightly is read as a sign of business prosperity, exam success, or rising honor. Being on fire yourself is also considered a positive omen. A fire going out or producing only smoke, on the other hand, is read as an ill omen.

*Psychological interpretation*: Symbolizes passion, transformation, and purification. Controlled fire tends to represent creative energy, while a wild, spreading fire can represent anger or overwhelming emotion.

**13. Water**
*Traditional folk interpretation*: Clear water or an overflowing river is read as a sign of wealth; drinking clean water is read as a sign of health and good fortune. Murky water or a flood is read as a signal to be cautious.

*Psychological interpretation*: Water symbolizes the unconscious itself. Calm water represents peace, rough waves represent emotional turbulence, and drowning represents being overwhelmed by emotion.

**14. The ocean**
*Psychological interpretation*: The ocean symbolizes both infinite possibility and the fear of the unknown. Sailing across a wide sea represents life's journey; drowning in the ocean represents being overwhelmed by circumstances or emotion.

### Dreams about people and relationships

**15. An ex appearing in your dream**
Having an ex-partner show up in a dream is an extremely common experience.

*Psychological interpretation*: This dream is less about the ex-partner themselves and more about reprocessing **the emotions or patterns** from that relationship. It tends to show up when there's unresolved feeling left over — lingering attachment, regret, or anger. Psychologists see it as part of integrating the lessons learned from that relationship.

**16. A stranger appearing**
*Psychological interpretation*: A stranger in a dream often symbolizes **an aspect of yourself.** An attractive stranger can represent undeveloped potential; a threatening stranger can represent your shadow — the parts of yourself you don't want to acknowledge.

**17. A celebrity appearing**
*Psychological interpretation*: Celebrities represent the qualities they're known for, or the ideals we project onto them. Dreaming of a favorite performer often reflects a longing for a trait they embody — confidence, talent, and so on.

**18. A deceased loved one appearing**
*Psychological interpretation*: A very natural part of processing grief. A dream where a deceased loved one reassures you that they're okay can help with the emotional work of acceptance.

### Situation and scenario dreams

**19. Being pregnant**
*Traditional folk interpretation*: Often read as a prenatal dream, or as a general omen of a new beginning and prosperity.

*Psychological interpretation*: Symbolizes the start of a new idea, project, or life chapter. It shows up often when creative energy is emerging, regardless of actual pregnancy.

**20. Dreams about a house**
*Psychological interpretation*: A house is one of the most powerful symbols for the self in dreams. Different rooms can represent different areas of the psyche.
- Basement: the unconscious, repressed memories
- Attic: old memories, neglected potential
- Discovering an unknown room: uncovering an unexplored part of yourself

**21. Finding or losing money**
*Psychological interpretation*: Money symbolizes energy, resources, and self-worth. Finding money reflects confidence and possibility; losing money reflects a lack of resources or a dip in self-efficacy.

**22. Being naked**
Suddenly realizing you're undressed or naked in a public place.

*Psychological interpretation*: Symbolizes anxiety about social exposure, vulnerability, and fear that your flaws will be seen. Commonly shows up before a new job, a presentation, or an important social event.

**23. Being lost**
*Psychological interpretation*: Reflects a loss of direction in life, unclear goals, or indecision. Common during major life transitions.

**24. Being late**
Missing an important train or flight, or being late to an appointment.

*Psychological interpretation*: Reflects time pressure or anxiety about missing an opportunity in real life. Especially common among perfectionists.

**25. A car out of control**
A car that won't stop, or brakes that fail.

*Psychological interpretation*: Reflects a feeling that some part of your life is out of your control, or anxiety about things moving too fast.

---

### Supernatural dreams

**26. Ghosts or spirits appearing**
*Psychological interpretation*: Symbolizes unresolved past issues, trauma, or regret. A "ghost" represents a part of yourself still tied to the past.

**27. Being chased by a demon or monster**
*Psychological interpretation*: One of the most intense Shadow dreams. An impulse or fear you don't want to acknowledge in yourself gets projected outward as an external threat.

**28. Flying (revisited)**
Mentioned above, but the quality of the flight adds detail to the interpretation.
- Flying freely and high: strong confidence, on the verge of achievement
- Struggling to fly: working toward a goal, but still facing obstacles

---

### Bodily-sensation dreams

**29. Being paralyzed (sleep paralysis)**
Trying to move but being unable to. Often linked with the experience some cultures call being "pinned down" while sleeping.

*Scientific explanation*: **Sleep paralysis** happens at the boundary between REM sleep and waking, when the brain wakes up but the body is still in REM's muscle-paralyzed state. Hallucinations can occur during this state. It's a completely normal phenomenon, and it happens more often under stress or sleep deprivation.

**30. Jolting awake with a falling sensation (hypnic jerk)**
Suddenly feeling like you're falling and waking with a start as you're drifting off.

*Scientific explanation*: Known as a **hypnic jerk**, experienced by 60-70% of people. It's a physiological muscle contraction that happens as the brain transitions into sleep.

## 4. Interpreting Prenatal Dreams (a Korean Tradition)

A **taemong (胎夢, "prenatal dream")** is a dream believed in Korean tradition to be had by a pregnant woman or a family member around the time of conception, said to foretell something about the future child. It's a long-standing cultural practice rather than a scientific claim.

### Well-known prenatal dream themes

**The most auspicious prenatal dreams**
- A dragon ascending to the sky: a great figure, a leader
- Holding the sun or moon: an exceptionally talented child
- A golden carp leaping: success and advancement

**Prenatal dreams associated with wealth**
- A fat pig entering the home: wealth and abundance
- Receiving gold or jewels: financial success

**Traditionally associated with a daughter** (folk belief, with no evidence behind it)
- Receiving flowers, dreaming of small animals

**Traditionally associated with a son** (folk belief, with no evidence behind it)
- Tigers, dragons, snakes, and other powerful animals

*Important: there is no scientific link between prenatal dreams and a child's sex or future. It's best understood as a cultural tradition through which expectant families express hope and anticipation.*

## 5. Three Ways to Deal With Nightmares

Recurring nightmares can seriously affect quality of life. These three approaches have solid research support.

### 1. Image Rehearsal Therapy (IRT)

Currently the best-supported nightmare treatment. The method:
1. Write down a recurring nightmare
2. Rewrite the dream's content into a more positive version
3. Vividly imagine the rewritten version for 10-20 minutes daily
4. Nightmare frequency and intensity typically drop noticeably after 2-3 weeks

### 2. Lucid dreaming training

A lucid dream is one where you become aware, mid-dream, that "this is a dream." Once lucid, you can deliberately change what happens in a nightmare.

**Reality-check practice**: Ask "is this a dream?" ten times a day and count your fingers as you do. In dreams, fingers often look wrong or appear in the wrong number. This habit tends to carry over into your dreams and trigger lucidity.

### 3. Better sleep hygiene

Nightmares get worse with irregular sleep, alcohol, caffeine, and intense stimulation before bed (horror movies, distressing news).
- Keep a consistent sleep and wake schedule
- Avoid phones and screens for 1-2 hours before bed
- Build a simple wind-down routine before sleep (breathing, stretching)
- Alcohol tends to make nightmares worse, not better

## 6. How to Keep a Dream Journal

Nothing beats a **dream journal** for remembering your dreams and exploring what they reveal.

### How to start

**1. What you'll need**: Keep a notebook and pen by your bed. A phone app works too, but screen light can wake you up further, so a paper notebook is preferable if possible.

**2. Write immediately upon waking**: Dream memory fades fast, usually within 5-10 minutes of waking. The moment your alarm goes off, close your eyes, recall the dream, and write it down right away.

**3. What to record**:
- Date and time
- The gist of the dream (even a rough summary is fine)
- Who appeared, and where
- What you felt
- The most vivid image or moment from the dream

**4. Look for patterns**: After a month, look back through your journal. Recurring themes, people, and places will emerge — these are the unconscious themes you're currently working through.

## Closing thoughts

Dreams are a letter from the unconscious. Not every dream carries a specific meaning or predicts the future. But a recurring dream, or one that leaves you with strong emotion after waking, can be a genuinely useful clue for understanding your own psychology.

What matters most in dream interpretation isn't the generic meaning — it's **the emotion you felt while dreaming.** The same snake dream might be read as a threat if you felt fear, or as power and transformation if you felt awe. Developing your own personal dream language is one of the more fascinating paths to understanding yourself.
