## Why post-processing matters

In digital photography, post-processing isn't optional — it's essential. What a camera records doesn't actually match what your eyes see. In particular, the wide dynamic range between highlights and shadows needs to be optimized for how it will be displayed.

Post-processing = the digital version of "developing and printing in a darkroom."

---

## RAW vs. JPEG

This is the most important foundational choice you'll make.

| | RAW | JPEG |
|------|-----|------|
| **File size** | 20–50MB | 3–10MB |
| **Image quality** | Best (lossless) | Lossy compression |
| **Room to edit** | Very wide | Limited |
| **White balance** | Freely adjustable | Limited |
| **Exposure recovery** | ±3–5 stops | ±1–2 stops |
| **Camera processing** | None (raw data) | Automatically processed by the camera |
| **Ready to use immediately** | Requires editing software | Can be shared right away |

**Bottom line**: shoot **RAW** for serious photography, and **JPEG** when fast sharing is the priority (or shoot RAW+JPEG simultaneously).

## A complete overview of the basic panel

### Exposure sliders

```
Exposure: overall brightness (-5 to +5 stops)
Highlights: adjusts only the bright areas (sky, whites)
Shadows: adjusts only the dark areas (shade, dark skin tones)
Whites: the brightest point (guards against clipping)
Blacks: the darkest point (sets deep black)
```

**Basic editing strategy**:
1. **Lower Highlights** → recover a blown-out sky
2. **Raise Shadows** → bring out detail in dark areas
3. **Lower Blacks** → create deep black tones (adds dimension)
4. **Raise Whites** → fine-tune overall brightness

### Presence (texture and clarity)

```
Texture: emphasizes mid-tone texture (skin pores, fabric weave)
Clarity: emphasizes mid-tone contrast (adds crispness)
Dehaze: removes haze and atmospheric fog (effective for landscapes)
Vibrance: selectively boosts less-saturated colors (natural-looking saturation)
Saturation: adjusts overall saturation (use with caution)
```

## Color correction

### Adjusting white balance

RAW files let you **freely change** white balance even after the shot.

```
Temp slider:
  Lower (left) → cooler, more blue
  Higher (right) → warmer, more yellow

Tint slider:
  Lower (left) → more green
  Higher (right) → more magenta
```

### The HSL/Color panel (adjusting by color)

A powerful tool for selecting and adjusting a single color at a time.

| Goal | Method |
|---------|------|
| Make the sky bluer | Adjust Blue Hue, raise Blue Saturation |
| Improve skin tone | Fine-tune Orange Hue/Saturation/Luminance |
| Make green grass pop | Raise Green Saturation |
| Emphasize a sunset | Raise Orange/Red Saturation |

### The tone curve

Applying an S-curve fine-tunes **contrast and color rendering**.

```
The basic S-curve:
  - Raise the top (highlights) → brighter highlights
  - Lower the bottom (shadows) → darker shadows
  Result: increased contrast, more punch
```

**A film look**: leaving the bottom of the curve slightly above pure black (rather than at 0) lifts the blacks and creates a film-like feel.

## Detail adjustments

### Noise reduction

```
Noise Reduction:
  Luminance: removes brightness noise (usually the main setting)
  → apply around 30–50
  → too high, and you lose fine detail (mushy look)
  
  Color: removes color noise (colored speckles)
  → apply around 25–40
```

### Sharpening

```
Sharpening:
  Amount: strength of sharpening (60–80 recommended)
  Radius: the radius over which sharpening is applied
  Detail: protects fine detail
  Masking: sharpens only certain areas (edge detection)
  → hold Alt while adjusting Masking: only white areas get sharpened
```

> **Rule of thumb**: noise reduction and sharpening pull in opposite directions — balance is key.

## Cropping and straightening

### Cropping

```
Basic crop: press R → crop to your desired ratio
  - Free: any ratio
  - 1:1: square (Instagram)
  - 4:3, 16:9: standard screen ratios
  - 2:3: 35mm film ratio (default)
```

### Straightening a horizon

If a photo isn't level, straighten it automatically or rotate it manually.

```
Drag the Level tool along a horizontal or vertical line → auto-correct
Fine-tune with the Angle slider
```

## Exporting

| Purpose | Recommended settings |
|------|----------|
| Print | JPEG, 300dpi, sRGB or AdobeRGB |
| Web/social media | JPEG, 72dpi, sRGB, long edge 2400px |
| Archiving | TIFF or DNG (preserves the original) |

## A sample before/after workflow

**A one-minute landscape editing recipe**:

```
1. Highlights -60 (recover the sky)
2. Shadows +50 (bring out the foreground)
3. Blacks -30 (add depth)
4. Clarity +15 (add crispness)
5. Dehaze +10 (cut through haze)
6. Blue Saturation +20 (strengthen the sky)
7. Slightly adjust Green Hue (make the grass look natural)
```

**Q1.** What's the biggest advantage of a RAW file?
- ① Smaller file size
- ② You can freely adjust white balance and exposure after the shot
- ③ You can share it on social media instantly
- ④ The camera optimizes it automatically

**Answer**: ②

**Q2.** What's the main effect of lowering the Highlights slider?
- ① Dark areas get brighter
- ② It recovers a blown-out sky or white areas
- ③ Overall brightness drops
- ④ Color saturation decreases

**Answer**: ②

**Q3.** If a photo shot at high ISO shows colored speckles, which slider fixes it?
- ① Clarity
- ② Luminance Noise Reduction
- ③ Color Noise Reduction
- ④ Sharpening

**Answer**: ③

**Q4.** What happens when you apply an S-curve to the tone curve?
- ① The photo gets darker overall
- ② Contrast increases
- ③ Saturation increases
- ④ Noise is removed

**Answer**: ②
