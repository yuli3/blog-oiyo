## Aristotle's Student

Born in 356 BCE as the son of Macedonian King Philip II, Alexander was tutored from age 13 by **Aristotle** for three years — studying philosophy, medicine, science, and literature under the greatest mind of the age.

He was passionate about Homer's *Iliad* and modeled himself on its hero Achilles. He reportedly slept with a copy of the *Iliad* and a dagger under his pillow every night.

---

## King at 20, Conqueror at 22

When Philip II was assassinated in 336 BCE, Alexander became king at 20. He swiftly crushed rebellions in Greece and consolidated Macedonian power.

In 334 BCE, at 22, he led 40,000 troops into Asia. His objective: the Persian Empire.

<Callout type="tip">
**The Gordian Knot**: At Gordium in Phrygia, Alexander encountered a knot said to be impossible to untie — with a prophecy that whoever untied it would rule Asia. He sliced it with his sword. The phrase "cutting the Gordian knot" — solving a complex problem with bold direct action — comes from this moment.
</Callout>

---

## The Undefeated General

Over 13 years of campaigning, Alexander **never lost a battle**.

**Battle of Issus (333 BCE)**: Routed the Persian army of Darius III, who fled abandoning his family.

**Battle of Gaugamela (331 BCE)**: Crushed the largest Persian army ever assembled. The Persian Empire collapsed.

**Egypt (332 BCE)**: Welcomed as a liberator from Persian rule, he was crowned pharaoh and founded **Alexandria** — which would become the ancient world's greatest city of learning.

---

## To the Edge of the World

After conquering Persia, Alexander drove east through modern Afghanistan and Pakistan, reaching the **northwestern frontier of India**. Only a near-mutiny by his exhausted troops — who had marched 17,000 km over 8 years — finally halted the advance.

In 323 BCE, Alexander returned to Babylon — and died suddenly at 32. The cause remains disputed: malaria, typhoid fever, poisoning, or complications from excessive drinking are all theories.

---

## The Hellenistic World

After his death, Alexander's generals (the Diadochi — "successors") divided the empire into competing kingdoms. But the cultural seed he planted flourished into the **Hellenistic World**.

Greek language and culture spread from Egypt to the borders of India. The great **Library of Alexandria** became the ancient world's greatest repository of knowledge. Greek became the lingua franca of the Mediterranean — which is why the New Testament was written in Greek rather than Hebrew or Aramaic.

---

## Conqueror or Visionary?

Alexander's legacy is genuinely ambiguous. He destroyed cities and massacred populations (the burning of Persepolis, the killing of Tyre's defenders). But he also pursued cultural synthesis — marrying a Persian princess, adopting Persian customs, encouraging his officers to do the same.

In 32 years, he permanently altered the course of history. Whether admired or critiqued, few individuals have shaped the world as profoundly.
