# Don't Put All Your Eggs in One Basket: Risk, Return, and Portfolio Theory

Welcome back. Previously, we learned how to measure the 'weight' of an asset through valuation. Today, we explore how to mix those assets to create the most delicious 'Income Cocktail'—the world of **Portfolio Theory**.

"High Risk, High Return." This phrase is the core truth of finance. However, simply taking risks is not the goal. Harry Markowitz, a giant of modern finance, mathematically proved <mark><strong>"A way to reduce risk for free."</strong></mark> Let's uncover the 'magic of diversification,' often called the only free lunch in economics.

---

## 1. The Nature of Risk: Volatility in Numbers

In finance, risk is not just the 'possibility of losing money.' It means <mark><strong>"The possibility that actual results will differ from expectations"</strong></mark>—in other words, **Volatility**.

### (1) Expected Return

The average value of returns weighted by the probability of various future scenarios (boom, recession, etc.).

### (2) Standard Deviation: The Yardstick of Risk

We measure how far returns fluctuate from the mean using **Standard Deviation ($\sigma$)**. A higher standard deviation indicates a riskier asset with an unpredictable future.

<mark><strong>The Decisive Role of Correlation</strong></mark>: If two stocks move exactly the same way (Correlation = +1), mixing them is useless. However, if you find a pair where one smiles while the other cries (Correlation < 1), you can keep your return while drastically reducing overall risk.

## 3. The Efficient Frontier

Out of thousands of possible combinations, which one should we choose?

<LectureTable
  title="The Dominance Principle (Rational Choice)"
  headers={["Scenario", "Criterion", "Conclusion"]}
  rows={[
    ["Risk is Equal", "Choose higher return", "Dominance Principle"],
    ["Return is Equal", "Choose lower risk", "Dominance Principle"],
  ]}
  highlightColumns={[1]}
/>

The line connecting the best portfolios that offer the highest return for a given level of risk is called the **Efficient Frontier**. A rational investor must always choose a portfolio on this line.

## 4. Systematic vs. Unsystematic Risk

Does diversification remove all risk? Unfortunately, no.

<LecturePieChart
  title="Composition of Total Risk"

- **Unsystematic Risk**: "A specific CEO made a mistake." Risk unique to a company. **CAN be eliminated.**
- **Systematic Risk**: "Global interest rates spiked." A wave hitting the entire market. **CANNOT be eliminated.**

## 5. Wrapping Up: Choose Your Risks Wisely

The heart of portfolio theory is not just avoiding risk, but <mark><strong>"Strategic boldness: eliminating avoidable risks and selecting only those risks that offer compensation."</strong></mark>
