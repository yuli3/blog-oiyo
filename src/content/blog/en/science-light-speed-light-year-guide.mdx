# We're Always Looking at a Past Version of the Universe

When you look at a star in the night sky, you're not seeing that star "right now." Light takes time to travel from the star to Earth. Even nearby moonlight is about 1.3 seconds old, and sunlight is about 8 minutes and 20 seconds old by the time it reaches us.

Once you grasp this single idea, astronomy suddenly feels far more three-dimensional.

> Looking at a distant object means looking across a vast distance — and, at the same time, into a distant past.

---

## 1. How Fast Is the Speed of Light?

In a vacuum, light travels at about 299,792 km per second. For everyday purposes, it's fine to simply remember it as **about 300,000 km per second**.

| Comparison | Distance / time sense |
|---|---|
| One trip around Earth | About 40,000 km |
| Distance light travels in 1 second | About 300,000 km |
| Time for light to reach us from the Moon | About 1.3 seconds |
| Time for light to reach us from the Sun | About 8 minutes 20 seconds |
| Time for light to travel from the Sun to Neptune | More than 4 hours |

To our everyday senses, light seems to arrive almost instantly. But at cosmic scale, even light is slow.

---

## 2. A Light-Year Is a Distance, Not a Time

The word "light-year" trips a lot of people up. Even though "year" is right there in the name, a light-year is not a unit of time — it's a unit of **distance**.

**1 light-year = the distance light travels in one year**

Light covers about 300,000 km every second, and if it kept moving at that speed for an entire year, it would cover about 9.46 trillion km. Rather than memorizing that huge number, it helps more to understand it this way:

## 3. Starlight Is an Old Letter

It helps to think of starlight as an old letter. There's a gap between the moment a star sends out its light and the moment we finally read it.

| Object | How old is the light we see? |
|---|---|
| The Moon | About 1.3 seconds old |
| The Sun | About 8 minutes 20 seconds old |
| Proxima Centauri | About 4.2 years old |
| The North Star | Over 400 years old |
| The Andromeda Galaxy | About 2.5 million years old |

Looking at the Andromeda Galaxy isn't seeing what Andromeda looks like right now — it's seeing light that set out long before humanity even existed.

---

## 4. Why Do Astronomers Say "Looking Farther Means Looking Further Into the Past"?

A telescope isn't simply a device for making distant things look bigger. It's a tool for gathering light that traveled from very far away and using it to read the past of the universe.

For example, observing a galaxy 10 billion light-years away means that its light has traveled for nearly 10 billion years to reach us. That's how we're able to trace what the early universe looked like.

Here it's important to note that "the universe is expanding." At truly vast distances, distance can't simply be calculated as speed of light multiplied by travel time. But at an introductory level, this one sentence is enough to remember:

> Light is the universe's recording medium, and a telescope is the device that reads that recording.

## 6. One-Page Summary

- Light travels at about 300,000 km per second.
- A light-year is not a unit of time — it's the distance light travels in one year.
- Moonlight is about 1.3 seconds old; sunlight is about 8 minutes 20 seconds old by the time it reaches us.
- Looking at stars and galaxies means reading light from the past.
- A telescope is both a tool for seeing far and a tool for seeing into the past.
