Vidar is one of Odin's sons and is remembered as a silent god, or a god of vengeance. He does not have many stories in ordinary times, but he takes on a decisive role at Ragnarok. After Odin is swallowed by Fenrir, Vidar avenges his father by stepping on the wolf's jaw with his strong shoe and tearing it apart.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Vidar's central scene is Ragnarok. At the moment even Odin, king of the gods, is swallowed by Fenrir, the order of the world seems to have collapsed completely. But Vidar does not retreat. He approaches Fenrir, plants one foot on the lower jaw, grips the upper jaw with his hand, and tears it apart.

One interpretation says that the special shoe he wears is made from scraps of leather discarded by people. This image creates a symbol in which small remnants become decisive strength at the moment of the end. Vidar is closer to the long-accumulated power of silence than to a dazzling hero.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

Vidar is an archetype of the **silent warrior** and the **survivor**. He does not reveal himself through words, but he acts at the moment when action is necessary. In modern terms, he resembles the scene of a person who has been quietly preparing and then takes responsibility in a crisis, or the process by which someone left behind after a great loss establishes an order of recovery rather than mere revenge. Vidar shows that real strength does not always appear noisily.

Vidar's vengeance is closer to a long-prepared response than to an emotional explosion. His thick shoe can be read as strength made from accumulated small pieces. In modern terms, it resembles the way daily training, quiet savings, or long-built trust can support a person at a decisive moment. Vidar is the archetype of the survivor who silently carries out what must be done.

## Figures to Read Together

Reading [Odin](/en/meaning-of-myth-odin/), [Fenrir](/en/meaning-of-myth-fenrir/), and [Tyr](/en/meaning-of-myth-tyr/) together shows the long history that precedes Vidar's vengeance.

When interpreting Vidar, it is best not to see vengeance only as simple anger. He shows how a person left behind after a collapsed order should respond.
That response is quiet, but it is an action that makes the world's next chapter possible.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Why is Vidar called a god of silence?**

A. Because in the tradition he does not appear as a talkative figure, but is remembered for quiet yet powerful strength and for his decisive action at Ragnarok.

**Q. Does Vidar die at Ragnarok?**

A. In the major traditions, he is mentioned as a god who survives. He kills Fenrir and is connected to the world after the end.
