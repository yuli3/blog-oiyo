Seolmundae Halmang is the representative giant woman of Jeju creation myth. She is remembered as the being who made Hallasan, the oreum, the sea, and the shape of the island, and she is deeply connected to the way Jeju people understand their terrain. Her name and story details vary by tradition. In some stories she spills soil while carrying it, creating the oreum; in others she tries to build a bridge or make clothes, only to fail. Seolmundae Halmang is both a vast creator and a being who leaves traces of incompletion.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

Seolmundae Halmang is said to have created Jeju by carrying soil in the folds of her skirt. Wherever the soil fell, an oreum appeared, and where her large body moved, boundaries between mountain and sea took shape. This story holds a sensibility that sees Jeju's nature not as a simple geological phenomenon, but as traces of a mythic body.

Another famous story says that Seolmundae Halmang tried to build a bridge connecting Jeju with the mainland. She asks people to make underclothes for her, or promises to build the bridge if certain conditions are met, but the work is never completed. The conditions and ending differ by tradition, and this incompletion can be read as a symbol explaining Jeju's condition as an island.

There are also several traditions about Seolmundae Halmang's death. Some say she fell into a deep pond such as Muljangori, while others tell of a tragedy connected with a large cauldron. Rather than fixing the details into a single version, it is more important to see that Jeju myth gives even a giant creator disappearance and limits.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian View

From a Jungian perspective, Seolmundae Halmang is an archetype of the **Earth Mother** and the **Incomplete Creator**. She gives birth to the landscape, but she does not complete every relationship. In modern terms, she resembles a person with a grand vision who cannot complete a plan without the help of a community, time, and material conditions.

The Seolmundae Halmang archetype is also a mirror of island identity. It desires connection but remains separated; it has immense power, yet can fail because of one small condition. For that reason, she symbolizes the fact that a world is completed only when the grandeur of creation and the concreteness of daily life are both present.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Seolmundae Halmang a mythic figure unique to Jeju?**

A. She appears especially strongly in Jeju tradition. However, the motif of a giant woman creating landforms can also be compared with tales from several regions of Korea.

**Q. Why do Seolmundae Halmang stories have multiple endings?**

A. Because they were transmitted as oral tales. Details vary by village, time of collection, and storyteller, so it is most accurate to say that they differ by tradition.
