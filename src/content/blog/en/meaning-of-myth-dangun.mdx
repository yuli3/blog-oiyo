Dangun Wanggeom is remembered in Korea's founding myth as the progenitor who established Gojoseon. In the account recorded in the *Samguk Yusa*, he is born between Hwanung and Ungnyeo, becoming a figure who joins the order that descended from heaven with the human endurance completed on earth. The name "Wanggeom" evokes both a political ruler and a ritual leader, and the Dangun myth is not only a story that explains the origin of a people. It is also symbolic language showing the order through which a community understood itself.

## Core myth — representative episode

The Dangun myth begins with Hwanung, the son of Hwanin, forming the intention to broadly benefit the human world. Hwanung descends with his followers beneath the sacred tree on Taebaeksan, and governs human life together with beings who rule wind, rain, and clouds. This scene shows that heavenly authority is not simply a distant power, but is connected to agriculture, order, law, and livelihood.

The episode in which a bear and a tiger wish to become human is also important. The bear eats mugwort and garlic and endures in darkness, finally becoming Ungnyeo. Dangun, born between Hwanung and Ungnyeo, is therefore a figure in whom heaven's will and earth's endurance are joined. Details and interpretations vary by tradition, but the core point is that founding a country is not only a matter of lineage. It is also the result of transformation endured through suffering.

Dangun is said to have made Pyeongyangseong his capital and founded Joseon, and later traditions add that he entered Asadal and became a mountain spirit. The layers of reading this as historical record and reading it as mythic symbol should be distinguished, but Dangun has long been a name that holds together the beginning of the state, the sacredness of mountains, and the sense of ancestor worship.

## Reading as an archetype — from a Jungian perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Dangun Wanggeom is close to the archetype of the **founding king** and the **culture hero**. He establishes the community's first name amid chaos and creates an order and time that people can share. In modern terms, he resembles the founder who first establishes an organization, the elder who resets the standard for a scattered family, or the symbol that appears when a community asks about its own identity.

When reading the Dangun archetype, the important question is not "who came first?" but "what did he enable people to begin together?" He becomes richer when seen not only as a blood ancestor, but as a figure who translates heavenly ideals into earthly rules of life. In this sense, Dangun is a symbol that reflects both the desire to find one's roots and the responsibility to establish a new order.

### Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is Dangun Wanggeom a historical figure or a mythic figure?**

A. Tradition presents him as the progenitor of Gojoseon, but in this mythic dictionary he is read less in terms of historical fact and more as a symbol explaining founding and communal origins.

**Q. What does it mean that Dangun became a mountain spirit?**

A. It can be interpreted to mean that the ruler's authority remains, even after death, within the order of mountains and ritual. It is a scene where mountain faith and ancestor worship overlap.
