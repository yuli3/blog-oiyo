## 1. Learning objectives

1. Explain how the method of seizure and the time it takes effect differ by type of property.
2. State the purpose of exempting property from seizure and classify the main types.
3. Calculate how much of a wage claim can be seized.
4. Explain the duty the head of the tax office bears after seizing a claim (starting collection within one year).

## 2. The type of property determines the method

The previous chapter said a demand is a requirement for seizure. Once the requirements are met, the next question is **what is seized and how**. The Act sets separate methods for each type of property.

<LectureTable

  headers={["Property", "Method", "Article"]}
  rows={[
    ["Registered real estate, factory foundations, mining foundations, ships", "Commission the registry office to register the seizure, attaching the seizure record", "Article 45(1)"],
    ["Motor vehicles, construction machinery, aircraft, registered ships", "Commission the head of the relevant administrative agency or local government to register the seizure", "Article 45(2)"],
    ["Movables and securities", "Taking possession", "Article 48"],
    ["Claims", "Notice of seizure to the third-party debtor, and notice of the fact to the defaulter", "Article 51"],
  ]}
/>

### When does seizure of a claim take effect?

**When the seizure notice is served on the third-party debtor** (Article 52(1)), not when the defaulter is told. For a bank deposit, it takes effect the moment the notice reaches the bank.

Once notice is given, the head of the competent tax office **subrogates the defaulting creditor** up to the amount in arrears (Article 52(2)). Having done so, it must, **within one year of the seizure**, demand performance from the third-party debtor and bring an action for performance (paragraph 3). Exceptions apply when an appeal is pending or collection is legally or practically impossible.

<Callout type="info">
Seizure is the disposition that "holds" property; collection is the act of "getting paid". Article 52(3) sets a one-year limit because a seizure left in place without follow-up would tie up the defaulter's property rights indefinitely.
</Callout>

## 3. Property exempt from seizure (Article 41)

This article protects the floor of daily life and livelihood. Its 18 items can be grouped as follows.

    ["Safety", "15", "Fire-fighting equipment, alarms and evacuation facilities required by law"],

  ]}
/>

Light vehicles in item 14 and living-expense accounts in item 18 show how the article has widened to follow **real-world means of livelihood** (including the June 2026 amendment).

## 4. Limit on seizing wage claims (Article 42)

Wages cannot be seized in full.

<FormulaBox
  label="The rule"
  formula="\text{Amount exempt from seizure} = \text{Total wage claim} \times 1/2"
  description="The total here is total earned income (excluding non-taxable items) minus income tax and the local income tax on it (Article 42(4))."
/>

Adjustments apply at both ends (paragraph 2):

- if one-half **falls below the amount set by presidential decree in light of minimum living costs**, that decree amount cannot be seized (protecting low earners);
- if one-half **exceeds the amount set by presidential decree in light of standard household living costs**, the exempt amount is capped at that amount (more can be seized from high earners).

**One-half** of severance pay also cannot be seized (paragraph 3).

### Worked example

Suppose a defaulter's monthly take-home pay after tax is ₩4 million.

| Step | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Exempt amount under Article 42(1) | ₩4 million × 1/2 = ₩2 million |
| Seizable amount (before adjustment) | ₩4 million − ₩2 million = ₩2 million |
| Adjustment | If ₩2 million exceeds the decree's cap, the exempt amount falls to the cap and the seizable amount rises accordingly |

<Callout type="warning">
The specific amounts are set by **presidential decree**. Exams ask about the structure: the rule of "one-half of the total" and the adjustments at both ends. In practice, check the decree amounts for the year in question.
</Callout>

## 5. Effect and release of seizure

Once property is seized, the defaulter **cannot dispose of it** (Article 43). The seizure also extends to the fruits of that property (Article 44).

Article 57 sets the requirements for release: when seizure is no longer needed because of payment, offset, suspension of public sale and the like, and cases such as the three months passing on a protective seizure before finalisation seen in the previous chapter.

## 6. Check your understanding

<Quiz>

**Q1.** The head of the tax office seized a defaulter's bank deposit. When does the seizure take effect?

> When the seizure notice is **served on the third-party debtor (the bank)** (Article 52(1)). Notifying the defaulter is a separate duty (Article 51(2)) but is not when the seizure takes effect.

**Q2.** For a wage claim with take-home pay of ₩3 million, what is the basic limit on the amount that can be seized? Cite the article.

> In principle, ₩1.5 million. Article 42(1) sets one-half of the total as the exempt amount. If one-half falls below the decree's floor or exceeds its cap, the adjustments in paragraph 2 apply.

**Q3.** Which of the following can be seized? (1) the defaulter's registered seal (2) an unpublished manuscript (3) a 3,000cc car owned by the defaulter (4) three months' food

> (3). The vehicles that Article 41(14) exempts are **light vehicles**. (1) is exempt under item 3, (2) under item 10 and (4) under item 2.

**Q4.** The head of the tax office seized a claim and did nothing for two years. Is that a problem?

> Yes. Article 52(3) requires that, when subrogating, the office demand performance and bring an action for performance **within one year of the seizure**, except where an appeal is pending or collection is legally or practically impossible.

</Quiz>

## 7. Limits of this chapter

- The specific exempt amounts are set by presidential decree and may change every year. This chapter covers only the structure of the articles.
- Requests for distribution and participating seizure (Articles 59–63) concern the relationship with other enforcement procedures and need a chapter of their own.
- Sale of seized property (public sale and private contract) and distribution are covered in the next chapter.

The next chapter covers **the sale and distribution of seized property**, and public sale procedures.
