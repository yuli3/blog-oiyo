## Memory Isn't a Recording

Memory isn't a recording device that stores an experience exactly and plays it back later. We encode information, store it, and retrieve it when we need it. Attention, meaning, emotion, and context all shape this process.

That's why memory is useful but never perfect. Even things you feel absolutely sure you remember can be reconstructed over time.

## Stages of Memory

| Stage | Meaning | Example |
|------|------|------|
| Encoding | Processing information into a memorable form | Linking a name to its sound and meaning |
| Storage | Holding information for a period of time | Keeping a word list in mind |
| Retrieval | Pulling stored information back out | Recalling a concept on a test |
| Reconstruction | Memory shifting to fit the current context | Details changing as you retell a story |

What matters for studying isn't just repetition — it's encoding material meaningfully and practicing retrieval in different contexts.

## Thinking and Judgment

Thinking is the process of manipulating information, solving problems, and making decisions. Instead of calculating every possibility, people rely on mental shortcuts, or heuristics. These shortcuts are fast, but they can also produce errors.

For example, if a recent example comes to mind easily, we tend to feel that it's more common than it actually is. This happens because thinking balances efficiency against accuracy.

## Example: Studying for a Test

Reading a textbook multiple times builds familiarity, but familiarity isn't the same as being able to retrieve the information. Explaining the material to yourself, solving problems, and recalling it again after some time has passed all strengthen retrieval practice.

## Key Takeaways

- Memory isn't stored like a recording — it goes through encoding, storage, retrieval, and reconstruction.
- Even confident memories can shift with context and time.
- Thinking relies on heuristics for fast judgment, but those shortcuts can also produce errors.
- Effective learning depends on retrieval practice, not just repeated reading.
