Anubis is the jackal-headed god in Egyptian mythology who cares for the dead and guards tombs. Before the cult of Osiris became firmly established, he was regarded as an important sovereign of the afterlife; later, he appears more prominently as a guide for funerary rites and judgment. He helps us understand death not only as something fearful, but as a passage that requires procedure and protection.

## Core Myth — Representative Episodes

Anubis's representative role appears in scenes where he handles and protects the body of Osiris. He was regarded as the god of the techniques and rites that preserve a dead body as a mummy. Mummification was not merely embalming, but a sacred procedure that helped the deceased maintain identity in the afterlife.

Another central scene is the judgment in which the heart of the dead is placed on the scales. When the heart is compared with the feather of Ma'at, Anubis appears as the guide who adjusts the scales and oversees the procedure. His role is less about the verdict itself than about helping the soul pass accurately through the threshold.

## Reading as an Archetype — A Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Anubis is the archetype of the **guide at the threshold** and the **guardian of ritual**. He stands between life and death, between the familiar world and the unknown one. In modern terms, he resembles figures who provide procedure and language at anxious boundaries: funeral directors, physicians, counselors, or mentors who help people move through crisis and transition.

The key to reading Anubis is that composure in dealing with death does not mean numbness. He does not erase grief, but he establishes ritual so that grief does not scatter into disorder. For this reason, the Anubis archetype symbolizes the power to pass through great change not by denying emotion, but through the order and care that the passage requires.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Anubis a god of death?**

A. He is deeply connected with death, but his primary role is the preservation and guidance of the dead. He is closer to a guardian of funerary rites than to a destroyer.

**Q. Why does Anubis appear as a jackal?**

A. Jackals were animals observed around tombs, so they became connected with the symbolism of funerals and burial grounds. The color black can also evoke the fertile soil of the Nile and regeneration rather than only decay.
