## Growth Mindset vs. Fixed Mindset

Stanford psychologist Carol Dweck's mindset theory distinguishes between two core belief systems.

**Growth Mindset**: the belief that ability and intelligence can be developed through effort. It views challenges as opportunities and failure as feedback.

**Fixed Mindset**: the belief that ability is set at birth. It views failure as proof of inadequacy and tends to avoid challenges.

---

## How Mindset Shapes Your Life

### Academic and Career Achievement

In Dweck's research, students with a growth mindset persisted longer on difficult tasks and achieved more over the long run.

### Response to Challenge and Failure

| Situation | Growth mindset | Fixed mindset |
|---|---|---|
| A difficult task | "This is a chance to learn." | "This isn't my level." |
| After failing | "What did I learn?" | "I knew I couldn't do it." |
| Someone else's success | Feels inspired | Feels threatened |
| Receiving criticism | Uses it as feedback | Becomes defensive |

<Callout type="tip">
Mindset can differ by domain. It's common to have a growth mindset in sports but a fixed mindset in math. Check in with yourself area by area.
</Callout>

## How to Shift Toward a Growth Mindset

### Use the Word "Yet"

Simply changing "I can't do this" to "I can't do this yet" opens the brain toward possibility.

### Praise the Process

Praise your own "effort and strategy" rather than the "outcome." Phrases like "I worked hard" and "I tried a different approach" sustain growth over time.

### Keep a Failure Journal

Recording setbacks and closing each entry with "what did I learn?" turns failure into growth data.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does having a growth mindset guarantee success?**
A: No. A growth mindset increases the direction and persistence of your effort, but whether your strategy is sound still matters. Dweck herself emphasizes "effective strategy combined with effort" over "blind effort."

**Q: Can adults still change their mindset?**
A: Yes. Thanks to neuroplasticity, the brain keeps changing throughout life. Deliberate practice and changes to your environment can shift your mindset.
