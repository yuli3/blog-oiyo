## Stakeholder Management

**Stakeholder**: Any individual, group, or organization that can affect or be affected by the project.

### Identification
Continuously identify stakeholders from project initiation onward — the stakeholder register is a living document.

### Analysis: Power/Interest Grid

```
Power (High)
  │  [Manage Closely]    [Engage Closely]
  │  High power, Low interest   High power, High interest
  │
  │  [Monitor]          [Keep Informed]
  │  Low power, Low interest    Low power, High interest
  └──────────────────────────────── Interest (High)
```

### Engagement Levels and Strategies

| Current Engagement | Strategy |
|-------------------|---------|
| Unaware | Inform them |
| Resistant | Address concerns, persuade |
| Neutral | Move toward support |
| Supportive | Maintain relationship |
| Leading champion | Leverage as an advocate |

## Team Development: Tuckman Model

Five stages of team growth:

**1. Forming**: Team meets for the first time. Roles unclear, high dependency on the PM.

**2. Storming**: Conflicts emerge over roles and approaches. The most difficult stage.

**3. Norming**: Common working agreements established. Collaboration begins.

**4. Performing**: High performance. Team operates autonomously toward goals.

**5. Adjourning**: Project ends, team disbands.

<Callout type="tip">
**PM's leadership shifts**: During Forming and Storming, directive leadership is needed. During Norming and Performing, switch to supportive and delegating styles. Matching leadership style to team maturity is the key insight.
</Callout>

---

## Leadership Styles

**Directing**: Detailed instructions, close supervision. For new or low-skill team members.

**Coaching**: Instructions + mentoring. For capable but unmotivated team members.

**Supporting**: Encouragement and facilitation. For capable but low-confidence team members.

**Delegating**: Minimal oversight. For highly skilled, self-directed team members.

## Conflict Resolution Methods (Best to Worst)

1. **Problem solving/Confronting**: Find a mutually acceptable solution. **Best**
2. **Collaborating**: Joint problem-solving. Good for long-term relationships
3. **Compromising**: Both parties yield something. Acceptable
4. **Smoothing**: Minimize differences. **Short-term** fix only
5. **Withdrawal/Avoiding**: Avoid the issue. Last resort
6. **Forcing**: Impose one party's position. Damages relationships

## Key Concept Cards

**Power/Interest Grid** ★★★★★
: Classify stakeholders by power and interest. Different engagement strategies for each quadrant.

**Tuckman's 5 Stages** ★★★★★
: Forming→Storming→Norming→Performing→Adjourning. PM adapts leadership style to each stage.

**Confronting (Problem Solving)** ★★★★★
: The best conflict resolution method on the PMP exam. Always choose addressing root causes over short-term smoothing.

## Practice Quiz

**Q1. Two team members disagree on a technical approach. What is the PM's best action?**
> Bring both members together, identify the root cause of the disagreement, and work collaboratively toward a solution both can accept (Confronting/Problem Solving). Simply taking sides or ignoring the conflict would harm team performance long-term.

**Q2. A newly formed team is confused about roles and responsibilities. Which Tuckman stage is this, and what should the PM do?**
> Storming stage. The PM should clearly define roles and responsibilities, help establish team norms, and apply directive leadership to guide the team toward the Norming stage quickly.
