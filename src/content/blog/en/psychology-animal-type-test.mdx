# The Animal Personality Type Test

DISC theory, one of the best-known models in behavioral psychology, has been used for decades in corporate training, leadership development, and team building since William Moulton Marston first proposed it in his 1928 book *Emotions of Normal People*.

But the letters D, I, S, and C aren't exactly intuitive on their own. This test maps each type onto an animal archetype instead, so you can get a much more intuitive — and more fun — read on your own behavior pattern.

## The 4 Animal Types

**🦁 The Lion (D — Dominance)**
Powerful drive, aimed straight at the goal. Someone with a "let me show you the results" mindset.

**🐬 The Dolphin (I — Influence)**
A natural communicator who energizes everyone around them. Someone who believes "things are more fun together."

**🐻 The Bear (S — Steadiness)**
The team's steady anchor — recognized as "someone you can count on."

**🦉 The Owl (C — Conscientiousness)**
The expert type, chasing the best possible result through data and analysis.

This test has 12 questions — pick whichever behavior feels closest to you for each one.

## How to Read Your Results

### Your Role on a Team

| Type | Ideal Role | Watch Out For |
|---|---|---|
| 🦁 Lion | Project lead, sales, planning | Making room for teammates' feelings |
| 🐬 Dolphin | Marketing, customer relations, team energy | Following through on the details |
| 🐻 Bear | Operations, support, keeping harmony | Practicing speaking up for yourself |
| 🦉 Owl | Data analysis, quality control, specialist roles | Practicing fast action and decisions |

### Compatibility Between Types

- 🦁 + 🦉: The lion's drive + the owl's analysis = powerful synergy
- 🐬 + 🐻: The dolphin's warmth + the bear's steadiness = a warm team culture
- 🦁 + 🐬: Two extroverted types → high energy, but watch the execution details
- 🐻 + 🦉: Two careful types → stable, but watch your speed adapting to change

<Callout type="tip">
**How to actually use this**: Once you know your own type, think about your teammates' types too. Suddenly "why does that person act that way?" starts to make sense. Strong teams are built from different animals working together.
</Callout>

This test is a self-understanding tool based on the DISC behavioral model (William Moulton Marston, 1928).
