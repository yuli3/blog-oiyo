Set is one of the most ambivalent gods in Egyptian mythology. He is famous as the antagonist who killed Osiris, but he also symbolizes the desert, storms, unfamiliar regions, and formidable fighting power. Rather than being simply an evil god, Set reveals the rough and dangerous forces that order must learn to confront.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

Set's most famous episode is the story of his murder of Osiris. He deceives and kills his brother Osiris, then tries to seize royal authority. This event becomes the starting point for a major narrative of death, mourning, and succession in Egyptian mythology. Set's violence appears as an internal fracture that brings down orderly kingship.

Yet Set is not always portrayed only negatively. In some traditions, he boards the solar god Ra's boat and appears as a powerful warrior who fights Apophis every night. The scene in which Set, a figure with chaotic power, drives back an even greater chaos shows that dangerous energy can become a defensive force when directed properly.

## Reading as an Archetype — Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Set is an archetype in which the **shadow** overlaps with the **warrior of the boundary**. He breaks order and exposes jealousy and violence, but he also possesses survival power and combat strength from beyond civilization. In modern terms, he resembles power struggles inside organizations, suppressed anger, and crisis situations where abilities outside the norm become necessary.

The key to reading Set is not to eliminate the shadow, but to recognize and handle it. When Set tries to seize kingship, he becomes a destroyer; when he guards Ra's boat, he becomes a defender. For that reason, the Set archetype can be read as a warning not to deny dangerous impulses, but to integrate them in a responsible direction.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Set a demon-like being?**

A. It is difficult to see him as a simple demon. In the Osiris myth he is a destroyer, but in other traditions he also appears as a warrior who protects the solar god.

**Q. Is the Set animal a real animal?**

A. It is difficult to identify precisely. It is depicted as a symbolic animal with a long snout and square ears, revealing Set's strange and boundary-oriented character.
