## Financial Health Checklist

Assess your current financial health across all dimensions.

### Emergency Fund
- [ ] 3–6 months of expenses in a high-yield savings account
- [ ] Account is liquid and NOT invested in stocks

### Debt
- [ ] No high-interest debt above 10% (especially credit cards)
- [ ] Total debt payments ≤ 30% of take-home pay

### Savings & Investing
- [ ] Savings rate ≥ 20% (ideally 30–50%)
- [ ] Contributing enough to 401(k) to get full employer match
- [ ] Maxing IRA (Roth if eligible)
- [ ] Diversified portfolio (stocks + international + bonds)

### Insurance
- [ ] Health insurance: covered
- [ ] Term life insurance: if you have dependents
- [ ] Disability insurance: short-term + long-term

### Taxes
- [ ] Maximizing all tax-advantaged accounts
- [ ] HSA if eligible (HDHP enrolled)
- [ ] Tax-loss harvesting in taxable accounts

## Decade-by-Decade Wealth Building

### Early 20s (22–27)
1. Pay off high-interest debt first
2. Build \$5,000–\$10,000 emergency fund
3. Start Roth IRA (\$50–\$100/month habit)
4. Take full 401(k) employer match

### Late 20s–Early 30s (27–35)
1. Savings rate 25%+
2. Emergency fund fully funded (3–6 months)
3. Decide: renting vs. buying with honest math
4. Roth IRA + 401(k) both maxed

### 30s–40s (35–45)
1. Net worth $250,000+ milestone
2. Investment portfolio on autopilot (DCA + annual rebalancing)
3. Life insurance reviewed (growing family)
4. Career income maximization (your biggest investment)

### 40s–50s (45–55)
1. Net worth \$500,000–\$1,000,000+
2. Retirement accounts front-loaded
3. College savings (529 plans) if applicable
4. Begin concrete retirement planning with projections

## 5 Actions You Can Take Today

1. **Calculate your net worth** (assets − liabilities). Takes 20 minutes.
2. **Set up automatic investing**: Pick one index ETF and set a monthly auto-investment at your brokerage.
3. **Verify your 401(k) match**: If not capturing the full match, increase your contribution today.
4. **Review your insurance**: Do you have term life? Is your emergency fund liquid?
5. **Open a Roth IRA** if you haven't already — contribute even $50/month to start.

## The One Financial Principle That Matters Most

Of all the complexity in personal finance, one rule drives the most outcomes:

> **Spend less than you earn. Invest the difference consistently. Give compound interest time to work.**

Everything else — tax optimization, asset allocation, insurance selection — is refinement around that core principle.
