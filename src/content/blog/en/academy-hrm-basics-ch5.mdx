## 1. Performance Management Framework

**Purpose**

- Link organizational goals to individual objectives
- Drive performance improvement and tie it to rewards
- Identify development needs

**Performance Management Cycle**

- Goal Setting → Ongoing Coaching → Performance Appraisal → Feedback

**MBO (Management by Objectives — Drucker)**

- Goals set jointly by manager and employee
- Promotes self-control and autonomy

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)**

- Measurable, specific metrics
- **BSC (Balanced Scorecard)**: Financial · Customer · Internal Process · Learning & Growth

Performance management is the ongoing work of agreeing expectations, monitoring evidence and supporting improvement. An appraisal is one judgment within that process. A dashboard metric does not represent the whole contribution of an employee. Define the unit, period, quality boundary and relevant constraints before linking a number to a reward.

OPM distinguishes an element—what work is required—from a standard—how well it must be performed. Its guidance includes quality, quantity, timeliness and cost-effectiveness, and requires descriptive measures to identify the judge and observable evidence. These analytical distinctions are useful beyond the US federal context; its agency-specific requirements are not universal legal rules. [OPM, Developing Performance Standards](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/performance-management/reference-materials/articles/developing-performance-standards/).

## 2. Performance Appraisal Methods

**Absolute Standards**

- Evaluate performance against pre-set objectives
- BARS (Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales): Uses behavioral examples as anchors
- **MBO**: Measures achievement of agreed objectives

**Relative Standards**

- **Forced Distribution**: Assigns employees to fixed percentage bands (e.g., Top 20% · Middle 70% · Bottom 10%)
- **Ranking**: Orders employees from best to worst

**360-Degree Feedback**

- Input from manager, direct reports, peers, and customers
- Best suited for development purposes
- Linking to compensation increases risk of distortion

State the purpose and data-access rules before collecting feedback. CCL's developmental approach emphasizes clarity, confidentiality and how participants use the results. That is a design policy, not proof that all multi-source ratings are accurate. [CCL, implementing 360 feedback](https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/how-to-implement-360-feedback-initiative/).

## 3. Rating Errors

**Halo Effect**

- One strong trait colors the entire evaluation
- An employee who excels in one area is rated highly on everything

**Central Tendency**

- Clustering ratings near the midpoint
- Avoiding extreme scores

**Leniency / Strictness Errors**

- **Leniency**: Consistently giving scores that are too high
- **Strictness**: Consistently giving scores that are too low

**Recency Error**

- Over-weighting recent performance
- Earlier performance (e.g., from the first half of the year) is forgotten

**Similarity Bias**

- Rating employees who are similar to the rater more favorably

## 4. Effective Feedback

**Feedback Principles**

- Behavior-specific (not vague impressions)
- **Timely**: Given close to the observed event
- **Goal-oriented**: Focuses on how to improve
- **Two-way**: Conversational, not one-directional

**Feedback Models**

- **SBI Model**: Situation → Behavior → Impact
- **Negative feedback**: Approach as coaching, not criticism
- **Positive reinforcement**: Acknowledge and strengthen desired behaviors

## 5. Separate a Rating Pattern from Its Cause

| Observation | What needs investigation |
|---|---|
| Most scores are high | The team may be strong, standards may be easy or raters may be lenient. A distribution alone does not identify the cause. |
| Most scores are near the midpoint | Performance may be similar, evidence may be weak or raters may avoid differentiation. Do not assume fear is the universal explanation. |
| One dimension predicts every rating | Check whether a genuine relationship or an overall impression is driving the pattern; examine dimension-specific evidence. |
| Raters disagree | They may observe different situations or use different standards. Disagreement is not automatically an error. |

Calibration means comparing evidence with agreed standards, not negotiating everyone into a predetermined curve. Behavioral anchors can clarify what to observe, but badly chosen anchors can omit important work. Rater training needs practice with common examples and reasons for judgments; simply naming halo or recency bias does not demonstrate that either has been reduced.

### A Quota Does Not Identify an Absolute Failure

In a fictional team of ten, all employees meet an independently defined minimum quality standard of 90 points. Their observed scores range from 90 to 99. A rule placing 10% in a bottom band puts one person there, even though nobody fell below the minimum. The band describes relative position under that rule, not failure to meet the standard.

Conversely, a team can contain ten employees who all miss an absolute requirement; a forced top band would not make its member satisfactory. If a quota is used, explain its purpose, comparison population, ties and consequences. Do not rename a relative classification as an evidence-based diagnosis of poor performance. The example illustrates the arithmetic of the rule, not a study of its effects on retention or morale.

### A KPI Can Reward the Wrong Boundary

Two fictional service employees work during the same evaluation period. A records 100 closed cases, of which 10 reopen within the specified follow-up window. B records 90, of which 2 reopen. Assume each reopened case is counted once and the follow-up window is complete for both cohorts.

| Employee | Recorded outcomes |
|---|---|
| A | 100 closures; 10% reopen; 90 remain closed |
| B | 90 closures; approximately 2.22% reopen; 88 remain closed |

Closure counts differ by ten, but cases remaining closed differ by two. Neither calculation establishes who performed better: case difficulty, assigned volume, team assistance and other quality requirements may differ. “Remaining closed” is itself only a proxy; silence from a customer does not establish a correct resolution. A target that rewards closures alone may create an incentive to close prematurely. Audit the definition and sample cases rather than assuming the highest count is the greatest contribution.

For 360 feedback, select people who have actually observed the relevant behaviors. More raters do not automatically provide independent evidence; they may share the same impression or observe the same incident. Keep source-group differences visible when meaningful, consider missing responses and do not promise anonymity that a tiny group or a distinctive comment cannot provide. Developmental interpretation and a formal employment decision need different evidence and governance.

## 6. Practice Quiz

**Q. What are the advantages and disadvantages of forced distribution?**
**Worked answer.** It makes relative differentiation explicit and constrains the distribution of labels, but does not establish the accuracy of the underlying judgments. In the ten-person example, the bottom-band employee still meets the absolute standard. Possible effects on competition, collaboration and trust depend on how the rule is used and should be investigated rather than asserted as universal outcomes. A claim about a named company's policy or employee departures requires dated evidence; it is not needed to understand the method's logic.

**Q. How can central tendency errors be reduced?**
**Worked answer.** First determine whether clustering reflects similar performance, unclear standards, missing evidence or reluctance to differentiate. Collect evidence across the review period, clarify anchors and practice applying them consistently. Allow employees to correct factual omissions. Additional observers can broaden coverage, but shared biases and different observation contexts remain possible. A quota changes the shape of the distribution, not necessarily its validity. Evaluate improvements by whether judgments are better supported, not merely by whether scores spread out.

Useful feedback names the event, observable conduct and its consequences, then invites the employee's account and agrees the next action. “You are not committed” is an inference about a person; “three handovers lacked the agreed escalation record” identifies evidence that can be checked. Consider access, workload and authority before attributing the behavior to attitude. Keep records proportionate and confidential, and distinguish development support from decisions about pay or continued employment.
