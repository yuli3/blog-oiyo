## What Is Caffeine?

The average person in many countries consumes coffee every single day. Yet for many of them, that daily habit is quietly stealing sleep. Studies consistently find that a significant portion of adults are in a state of chronic caffeine overconsumption without realizing it.

**Caffeine** is a **xanthine alkaloid** that occurs naturally in coffee, tea, chocolate, and energy drinks. It works by blocking adenosine receptors in the brain, producing an alerting effect.

Adenosine is a molecule that accumulates in the brain the longer you stay awake, gradually building the pressure to sleep. Caffeine **temporarily blocks** those receptors, cutting off the drowsiness signal — it does not eliminate fatigue, it just masks it for a while.

## 4. Caffeine Half-Life and Sleep

What a 5.5-hour half-life means for a standard americano (~150 mg):

| Time Consumed | Caffeine Remaining at 11 p.m. | Sleep Impact |
|--------------|-------------------------------|--------------|
| 8 a.m. | ~6% (~10 mg) | Negligible |
| 12 p.m. | ~15% (~22 mg) | Minimal |
| 2 p.m. | ~21% (~32 mg) | Mild |
| 4 p.m. | ~30% (~45 mg) | Noticeable |
| 6 p.m. | ~43% (~65 mg) | May delay sleep onset |
| 8 p.m. | ~61% (~91 mg) | Reduces sleep quality |

<Callout type="tip" title="Caffeine Cut-Off Formula">
**Desired bedtime − 10 hours** = last recommended caffeine intake

Example: Want to sleep at 11 p.m. → Avoid caffeine after 1 p.m.  
On days when sleep quality is especially important, finish all caffeine before noon.
</Callout>

Individual caffeine sensitivity is determined largely by the **CYP1A2 gene**.

### Self-Assessment Checklist

If three or more of the following apply to you, you are likely a slow/sensitive caffeine metabolizer:
- [ ] Drinking coffee after 3 p.m. makes it hard to fall asleep
- [ ] After coffee, you notice a racing or fluttering heartbeat
- [ ] Coffee on an empty stomach causes stomach discomfort
- [ ] You feel more anxious on days you drink coffee
- [ ] Even one cup of coffee sends you to the bathroom more frequently
- [ ] Coffee makes headaches worse rather than better

During the times when cortisol is naturally high (8–10 a.m.), caffeine is **less effective** and may actually build **cortisol tolerance** over time.

**Optimal caffeine timing**:
- **90 minutes after waking** (roughly 9:30–10:30 a.m.)
- **Second cup during the afternoon energy slump** (2–3 p.m.)
- Avoid caffeine within 10 hours of your planned bedtime

## 7. Caffeine Side Effects and Overconsumption

| Symptom | Threshold | Response |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| Anxiety and restlessness | 400+ mg, or 200+ mg in sensitive types | Drink plenty of water; take a walk outside |
| Heart palpitations | Highly individual (100+ mg in sensitive types) | Stop consuming immediately; rest |
| Insomnia | Consumed within 6 hours of bedtime | Set a caffeine cut-off time |
| Headache | Sudden caffeine withdrawal | Taper gradually (reduce by ~10% per day) |
| Acid reflux | Consumed on an empty stomach | Drink coffee 30 minutes after eating |
| Diuretic effect | High intake | Match with equal water intake |

<Callout type="info" title="Caffeine Dependence and Withdrawal">
Regular consumption of 250 mg or more daily can lead to **caffeine dependence**. Stopping suddenly triggers withdrawal symptoms — headache, fatigue, difficulty concentrating — within **24–48 hours**. A two-week gradual reduction is recommended.
</Callout>
