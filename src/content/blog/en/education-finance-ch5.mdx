# Chapter 5. The Moment of Decision: Capital Budgeting (NPV vs. IRR)

Should a company build a new semiconductor factory that costs $10 billion? Should a movie studio greenlight a $200 million blockbuster? These are the high-stakes decisions of **Capital Budgeting**.

Today, we will learn how to transition from "hope" to "logic" by using mathematical tools to determine if a project will actually create value for shareholders.

---

## 1. NPV: The Gold Standard

**Net Present Value (NPV)** is the cornerstone of corporate finance. it is calculated as:
**<mark>NPV = (Present Value of Future Cash Inflows) - (Initial Investment)</mark>**

- **NPV > 0**: The project creates value. <mark><strong>Accept!</strong></mark>
- **NPV < 0**: The project destroys value. <mark><strong>Reject!</strong></mark>

## 2. IRR: The Rate of Intuition

**Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** is the discount rate that makes the NPV of a project exactly zero. It represents the "break-even" rate of a project.

<LectureProcess
  steps={[
    { label: "Cost of Capital", description: "Know your required rate of return (k)" },
    { label: "Calculate IRR", description: "Solve for the rate where PV(Inflow) = PV(Outflow)" },
    { label: "Compare", description: "Is IRR > k? (Return > Cost)" },
    { label: "Decision", description: "If Return is higher than Cost, Accept!" },
  ]}
/>

<mark><strong>The Clash of Titans</strong></mark>: Most of the time, NPV and IRR agree. However, when projects have different scales or timing, **NPV is always the superior choice** because it measures the _absolute_ wealth created, not just a percentage.

## 4. Conclusion: Wealth Creation in Action

Capital budgeting is not just about crunching numbers. It’s about <mark><strong>"Strategic Resource Allocation."</strong></mark> A company that consistently accepts (+) NPV projects will see its stock price soar as it builds a future of sustainable cash flows.
