Advanced accounting moves beyond a single entity to relationships between entities. The first topic is the business combination, in which one entity obtains control of another **business**. K-IFRS 1103 *Business Combinations* (IFRS 3) requires the **acquisition method** for all business combinations. Consolidating the subsidiaries acquired this way continues from [chapter 2](/en/education-accounting-ch9/).

## 1. A business or an asset acquisition?

A business is an integrated set of activities and assets with inputs and a **substantive process** that together contribute to the ability to create outputs. If it is not a business, the transaction is treated as the acquisition of a group of assets: the cost is allocated by relative fair values and no goodwill is recognized.

The 2020 amendment introduced an optional **concentration test**. If substantially all the fair value of the gross assets acquired is concentrated in a single identifiable asset (or group of similar assets), one can conclude simply that it is not a business. Buying a single let building is usually an asset acquisition.

## 3. Worked example

On 1 January 20X1 company P acquired 80% of S for ₩10 million in cash and agreed to pay a further ₩1 million if S's profit exceeds a target after one year (fair value of the contingent consideration at the acquisition date: ₩500,000). Due diligence and legal fees were ₩300,000. S's net assets have a carrying amount of ₩8 million at the acquisition date; its land has a fair value ₩1.5 million above carrying amount; and a customer relationship intangible not on S's books has a fair value of ₩500,000.

- Fair value of identifiable net assets: $800 + 150 + 50 = 1{,}000$ (₩10,000), i.e. ₩10 million
- Consideration transferred: $1{,}000 + 50 = 1{,}050$ (including contingent consideration)
- Acquisition-related costs of ₩300,000: not included in consideration; **expensed as incurred**

### A. Goodwill by method of measuring non-controlling interests

For each business combination, non-controlling interests can be measured at **fair value** or at the **proportionate share of identifiable net assets**.

It is common for the fair value of the NCI to be lower per share than the price paid for the controlling interest, because the control premium attaches only to the parent's share.

### B. Subsequent treatment of contingent consideration

If contingent consideration is classified as a **financial liability**, it is remeasured at fair value at each reporting date, with changes in profit or loss; goodwill is not adjusted. However, changes arising from additional information about facts that existed at the acquisition date and identified within the **measurement period** (up to one year from the acquisition date) adjust goodwill retrospectively. Contingent consideration classified as **equity** is not remeasured.

## 4. Bargain purchases and the measurement period

If the consideration transferred is less than the fair value of identifiable net assets, the entity first reviews whether all assets and liabilities were correctly identified and the measurement procedures were appropriate. Any remaining difference is recognized as a **bargain purchase gain** in profit at the acquisition date. It can arise when a seller is forced to sell quickly.

If the combination was accounted for with provisional amounts at the acquisition date, those amounts can be adjusted retrospectively during the measurement period as information is obtained about facts existing at the acquisition date. Adjustments after the measurement period are made only to correct errors.

<Callout type="warning">
Goodwill is not amortized but only tested for impairment annually ([Intermediate Accounting chapter 5](/en/academy-intermediate-accounting-ch5/)), so large goodwill can turn into a large impairment in a single year. And identifying fewer intangibles such as customer relationships or technology enlarges goodwill and reduces amortization, making later profits look better. For acquisitive companies, check the ratio of intangibles to goodwill in the purchase price allocation (PPA) note.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

Company Q acquired 60% of R for ₩9 million in cash and 1,000 of Q's own shares (share price at the acquisition date ₩10,000). Share issue costs were ₩200,000 and legal fees ₩250,000. The fair value of R's identifiable net assets is ₩20 million, and NCI is measured at the proportionate share. What is goodwill (or the bargain purchase gain), and how are the two costs treated?

Consideration transferred is $900 + 1{,}000 = 1{,}900$ (₩10,000). NCI is $2{,}000 \times 40\% = 800$. Goodwill is $1{,}900 + 800 - 2{,}000 = 700$, i.e. ₩7 million. The share issue costs of ₩200,000 are deducted from the issue proceeds of the shares (share premium), and the legal fees of ₩250,000 are expensed as incurred. Neither is included in consideration transferred or goodwill.
