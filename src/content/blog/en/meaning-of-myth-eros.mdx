Eros is the personification of love, desire, and attraction in Greek mythology, and he symbolizes the impulse toward union that sets the world and human beings in motion.

## Core Myths

Eros appears in very different forms depending on the tradition. In Hesiod's *Theogony*, he is one of the primordial forces that emerges after Chaos: a cosmic principle that draws beings toward one another and makes generation possible. Here, Eros is not so much a cute god of love as a fundamental desire that moves the world.

In later traditions, he is often described as Aphrodite's son, a playful boy who shoots his bow and arrows to make gods and humans fall in love. In the story of Psyche, Eros himself is wounded by love and grows through it. The central point of this myth is that the being who causes love also undergoes love's trials.

Eros's arrows show that love is not chosen by will alone. Planning whom to love does not mean love will follow the plan, and deciding not to love does not always make the heart stop. Yet the myth does not turn desire into an excuse for irresponsibility. The attraction Eros awakens must pass through trust, testing, hurt, and patience before it becomes a relationship.

## Reading as an Archetype

From a Jungian perspective, Eros is the archetype of the life impulse toward connection, desire, and relationship. In modern life, he appears in the moment the heart moves toward someone, in the longing that begins creative work, and in the desire to become intimate with knowledge or the world. Eros opens the separated self outward.

His shadow is projection. When we cover another person with our own lack or ideal, love becomes fantasy rather than encounter. The Eros archetype tells us not to be ashamed of desire, but also warns against the hasty conclusion that attraction itself is truth.

Eros moves a person beyond the self. Without the wish to learn, meet, make, and touch, life may be safe, but it loses vitality. For this reason, Eros flows not only through romance but also through study, art, faith, and friendship. Mature Eros creates connection while respecting difference. Immature Eros does not see the other as a real person, but consumes them only as an object of one's own desire. This archetype demands the process of maturing attraction into relationship.

Eros is a signal of lack, but also a signal of possibility. By seeing what I am drawn to, I can recognize the desires and imagination that are still alive within me. Attraction, however, is only the starting point. For love to become a relationship, it must also accept the other person's freedom, time, wounds, and reality.

If that process is not passed through, Eros remains a force that repeats the same fantasy rather than one that expands life. When it is passed through, he leads people toward deeper encounter and creation.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Eros the same being as Cupid?**

A. Cupid or Amor in Roman mythology corresponds to the Greek Eros. However, it is important to distinguish primordial Eros from the later image of the boy god.

**Q. Does Eros mean only sexual desire?**

A. Sexual attraction is included, but more broadly Eros means the force that moves toward life, creation, relationship, and union.
