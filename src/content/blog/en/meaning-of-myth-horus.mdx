Horus is the falcon god who symbolizes the sky and kingship in Egyptian mythology. He appears in many forms across regional traditions, but his best-known image is as the son of Osiris and Isis, the heir who fights Set to reclaim his father's kingship. The pharaoh was understood as the living Horus, and the legitimacy of royal authority was deeply tied to the myth of Horus.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

The core myth of Horus is the struggle for kingship against Set. After Osiris dies, Horus grows up under the protection of his mother Isis, and once he reaches adulthood, he demands the throne from Set. The conflict between the two gods is transmitted as a long process involving not only violence, but also trials, deception, tests, and the judgment of the gods.

In this struggle, Horus loses an eye or is wounded. Later, that eye is restored and becomes the "Eye of Horus," a symbol of protection and healing. Horus's victory is not simple revenge, but the act of reestablishing the order of the dead king Osiris on earth. For this reason, Horus is both a young warrior and a symbol of the legitimate heir.

## Reading as an Archetype — Jungian Archetypal View

From a Jungian perspective, Horus is the archetype of the **wounded hero** and the **legitimate heir**. He is not a perfected king from birth; he is protected, grows, and gains his place through conflict with a rival. In modern terms, he resembles scenes of family business succession, generational transition within an organization, or a young leader standing against unjust power.

The key point when reading Horus is that victory does not mean the wound has been erased. Instead, the restored eye becomes his symbol. The Horus archetype therefore shows that a wound can become not a defect in identity, but a broader field of vision and a power of protection.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. What is the relationship between Horus and the pharaoh?**

A. The Egyptian king was understood as the living Horus. In other words, kingship was seen as the role that embodied Horus's order and protection on earth.

**Q. What does the Eye of Horus symbolize?**

A. It symbolizes wounding and recovery, protection, healing, and wholeness. It became widely used as a symbol from the story in which the lost eye is restored.
