Wadjet is the cobra goddess and the guardian deity of Lower Egypt in Egyptian mythology. She appears as the Uraeus cobra on the pharaoh's forehead, symbolizing the power to protect the king and incinerate enemies. While Nekhbet is the vulture goddess of Upper Egypt, Wadjet serves as the cobra goddess of Lower Egypt, together forming the unified kingship of the two lands.

## Core Mythology — Key Episodes

The most significant symbol of Wadjet is the Uraeus on the crown. The cobra rising from the pharaoh's forehead is not merely an ornament, but a living warning that guards the king's divine authority. It was believed that if an enemy approached the throne, Wadjet would defend it with the power of fire and venom.

As the guardian goddess of Lower Egypt, Wadjet also held great political significance. The unification of Upper and Lower Egypt was expressed through the union of the deities of both regions, and Wadjet represented the identity of the northern land. She became both a regional guardian and an essential emblem of the unified monarchy.

## Reading as an Archetype — Jungian Perspective

From a Jungian perspective, Wadjet is the archetype of the **protective boundary** and the **royal eye**. She detects threats quickly and issues warnings before an intrusion occurs. In a modern context, she resembles symbols of security systems, legal authority, a leader's sense of vigilance, and the protection of an organization's core values.

The key to understanding Wadjet is that protection is not achieved through invisible kindness alone. Some forms of protection require clear warnings and the prohibition of access. Thus, the Wadjet archetype symbolizes the fact that for authority to be healthy, it must monitor not only external threats but also internal arrogance. The guardian on the king's forehead is a sign of both the power and the responsibility of kingship.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Wadjet a cobra goddess?**

A. Yes. Wadjet is the goddess of the cobra and the Uraeus, a being who protects Lower Egypt and the pharaoh's kingship.

**Q. What does the Uraeus signify?**

A. It is the image of a cobra standing before the crown. It symbolizes the king's divine authority, warning, and the protective power to repel enemies.
