# Microeconomics — Choice Starts with the Technology Constraint, Before Any Price Appears

If [Introduction to Economics, Chapter 1](/en/academy-economics-basics-ch1/) pinned down the definition of opportunity cost, this chapter writes that cost as **a slope on the production technology**. Every optimisation problem in microeconomics starts by writing down, in quantities, "what do I give up?" The consumer-side counterpart of this slope (the marginal rate of substitution) returns in Chapter 4, and the efficiency condition where the two meet returns in Chapter 10.

## 1. Opportunity cost counts costs that never appear in the books

The **opportunity cost** of a choice is the value of the next-best alternative given up. It includes not only **explicit costs**, which are paid in money, but also **implicit costs**: income forgone even though no money changes hands.

Suppose an employee quits a job paying KRW 35 million a year to spend one year in a graduate programme with KRW 10 million tuition.

- Explicit cost: tuition, KRW 10 million
- Implicit cost: forgone salary, KRW 35 million
- Total opportunity cost: KRW 45 million

Living expenses are left out. You eat and sleep whether you study or keep working, so they are not an **additional** cost created by this choice. Opportunity cost is the difference between "what you spend if you choose it" and "what you would spend anyway".

## 2. The slope of the PPF is the marginal opportunity cost

Suppose an economy uses its resources and technology to produce only chips and wheat. The line joining the maximum combinations it can produce when all resources are used efficiently is the **production possibilities frontier (PPF)**.

Moving from A to B gains 2 chips and gives up 4 units of wheat, so one chip costs 2 units of wheat. Moving from B to C gains 4 chips for 10 units of wheat, so the opportunity cost of a chip rises to 2.5 units of wheat. The fact that each additional chip costs more wheat is called **increasing opportunity cost**.

<FormulaBox
  label="Marginal rate of transformation"
  formula="MRT = \left|\frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta X}\right| = \frac{MC_x}{MC_y}"
  description="The absolute value of the PPF slope. It is the wheat that could have been produced with the resources needed for one more chip, so it equals the ratio of the two goods' marginal costs."
/>

Why is the MRT a ratio of marginal costs? Let one unit of resources be worth $w$. One chip needs $MC_x/w$ units of resources. Used for wheat, those resources produce $(MC_x/w)/(MC_y/w)=MC_x/MC_y$ units of wheat, because each unit of wheat needs $MC_y/w$ units.

**Why is it concave?** Because resources are not equally useful for both goods. When we make only a few chips we move the resources best suited to chips first (skilled engineers, clean rooms). As we make more chips we must also move resources better suited to wheat (farmland, farm labour), so each extra chip costs more wheat. If every resource were equally useful for both goods, the PPF would be a straight line and the MRT constant.

## 3. Being on the curve does not make a point optimal

A point on the PPF is **technically efficient**: no resources are left idle. Whether that combination is what people want is a separate question. **Allocative efficiency** is reached when the rate at which production can trade the goods (the MRT) equals the rate at which consumers are willing to trade them (the marginal rate of substitution, MRS).

<FormulaBox
  label="Allocative efficiency condition"
  formula="MRT_{xy} = MRS_{xy}"
  description="In competitive markets firms choose MRT = Px/Py and consumers choose MRS = Px/Py, so prices link the two conditions. Chapters 4 and 10 return to this."
/>

If everyone wants much more wheat but the economy produces at C (8 chips, 4 wheat), production is technically efficient but allocatively inefficient. "On the PPF = optimal" is a false statement.

## 4. Why the curve shifts

- **More resources** (labour and capital accumulation): both intercepts move outward.
- **Technical progress in one good**: only that good's intercept moves. If chip technology doubles, the chip intercept moves from 9 to 18 while the wheat intercept stays at 20. Because chips now need fewer resources, the freed resources can go to wheat, so at most combinations the economy can also have more wheat.

## 5. Sunk costs are not opportunity costs

A cost already paid that cannot be recovered is not an opportunity cost, because nothing is given up by future choices. Suppose KRW 30 million has been spent on unrecoverable equipment, and a further KRW 8 million would let the project recover KRW 12 million. The question is "do I get KRW 12 million by spending another KRW 8 million?" The net benefit is +KRW 4 million, so the project should continue. Adding the KRW 30 million already spent — "we spend KRW 38 million to recover 12" — leads to the wrong decision to stop. The opposite tendency, continuing a losing project because of sunk costs, is covered in [Behavioural Economics, Chapter 6](/en/academy-behavioral-economics-ch6/).

<Callout type="warning">
The PPF contains only private costs. If a chip factory pollutes water and lowers the wheat harvest, the economy's social MRT is steeper than the private MRT. The output mix a market chooses using the private MRT contains too many chips from society's point of view. That gap is covered in [Chapter 10](/en/education-economics-ch10/) and [Public Finance, Chapter 1](/en/academy-public-finance-ch1/).
</Callout>

If two countries have different MRTs, each can specialise in the good with the lower opportunity cost and trade, so that both consume combinations outside their own PPFs. This is comparative advantage; the calculations are in [International Trade](/en/education-economics-ch15/).

## Check your understanding

A straight-line PPF allows at most 10 chips or 20 units of wheat. If the economy makes 6 chips, what is the most wheat it can make? The opportunity cost of a chip is a constant $20/10=2$ units of wheat, so wheat is $20-2\times 6=8$. The point (6, 6) lies inside the curve. The opportunity cost has not changed; resources that could make 2 units of wheat are sitting idle.
