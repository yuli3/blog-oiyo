## What Is a Labor and Employment Attorney?

A **Labor and Employment Attorney** is a licensed attorney who specializes in the legal relationship between employers and employees — advising on hiring practices, wage and hour compliance, discrimination claims, wrongful termination, collective bargaining, and workplace safety.

**Licensing authority**: Each state's bar association (overseen by the state Supreme Court)  
**Annual new bar admissions**: Approximately 40,000–50,000 per year nationally  
**Exclusive practice areas**: Court representation, filing EEOC charges, negotiating CBA terms, representing clients before the NLRB

The US labor and employment field is among the most active areas of law, driven by evolving federal and state statutes, agency rulemaking, and a steady volume of litigation.

---

## Eligibility Requirements

Becoming a licensed attorney in the US requires the following:

1. **Juris Doctor (JD) degree** from an ABA-accredited law school (3 years full-time, or 4 years part-time)
2. **Bar exam passage** in the state(s) where you intend to practice
3. **Character and fitness review** by the state bar
4. **MPRE (Multistate Professional Responsibility Exam)** — required in most states

**Disqualifying factors** (vary by state):
- Felony convictions, particularly for crimes of moral turpitude
- Prior attorney discipline or disbarment
- Serious financial misconduct (fraud, embezzlement)

## Exam Structure

### The Bar Exam

Most states administer the **Uniform Bar Exam (UBE)**, which is accepted in over 40 jurisdictions and consists of:

| Component | Format | Weight |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Multistate Bar Exam (MBE) | 200 multiple-choice questions, 6 hours | 50% |
| Multistate Essay Exam (MEE) | 6 essay questions, 3 hours | 30% |
| Multistate Performance Test (MPT) | 2 practical tasks, 3 hours | 20% |

**Passing score**: 266 on a 400-point scale for most UBE states (some states set higher minimums).

**Minimum passing score for labor/employment-heavy states**:
- California: Separate state exam, non-UBE (higher pass threshold, ~40–45% pass rate)
- New York, Texas, Illinois: UBE jurisdictions with significant labor and employment bars

### MPRE (Professional Responsibility)

A separate 60-question multiple-choice exam covering the ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct. Most states require a scaled score of 75–85. Typically taken during law school.

## Subject Areas and Study Strategy

### Federal Labor Law (Core)

The backbone of US employment practice:

**Key statutes**:
- **FLSA** (Fair Labor Standards Act) — minimum wage, overtime, child labor
- **NLRA** (National Labor Relations Act) — union rights, collective bargaining, unfair labor practices
- **Title VII** (Civil Rights Act, 1964) — employment discrimination (race, color, religion, sex, national origin)
- **ADEA** (Age Discrimination in Employment Act) — workers 40+
- **ADA** (Americans with Disabilities Act) — disability accommodation
- **FMLA** (Family and Medical Leave Act) — unpaid protected leave
- **OSHA** (Occupational Safety and Health Act) — workplace safety

Study strategy:
- Master the elements and defenses for each major statute
- Practice issue-spotting essays with overlapping statute scenarios
- Review EEOC enforcement guidance and key Supreme Court precedents

### Civil Procedure (MBE Core)

Heavily tested on the MBE. For employment attorneys, civil procedure knowledge is essential for:
- Filing in federal district court (EEOC right-to-sue → federal lawsuit)
- Class action certification (Rule 23 — wage-and-hour class actions are major litigation)
- Discovery rules (employee records, communications in discrimination cases)

### Contracts (MBE Core)

Employment contracts, non-compete agreements, arbitration clauses, and severance agreements are daily tools.

- Formation, consideration, breach, and remedies
- Enforceability of non-competes (highly state-dependent — California effectively bans them)
- Mandatory arbitration agreements in employment contexts (post-*Epic Systems* landscape)

### Constitutional Law (MBE Core)

Public-sector employment (government employees) adds constitutional dimensions:
- First Amendment and public employee speech
- Fourteenth Amendment due process for tenured employees
- Equal protection and employment classifications

### Torts (MBE Core)

- Intentional infliction of emotional distress (IIED) in harassment cases
- Defamation in employment references
- Negligent hiring / negligent supervision claims

### Administrative Law (Employment-Specific)

- NLRB (National Labor Relations Board) practice
- EEOC charge process and investigation
- OSHA inspection and citation procedures
- Department of Labor (DOL) wage and hour enforcement

### MEE/Essay — Employment Law Application

While the MBE tests broad law, MEE essays may test:
- Unfair labor practice scenarios (NLRA Section 7/8 analysis)
- Title VII disparate treatment vs. disparate impact
- FMLA interference vs. retaliation analysis
- ADA interactive accommodation process

## Study Timeline by Background

### Standard JD Path (Non-Law Undergraduate)

**3 years JD + 2–3 months bar prep** before licensure.

- 1L: Foundational courses (Contracts, Torts, Civil Procedure, Property, Crim Law)
- 2L–3L: Employment Law, Labor Law, Administrative Law, Discrimination Law clinics
- Bar prep: Commercial course (Themis, Barbri, Kaplan) — 8–10 weeks intensive

### HR Professional Returning to Law School

Law school admission is possible at any age. HR professionals bring valuable context:
- FLSA wage-and-hour knowledge transfers directly
- FMLA and ADA administration experience is a differentiator
- The professional context aids law review writing and clinical work

### Non-Attorney HR or Labor Relations Professionals

If full law school is not the path, consider:
- **SHRM-CP/SHRM-SCP** certification (HR credential)
- **NALA Certified Paralegal** — support attorneys in employment cases without a JD
- **PHR/SPHR** (HR certification)
- **Cornell ILR certificate programs** — labor relations specialization for non-attorneys

## Career Paths After Bar Admission

### Private Practice — Employment Defense

Representing employers in EEOC charges, wage-and-hour audits, discrimination lawsuits, and union negotiations. Large law firms (Am Law 200) and boutique employment defense firms both hire actively.

### Private Practice — Plaintiff Employment

Representing employees in discrimination, harassment, wrongful termination, and wage theft cases. Many plaintiff-side firms work on contingency. Organizations like the National Employment Law Project and Legal Aid provide pro bono and subsidized work.

### In-House Corporate Employment Counsel

Fortune 500 companies maintain in-house employment counsel teams to manage HR legal risk, draft policies, advise on terminations, and defend employment litigation. This is a prestigious and well-compensated path.

### Government and Agency Practice

- **NLRB**: Staff attorneys and field examiners investigating and litigating unfair labor practice charges
- **EEOC**: Attorneys in field offices investigating discrimination charges
- **Department of Labor (Wage and Hour Division)**: Enforcement and litigation
- **State labor departments**: Similar roles at the state level

### Union-Side Practice

Labor unions (AFL-CIO affiliates, SEIU, UAW, etc.) employ staff attorneys to negotiate collective bargaining agreements, represent members in grievance arbitrations, and enforce labor law.

## Key Comparison: Attorney vs. HR Professional vs. Labor Relations Specialist

| Role | Credential | Core Work | Court/Agency Access |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------------------|
| Employment Attorney | JD + Bar | Litigation, compliance, contract | Yes — full representation |
| SHRM-CP/SCP | HR certification | HR policy, administration | No — advisory only |
| NALA Paralegal | Paralegal cert | Document prep, support | Supervised only |
| Labor Relations Specialist | Experience-based | CBA admin, grievances | No — except arbitration advocacy |
