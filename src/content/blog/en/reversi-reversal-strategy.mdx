### Introduction: "A Minute to Learn, a Lifetime to Master"

That's the tagline for Reversi (also known as Othello) — and it earns every word. Black and white tiles flip each other's colors as players bracket their opponent's pieces. The rules are simple enough for a child; the strategy is deep enough to fuel decades of competitive play and AI research.

What makes Reversi fascinating beyond the board is how closely it mirrors real-world dynamics: a market leader can be disrupted in a single strategic move, or a small startup can outflank an established giant by controlling the right corners. Today, let's use Reversi to explore what "real ownership" of position — in games, business, or life — actually looks like.

## 2. The Expert's Formula for Reversal

### ① The Value of Corners and Edges
The four corner squares of the Reversi board cannot be flipped once taken — they are permanent positions. In business, the equivalent is securing something that can't be taken away: a **core technology**, a **network effect**, or a **structural distribution advantage**. Competing furiously in the center while ignoring corners is a common beginner mistake — in games and in strategy.

### ② Mobility Matters More Than Territory
Novice players try to maximize their tile count every turn. Experts focus on **limiting the opponent's legal moves** — reducing their options until they are forced to play where you want them to play. This "mobility strategy" is the core of negotiation leverage: whoever controls the available choices controls the outcome.

### ③ The Strategic Concession
Expert players will sometimes deliberately place a tile in an unfavorable position — giving up edge squares to secure a corner later. This looks like losing in the short term but is part of a larger plan. Business leaders make the same trade: accepting a quarter of lower revenue to protect long-term margins, or ceding a product category to dominate a more defensible one.

## 3. How to Find the Turning Point When You're Behind

The board filling up doesn't mean the game is over — it often means a reversal is imminent.

1. **Don't concede until the final move**: In Reversi, a single late-game placement can flip dozens of pieces simultaneously. Comebacks happen right at the end.
2. **See the whole board**: Each move creates diagonal and horizontal chains that will matter several turns later. Train yourself to think two or three moves ahead, not just the immediate flip.
3. **Let them take the center**: Allowing your opponent to feel comfortable in the middle while you quietly surround the edges is classic misdirection. The player who controls the perimeter often wins the interior.

## Conclusion: The Board Can Always Flip

Reversi's greatest lesson is that being behind now tells you almost nothing about where you'll finish. The same is true in business and in life. If you feel stalled or outpaced right now, the question to ask isn't "how did I get here?" — it's "where is the one corner position I haven't claimed yet?"

Today's game might just give you the insight you need to find it.
