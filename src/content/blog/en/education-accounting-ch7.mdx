K-IFRS 1115 (IFRS 15) applies the same five-step model to every customer contract regardless of industry. Revenue is recognized **in line with how and when control of promised goods or services passes to the customer**, at the amount of consideration the entity **expects** to be entitled to. The basic concepts were covered in [Principles of Accounting chapter 6](/en/academy-accounting-basics-ch3/). Compound instruments such as convertible bonds have moved to [chapter 11](/en/academy-intermediate-accounting-ch11/).

## 2. Performance obligations and allocation: a bundled phone contract

A telecom company gives a customer a phone for ₩0 in exchange for a 24-month commitment to a plan at ₩50,000 a month. The transaction price is $5 \times 24 = 120$ (₩10,000), i.e. ₩1.2 million. The phone's stand-alone selling price is ₩800,000, and the same service sold separately costs ₩30,000 a month (₩720,000 over 24 months).

The phone and the service can each benefit the customer on their own and are separable in the contract, so there are two performance obligations. The transaction price is split by relative stand-alone selling prices.

<FormulaBox
  label="Allocation by relative stand-alone selling prices"

Revenue of ₩632,000 is recognized on the day the phone is handed over, but no cash has been received. Because the right to consideration depends on something other than the passage of time (providing the service), a **contract asset** of ₩632,000 is recognized. Each month, when ₩50,000 of cash is received, service revenue of about ₩23,700 ($56.8/24$) is recognized and the contract asset is reduced by the remaining ₩26,300 or so.

## 3. Transaction price: variable consideration

When consideration varies because of discounts, rebates, performance bonuses, returns and the like, it is estimated using whichever method better predicts it: the **expected value** (when there are many similar contracts) or the **most likely amount** (when there are only two outcomes). It is then included in the transaction price only to the extent that it is **highly probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur** when the uncertainty is resolved (the constraint on variable consideration).

A manufacturer sells parts to a dealer at ₩100,000 each, but if annual purchases exceed 1,000 units the price is retrospectively reduced to ₩90,000 for all units. It sold 300 units in the first quarter, and it is highly probable the dealer will buy 1,200 units for the year. First-quarter revenue is $300 \times 9 = 2{,}700$ (₩10,000), i.e. ₩27 million, and ₩3 million of the ₩30 million received from the dealer is a **refund liability**.

## 4. Revenue over time: measuring progress

Revenue is recognized over time if any of the following applies: the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits (cleaning services); the entity creates or enhances an asset the customer controls (construction on the customer's land); or the entity creates an asset with no alternative use and has an enforceable right to payment for performance to date.

In year 2, total estimated cost rose to ₩9 million and the progress calculation changed. This is a change in estimate, so prior years are not restated; the cumulative figures catch up. If total estimated cost exceeds the contract price, onerous contract treatment, recognizing the whole expected loss immediately, is considered ([chapter 8](/en/education-accounting-ch5/)).

## 5. Rights of return and principal versus agent

**Sales with a right of return:** 100 units are sold at ₩100,000 each (cost ₩60,000 each), and 5% are expected to be returned. Revenue is $95 \times 10 = 950$ (₩10,000), i.e. ₩9.5 million, with a **refund liability** of ₩500,000. The right to recover the goods expected to be returned is recognized as an **asset for right to recover returned goods** of ₩300,000 ($5 \times 6$), and cost of sales is ₩5.7 million.

**Principal and agent:** if the entity **controls** the goods before they pass to the customer, it is a principal and recognizes revenue gross; if it only arranges for another party to supply them, it is an agent and recognizes its fee net. Inventory risk, discretion over price and responsibility for fulfilment are indicators of control. Online marketplaces are usually agents.

<Callout type="warning">
Revenue is the most common target of accounting fraud. Typical schemes include channel stuffing — pushing inventory into dealers at quarter-end — sales that conceal returns or side agreements, and understating total estimated cost for percentage-of-completion. Be suspicious of revenue recognition when receivables and contract assets grow faster than sales, when the share of sales at quarter-end is abnormally high, or when returns pile up in the following quarter.
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

Company BB sold a bundle of a software licence, installation and one year of technical support for ₩18 million. Their stand-alone selling prices are ₩12 million, ₩3 million and ₩5 million. Installation is a standard job that many other firms could perform; the licence was delivered on the contract date and installation was completed within a month. Support runs for one year from the contract date. How much revenue is recognized by the end of the first month?

All three elements are distinct, so there are three performance obligations. Total stand-alone selling prices are ₩20 million, so the ₩2 million discount is allocated proportionately: licence $1{,}800 \times 1{,}200/2{,}000 = 1{,}080$ (₩10,000), installation 270 and support 450. By the end of the first month, the licence (point in time) of ₩10.8 million, installation of ₩2.7 million and one month of support, $450 / 12 = 37.5$, are recognized — ₩13.875 million in total.
