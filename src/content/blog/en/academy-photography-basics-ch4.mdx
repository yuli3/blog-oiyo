## Light is everything in photography

The word "photography" comes from Greek and literally means **"drawing with light."** A camera is a device for recording light, and a photographer is someone who seeks out and works with it.

The same subject can look completely different depending on the direction, intensity, and color temperature of the light. Chasing good light is at the core of getting better at photography.

---

## Direction of light

Where light comes from shapes both the sense of depth and the mood of your subject.

### Front light

Light hits the subject **head-on** (coming from behind the photographer).

- **Traits**: shadows fall behind the subject → flat, little sense of depth
- **Pros**: even exposure, bright and vivid color
- **Cons**: can make a person squint
- **Uses**: commemorative photos, building facades at night

### Side light

Light hits the subject **from the side**.

- **Traits**: shadows fall on the opposite side → creates depth and emphasizes texture
- **Pros**: dramatic mood, emphasizes facial contours and texture
- **Cons**: strong shadows can leave half the face too dark
- **Uses**: dramatic portraits, texture-focused photos (stone, wood, skin)

### Back light

Light hits the subject **from behind** (the subject is facing away from the light).

- **Traits**: creates a **rim light** (a glowing outline) around the subject
- **Pros**: a mysterious, beautiful mood; silhouette effects
- **Cons**: exposure is tricky to nail; lens flare can appear
- **Uses**: sunset silhouettes, rim-lit portraits, light shining through leaves

> **Tip**: to brighten a backlit subject's face, use a reflector or fill flash.

### Top light

Light comes from **above**, shining down.

- **Midday sun**: creates shadows around the eyes — unfavorable for portraits
- **Studio lighting**: useful as a hair light
- **Food photography**: can be used effectively

## Natural light by time of day

### Golden hour

The **hour after sunrise** and the **hour before sunset**, when light takes on a golden glow.

- **Color temperature**: 2500–3500K (warm orange and gold)
- **Light angle**: low, producing a side-lighting effect → rich sense of depth
- **Traits**: long, soft shadows; everything is bathed in gold
- **Uses**: ideal for portraits, landscapes, and cityscapes

> **A photographer's sweet spot**: most standout photos are shot during golden hour.

### Blue hour

The brief **blue-toned window** right after sunset or right before sunrise (about 20–30 minutes).

- **Color temperature**: 5000–6000K (cool blue)
- **Traits**: a balance between natural light and artificial light (buildings, streetlights)
- **Uses**: city night scenes, building photography — right before the sky turns fully black

### Midday (hard light)

When the sun sits high overhead (roughly 10am–3pm).

- **Traits**: strong direct light, sharp shadows, high contrast
- **Cons**: unfavorable for portraits (under-eye shadows, overexposure risk)
- **Uses**: minimalism, emphasizing strong patterns, using shadow itself as the subject

### Overcast days (soft light)

Clouds act as a natural diffuser.

- **Traits**: soft shadows, even lighting, flattering skin tones
- **Pros**: excellent for portraits and food photography
- **Cons**: can feel flat or lack mood

## Color temperature and white balance

### Color temperature

The color of light, measured in **Kelvin (K)**.

| Color temperature | Light color | Light source |
|--------|---------|------|
| 1000–2000K | Very warm orange | Candlelight, right at sunrise/sunset |
| 2500–3500K | Warm gold | Golden hour, tungsten bulbs |
| 4000–5000K | Neutral white | Fluorescent lighting, overcast days |
| 5500–6500K | Cool white | Direct sun on a clear day |
| 7000–9000K | Cool blue | Shade on an overcast day, blue hour |

### White balance

The feature that adjusts your camera to "recognize white as white."

| White balance setting | Best for |
|-----------------|------------|
| **Auto (AWB)** | General situations, fast shooting |
| **Daylight (5500K)** | Outdoors on a clear day |
| **Cloudy (6000K)** | Overcast days, shade |
| **Fluorescent** | Indoors under fluorescent lighting |
| **Incandescent/tungsten** | Yellow-toned bulb lighting |
| **Flash** | When using a camera flash |
| **Custom** | When precise color control is needed |

> **Creative use**: deliberately setting a different white balance can create a distinctive mood. For example, using the "Cloudy" setting on a clear day produces a warmer-toned photo.

## Light-modifying tools

### Using natural reflectors

- **White walls, white fabric**: soft, gentle reflected light
- **Silver reflector**: cool light, bright reflection
- **Gold reflector**: warm light, mimics golden hour
- **Grass, leaves**: reflect green light (be careful with portraits)

### Diffusion

Softening harsh direct light.

- **Natural diffusers**: overcast clouds, white curtains, paper
- **Artificial diffusers**: softboxes, umbrella lighting, frosted plastic

---

## The basics of artificial lighting

### Continuous light vs. strobe light

| | Continuous light | Strobe/flash (instant light) |
|------|--------|------------------------|
| Types | LED, fluorescent, tungsten | Speedlights, studio strobes |
| Preview | Possible | Not possible |
| Heat | High (tungsten) | Low |
| Brightness | Relatively weak | Very strong |
| Use | Video, studio work | Primarily photography |

### Fill flash

A technique that uses flash **as a supplement**, to brighten a subject's face in backlit or shaded conditions.

```
Setting: flash exposure compensation of -1 to -2 stops
(weaker than natural light → produces a natural-looking result)
```
