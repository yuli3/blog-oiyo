The trend analysis of [chapter 1](/en/academy-financial-accounting-advanced-ch1/) showed FA's receivables and inventories growing faster than revenue. This chapter checks that signal with turnover and days, and calculates how much cash is tied up in operations (unit: ₩100 million; 365 days a year).

## 1. Working capital

**Net working capital** is the current assets needed for operations minus operating current liabilities, commonly calculated as receivables + inventories − payables. FA's rose by ₩5 billion, from $120 + 160 - 100 = 180$ at the end of 20X1 to $150 + 200 - 120 = 230$ at the end of 20X2. That ₩5 billion is money earned as profit but tied up in operations instead of coming in as cash. Net working capital grew about 28%, faster than the 20% growth in revenue.

## 2. Turnover and days

Payables turnover should strictly be calculated on purchases; without purchases data, cost of sales is used instead. Purchases can be estimated as cost of sales + closing inventory − opening inventory (₩88 billion for FA, giving 45.6 days).

## 3. The cash conversion cycle

<FormulaBox
  label="Cash conversion cycle"
  formula="\text{CCC} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO} = 41.1 + 78.2 - 47.8 = 71.5\text{ days}"
  description="The time from paying for raw materials to receiving cash from customers. During that time the company funds its operations with its own or borrowed money."
/>

There are no opening balances for 20X1, so comparing on year-end balances gives DSO of $120 / 1{,}000 \times 365 ≈ 43.8$ days, DIO of $160 / 720 \times 365 ≈ 81.1$ days and DPO of $100 / 720 \times 365 ≈ 50.7$ days. On year-end balances for 20X2, DSO is 45.6 days, DIO 86.9 days and DPO 52.1 days. On a like-for-like basis, both receivables days and inventory days have lengthened.

## 4. Turning days into money

Each extra day ties up that much more cash.

<FormulaBox
  label="Cash effect of a change in days"
  formula="\text{Cash effect} = \Delta\text{Days} \times \frac{\text{Annual revenue (or cost of sales)}}{365}"
  description="One day of FA's 20X2 revenue is about ₩329 million. Cutting receivables days from 45.6 to 40 releases about ₩1.84 billion of cash."
/>

Cutting inventory days from 86.9 to 75 releases one day of cost of sales, about ₩230 million, × 11.9 days ≈ ₩2.74 billion. Together the two recover most of the ₩5 billion increase in net working capital. Managing working capital is a way to generate cash without new financing.

<Callout type="warning">
Higher turnover is not always better. Cutting inventory too far loses sales through stock-outs, and forcibly delaying payables damages supplier relationships or forfeits discounts. Factoring receivables or cutting inventory only at year-end also inflates turnover based on year-end balances. Supplier finance (reverse factoring) increases payables and makes DPO look longer, so check the notes' disclosure of supplier finance arrangements ([Principles of Accounting chapter 11](/en/academy-accounting-basics-ch4/)).
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

FD's annual revenue is ₩73 billion and cost of sales ₩54.75 billion. Average receivables are ₩10 billion, average inventory ₩9 billion and average payables ₩6 billion. What are DSO, DIO, DPO and the cash conversion cycle? If negotiation with suppliers lengthens DPO by 10 days, how much does cash increase?

One day of revenue is ₩200 million and one day of cost of sales ₩150 million. DSO is $100 / 2 = 50$ days, DIO $90 / 1.5 = 60$ days and DPO $60 / 1.5 = 40$ days. The cash conversion cycle is $50 + 60 - 40 = 70$ days. Lengthening DPO by 10 days raises payables by $10 \times 1.5 = 15$, i.e. ₩1.5 billion, and that much cash stays in the company.
