# Why Does Korean Tax Feel So Complex?

Korean tax calculations feel difficult because **the structure is not visible**. Every tax type has its own calculation flow. Those who have traced that flow from beginning to end — even once — understand tax very differently.

This article walks through the official calculation structures for 6 major Korean taxes, step by step, with live interactive inputs.

---

## 1. The Common Skeleton of All Taxes

Every tax type follows the same skeleton:

```
Income or asset value
  − Deductions
= Taxable income (Tax base)
  × Tax rate
= Calculated tax
  − Tax credits
= Final tax (amount due)
```

The differences lie in **which deductions** apply and **which rate schedule** is used.

## 3. Why Income Tax Is the Foundation

Income tax is the most fundamental tax type. Understanding its **progressive rate structure** makes every other rate schedule readable.

The key insight of progressive taxation: high rates do not apply to all income. Only income up to KRW 14 million is taxed at 6%. The excess above that threshold is taxed at 15%.

This is called the **marginal tax rate**.

<Callout type="info">
**What is the progressive deduction?** It simplifies the calculation when crossing rate brackets. The formula — Taxable income × Rate − Progressive deduction = Tax — lets you skip bracket-by-bracket calculation.
</Callout>

## 5. Common Points of Confusion

**Income Tax vs Corporate Tax**

Income tax applies to individuals; corporate tax applies to corporations. Sole proprietors pay income tax. Corporations pay corporate tax, and then shareholders pay dividend income tax on distributions — a form of double taxation.

**Who Really Pays VAT?**

VAT is remitted by businesses, but the actual economic burden falls on the final consumer. Businesses can deduct input VAT they paid on purchases, passing the net obligation down the chain.

**Capital Gains Tax and Holding Period**

Real estate held for less than one year is subject to a 70% rate — a punitive surcharge designed to deter short-term speculation. The long-term holding deduction (available after 3 years) can significantly reduce the tax.

<Callout type="tip">
**The core of tax planning**: which deductions apply and the timing of transactions determines the final tax owed. Understanding the formula reveals where the leverage points are.
</Callout>

**Inheritance Tax vs Gift Tax**

Both tax the transfer of wealth. Gifts occur during one's lifetime; inheritance occurs at death. Pre-death gifts within 10 years are added back to the inheritance tax base — long-term planning is essential.
