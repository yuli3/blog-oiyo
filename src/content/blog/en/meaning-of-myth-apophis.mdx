Apophis is the giant serpent who appears in Egyptian mythology as the greatest enemy of the sun god Ra. He represents the force of chaos that tries to swallow the world from outside created order. Apophis was not so much a god to be worshiped as a being to be ritually cursed and subdued, appearing as a threat repeated every day during Ra's night voyage.

## Core Myth — Representative Episode

The representative episode of Apophis is the nightly battle in which he attacks Ra's solar barque. As Ra passes through the underworld toward dawn, Apophis tries to stop or swallow the solar barque and plunge the world into darkness. The gods and guardians bind, stab, and cut him so that the sun can be reborn.

This battle does not end with a single victory. Apophis is chaos that returns every night. In ancient Egypt, there were rituals that damaged the image of Apophis or cursed his name, acts meant to symbolically control chaos. Order is not a state completed once and for all, but a balance that must be protected repeatedly.

## Reading as an Archetype — From Jung's Perspective

From Jung's perspective, Apophis is an archetype of the **shadow of chaos** and the **devouring unconscious**. He symbolizes forces that surge in without clear form, such as destructive impulses, depressive erosion, and fear that paralyzes a community. In modern terms, he resembles panic in a crisis, rumors that bring an organization to a halt, and recurring patterns of self-destruction.

The key to reading Apophis is that chaos cannot be eliminated completely. The fact that he must be fought again every night shows that anxiety and disorder also return repeatedly in human life. For this reason, the Apophis archetype can be read as a symbol of naming fear and controlling it through ritual, habit, and collective action.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Apophis an evil god?**

A. Rather than a god who was generally worshiped, he is a chaotic being who threatens created order. In Egyptian ritual practice, he was an object of cursing and subjugation.

**Q. Why does Apophis appear again every day?**

A. Because chaos was understood as a force that does not disappear after being defeated once, but keeps returning. The rebirth of the sun requires a daily victory.
