## Characteristics of the Part 2 Exam

Part 2 of the US labor law professional exam is an **essay and case-analysis format**. Unlike a pure memorization test, it evaluates your ability to **resolve a concrete workplace dispute using applicable federal and state law**.

Scoring criteria:
- Accuracy of issue identification
- Citation of relevant statutes and case law
- Logical derivation of a conclusion
- Answer structure and readability

---

## IRAC Answer Structure

The standard answer framework for law-school and professional credential exams is **IRAC**.

| Step | Meaning | Content |
|------|------|------|
| **I** | Issue | The specific legal question to be resolved in this fact pattern |
| **R** | Rule | Applicable statutes, regulations, case holdings, or agency guidance |
| **A** | Application | Systematically apply the rule to the facts |
| **C** | Conclusion | The final determination |

---

## Sample IRAC Answer

**Question**: Company A (30 employees) terminated Employee B due to frequent absences. B claims wrongful termination. Analyze whether the termination was lawful.

---

**[Issue]**

The issue is whether Company A's termination of Employee B was based on a legitimate, non-discriminatory reason and complied with applicable procedural requirements under the FLSA, FMLA, and applicable state law.

**[Rule]**

Federal law does not generally require just cause for termination (employment at will), but the FMLA (29 U.S.C. § 2615) prohibits terminating an employee for taking FMLA-protected leave. Additionally, the ADA (42 U.S.C. § 12101 et seq.) prohibits terminating an employee for disability-related absences when reasonable accommodation is available. Courts apply the McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting framework: the employee establishes a prima facie case; the employer must articulate a legitimate reason; the employee may then show pretext.

**[Application]**

Company A has 30 employees, so both the FMLA (50+ employees required) and WARN Act (100+ required) thresholds are not met, limiting federal leave-protection claims. However, ADA may apply if B's absences were disability-related. Key questions: (1) What was B's absence rate and cause? (2) Did A engage in the interactive process before terminating? (3) Was written notice of termination provided? (4) Were B's absences linked to a serious health condition that would qualify under state leave law?

**[Conclusion]**

If B's absences were unrelated to a protected medical condition and A provided adequate notice and documentation, the termination is likely lawful. If B's absences were ADA- or state-law-protected, and A failed to engage in the interactive process, the termination is potentially unlawful.

## Top Tested Areas

### FLSA / Wage-and-Hour Issues

**Frequently tested topics**:
- FLSA overtime exemption analysis (executive, administrative, professional)
- Calculating the regular rate of pay for overtime purposes
- Employer ULPs under the NLRA
- FLSA misclassification of employees as independent contractors
- Legality of the fluctuating workweek method

**Key case holdings to know**:
- *IBP, Inc. v. Alvarez*, 546 U.S. 21 (2005) — donning and doffing compensability
- *Christopher v. SmithKline Beecham*, 567 U.S. 142 (2012) — outside sales exemption
- *Encino Motorcars v. Navarro*, 138 S. Ct. 1134 (2018) — FLSA exemptions narrow construction

### NLRA / Collective Bargaining Issues

**Frequently tested topics**:
- Duty to bargain in good faith on mandatory subjects
- Legality of strike activity
- Employer ULPs and union ULPs
- Effect of a union-security clause

### Benefits Law Issues

- ERISA fiduciary duties and prohibited transactions
- COBRA continuation coverage eligibility and deadlines
- Workers' comp exclusive-remedy doctrine and exceptions
- Social Security disability vs. workers' comp interaction

## High-Score Tips

### Citing Case Law

You do not need a perfect citation. Writing "In *Chevron USA v. NRDC*, the Court established that courts defer to a reasonable agency interpretation of ambiguous statutes" accurately captures the rule's effect. **The principle behind the case matters more than the exact citation.**

### Using the Fact Pattern

You must **specifically apply the rule to the given facts**. An answer that states only abstract legal principles without connecting them to the facts will lose points.

### Writing a Clear Conclusion

Avoid "this may or may not be lawful depending on circumstances." **Examine arguments on both sides, then commit to a reasoned conclusion.** Graders reward decisiveness grounded in analysis.
