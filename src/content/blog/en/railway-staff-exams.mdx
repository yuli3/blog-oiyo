### Introduction: Railway Safety Starts with People

The core of moving a massive railway system is ultimately 'people.' No matter how good the safety management system is, if the physical and mental state of the personnel who drive and control the train is not ready, accidents cannot be prevented. Therefore, the Railway Safety Act grants strict physical and mental qualification requirements to **Railway Personnel**.

Understanding the differences between **Physical Exams** and **Aptitude Tests**, their validity periods, and who must take these exams is crucial for anyone studying the South Korean railway industry. Today, we will thoroughly explore the core of personnel safety management.

---

### 1. Definition of Railway Personnel (Article 2, item 10)

Again: not every employee is 'railway personnel'. The term covers people doing the work the law lists.

- **Drivers:** People engaged in driving rolling stock (train drivers, etc.).
- **Traffic controllers:** People who centrally control, direct and monitor train operation.
- **Passenger crew:** People providing on-board services to passengers.
- **Station staff:** People providing station services to passengers.
- **Work supervisors and operation safety managers:** People doing or scheduling facility construction and maintenance work on or near running lines.
- **Others** related to railway operation or facility management, as set by Presidential Decree.

### 2. Two Rounds of Physical and Aptitude Checks: At Licensing (Arts. 12, 15) and On the Job (Art. 23)

Physical exams and aptitude tests appear at **two stages**, and exams like to mix them up.

- **When getting a license:** Applicants must pass the Minister's physical exam (Art. 12) and the driving aptitude test (Art. 15). The Minister may have the physical exam done at a designated medical institution (Art. 13).
- **While working (Art. 23):** People doing driving, traffic control and other work set by Presidential Decree must take physical exams and aptitude tests **regularly**. Timing, methods and pass standards are set by ministerial ordinance (Art. 23(2)).
- **If they fail:** Operators must not let them do that work (Art. 23(3)).
- **Testing bodies:** Operators may entrust the tests to designated medical institutions and aptitude test bodies (Art. 23(5)).
- **Validity and cycles:** The age-based cycles are in the Enforcement Rule and its appendices. They change often, so learn the numbers from the current Enforcement Rule on the National Law Information Center. For reference, license applicants may only submit a physical exam result from the last **2 years** and a driving aptitude result from the last **10 years** (Enforcement Rule Art. 26).

### 3. Aptitude Test

The aptitude test goes beyond health and measures the mental fitness needed for railway work: cognition, judgment and reaction speed.

- **Who takes it:** License applicants (driving aptitude test, Art. 15) and workers in driving, control and other duties set by Presidential Decree (regular aptitude test, Art. 23).
- **Retake limits:** Anyone who fails or cheats cannot retake it for a set period (Art. 23(4), Art. 15(2)).
- **Main items:** Selective attention, distance perception, personality traits, etc.

### 4. Limits on Alcohol and Drugs (Article 41)

These are among the strictest safety rules.

- **Who (Art. 41(1)):** Drivers, traffic controllers, passenger crew, work supervisors, operation safety managers, people handling signals and switches or train composition at stations, and people inspecting or maintaining vehicles and facilities. People in practical apprenticeship are included.
- **Blood alcohol threshold (Art. 41(3)):** **0.02% or more** counts as having drunk. For work supervisors, operation safety managers and station signal and composition staff, the line is **0.03% or more**. For drugs, a positive result counts.
- **Refusing a check (Art. 41(2)):** Checks are done by **the Minister or a provincial governor**, and workers may not refuse. A license holder who refuses can have the license revoked or suspended (Art. 20(1) item 7).
