# Separate Tax vs. Comprehensive Tax Calculator

One of the most common questions for Korean investors and savers: **should I worry about the ₩20 million financial income threshold?**

In Korea, interest and dividend income below ₩20 million (2,000만원) per year is taxed separately at a flat 14% (15.4% including local tax), withheld at source — no filing needed. Once financial income exceeds ₩20 million, the entire amount gets added to your comprehensive income and taxed at progressive rates of 6–45%. This is called 금융소득종합과세 (financial income comprehensive taxation).

The key question is whether your **marginal tax rate** — the rate applied to the last won of income — is higher or lower than the flat 14%. If it's higher, staying below ₩20M is advantageous. If it's lower (rare for most salaried workers), comprehensive taxation may actually result in less tax.

## The ₩20 Million Threshold Explained

| Scenario | Tax Treatment |
|----------|---------------|
| Financial income ≤ ₩20M | Separate tax (14% + 1.4% local = 15.4%), no filing required |
| Financial income > ₩20M | Mandatory comprehensive taxation, file by May each year |

<Callout type="warning">
**The cliff effect**: Going even ₩10,000 over ₩20M doesn't just tax that small excess — the entire financial income (including the first ₩20M) is added to comprehensive income. Savvy investors intentionally manage their financial income to stay just under this threshold.
</Callout>

## When Each Is Better

### Separate tax is better when:
- Your comprehensive income pushes your marginal rate above 14%
- You earn ₩50M+ from employment/business (marginal rate: 24%+)
- Simplicity matters — no additional filing needed

### Comprehensive tax is better when (rare):
- Total income is very low (marginal rate below 14%)
- You have many deductions and credits that bring effective tax below 15.4%

## Strategies to Minimize Financial Income Tax

- **ISA accounts**: Interest and dividends earned inside an ISA account are excluded from the ₩20M threshold (first ₩2M tax-free, excess at 9.9%)
- **Pension savings (IRP/연금저축)**: Contributions reduce taxable income
- **Time your dividends**: Spread dividend-paying stocks across years to stay under ₩20M annually
- **Long-term insurance products**: Savings-type insurance held 10+ years may generate tax-exempt interest
